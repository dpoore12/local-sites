## symptom_1
Florida stopped treating a parenting schedule as a prize to be won. Since July 1, 2023, section 61.13 opens with a rebuttable presumption that equal time-sharing serves the child, and a parent who wants something different must rebut it by a preponderance of the evidence. The plan also has to fix school, health care, communication and exchange terms. Bring the real school week rather than a wish list.

## symptom_2
Guideline support rests on figures that go stale fast: net income, health insurance, day care, and the overnight count written into the plan. At 20 percent of the year's overnights the calculation switches to a gross-up method, and the transfer amount can move sharply in either direction. Reopening an order takes a guideline difference of at least 15 percent or $50, whichever is greater. A handshake does not amend anything.

## symptom_3
A notarized acknowledgment of paternity can be rescinded within 60 days. After that window closes it establishes paternity outright, and undoing it requires fraud, duress or a material mistake of fact. Until parentage is established under chapter 742, an unmarried father is not a natural guardian, and the mother holds primary residential care by statute. Support can also be ordered back two years.

## symptom_4
Permanent alimony is no longer one of the forms Florida recognizes. For initial petitions pending or filed on or after July 1, 2023, the statute lists temporary, bridge-the-gap, rehabilitative and durational support only. Durational awards are capped against the length of the marriage, and the amount may not exceed 35 percent of the gap between the parties' net incomes. Actual need and ability to pay are decided first.

## services_summary
Four questions carry most Hillsborough County family files, and the 2023 Legislature rewrote the statute behind each one. Parenting disputes now start from a presumption of equal overnights. Child support turns on an overnight count with a hard threshold buried inside it. Parentage decides whether an unmarried father is a legal guardian at all before any schedule becomes enforceable. Spousal support lost its permanent form and gained duration caps measured against the length of the marriage. Each page below gives the controlling section, the numbers a judge actually applies, and the extra steps the Thirteenth Judicial Circuit adds when minor children are involved.

## svc_child_custody_attorney_lede
Florida rewrote the starting point for parenting cases in 2023. A judge in this circuit now begins from equal overnights and reasons outward, which changes both what a parent has to prove and what a written plan is required to say.

## svc_child_custody_attorney_body
### Signs the fight is about the calendar, not the title

Custody left the Florida vocabulary years ago, and a written parenting plan took its place. Parents who ask who gets the children are usually asking three different questions at once: who makes the major decisions, how the overnights divide, and who carries the daily work of school, medicine and driving. Section 61.13 treats those as separate rulings, and a plan can split them in combinations that surprise people. Shared parental responsibility is the default on decision-making unless the court finds it detrimental to the child, and the statute attaches rebuttable presumptions of detriment to certain domestic violence convictions and enumerated offenses. The overnight schedule runs on its own analysis with its own presumption. So if the real dispute is a school-boundary address in Seminole Heights, a rotating shift, or who keeps the child during a summer block, that is a time-sharing question rather than a responsibility question, and naming it correctly changes what evidence matters.

### The presumption that arrived on July 1, 2023

Until that date the statute took no position on how many overnights each parent should have. Chapter 2023-301, the enacted form of CS/HB 1301, inserted a rebuttable presumption that equal time-sharing of a minor child is in the child's best interests. A parent seeking an unequal schedule now carries the job of rebutting that presumption by a preponderance of the evidence. The same act deleted the older requirement that a change in circumstances be unanticipated before a schedule could be modified, although the change must still be substantial and material. Where the parents do not agree, or the judge declines to approve what they agreed to, the court has to evaluate every best-interest factor listed in subsection (3) and place specific written findings in the order. That written-findings duty is worth understanding, because findings are what an appellate court reviews.

### What a plan must spell out before a judge will sign it

A minimum content list sits inside the statute. The plan has to describe in detail how the parents will share the daily tasks of child-rearing, set out the time-sharing schedule, and designate who is responsible for health care, school-related matters and other activities. School-related matters include the physical address used for school-boundary determination, which is why an intended move belongs in the plan rather than in a later motion. The plan must also describe the methods and technologies each parent will use to communicate with the child, and it must designate authorized locations for exchanges. Loose language defeats all of this. Phrases such as reasonable notice and as mutually agreed generate enforcement motions later, while a plan naming an exchange point, a cutoff time and a make-up rule for a missed weekend usually does not.

### Your opening two weeks in this circuit

Start with the standing temporary order that travels with a petition involving minor children. It directs both parents to complete an approved parent-education course within 60 days of filing, and the court will not sign a final judgment unless both certificates are in the file. Section 61.21 sets the course at a minimum of four hours, due within 45 days of filing for the petitioner and within 45 days of service for everyone else. That same standing order requires mediation before a temporary-relief hearing and before a final hearing. Family filings are handled at the George E. Edgecomb Courthouse on East Twiggs Street downtown, a short trip from Davis Islands or Ybor City, with the clerk's family division on the first floor in Room 101 and the circuit's domestic relations case management unit in Room 208.

### The mistake that quietly hands over the schedule

The costly error is almost never dramatic. A parent leaves the marital home, lets the children stay put, and accepts an informal midweek dinner arrangement while things cool off. Six months later that arrangement is the child's routine, and routine is evidence. Because a later change requires a substantial and material change of circumstances, the temporary pattern you tolerated becomes the pattern you must now prove is wrong. Anything you sign or acquiesce to in the first weeks should be labeled temporary in writing, dated, and tied to a review point.

### When one parent wears a uniform

With two joint combatant commands and an air refueling wing at MacDill, this is a routine fact pattern here rather than an exotic one. Florida adopted the Uniform Deployed Parents Custody and Visitation Act as part IV of chapter 61 in 2018, and section 61.713 states flatly that a court may not consider a parent's past deployment or possible future deployment in determining the best interest of the child. A deploying parent must notify the other parent of a pending deployment in a record within seven days of receiving notice, then propose a plan allocating custodial responsibility during the absence. A temporary agreement granting that responsibility terminates automatically 30 days after the deploying parent gives notice of return, and the court is directed to grant reasonable contact in the interim. Federal law supplies a second tool: on proper application, including a commanding officer's letter, a court shall stay a civil proceeding, including a child custody proceeding, for not less than 90 days. Separately, a permanent change of station triggers the relocation statute once the new residence is at least 50 miles away for 60 consecutive days.

### How the fee side works in a Florida parenting case

Domestic relations work in this state is billed hourly or flat under a signed written contract, and bar rule 4-1.5(f)(3)(A) forbids tying a fee in a domestic relations matter to securing a divorce or to the size of a support or property outcome. Section 61.16(1) then allows the court to order one parent to pay a reasonable sum toward the other's fees, suit money and costs after weighing both parties' financial resources, so an income gap is an argument rather than a wall. Court costs sit outside the fee entirely. The clerk here charges $408 to open a dissolution and $300 for another family action, and section 44.108(2) sets court-ordered mediation at $120 or $60 per person per session by combined income, with nothing assessed against an indigent party.

## svc_child_support_attorney_lede
Florida support is a formula, which sounds reassuring until you see how many of its inputs are contestable. Income, insurance, day care and the overnight count each move the number, and one threshold inside the statute moves it a long way.

## svc_child_support_attorney_body
### Signs the order and the household have drifted apart

Support disputes rarely begin with the guidelines. They begin with a raise nobody disclosed, a layoff, a day care that closed, a child who moved onto one parent's insurance, or a schedule that quietly became something other than what the order describes. Section 61.30 builds the obligation from each parent's net income after statutorily allowable deductions, then adds day care and health insurance premiums for the child and divides the total by income share. Every one of those inputs is documentary. Before deciding whether an order is wrong, gather what proves the inputs: pay records, the last three years of returns, the insurance declaration, day care invoices, and a calendar showing where the child actually slept for the past year.

### The overnight count, and the line at 20 percent

Here is the threshold that decides more cases than any argument about expenses. Section 61.30(11)(b)8 defines a substantial amount of time as a parent exercising time-sharing at least 20 percent of the overnights of the year. Cross that line and the calculation changes method entirely. Each parent's basic obligation is multiplied by 1.5, each parent's percentage of overnights is cross-multiplied against the other's obligation, the difference becomes the transfer payment, and day care and insurance are then reallocated. The practical effect is that a handful of overnights near the boundary can swing the monthly figure more than a five-figure difference in salary. Below the line the court is not powerless, because one of the enumerated deviation factors covers a child who spends a significant amount of time, but less than 20 percent of the overnights, with one parent.

### Income a court can assign to a parent who is not earning it

Voluntary unemployment or underemployment opens the door to imputed income, and the statute supplies a fallback presumption: absent reliable evidence of income history, the court applies a rebuttable presumption of the median income of year-round full-time workers as reported by the United States Census Bureau. Two other numbers belong in the same conversation. A deviation of more than 5 percent from the guideline figure requires a written finding explaining why the guideline amount would be unjust or inappropriate. And an obligation consuming more than 55 percent of a payor's gross income is itself a listed deviation factor, which is the closest thing the guidelines have to a ceiling.

### The first two weeks, in order

Mandatory disclosure under rule 12.285 is the spine of the case, and it runs on a clock. The documents are served on the other party within 45 days of service of the initial pleading on the respondent. The affidavit form depends on gross annual income: form 12.902(b) below $50,000 and form 12.902(c) at $50,000 or more. The affidavit requirement cannot be waived by agreement, and unlike most disclosure the affidavits are filed with the court rather than merely exchanged. Expect to produce three years of returns, pay stubs covering the three months before service, three months of checking statements and twelve months for other accounts, retirement statements, and credit card statements. If a temporary relief hearing is coming, the responding party's deadline compresses to two business days before the hearing when documents are hand delivered.

### The handshake that becomes arrears

Parents renegotiate constantly and almost never on paper. One agrees to drop the payment while the other covers the car insurance, the arrangement holds for two years, and then it does not. The order was never modified, so the unpaid difference is arrears, and the paying parent's evidence is a text thread. Two statutory facts follow from that. Reopening the guidelines requires a difference of at least 15 percent or $50, whichever is greater, between the existing order and a fresh calculation. And on an initial determination the court may reach back to the date the parents stopped sharing a household with the child, up to 24 months before the petition was filed, though it should consider an installment plan for what accumulates.

### What the other side is preparing while you decide

If the Department of Revenue is involved, an administrative track may be running parallel to anything filed at the courthouse. A private opponent will be doing something narrower: reading your affidavit against your bank statements, counting overnights from your own text messages, and looking for the perquisite, the bonus, or the side income the affidavit omitted. That is why the affidavit deserves more care than any pleading in the file. Note also that this circuit treats mediation as a required step in post-judgment cases that do not involve the Department of Revenue, and a supplemental petition must be filed and served before mediation can happen. Parties are expected to attempt agreement before a general magistrate hearing will be set.

### Filing costs, fee shifting, and one narrow exception

A support matter is billed hourly or flat under a signed written contract, because bar rule 4-1.5(f)(3)(A) bars any fee in a domestic relations matter contingent on securing a divorce or on the amount of alimony, support or a property settlement. There is one carve-out worth knowing: the rule permits an outcome-based fee for collecting a balance already due under a support, alimony or other financial order, so collection work is treated differently from the case that created the obligation. Section 61.16(1) lets the court shift a reasonable sum for fees, suit money and costs after considering both parties' financial resources. Clerk costs are separate and modest by comparison, at $300 to open another family action, $50 to reopen a closed family file, and $188 for a writ of garnishment.

## svc_paternity_attorney_lede
Parentage is the gate every other order passes through. Until it is legally established, a Florida court has nothing to attach a parenting plan, a support obligation or a decision-making right to, and the 2023 session sharpened what that means.

## svc_paternity_attorney_body
### How to tell whether parentage is actually settled

Being on the birth certificate, paying for everything, and having the child four nights a week are not the same as being a legal parent. Chapter 742 recognizes a small set of routes: a notarized voluntary acknowledgment, an administrative or judicial determination, or a court order in a proceeding brought under section 742.011. Anything else is a factual relationship without legal standing. Start by locating the documents that would settle it: the birth record, any acknowledgment signed at the hospital, notices from the Department of Revenue, and any prior order from any state. Then build a plain timeline of the birth date, the periods the parents lived together or apart, and who performed which parenting tasks.

### Sixty days to undo an acknowledgment

A notarized voluntary acknowledgment creates a rebuttable presumption of paternity the moment it is signed, and it can be rescinded within 60 days after signing or the date of a related administrative or judicial proceeding, whichever comes first. Once that window closes the acknowledgment establishes paternity, and it may be challenged only on the basis of fraud, duress or material mistake of fact. The burden sits on the person challenging it, and the support obligation is not suspended while the challenge is pending unless the court finds good cause. One more provision catches people off guard: however paternity was established, parental responsibility, support, a parenting plan and a time-sharing schedule still have to be established in an action under section 742.011.

### The guardianship line the 2023 session redrew

Chapter 2023-209 took effect on July 1, 2023 and amended the natural guardianship statute. The mother of a child born outside marriage is a natural guardian, and so is a father who has established paternity under section 742.011 or section 742.10. Where the father has not established paternity, the mother is the natural guardian and is entitled to primary residential care and custody unless a court orders otherwise. Read that sequence carefully, because it is the reason an unmarried father who waits is negotiating from a weaker position than he believes. The same chapter law reworked section 742.011 so that after a child is born, a parent may ask the circuit court to determine parental responsibility and support and to create a parenting plan and time-sharing schedule under chapter 61.

### Testing, a 95 percent presumption, and a 10-day objection

Scientific testing under section 742.12 is ordered on request in a contested case, and the statute puts real weight on the result. A statistical probability of paternity of 95 percent or more creates a rebuttable presumption of biological fatherhood and can support a summary judgment on the issue. Written objections to the test results must be filed at least 10 days before a hearing at which they will be offered, and that short deadline is easy to miss. If the results exclude the alleged father, the case against him is dismissed with prejudice. The court apportions testing costs, so the expense is not automatically the requesting party's problem.

### What the first filing should actually ask for

A petition that establishes paternity and stops there leaves the client where he started. The pleading should ask for the parenting plan and time-sharing schedule, parental responsibility, support with a guidelines worksheet, and the child's health insurance in one instrument. In this circuit a respondent in a paternity case must file an answer within 20 days of service, so a served father has less room than he expects. Once minor children are in the file, the standing temporary order applies: parent education within 60 days, mandatory financial disclosure, and mediation before a temporary-relief hearing or a final hearing. The circuit's case management unit publishes a pro se reference guide and answers procedural questions by phone and email, though it is not staffed by attorneys and cannot give legal advice.

### Support that can reach back two years

On an initial determination, whether in a parentage action or a dissolution, the court has discretion to award support retroactive to the date the parents stopped living in the same household with the child, capped at 24 months before the petition was filed. The court applies the guidelines schedule in effect at the hearing, subject to the obligor demonstrating actual income during the retroactive period, and an obligor who cannot document that period will be measured by present income. Payments already made directly to the other parent, to the child, or to third parties for the child's benefit are credited. The statute also invites the court to consider an installment plan rather than a lump demand.

### How the fee rules apply to a parentage case

This is a domestic relations matter, so bar rule 4-1.5(f)(3)(A) applies and the fee cannot ride on the outcome; the engagement is hourly or flat under a signed written contract. Section 61.16(1) fee shifting reaches chapter 61 relief sought in the case, which is one more reason to plead the parenting and support relief rather than parentage alone. Costs to expect beyond the fee include a clerk charge of $300 to open another family action, $10 per summons and $7 per subpoena, the apportioned share of genetic testing, and mediation at the statutory rate of $120 or $60 per person per session depending on combined income.

## svc_spousal_support_attorney_lede
Almost everything the public believes about Florida alimony describes a statute that no longer exists. The 2023 rewrite removed the permanent form, capped duration against the length of the marriage, and put a percentage ceiling on the amount.

## svc_spousal_support_attorney_body
### How to tell which form is even available

Before arguing about amount, work out which of the statutory forms your facts can support. Section 61.08 lists four: temporary, bridge-the-gap, rehabilitative and durational. Permanent alimony is not among them. The court must first make a specific factual determination that the requesting spouse has an actual need and that the other spouse has the ability to pay, and the burden of proving both rests on the party asking. Only after that finding does the court reach the type, the amount and the duration. A demand written without those two findings in mind is a negotiating position, not a claim.

### The four forms Florida kept

Bridge-the-gap support helps a spouse move from married to single life and may not exceed two years, and it cannot be modified in amount or duration once awarded. Rehabilitative support requires a specific and defined written rehabilitative plan, and it may not exceed five years. Durational support provides for a set period and is unavailable at all for a marriage lasting under three years. Temporary support covers the pendency of the case. Each subsection carries its own proof burden, which is why an early decision about which form to pursue shapes what evidence the case needs.

### Duration measured against the length of the marriage

The statute defines its own categories. A marriage under 10 years is short-term, 10 years up to 20 is moderate-term, and 20 years or more is long-term, measured from the date of marriage to the date the petition is filed. Durational support may then run no longer than 50 percent of the length of a short-term marriage, 60 percent of a moderate-term marriage, or 75 percent of a long-term marriage. Extending beyond those caps requires clear and convincing evidence of exceptional circumstances, applying factors the statute lists. This applies to all initial petitions for dissolution pending or filed on or after July 1, 2023, the effective date of chapter 2023-315, the enacted form of CS/SB 1416.

### The ceiling on the amount, and the floor under the payor

Two limits bracket the number. The amount of durational support is the obligee's reasonable need, or not more than 35 percent of the difference between the parties' net incomes, whichever is less. Separately, an award may not leave the paying spouse with significantly less net income than the net income of the recipient unless the court makes written findings explaining exceptional circumstances. Both limits are arithmetic applied to disclosed income, which pushes the fight backward into the financial affidavit and the tax returns.

### Retirement as a reason to reduce or end an award

The 2023 legislation added a defined retirement pathway that did not exist before. A court may reduce or terminate an award on written findings that the payor has reached normal retirement age as defined by the Social Security Administration, or the customary retirement age for that profession, and has taken demonstrative and measurable steps toward retiring or has actually retired. The payor proves those elements by a preponderance, after which the burden shifts to the recipient to show that the award should continue. A petition may be filed in reasonable anticipation of retirement, but no earlier than six months before it. A separate provision allows reduction or termination where the recipient has been in a supportive relationship within the preceding 365 days.

### Your first two weeks, and the disclosure that decides them

One spouse must have resided in the state for six months before a petition is filed, and no final judgment can be entered until at least 20 days have elapsed from that filing unless the court finds injustice would result from the delay. There is no separation period to wait out. What actually governs the calendar is rule 12.285 disclosure, due within 45 days of service on the respondent, with form 12.902(b) below $50,000 of gross annual income and form 12.902(c) at or above it. Property runs alongside support. Section 61.075 directs the court to set apart nonmarital assets, then begin from the premise of an equal split of marital assets, with the cut-off date being the earlier of a valid separation agreement or the filing of the petition. Retirement benefits accrued during the marriage are marital, and where the marriage overlapped 10 years of creditable uniformed service, section 61.076 requires the judgment to identify the member, certify that federal servicemember protections were observed if the decree issued while the member was on active duty and unrepresented, and state the share in dollars or as a percentage of disposable retired pay. Federal law caps what the pay center will send under all court orders at 50 percent of disposable retired pay.

### Why nobody in this field may charge on the outcome

Bar rule 4-1.5(f)(3)(A) prohibits a fee in a domestic relations matter that is contingent on securing a divorce or on the amount of alimony, support or a property settlement in lieu of it, and rule 4-1.5(f)(2) requires a signed written contract. That leaves hourly or flat arrangements. The counterweight is section 61.16(1), which lets the court order one party to pay a reasonable sum toward the other's fees, suit money and costs after considering the financial resources of both, so the lower-earning spouse in a long marriage is not automatically priced out. Costs outside the fee include the clerk's $408 dissolution filing charge here, $295 for a counter petition, and statutory mediation at $120 or $60 per person per session by combined income band.
