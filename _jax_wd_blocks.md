## symptom_1

Signal Four Analytics counted 154 traffic deaths in Duval County during 2024, and lane departures accounted for 63 of them. When a crash kills someone, the officer's long-form report is due to the state within ten days, and for the next sixty days only a short list of people may obtain it. Write down the investigating agency and the report number before anything else.

## symptom_2

Chapter 440 makes an employer's compensation liability exclusive, and the intentional-tort exception has to be proved by clear and convincing evidence. A claim against a staffing firm, a site owner, or an equipment supplier is a separate matter with separate proof. Keep the incident number, the shift records, and the name of every company working that site.

## symptom_3

Section 768.21(8) still bars a child aged 25 or older from recovering for grief when a parent dies from medical negligence, and bars the parent of an adult child too. Repeal cleared the Florida House in January 2026 and then died in the Senate. Ages, survivor order, and the presuit calendar therefore decide a great deal here.

## symptom_4

One person files: the estate's personal representative, on behalf of every survivor and the estate itself. The complaint has to identify all of them and plead each relationship. Recoverable losses then differ by relationship and by age, so list the spouse, children, parents, and any dependent relatives with birthdates before anyone signs, releases, or accepts a proposal.

## services_summary

Four subjects blur together after a death in Duval County, and Florida treats each one differently. A fatal crash turns on the officer's report, the road agency, and the fault bar the Legislature rewrote in 2023. A workplace death starts inside chapter 440 and asks who besides the employer was on that site. A death after treatment carries a presuit notice, a ninety-day waiting period, and the noneconomic-damages exclusion in section 768.21(8). Damages themselves follow relationship and age rather than grief. Confusing the four costs a family a deadline, a record that gets purged, or a survivor's entire share.

## svc_fatal_car_accident_claim_lede

A deadly crash on an Arlington arterial or a Southside interstate ramp starts three clocks at once. The investigating agency builds a record, the liability insurer opens a file, and nothing can be sued over until a probate estate exists with someone appointed to run it.

## svc_fatal_car_accident_claim_body

### The claim belongs to the estate, and only one person files it

[Section 768.20](https://www.leg.state.fl.us/Statutes/index.cfm?App_mode=Display_Statute&URL=0700-0799/0768/Sections/0768.20.html) assigns the action to the decedent's personal representative, who recovers on behalf of the survivors and the estate together. Section 768.21 opens by requiring that every potential beneficiary, the estate included, be identified in the complaint with each relationship pleaded. A sibling who has been fielding the adjuster's calls has no authority from that fact alone.

Who gets appointed follows an order. [Section 733.301](https://www.leg.state.fl.us/Statutes/index.cfm?App_mode=Display_Statute&URL=0700-0799/0733/Sections/0733.301.html) puts the will's nominee first in a testate estate, then a person chosen by a majority in interest of those entitled to the estate, then a devisee. Where there is no will, preference runs to the surviving spouse, then to a person selected by a majority in interest of the heirs, then to the heir nearest in degree. The Fourth Judicial Circuit publishes [probate self-help material](https://www.jud4.org/self-help/probate), including an affidavit of heirs, and the Duval County Clerk lists [formal administration at $401](https://www.duvalclerk.com/departments/civil-court-services/probate) with summary administration at $346 for an estate of $1,000 or more.

### How the crash report is built, and who may read it for sixty days

Under [section 316.066](https://www.leg.state.fl.us/Statutes/index.cfm?App_mode=Display_Statute&URL=0300-0399/0316/Sections/0316.066.html), a Florida Traffic Crash Report, Long Form must be completed and submitted to the department within 10 days after the officer finishes investigating a crash involving death or injury. That report has to carry the vehicles, the parties and passengers, witnesses, the officer's badge number and agency, and the insurers for each party.

Then the statute closes it. Crash reports revealing personal information about the people involved are confidential and exempt from public records for 60 days after filing. The same subsection makes them immediately available to the parties, their legal representatives, their licensed insurance agents, their insurers, law enforcement, victim services programs, and government agencies. A grieving relative who is not a party to the crash can be told no at the counter while an insurer already holds a copy.

### What this county's numbers say about the crash that killed someone

FDOT District Two's [2024 Duval crash facts](https://trafficsafetyteam.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/CTSP-2024-Duval-Crash-Facts.pdf) record 38,584 crashes, 154 fatalities, and 498 serious injuries against a county population of 1,055,159. Impaired driving is tied to 53 deaths, pedestrians and bicyclists to 51, intersections to 38, and motorcycle and scooter riders to 37.

The city's [Vision Zero Action Plan](https://www.jacksonville.gov/departments/planning-department/transportation-planning/ped-bike-planning/vision-zero-action-plan-(vzap)) reviewed 2018 through 2023 and counted 921 deaths across 196,453 crashes, with 211 of the dead on foot. Sixty percent of the severe pedestrian and bicycle crashes happened at night, and drugs or alcohol figured in 54 percent of fatal vehicle crashes. None of that assigns blame in one case. It does explain why lighting, crossing design, and speed evidence matter on a Springfield or Mandarin street in a way a plain rear-end description never captures.

### Two years, and the 2023 change that produced them

[Section 95.11](https://www.leg.state.fl.us/Statutes/index.cfm?App_mode=Display_Statute&URL=0000-0099/0095/Sections/0095.11.html) lists an action for wrongful death, and an action founded on negligence, in the two-year column. Negligence used to sit in the four-year column. Chapter 2023-15 moved it, and the [statutory note](https://www.flhouse.gov/Statutes/2023/0095.11/) confirms the amendments apply to causes of action accruing after March 24, 2023, the date [the Governor approved the bill](https://www.flsenate.gov/Session/Bill/2023/837). Deaths before and after that date can sit under different rules, which is a reason to write the exact date down rather than estimate it.

### Above half the fault, the recovery disappears

The same 2023 act added subsection (6) to [section 768.81](https://www.leg.state.fl.us/Statutes/index.cfm?App_mode=Display_Statute&URL=0700-0799/0768/Sections/0768.81.html). A party found more than 50 percent at fault for their own harm now recovers nothing, where the old rule merely reduced the award. Below that line, contributory fault still diminishes economic and noneconomic damages proportionately.

Defense lawyers also work the nonparty rules. To put an absent driver or contractor on the verdict form, a defendant must affirmatively plead that nonparty's fault and then prove it at trial by a preponderance of the evidence. Evidence about a driver who was never sued is not automatically admitted, and the pleading requirement is worth checking early.

### When the other vehicle or the road belongs to a government body

[Section 768.28](https://www.leg.state.fl.us/Statutes/index.cfm?App_mode=Display_Statute&URL=0700-0799/0768/Sections/0768.28.html) waives immunity only so far. Payment is limited to $200,000 for any one person and $300,000 for everyone injured in the same incident, and anything above that can be paid only by a further act of the Legislature, the process known as a claim bill. A wrongful death claim must be presented in writing to the Department of Financial Services within 2 years, while claims against a municipality or county go to the agency itself. In a death case, ninety days of silence counts as a denial, and the limitations period is tolled while the claim sits.

Those figures nearly changed. HB 145 in the 2026 session would have raised them, passing the House 104 to 7 and the Senate 36 to 0, then clearing a concurrence vote of 108 to 1 before [the Governor vetoed it on June 30, 2026](https://www.flsenate.gov/Session/Bill/2026/145). The older limits therefore still govern.

### Coverage, and how the state's fee schedule applies to a death case

[Section 627.4137](https://www.leg.state.fl.us/Statutes/index.cfm?App_mode=Display_Statute&URL=0600-0699/0627/Sections/0627.4137.html) gives a written request real force. Within 30 days a liability insurer must supply a sworn statement naming itself and each insured, stating the limits, disclosing any coverage defense it believes it has, and attaching the policy. Excess and umbrella coverage is included, and the statement must be amended when new facts surface.

Florida's fee rule for these cases is a published sliding scale rather than one flat number, with a lower first tier while the matter resolves before an answer and a court-approval route for anything above the schedule. Under [section 768.26](https://www.leg.state.fl.us/Statutes/index.cfm?App_mode=Display_Statute&Search_String=&URL=0700-0799/0768/Sections/0768.26.html), the representative pays fees and litigation expenses out of the recovery, deducted from each survivor's award and the estate's award in proportion. The pricing page here lays the tiers out line by line.

## svc_fatal_work_accident_claim_lede

Two bodies of Florida law open at once when someone dies on the job in this county. Chapter 440 governs what the employer owes, and it usually forbids a lawsuit against that employer. Everyone else who was on that site is a separate question.

## svc_fatal_work_accident_claim_body

### Why the employer is usually the one party who cannot be sued

[Section 440.11](https://www.leg.state.fl.us/Statutes/index.cfm?App_mode=Display_Statute&URL=0400-0499/0440/Sections/0440.11.html) makes the compensation liability exclusive, in place of all other liability to the employee, the legal representative, a spouse, parents, dependents, and next of kin. Two exceptions matter after a death. Where an employer failed to secure payment of compensation, the legal representative may elect an action at law instead, and the defense cannot then blame a fellow employee or the worker's own comparative negligence.

The second exception is narrow by design. An employer's act counts as an intentional tort only on clear and convincing proof of deliberate intent to injure, or of conduct the employer knew, from prior similar accidents or explicit warnings identifying a known danger, was virtually certain to kill or injure. The statute adds that the worker must have been unaware of the risk because the employer concealed or misrepresented it. Prior incident reports and safety warnings are therefore evidence, not background.

### What the compensation side actually pays a family

[Section 440.16](https://www.leg.state.fl.us/Statutes/index.cfm?App_mode=Display_Statute&URL=0400-0499/0440/Sections/0440.16.html) covers actual funeral expenses up to $7,500, payable within 14 days after the bill arrives. Death benefits are capped at $150,000 in total. A surviving spouse with no child receives 50 percent of the average weekly wage, with another 16 2/3 percent added for a child or children, and each child receives 33 1/3 percent where no spouse survives. Each dependent parent receives 25 percent while dependency continues, and a brother, sister, or grandchild receives 15 percent.

Timing is built into the statute. The death must result from the accident within 1 year, or follow continuous disability and result from the accident within 5 years. A surviving spouse who remarries takes a lump sum equal to 26 weeks of compensation instead of further indemnity benefits. Those amounts are the reason a family sometimes believes the file is closed when the civil questions have never been asked.

### The report that exists within hours of the death

Florida runs no OSHA-approved plan of its own for private employers, as the [state plan list](https://www.osha.gov/stateplans) shows, so a private-sector death here is federal OSHA's business. A work-related fatality [must be reported within 8 hours](https://www.osha.gov/report). That produces a federal record with a date stamp long before a family has decided anything, and an inspection file that may name conditions the employer already knew about.

The county's medical examiner builds the other early record. The [District 4 office at 4368 North Davis Street](https://www.jacksonville.gov/departments/finance/liaison-agencies/medical-examiner) covers Clay, Duval, and Nassau counties, and the same Jacksonville office serves District 3 in Columbia and Hamilton. Autopsy reports are requested online or in writing. Keep the examiner's case number with the employer's incident number, because they answer different questions.

### The parties who are not the employer

A death at a Northside distribution warehouse, a port terminal, or a Mandarin re-roof often involves a staffing agency, a general contractor, a property owner, a delivery driver, or an equipment manufacturer. None of those inherit the employer's immunity by standing nearby. Their exposure runs through [section 768.19](https://www.leg.state.fl.us/Statutes/index.cfm?App_mode=Display_Statute&URL=0700-0799/0768/Sections/0768.19.html), which makes anyone whose wrongful act, negligence, default, or breach of warranty caused the death liable in damages.

Comparative fault still applies to those defendants. Since 2023, a claimant found more than half at fault for their own harm recovers nothing under section 768.81, and a defendant who wants an absent company blamed must plead that nonparty's fault and prove it. Contracts, subcontracts, and site-control documents often decide that argument, and they are held by the companies rather than the family.

### The survivor list has a legal definition and a birthdate problem

[Section 768.18](https://www.leg.state.fl.us/Statutes/index.cfm?App_mode=Display_Statute&URL=0700-0799/0768/Sections/0768.18.html) counts a spouse, children, and parents as survivors, along with blood relatives and adopted siblings who were partly or wholly dependent on the person for support or services. For this act, a minor child means a child under 25. Section 768.21 then measures each survivor's lost support and services using the relationship, the decedent's probable net income available to that survivor, and the replacement value of the services performed.

Dependency has to be documented while the records still exist. Deposit histories, rent receipts, school and childcare arrangements, and the household chores a person actually did are all part of it. A niece who lived in the home and a grown son who never did can end up in very different positions.

### A public employer changes the deadline before anything else

Where the work was for a state agency, the county, or an independent municipal authority, [section 768.28](https://www.leg.state.fl.us/Statutes/index.cfm?App_mode=Display_Statute&URL=0700-0799/0768/Sections/0768.28.html) controls. A written claim must be presented, damages are limited to $200,000 per person and $300,000 per incident, and any excess needs a legislative claim bill. In a wrongful death matter, the claim goes to the Department of Financial Services within 2 years, and ninety days without a decision is treated as a denial.

### What the first two weeks look like, and how fees are measured

Preserve the employer's incident report, the training and equipment records, the schedule for that shift, photographs, and the names of coworkers who saw it happen. Keep every letter from the compensation carrier without signing anything that resolves more than medical bills. Ask whether an estate has been opened, since the wrongful death action cannot proceed without a personal representative.

Fees follow the state's published schedule rather than a negotiation, with tiers that fall as a recovery grows and a court-approval path for anything outside them. [Section 768.26](https://www.leg.state.fl.us/Statutes/index.cfm?App_mode=Display_Statute&Search_String=&URL=0700-0799/0768/Sections/0768.26.html) then has the representative pay fees and expenses from the recovery, deducted in proportion from each award. Compensation benefits and a civil recovery are accounted for separately, and the lien question belongs on the table early.

## svc_medical_negligence_death_claim_lede

Florida's hardest sentence to say out loud is this one. A hospital or a physician can cause a death here and owe the grown children nothing at all for their grief. That exclusion survived a veto in 2025 and a second attempt in 2026.

## svc_medical_negligence_death_claim_body

### What section 768.21(8) removes, exactly

Section 768.21 gives minor children, and all children where no spouse survives, a claim for lost parental companionship, instruction, and guidance plus mental pain and suffering. It gives each parent of a deceased minor child a claim for mental pain and suffering, and each parent of an adult child the same claim if there are no other survivors. Then [subsection (8)](https://www.leg.state.fl.us/Statutes/index.cfm?App_mode=Display_Statute&URL=0700-0799/0768/Sections/0768.21.html) withdraws both of those where the claim is one for medical negligence as defined by section 766.106(1).

Age decides who is affected, because [section 768.18](https://www.leg.state.fl.us/Statutes/index.cfm?App_mode=Display_Statute&URL=0700-0799/0768/Sections/0768.18.html) defines a minor child as a child under 25 for this act. So a 30-year-old whose widowed mother dies from negligent hospital care has no claim for that loss. Neither do the parents of a 26-year-old who leaves no spouse and no children. The exclusion is why some families in Duval County are told there is no case when what they were told is that there are no noneconomic damages.

### The 2025 veto and the 2026 death in Senate Rules

Repeal has come close twice. HB 6017 passed the House 104 to 6 in March 2025 and the Senate 33 to 4 on May 1, was presented on May 23, and was [vetoed on May 29, 2025](https://www.flsenate.gov/Session/Bill/2025/6017). The Governor's office framed the veto around the absence of caps on damages and attorney fees in the bill, in a [statement issued that day](https://www.flgov.com/eog/news/press/2025/governor-ron-desantis-issues-veto-safeguard-florida-against-misuse-medical).

A second run followed immediately. HB 6003 cleared the Civil Justice and Claims Subcommittee 16 to 2 and the Judiciary Committee 15 to 1, according to the [House staff analysis](https://www.flsenate.gov/Session/Bill/2026/6003/Analyses/h6003c.JDC.PDF). The chamber passed it 88 to 17 on January 15, 2026, and the official [House bill history](https://www.leg.state.fl.us/data/session/2026/citator/Daily/hsehist.pdf) records that it died in Senate Rules on March 13, 2026. Anyone reading a 2025 news story that says the ban was lifted is reading about a bill that never became law.

### What remains recoverable when grief is off the table

Economic loss does not disappear with subsection (8). Each survivor may still recover lost support and services, past and future, and a survivor who paid medical or funeral bills may recover those. The estate has its own column: lost earnings from the date of injury to death with interest, and prospective net accumulations where the survivors include a spouse or lineal descendants, or where the decedent was not a minor child, no lost support is recoverable, and a parent survives.

Those categories reward documents. Pay records, tax returns, retirement statements, the value of care a parent provided to a household, and the bills that became a charge against the estate all carry weight that a narrative cannot. Estate awards are also subject to creditor claims filed under probate law, which affects what a family actually receives.

### The presuit process is a second calendar, not a formality

Before a complaint can be filed, [section 766.203](https://www.leg.state.fl.us/Statutes/index.cfm?App_mode=Display_Statute&URL=0700-0799/0766/Sections/0766.203.html) requires an investigation and a verified written medical expert opinion corroborating reasonable grounds, submitted when the notice of intent is mailed. That expert review takes complete records, which is the practical reason record requests come first.

[Section 766.106](https://www.leg.state.fl.us/Statutes/index.cfm?App_mode=Display_Statute&URL=0700-0799/0766/Sections/0766.106.html) then governs the notice itself. Service must go by certified mail, tracked mail, a commercial carrier, or a process server, and the notice includes provider lists and the statutory authorization form. No suit may be filed for 90 days while the prospective defendant investigates. The response either rejects the claim, offers settlement, or offers arbitration with liability admitted, and silence for 90 days counts as a final rejection. Mailing the notice tolls the limitations period through that window, and a stipulated extension tolls it further.

### The outer wall on time

[Section 95.11](https://www.leg.state.fl.us/Statutes/index.cfm?App_mode=Display_Statute&URL=0000-0099/0095/Sections/0095.11.html) sets 2 years from the incident, or from when it was discovered or should have been discovered with due diligence, and in no event later than 4 years from the incident. Fraud, concealment, or intentional misrepresentation that prevented discovery extends the period 2 years from discovery, capped at 7 years. Records requested late can eat that runway quickly, especially when the presuit expert has to read a long hospital chart.

### One rule from the 2023 reforms that does not reach this claim

The modified comparative fault bar has a carve-out worth knowing. Subsection (6) of [section 768.81](https://www.leg.state.fl.us/Statutes/index.cfm?App_mode=Display_Statute&URL=0700-0799/0768/Sections/0768.81.html) blocks recovery by a party more than 50 percent at fault, then states plainly that it does not apply to an action for personal injury or wrongful death arising out of medical negligence under chapter 766. Comparative fault still reduces an award proportionately in these cases, but the hard bar is absent.

### First moves, and the fee limit written into the constitution

Request the complete chart rather than a summary, including nursing notes, medication administration records, imaging, and portal messages. Build a dated timeline of admissions, transfers, and changes in condition. Ask the [District 4 medical examiner's office](https://www.jacksonville.gov/departments/finance/liaison-agencies/medical-examiner) on North Davis Street about an autopsy report if the death was reported there, and keep the death certificate with it.

Fees in these claims answer to Florida voters as well as to the bar. [Article I, section 26](https://www.flsenate.gov/laws/constitution#A1S26) of the state constitution, adopted in 2004, entitles a claimant in a medical liability claim to no less than 70 percent of the first $250,000 of damages, exclusive of reasonable and customary costs, and to 90 percent of everything above that figure. The provision is self-executing. The pricing page sets it against the bar's published tiers.

## svc_wrongful_death_damages_claim_lede

Florida never asks a jury what a life was worth. It asks, category by category, which survivor lost what, and it gives the estate a separate list from the family's. Relationship and age move the answer more than the size of the loss does.

## svc_wrongful_death_damages_claim_body

### Each survivor recovers a different list

[Section 768.21](https://www.leg.state.fl.us/Statutes/index.cfm?App_mode=Display_Statute&URL=0700-0799/0768/Sections/0768.21.html) begins with lost support and services, from the date of injury to death with interest and forward from the death reduced to present value. In valuing that, the statute allows the relationship, the decedent's probable net income available for distribution to that survivor, and the replacement value of the services to be considered, along with joint life expectancies and the period of minority.

Noneconomic categories are assigned person by person. A surviving spouse may recover for lost companionship and protection and for mental pain and suffering from the date of injury. Minor children, and all children where no spouse survives, may recover lost parental companionship, instruction, and guidance plus their own mental pain and suffering. Each parent of a deceased minor child may recover for mental pain and suffering, and each parent of an adult child may do so only if no other survivors exist. A survivor who paid medical or funeral bills recovers those.

### The estate's column, and what shrinks it

The representative recovers separately for the estate. That includes lost earnings from injury to death, less lost support of survivors and excluding contributions in kind, with interest. It also includes prospective net accumulations, reduced to present value, where the survivors include a spouse or lineal descendants, or where the decedent was not a minor child, no lost support is recoverable, and a parent survives. Medical and funeral expenses that became a charge against the estate belong here too.

Two limits sit in the same section. Estate awards are subject to the claims of creditors who complied with probate law. Evidence of the surviving spouse's remarriage is admissible, which is a defense tool rather than a footnote.

### Who counts as a survivor, and the age line inside the definition

[Section 768.18](https://www.leg.state.fl.us/Statutes/index.cfm?App_mode=Display_Statute&URL=0700-0799/0768/Sections/0768.18.html) recognizes a spouse, children, and parents, plus blood relatives and adopted siblings who were partly or wholly dependent on the decedent for support or services. Minor children means children under 25 here, which is not the age used in most other contexts. Section 768.20 then requires that the single action be brought by the personal representative, and the complaint must identify all potential beneficiaries, the estate included, and allege each relationship.

Getting that list wrong is expensive in both directions. A dependent relative left off the pleading may be left out of an apportionment. A relative added without documented dependency invites a fight that delays everyone else's share.

### What reduces the number before anyone argues about value

Two rules cut before valuation. Under [section 768.81](https://www.leg.state.fl.us/Statutes/index.cfm?App_mode=Display_Statute&URL=0700-0799/0768/Sections/0768.81.html), contributory fault reduces economic and noneconomic damages proportionately, and since the 2023 amendments a party more than 50 percent at fault for their own harm recovers nothing. Damages are also apportioned against each liable party by percentage of fault rather than jointly.

Where a government body is responsible, [section 768.28](https://www.leg.state.fl.us/Statutes/index.cfm?App_mode=Display_Statute&URL=0700-0799/0768/Sections/0768.28.html) caps payment at $200,000 for one person and $300,000 for all claims arising from the same incident, with anything beyond that payable only by a legislative claim bill. The 2026 attempt to lift those figures, HB 145, passed both chambers and was [vetoed on June 30, 2026](https://www.flsenate.gov/Session/Bill/2026/145), so the caps stand where they have long stood.

### Finding out what coverage exists

Guesswork about policy limits is unnecessary. [Section 627.4137](https://www.leg.state.fl.us/Statutes/index.cfm?App_mode=Display_Statute&URL=0600-0699/0627/Sections/0627.4137.html) obliges each liability insurer, on written request, to provide a sworn statement within 30 days giving its name, each insured, the limits of coverage, any coverage defense it reasonably believes available, and a copy of the policy. Excess and umbrella policies are covered, and the insured or the agent must identify known insurers and forward the request.

### Approval, apportionment, and money that belongs to a child

A settlement in a death case is not always the family's decision alone. Section 768.25 requires court approval of the amount or the apportionment whenever a survivor objects, or where a survivor who is a minor or incompetent is affected. Under [section 768.26](https://www.leg.state.fl.us/Statutes/index.cfm?App_mode=Display_Statute&Search_String=&URL=0700-0799/0768/Sections/0768.26.html), the representative pays fees and litigation expenses from the recovery, deducted from each survivor's award and the estate's award in proportion, except expenses incurred for a single survivor.

Children's money has its own gate. [Section 744.387](https://www.leg.state.fl.us/Statutes/index.cfm?App_mode=Display_Statute&Search_String=&URL=0700-0799/0744/Sections/0744.387.html) lets a natural guardian settle a claim up to $15,000 without bond, and a larger net amount requires appointment of a guardian of the property. The Duval County Clerk lists [guardianship and probate charges](https://www.duvalclerk.com/departments/civil-court-services/probate) alongside the $401 formal administration fee, and someone has to advance them.

### Where the file sits, and how the fee is calculated

Duval County belongs to the Fourth Judicial Circuit, whose [courthouse stands at 501 West Adams Street](https://www.jud4.org/news/2021-03-30-welcome-to-the-official-website-of-the-4th-judicial-circuit-court) and which also serves Clay and Nassau. A death just south of the county line falls instead to the [Seventh Judicial Circuit](https://circuit7.org/), covering Flagler, Putnam, St. Johns, and Volusia. Venue depends on where the event happened and where the defendants are, not on where the family lives.

Fees run on a published sliding scale that steps down as a recovery grows, rises once an answer is filed, and can be exceeded only with court approval sought by the client. Because section 768.26 spreads the fee across the awards, the apportionment among survivors changes what each person nets. The pricing page here sets out every tier and the probate charges that come with a death claim.
