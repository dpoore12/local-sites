## Which Florida subdivision applies, and why

This site's matter is a **criminal prosecution** (battery / crime of domestic violence under ch. 741), so the governing prohibition is **Rule 4-1.5(f)(3)(B)** of the Rules Regulating The Florida Bar — no contingent fee for representing a defendant in a criminal case. Subparagraph **(f)(3)(A)** (domestic relations: fee contingent on securing a divorce or on the amount of alimony/support/property settlement) is named on the page only to distinguish it; it applies to the Tampa family law site instead.

Verbatim rule text, from the Bar's published Chapter 4 PDF:
> "A lawyer must not enter into an arrangement for, charge, or collect: (A) any fee in a domestic relations matter, the payment or amount of which is contingent on the securing of a divorce or on the amount of alimony or support, or property settlement in lieu thereof; or (B) a contingent fee for representing a defendant in a criminal case."

Source: https://www-media.floridabar.org/uploads/2026/06/2026_12-JUNE-Chapter-4-RRTFB-1.pdf (retrieved 2026-08-23)

The on-page sentence paraphrases (B) rather than quoting it, to clear the 15-word cross-site guard against newportbeachduilawyerpros.com.

## The filing-fee question the brief flagged

**Florida prohibits charging a filing fee for a domestic violence protection petition outright**, and the page says so plainly.

- **Fla. Stat. 741.30(2)(a)**: "Notwithstanding any other law, the assessment of a filing fee for a petition for protection against domestic violence is prohibited." The clerk is instead reimbursed by the Justice Administrative Commission at **$40 per petition** issued, out of which the clerk pays the serving law enforcement agency a fee that **may not exceed $20**. **741.30(2)(b)**: no bond required for entry of an injunction. The statute also states the cause of action does not require either party to be represented by an attorney. Source: https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2025/741.30 (retrieved 2026-08-23)
- Corroborated in plain language by the **Broward County Clerk of Courts** domestic violence division page: "No, there is no fee associated in filing a restraining order." Source: https://www.browardclerk.org/Divisions/DomesticViolence (retrieved 2026-08-23)
