# Pricing research — dallaswindowreplacementexperts.com (Dallas, TX / window replacement)

All figures retrieved 2026-08-23. Every URL below was fetched in this session.

## Anchors

1. **City of Dallas development services fee schedule (adopted)** — <https://dallascityhall.com/departments/sustainabledevelopment/DCH%20documents/DSD%20Fees.pdf>
   Alterations or repairs of single-family and duplex structures: **$181 per dwelling unit**, plus **$100** for additional trades; minimum inspection fee **$125** for one trade. **No line item names windows**, which is why permit practice varies by scope (glass/sash swap vs altering an opening or header). Recorded honestly on the page rather than asserting a "window permit fee."
2. **ENERGY STAR federal tax credits, windows and skylights** — <https://www.energystar.gov/about/federal-tax-credits/windows-skylights>
   25C credit: **30% of product cost, $600 annual cap** for windows/skylights meeting ENERGY STAR Most Efficient criteria, for products purchased and installed **January 1, 2023 – December 31, 2025**.
3. **IRS FAQs on P.L. 119-21 (OBBB) modifications to §25C etc.** — <https://www.irs.gov/newsroom/faqs-for-modification-of-sections-25c-25d-25e-30c-30d-45l-45w-and-179d-under-public-law-119-21-139-stat-72-july-4-2025-commonly-known-as-the-one-big-beautiful-bill-obbb>
   The credit "will not be allowed for any property placed in service after December 31, 2025." This directly contradicts sales pages still citing "25C runs through 2032" (Roof Raiders' page does exactly that), which is why the site states the closed status with the IRS citation.

Texas divergence note: this site's permit/code discussion is confined to the Dallas fee schedule and the 25C credit. The Austin roof site uses TREC standards and Austin's permit-exempt list; no permit sentence, authority, or code framing is shared between the two Texas sites.

### Arithmetic (per-unit rows totaled to a stated quantity)

- **Fiberglass / clad-wood whole house, 18 openings.** Bradley James posts fiberglass at $700–$1,000 per window installed: 18 × $700 = **$12,600** (row low). Roof Raiders posts an explicit 18-window clad-wood package topping out at **$34,000** (row high). Basis string states "flat, 18 openings."
- **Vinyl whole house, 15–20 openings.** WME posts the $8,000–$25,000+ total directly; no multiplication used for the bounds. Cross-check: $750 × 15 = $11,250 and $1,500 × 20 = $30,000 from the same page's per-window figure, so the posted total is conservative relative to its own per-window math — the posted total was used.
- **Brennan Corp Andersen figures exclude installation and tax** by their own wording, so they were not used to set the row low; Bradley James' installed figure was.

### Job-matching discipline

- Artisan Glass posts **single-pane** glass at $140–$250. Single-pane glass in a garage or shed sash is **not** a fogged insulated-unit swap, so that figure was **rejected** as the row low and the low was reset to King's $165 IGU figure. (Earlier draft had 140; corrected.)
- Full-frame vs insert distinction: Advanced Window and Door's per-window figures ($400–$1,400 vinyl full-frame, $292–$641 aluminum casement, $1,400–$1,806 wood, $525–$1,918 fiberglass) do not state whether installation is included, so that page was read but not cited.
