# Tampa Family Law Attorney Pros — research and validation record

## Three sourced facts

1. **Florida's current alimony framework.** Florida Statutes section 61.08 applies the current framework to initial dissolution and support petitions pending or filed on or after July 1, 2023. It lists temporary, bridge-the-gap, rehabilitative, and durational alimony; it classifies marriages by length and establishes presumptive durational limits of 50%, 60%, and 75% respectively for short-, moderate-, and long-term marriages.
   - https://www.leg.state.fl.us/statutes/index.cfm?App_mode=Display_Statute&URL=0000-0099/0061/Sections/0061.08.html

2. **Twenty-day minimum and mandatory financial disclosure.** Section 61.19 generally prohibits a final dissolution judgment until 20 days after the original petition is filed, subject to the injustice exception. Florida Family Law Rule of Procedure 12.285's official compliance form states that mandatory disclosure is generally served within 45 days after service of the initial or supplemental petition on the respondent.
   - https://www.leg.state.fl.us/statutes/index.cfm?App_mode=Display_Statute&URL=0000-0099/0061/Sections/0061.19.html
   - https://www.flcourts.gov/content/download/685889/7661881

3. **Thirteenth Judicial Circuit minor-child procedure.** Hillsborough County's standing temporary order requires both parents to complete an approved parent-education course within 60 days after filing in minor-child family cases. Without both certificates on file, the court may not sign a final judgment; mediation is required before temporary-relief and final hearings can be scheduled.
   - https://www.fljud13.org/Portals/0/Forms/pdfs/family/StandingTempOrderWithMinorKidsOrd.pdf
   - https://www.fljud13.org/AdministrativeOrders/FamilyLaw.aspx
