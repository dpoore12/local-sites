## pricing_body

### Three different products all called an inspection

Most confusion about price here is really confusion about product. A roofing company's free look is a sales call with a ladder: useful, honest enough, and aimed at deciding whether there is work to sell. A paid roof inspection with a photo report is a document, delivered whether or not a repair follows. A licensed inspector's report is a regulated deliverable with a defined scope, produced by somebody whose license is on the line.

That last category has rules attached. Under the TREC standards of practice for inspectors, the roof covering is inspected from the surface of the roof where it is safe to do so. The inspector is expressly not required to opine on remaining life, count the layers of shingle underneath, or find hail bruising that has not yet shown itself. Read that carefully, because it explains a common frustration: a report that says the roof is functional today is not a report that says the roof has eight years left. Those are separate questions and only one of them is inside the standard.

### What actually moves the number

Access is first. A 6:12 asphalt roof on a Hyde Park bungalow gets walked in twenty minutes. Standing seam metal, clay tile, or anything over about 8:12 gets inspected from a ladder, a drone, or not at all, and the substitution costs money. Second is area, since paid inspections and full home inspections alike are priced off square footage tiers rather than effort. Third is what the client needs on paper. A verbal summary sits at the floor. A certification letter a carrier will accept, with elevation photos and a stated condition, is a different scope.

Age matters in a way specific to this market. Much of central Austin predates modern flashing details, so the inspection spends its time where water actually gets in: chimney counterflashing, sidewall step flashing on additions, and the tie-in where a 1950s hip meets a later room extension. Those are also the leaks that cost most to fix properly, which is why the repair row above spans an order of magnitude.

### The Austin wrinkle nobody mentions on the invoice

Clay is the reason roof problems here get discovered late. The Blackland Prairie soils east of I-35 swell in the wet and shrink hard in drought, and a house that racks even slightly through a summer opens joints at the ridge, pulls fasteners at the deck, and cracks the mortar crown on a chimney. The resulting ceiling stain reads as a roof failure when the cause is structural movement, so a useful report notes what the framing and the chimney are doing.

### Where the city does and does not get involved

Straight shingle-for-shingle replacement in Austin sits on the work exempt from building permits list, and the exemption only breaks if the property is in the Wildland-Urban Interface area and half or more of the roofing is being replaced. That matters at inspection time because it means most re-roofs on this side of town were done without a city inspector ever setting foot on them. There is no permit record to pull, no inspection sign-off to review, and the only evidence of workmanship quality is what somebody can see now.

### Reading a fair inspection quote

An honest quote states the square footage tier it is priced against, whether the roof will be walked or observed from a ladder and drone, whether attic access is included, how many photos and what report format arrives, and how long the turnaround runs. Ancillary services belong on their own lines: termite, sewer scope, pool and elevation readings are each a separate scope. It says plainly whether a re-inspection after repairs costs extra. And if the inspector is licensed, it names the license tier, which the TREC fee schedule prices at $120 to apply as a Professional Inspector and $60 to renew.

### The trap in this trade

Two, running opposite directions. The first is the free storm inspection that finds enough damage for a full replacement claim on a roof with eight good years in it, sometimes with the carrier deductible waved around as a reason to move fast. The second is the reverse: a bargain paid inspection with a two-paragraph report, no photographs, and no attic look, which is worthless in a negotiation and worse than nothing in a claim.

### What is not in the number

The figures above buy a look and a document. They do not buy the repair, the engineer's letter if framing is the real story, the tree work needed before anybody can safely reach a rear slope, or a second visit after a hail event to compare against the baseline.

## services_summary

Four different jobs hide behind the phrase roof inspection here, and paying for the wrong one is how an Austin homeowner buys two visits. A general inspection reads the whole covering, the decking and the attic against a published standard. A leak inspection chases one water path to the component that actually failed. ## svc_roof_inspection_lede

A roof inspection in this state has a published floor. The standards of practice tell a real estate inspector exactly what to walk, what to report, and what to call deficient. Knowing that floor is how you tell when an inspection stopped short of what you needed.

## svc_roof_inspection_body

### What the standards of practice actually require

Texas writes the baseline down, and it is worth reading before you pay for anything. Under 22 TAC 535.228(c), the inspector examines the roof covering from the surface of the roof and reports the vantage point used, evidence of water penetration, and evidence of previous repairs to the covering, flashing, skylights and other penetrations. Deficiencies in fasteners, adhesion, covering material, flashing details, skylights and penetrations must be reported as deficient. That is a genuine floor. It is also, in a few places people never expect, a ceiling.

### Four things that baseline does not promise

The same rule spells out what is not required. Remaining life expectancy is not required. Neither is the number of roofing layers. Latent hail damage sits expressly outside the required scope, as does an exhaustive examination of every fastener. The inspector may also stay off the surface where climbing it is unsafe or where walking it could cause significant damage. So if you need remaining life, a layer count or a hail opinion, you are buying work beyond that baseline, and the scope should say so in writing before the ladder comes off the truck.

### The attic half of the job

Subsection (d) covers roof structures and attics, and that is where the expensive findings usually live. The inspector reports the approximate average depth of attic insulation and any evidence of water penetration. Ventilation that is not performing has to be reported as deficient. So do deflections or depressions in the roof surface tied to framing or decking performance, along with deficiencies in the installed framing members and decking themselves. Physical limits are written into the rule too. Nobody is required to enter an attic through an opening smaller than 22 by 30 inches, or where headroom is under 30 inches.

### Ventilation is arithmetic before it is opinion

Under the residential code this city has adopted, the minimum net free ventilating area is 1/150 of the vented space. An exception cuts that to 1/300 where between 40 and 50 percent of the required vent area sits high in the space, within 3 feet of the ridge, with the rest of it low. A vapor retarder condition rides along with that exception, but it reaches only Climate Zones 6 through 8, which is not this part of the country. The honest field test here is the split between ridge and soffit, and whether those soffit vents are open or painted and insulated shut.

### Which roof the inspector is standing on

Neighborhood rules change what a finding means. Inside the Hyde Park historic district, the design standards direct owners to retain original roof pitches and profiles, to consider the original material first when a covering is replaced, and to preserve original gable vents and roof brackets. Metal is acceptable there; scalloped or diamond shingles are not, unless they were original to the house. Mueller runs on its own guidelines, where principal slopes generally fall between 6:12 and 9:12 and rooftop equipment and solar have to be integrated rather than visible from the street. Southwest of town, in Circle C Ranch, the question is usually simpler and harsher: a first-generation covering tends to reach the end of its life across every slope at once.

### Shade is not the mercy it looks like

Tarrytown, Bouldin Creek and East Cesar Chavez sit under serious canopy, and the city measured tree cover at 41 percent citywide in 2022. Limbs that touch a roof abrade the same courses for years, shaded slopes hold moisture and organic growth longer, and leaf litter parks itself in valleys and behind chimneys where the assembly depends on water moving. An inspector reads those slopes differently from an open one, and the finding is often a maintenance instruction rather than a repair.

### Heat does more damage than any single storm

The local weather service ranking puts the 1991 to 2020 average at 29 days a year at or above 100 degrees, against a long-period average of 15, with 2023 the warmest year on record at 72.2 degrees. Asphalt does not recover from that. It stiffens, sheds volatiles, and a shingle that would flex somewhere milder snaps when a hand lifts it. That is why brittleness, seal failure along the laminate line and thinning granule cover usually show up first on the south and west slopes.

### What the visit looks like and what moves the price

Expect a walk of every accessible slope, close photographs tied to their position on the roof, then the attic for decking, ventilation and staining. The report should name the vantage point used for each slope and state plainly where access was limited. Range drivers are slope, height, complexity, and add-ons such as drone imagery, infrared or a full home inspection wrapped around the roof work. No figure published anywhere is an offer from anyone; the number that binds comes from whoever is scheduled to climb.

## svc_roof_leak_inspection_lede

A ceiling stain is a location problem before it is a roofing problem. Water enters at a penetration or a flashing, travels the underside of the deck until something stops it, then leaves at the first seam it finds inside the house.

## svc_roof_leak_inspection_body

### Why the stain is rarely under the opening

Decking is a slide, not a sponge. Water that gets past the covering runs the underside of the sheathing, follows a rafter or a truss chord downhill, and shows up wherever drywall, a can light or a taped seam interrupts it. That can be two rooms away from the entry point on a complicated roof. Which is why a competent leak visit starts with roof geometry rather than with the stain, mapping penetrations and transitions above the wet area and uphill of it.

### The parts that give up first

Rank the suspects before anyone starts pulling material. Pipe boots crack at the collar. Exhaust and attic vents lose their seals. Skylight and chimney flashings fail at the head and the sides. Roof-to-wall transitions go when siding or stucco was installed over the flashing instead of behind it. Valleys hold debris and slow the water down. Fasteners back out of metal panels. A single storm can also drive rain sideways into an edge detail that sheds fine in ordinary weather, so the direction of the wind that day is real evidence.

### Underlayment and fasteners are where corners get cut

State guidance on repairing a roof after a storm is unusually specific about the layers under the shingle. A minimum of No. 15 felt is required for asphalt shingle work. Two layers are required on slopes from 2:12 up to 4:12, and one layer at 4:12 and steeper. Asphalt shingles can go only on slopes of 2:12 or greater at all. Nails have to be galvanized or stainless steel, at least 12 gauge with a 0.105-inch shank and a head at least 3/8 inch across, and the same guidance states flatly that the code does not permit staples. Leaks that come back are often installation shortcuts at exactly these points.

### The wind number this city designs to

Local code fills in an ultimate design wind speed of 105 mph, and the shingle wind-classification table in that code starts one step above it, at 110 mph. State storm guidance points homeowners toward shingles tested to ASTM D3161 Class F or ASTM D7158 Class H. None of that helps a shingle whose factory seal strip never bonded, and cool-weather installations and dusty surfaces are the usual reason it did not. An unbonded course lifts in gusts far below any design figure, then reseals itself as the day heats up, which is exactly why the leak seems to come and go.

### Decking, and the thing a fast patch destroys

Storm guidance is direct: have the deck inspected and replace deteriorated or damaged decking before a new covering goes on. Soft sheathing around a chronic leak is a finding, not a detail, because fasteners in a wet deck do not hold. The other risk is evidence. Roofing guidance notes that puncturing a blister or spreading a coating or mastic covers up the evidence a roofer needs to identify the problem. A tube of sealant smeared across a suspect boot on a Saturday can turn a one-component repair into a guess.

### Permits, and what a repair does not trigger

Two lines matter locally. Asphalt shingles replacing existing asphalt shingles are on the city's list of work exempt from a building permit, as is replacement of any roof covering that does not adversely affect the roof structure. Both exemptions drop away if the property sits in the Wildland-Urban Interface area and half or more of the roofing is being replaced. Roof decking is exempt up to 128 square feet, and beyond that a permit is required. Exempt work still has to comply with the codes, which is the sentence people skip.

### What the visit looks like, and how it gets priced

The sequence is interior first, then exterior. Moisture readings and photographs inside, the attic underside above the stain, then the slopes uphill of it, checking each candidate detail. Where the source stays ambiguous, a controlled water test isolates one detail at a time, starting low and working up. The output should name a component, not a neighborhood of the roof. Cost tracks access, slope and how much diagnosis the roof demands, and a repair scope that follows should list the component, the material and what happens if the deck under it turns out to be soft.

## svc_storm_damage_roof_inspection_lede

Hail days in this county are documented, dated and public. A storm inspection ties what is on your slopes to one of those dates, in photographs, before four other people have walked the roof and added their own marks to it.

## svc_storm_damage_roof_inspection_body

### What the county's hail record actually says

The federal storm events database lists 19 separate days with hail of 1.50 inches or larger in Travis County between 2010 and 2025, and 39 days at 1 inch or larger. September 24, 2023 was the worst of them. The entry for that evening records three-inch hail on Burnet Lane in the Brentwood area. It carries $300 million in property damage on the Travis County line, part of a complex the agency ties to $1.3 billion in hail losses across three states. April 9, 2024 put 2.50 inch stones down near Camp Mabry. For scale, forecasters read 1 inch as a quarter, 1.75 inches as a golf ball, and 3 inches as a tea cup.

### What hail actually does to a composition shingle

National roofing guidance draws a hard line: an asphalt shingle has to be punctured or fractured before it counts as functionally damaged. Fractures start on the underside, where tensile strain peaks, and travel upward, which is why an impact fracture is always detectable from below. On the surface, the same impact often reads as a localized soft spot you can feel with a fingertip, described in that guidance as a bruise. Granule loss by itself is not functional damage; it begins the day the shingle is manufactured. The test is whether water-shedding capability has been reduced or expected service life shortened. The same source publishes threshold sizes, the smallest stones that can damage a covering: 1 inch for lightweight three-tab shingles, 1.25 inches for heavyweight laminated shingles, 1.50 inches for clay tile and 1.75 inches for concrete tile.

### Three things routinely mistaken for hail bruises

Blisters are the first. Trapped gases such as air or water vapor expand inside the shingle and leave round craters that never came from the sky. Foot traffic is the second, and it has its own signature. On wood, footfall splits lack the surface indentation an impact leaves; on slate and tile, near-linear fractures across a piece usually mean somebody walked it. The third is repair itself, because a spread of mastic or a punctured blister erases the record. All three are arguments for documenting a roof early, while the surface still holds only what the storm put there.

### Texas does not license roofing contractors

Check this before you hand anyone a signature. The state roofing trade association says on its own licensing page that the Texas Department of Licensing and Regulation does not administer licensure for roofing contractors. Anyone can call themselves a roofer, and a roofer is not required to be knowledgeable, insured, licensed or registered with the state. That association runs a voluntary credential instead. It asks for a principal of a Texas-domiciled roofing company for at least two continuous years, a fixed physical address, and exams passed at 70 percent or better. It also asks for general liability coverage of $500,000 combined single limit on commercial work, or $300,000 residential. Private credential, not a state one, and the difference changes how you check a company.

### What the person on your roof may not legally do

Insurance Code section 4101.251(b) is one sentence long. A roofing contractor may not act as an adjuster or advertise to adjust claims for any property where it is providing or may provide roofing services, whether or not it holds an adjuster license. The state insurance department adds that a website counts as advertising under its own rule. A contractor is still free to give you and the insurer an estimate and to talk through the technical detail in it. What it cannot do is advocate for you on the claim or tell you what your policy covers and excludes.

### Your deductible is set by statute, not by an estimate

Insurance Code section 707.002 requires the insured to pay any deductible on a first-party property claim. Business and Commerce Code section 27.02 makes it an offense to pay, waive, absorb, rebate or credit that deductible, or to advertise doing it, and grades the offense a Class B misdemeanor. That section also requires a 12-point boldface notice in any insurance-funded repair contract priced at $1,000 or more. Section 707.004 then lets an insurer hold back recoverable depreciation until it receives reasonable proof the deductible was paid, such as a canceled check, a money order receipt, a credit card statement or an executed financing contract. An offer to make your deductible disappear is an offer of a paperwork problem.

### What you are signing when someone knocks

Read the assignment language first. The insurance department warns specifically against signing anything saying the homeowner agrees to the listed repairs for the value of insurance proceeds, because the contractor then collects every dollar of the payment. Two statutes help after a disaster. Business and Commerce Code section 58.003 requires a written contract and bars payment before work begins, with partial payments limited to what completed work supports. That chapter does not reach a contractor who has kept a physical business address in this county or an adjacent one for the year before the contract, which is one practical reason to prefer a local operation. Under the state's door-to-door rules, a sale over $25 made away from the seller's place of business generally carries three business days to cancel.

### What the visit looks like

Test squares get marked on each slope and the impacts inside them counted, so a density can be reported instead of an adjective. Soft spots get located by hand, photographed with something for scale, and tied to their position on the roof. Metal accessories and vents get read as corroborating evidence, since a dented turbine above an unmarked shingle field is a story in itself. The report should separate what was observed from what it might mean, name the date of loss you supplied, and stop there. Cost follows slope count and access.

## svc_roof_certification_inspection_lede

A roof certification is a narrow document with a specific audience. A lender, an underwriter or a buyer wants a third party to say what the covering looks like today and how much service life it plausibly has left.

## svc_roof_certification_inspection_body

### Who asks for one, and what they need to see

The request almost always arrives from a file rather than a homeowner. An underwriter reviewing an older covering, a mortgage appraiser who could not resolve the roof, a buyer's agent working an option period, or a seller trying to keep the roof from becoming the last item in negotiation. What they need is narrow: material type, condition observed, defects with photographs, and a defensible statement about remaining service life. What they do not need, and what nobody honest supplies, is a guarantee that the roof will not leak.

### The federal loan rule is the clearest version of the question

Government-insured lending puts a number on it. The FHA single family handbook requires the appraiser to notify the lender if the roof covering does not prevent moisture entry or provide reasonable future utility, durability and economy of maintenance, and does not have a remaining physical life of at least two years. The appraiser has to identify the roofing material and its condition in the report, must report a roof with less than two years of remaining life, and must make the appraisal subject to inspection by a professional roofer. Where the roof cannot be viewed at all, the appraiser has to explain why and report on the underside, the attic and the ceilings instead. That is the two-year hurdle a certification is usually being asked to clear.

### Why a standard real estate inspection does not settle it

The state's inspection standards deliberately leave this alone. A real estate inspector is not required to determine remaining life expectancy, is not required to determine the number of roofing layers, and is not required to identify latent hail damage. Those three exclusions are precisely the content an underwriter is asking about. So a general inspection report and a certification are not substitutes for one another, and handing over the wrong one usually costs a week.

### What the certifier is reading up there

Granule cover comes first, because exposed asphalt on the mat is the visible start of the end. Then seal integrity along laminate lines, the condition of every flashing termination, fastener exposure, and repairs that were done with the wrong material. Underneath matters as much. Deflection between rafters read from the attic, staining on the sheathing, and ventilation performance all bear on how long the covering survives its own heat. Layer count gets checked at a rake or an edge, and it carries a consequence: the residential code does not permit a recover where the existing roof is water soaked or deteriorated, or where two or more applications of covering are already up there.

### Historic districts and Mueller change the replacement conversation

If the certification points toward replacement, the next question is what may go back on. In a local historic district, a certificate of appropriateness is required for non-routine exterior work. The Hyde Park standards ask an owner to consider the original material first, then a product resembling it. Metal counts as acceptable there, while scalloped or diamond shingles are ruled out unless they were original. At Mueller, guidelines govern slope and appearance, keeping principal roof pitches generally between 6:12 and 9:12 and requiring solar and rooftop equipment to be integrated rather than visible from public streets. Those rules affect timing and cost, not just aesthetics, and they belong in a report written for a transaction.

### The permit line, and what belongs in the file

Certification files are thin in a predictable way, so build the paperwork while somebody is already on the roof. Shingles replacing shingles, and any covering replacement that does not adversely affect the structure, are exempt from a city building permit unless the property is in the Wildland-Urban Interface area and half or more of the roofing is going. Roof decking is exempt up to 128 square feet, and past that a permit applies. Keep the exemption in mind rather than in the past tense, because exempt work still has to meet code, and a future buyer's inspector will read the roof, not the receipt.

### What the visit looks like, and what it is not

Every accessible slope gets walked and photographed, penetrations and flashings get examined individually, the attic gets entered where the opening allows, and limitations get written down instead of glossed. The document that comes back states what was inspected, from where, what was observed, and what condition supports. Since this state issues no roofing license, the word certified on a letterhead reflects the company behind it and nothing more, so the value sits in the photographs and the specificity. Range drivers are roof size, slope, height, layer complexity and how fast the report is needed.
