## Angle taken

This is a recurring-maintenance gutter site, not an emergency-trade site. The copy starts with Charlotte’s mature tree cover, then makes the real value proposition concrete: remove pine straw and oak-leaf buildup, prove the downspout path is clear, and keep concentrated roof runoff away from fascia, mulch, foundation edges, and crawlspace perimeters. It recommends a spring cleanout plus a post-leaf-drop visit where the canopy over the roof warrants it, rather than pretending every overflow needs an urgent dispatch.

## Three sourced Charlotte facts

1. **City-scale tree canopy:** Charlotte’s published 2022 assessment reports **47% canopy cover** inside city limits and attributes **34 million gallons of annual runoff prevention** to the canopy. The City also estimates about **200,000 street trees**, with willow oaks a significant share. That gives the site a grounded reason to discuss a property-specific, often twice-yearly cleaning rhythm rather than a generic national calendar. Sources: [Charlotte Tree Canopy Assessment Factsheet](https://treescharlotte.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/11/Charlotte-NC-Tree-Canopy-Assessment-Factsheet.pdf) and the [City’s street-tree FAQ](https://www.charlottenc.gov/City-Government/Departments/General-Services/Tree-Management/Commonly-Asked-Questions).

2. **Tree ordinance context:** Charlotte defines a Heritage Tree as a North Carolina native tree with a **30-inch-or-greater DBH** and protects heritage trees in city limits; qualifying removals require a City-issued tree-work permit and approval. That supports the copy’s distinction between clearing gutters and treating pruning or removal as a separate, regulated tree-care decision. Sources: [Charlotte Tree Ordinance](https://www.charlottenc.gov/files/sharedassets/city/v/1/growth-and-development/getting-started/documents/charlotte-tree-ordinance-adopted.pdf) and [Charlotte Urban Forestry](https://www.charlottenc.gov/Growth-and-Development/Getting-Started-on-Your-Project/Urban-Forestry).

3. **Stormwater context:** Charlotte’s stormwater regulations state that local rules seek to minimize public and private flood losses and protect surface waters; the City says it maintains and improves the public drainage system to move rainwater safely through the community. The site uses that as context for checking a private roof’s discharge point, not as a claim that cleaning gutters is a regulated or permitted activity. Sources: [Charlotte Stormwater Regulations](https://www.charlottenc.gov/Services/Stormwater/Stormwater-Regulations) and [Charlotte Storm Water Services](https://www.charlottenc.gov/Services/Stormwater).
