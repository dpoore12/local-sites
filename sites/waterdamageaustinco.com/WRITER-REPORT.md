# Austin water-damage research notes

1. **Austin floodplain mapping:** Austin Watershed Protection says FloodPro lets users see floodplain maps, storm-drain systems, models, and elevation certificates. The city’s flood-safety page says about 10% of Austin land lies in floodplain. This supports advising homeowners to distinguish outdoor flooding from internal plumbing damage and to check mapped drainage/floodplain information after conditions are safe.
   - https://www.austintexas.gov/services/view-floodplain-maps-and-storm-drain-infrastructure
   - https://www.austintexas.gov/watershed-protection/atx-flood-safety

2. **Dated flash-flood event:** City open data records that on October 31, 2013, Onion Creek at US 183 hit a record 41 feet and rose 11 feet between 6:15 and 6:30 a.m.; 659 homes in the Onion Creek neighborhood were damaged. This supports the warning that creek and outdoor water incidents in Austin can escalate quickly and must be treated as floodwater.
   - https://data.austintexas.gov/stories/s/2013-Halloween-Flood/fr92-dkxr/

3. **Creek/floodplain exposure:** Austin Watershed Protection states there are more than 9,000 buildings in Austin’s 100-year floodplain and more than 300 bridges subject to flooding. This supports describing creek geography as a real service consideration rather than generic storm language.
   - https://www.austintexas.gov/watershed-protection/projects/creek-flooding

Supporting service facts used in copy:
- Texas Department of Insurance: most homeowner/renter policies cover sudden accidental water damage such as a burst pipe, but most home policies do not cover flooding; policy-specific terms apply.
  - https://www.tdi.texas.gov/tips/when-are-water-damage-and-mold-covered-by-insurance.html
- EPA: dry water-damaged areas and items within 24–48 hours to help prevent mold growth; this is guidance, not a guarantee.
  - https://www.epa.gov/mold/brief-guide-mold-moisture-and-your-home

Neighborhoods: Zilker; Mueller; Hyde Park; Allandale; East Cesar Chavez; Onion Creek.
