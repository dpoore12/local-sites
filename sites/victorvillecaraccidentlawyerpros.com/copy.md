## pricing_body

### The county surcharge is real, and it is in the appendix

Most California civil filing fees are uniform statewide. Three counties are carved out, and San Bernardino is one of them: a local courthouse construction surcharge of $35 is added to first-paper filings. The court's published schedule shows the arithmetic. A limited civil complaint pleading up to $10,000 costs $240 here instead of the statewide $225. A limited civil complaint pleading over $10,000 and up to $35,000 costs $380 instead of $370. In the unlimited tier the surcharge is offset, so a complaint pleading more than $35,000 costs $435, matching the rest of the state.

Those sums are small against an injury claim, but they make a point. Courthouse numbers are published and identical for everyone who walks in. Lawyer numbers appear nowhere, because no state authority publishes them.

### The rule that governs a fee sets a standard, not a rate

Rule 1.5 of the California Rules of Professional Conduct prohibits making an agreement for, charging, or collecting an unconscionable or illegal fee. Thirteen factors decide whether a fee crossed that line, weighed on the circumstances existing when the agreement was signed. Among them are overreaching during the negotiation, undisclosed material facts, and the fee set against the value of the work performed. The list also reaches the difficulty of the questions, the sum at stake and the result obtained, the hours involved, and whether the client gave informed consent.

Nowhere in that list is a number. Anybody claiming that California limits an auto injury fee to a set percentage is describing a different statute, named next.

### Section 6146 is the malpractice rule, and only the malpractice rule

Business and Professions Code section 6146 does cap percentages. Where a patient sues a health care provider for professional negligence, the fee is held to twenty-five percent of what is recovered if the matter ends in a settlement and release before any complaint or arbitration demand goes on file. It rises to thirty-three percent if the case ends later, and going past either figure takes a motion granted for good cause. "Recovered" is the net after disbursements and costs, and neither the patient's treatment bills nor the lawyer's overhead may be counted among those deductions.

None of that governs a collision at Bear Valley Road, a truck merging off Interstate 15, or a driver hit at the D Street crossing. Those are ordinary negligence claims, outside section 6146 entirely. Rule 1.5 does forbid a contingent fee outright in one setting, at subdivision (c)(2), which covers defending someone in a criminal case.

### What section 6147 forces into the contract

Since the rate is negotiated, the statute regulates the paper. Section 6147(a) requires a written contract signed by both sides, and a duplicate signed copy handed to the plaintiff at signing. The contract must state the agreed rate, explain how disbursements and costs affect both the fee and the recovery, and cover compensation for related matters outside it. Unless the claim falls under section 6146, it must also say that no law fixes the fee and that the rate is negotiable. Under section 6147(b), missing any of that makes the agreement voidable at the plaintiff's option, leaving the lawyer entitled only to a reasonable fee.

### Distance is what makes a High Desert file expensive

Cost, not fee, is where geography shows up. Records come from providers spread between this city, Apple Valley, Hesperia and the hospitals below the pass. Depositions of a commercial driver or a corporate representative are often set in San Bernardino or Los Angeles County, turning one session into a travel day plus a transcript. Retained experts bill by the hour wherever the crash happened.

Code of Civil Procedure section 1033.5 decides which of those a winning party can shift to the other side. Recoverable: filing, motion and jury fees, deposition taking, transcription and travel, service of process, court-ordered expert fees, statutory reporter fees. Not recoverable, absent express authority: experts the court never appointed, investigation expenses in preparing for trial, postage, telephone and photocopying outside exhibits. A file can therefore be won and still carry costs nobody reimburses, which is exactly why the contract has to say who advanced them and how they come out.

### The reimbursement claim that arrives after the settlement

Civil Code section 3040 caps a managed-care or medical-group reimbursement lien at what the plan actually paid providers. Where the injured person engaged a lawyer, it caps the lien again at one-third of the money due under the judgment or settlement. The lien then reduces pro rata for the attorney fees and costs that created the fund, and by the same comparative-fault share applied to the recovery. Hospital liens, Medi-Cal recovery and workers' compensation reimbursement sit under different statutes, so care received through more than one system means more than one set of rules.

### Three questions the paperwork should answer without being asked

Whether the percentage changes if a complaint is filed. Whether costs come off before or after the fee is computed. Who carries the cost of an out-of-county deposition and a reconstruction expert if the case gets there. Those answers belong in the signed contract, in writing, before anything is filed at the Victorville courthouse.

### The distance nobody prices at the start

Geography is a damages issue in this valley, not just an inconvenience. On the state's designated trauma center list, San Bernardino County's Level I centers are Arrowhead Regional in Colton and Loma Linda University Medical Center, both below the pass. Providence St. Mary in Apple Valley and Hi-Desert Medical Center in Joshua Tree are designated at Level IV. A serious crash here therefore tends to produce a transfer, a specialist relationship forty-odd miles away, and months of appointments that cost a working day each. Mileage, hours lost to the road, a relative doing the driving, and shifts given up for travel are recoverable losses only if somebody writes them down.

### Dust, wind and the visibility argument

Weather is the other local lever. The National Weather Service office in San Diego, which forecasts the Apple and Lucerne Valleys zone that covers this city, issues a blowing dust advisory when visibility falls between a quarter mile and a mile, and a dust storm warning when it drops below a quarter mile. In the deserts a wind advisory takes sustained winds of at least 30 mph or gusts of at least 45 mph, and a high wind warning takes sustained 45 mph or gusts of 58 mph. Pin down the conditions for your hour early. Otherwise an insurer gets to describe a clear afternoon as a dust event, or to ignore that its driver kept highway speed through one.

### Two subtractions the other side will attempt

First comes percentage. Under the standard instruction, a defendant who proves you were negligent and that your negligence substantially contributed gets the award reduced by the share the jury assigns, so the defense does not need to win anything outright. Second comes Civil Code 3333.4, which bars non-economic damages where the injured person owned an uninsured vehicle in the crash, or was driving without being able to establish financial responsibility. Those two subtractions stack, and both get argued long before any courtroom.

### What a release actually closes

Read the document rather than the amount. Property damage, bodily injury, loss of use, future care and other people insured on the same policy are separate items that do not have to be settled together. Insurance Code 11580.2 pulls uninsured motorist coverage when a settlement with a potentially liable party is made without written consent from your own carrier, so a modest liability payment can quietly end the coverage meant to carry the remainder. Reimbursement claims belong in the same review: Civil Code 3040 limits a managed-care or medical-group lien to one-third of the money due where the injured person engaged a lawyer, and reduces it further for the fees and costs that produced the fund.

### Clocks that keep running, and what governs a fee

Negotiation pauses nothing. A personal injury suit runs two years from the injury under Code of Civil Procedure 335.1, damage to the vehicle runs three, and a claim on your own uninsured motorist coverage needs suit, an agreed amount, or a formally instituted arbitration within two years of the crash. Any lawsuit that follows would be filed at the Civil Division of the Victorville District, which takes limited and unlimited civil matters for the High Desert communities it serves. On fees, this state controls structure and disclosure rather than price. Rule 1.5(a) prohibits an unconscionable or illegal fee, rule 1.5(c)(2) forbids a contingent fee for defending a criminal case, and Business and Professions Code 6147 requires the injury agreement to be written, signed, duplicated to the client, and explicit that the rate is negotiable.

## svc_air_ambulance_and_medical_bills_lede

Distance is the defining medical fact of a serious High Desert crash. A helicopter is not an unusual event out here, and the bills that follow it arrive from several organizations at once, under rules that changed materially for air transport in recent years.

## svc_air_ambulance_and_medical_bills_body

<h2>How the flight decision actually gets made</h2>
<p>Air transport in San Bernardino County is not a paramedic's improvisation. The county emergency medical services agency requires that all field requests for an emergency aircraft be coordinated through its designated aircraft dispatch center, which assigns the closest available aircraft using automatic flight following as the determining factor, and applies a rotation system where two or more aircraft are co-located or within less than one mile of each other. Aircraft arrival time is measured from dispatch until the aircraft is over the scene, and expressly includes crew preparation, flight planning, pre-flight, take-off, aircraft reconfiguration and flight time. Every transport receives a quality improvement review after the fact.</p>
<h2>The fifteen-minute standard, written down</h2>
<p>The policy also states the threshold. Air transport is appropriate where the injury or illness is time-sensitive and critical and requires specialty care center services, and where the benefit of aircraft transport is clearly greater than ground transportation, with an acceptable standard described as a fifteen-minute time differential in favor of air transportation. The needs of the patient and scene management supersede other considerations, and a dispatched aircraft is canceled if the incident commander, in consultation with the most medically qualified first responder, determines air transport is not needed. That written standard is why a flight from a rural stretch of highway forty minutes from a trauma center is a routine clinical decision rather than an extravagance an insurer can characterize as unnecessary.</p>
<h2>The federal protection that now covers air transport</h2>
<p>Air ambulance charges were, for years, the most notorious surprise bill in American medicine. The federal No Surprises Act changed that. As the Centers for Medicare and Medicaid Services describes it, protections beginning in 2022 shield people covered under group and individual health plans from surprise bills for most emergency services, for non-emergency services from out-of-network providers at in-network facilities, and for services from out-of-network air ambulance service providers. The law also establishes an independent dispute resolution process for payment disputes between plans and providers, so the argument over the amount happens between the plan and the provider rather than on the patient's kitchen table.</p>
<h2>What the protection does not cover</h2>
<p>Two gaps matter locally. Air ambulance is named in the protected categories; ground ambulance is not among them, which is significant in a valley where many patients travel by ground down the pass. And the protections operate through a health plan, so a person who is uninsured or who chooses not to use insurance sits in a different part of the statute: that person can often obtain a good faith estimate of the cost of care in advance, and can dispute a bill that is substantially greater than the estimate through a separate process the agency describes. Reading which category applies before paying anything is the first useful step.</p>
<h2>The lien that sits behind the settlement figure</h2>
<p>Even where a plan pays, a provider may assert a claim against the recovery. Civil Code 3045.1 gives a hospital that furnishes emergency and ongoing services to a person injured by another's act a lien upon the damages recovered by settlement, compromise or judgment, for the reasonable and necessary charges of that care. Civil Code 3040 then caps how much of a recovery health care liens may absorb. The result is that a settlement number and the amount that reaches an injured household are different figures, and anyone evaluating an offer without a written list of every asserted lien is evaluating an incomplete document.</p>
<h2>Which records exist, and who holds each one</h2>
<p>A single serious crash generates records at four or five organizations. The responding ground agency writes a patient care report. The air operator keeps its own flight and clinical record. The receiving trauma facility holds the admission. Where a patient was stabilized locally and transferred, a second facility holds the earlier chart. The state emergency medical services authority publishes the designated trauma centers, and in this county those designations include Level I facilities well down the pass alongside Level IV designations closer to the desert communities. Each organization requires its own request, and a claim built from one hospital's summary is missing the part of the record where the injury was first described.</p>
<h2>The order to work in</h2>
<p>Do not pay a large transport bill before determining whether the federal protections apply to your coverage. Request an itemized statement rather than a balance summary, since a balance figure cannot be checked against anything. Ask the plan for its explanation of benefits and compare the two documents line by line. Collect every provider's records in writing, including the field and flight records. Then keep the injury claim on its own calendar, because the medical billing dispute and the liability claim are separate matters and the deadline for a personal injury claim remains two years from the injury under Code of Civil Procedure 335.1.</p>

## svc_comparative_fault_in_california_lede

Almost no serious desert crash claim gets denied outright. It gets discounted. The adjuster assigns you a share of the blame, subtracts it, and calls the reduced figure an evaluation, and California law does let a share be subtracted, though not in the loose way a claim letter implies.

## svc_comparative_fault_in_california_body

<h2>What has to be proved before any share is subtracted</h2>
<p>The Judicial Council's standard instruction on this question, CACI No. 405, puts the burden on the side asserting it. The defense must prove two things: that the injured person was negligent, and that the negligence was a substantial factor in causing the harm. Only if both are proved do damages drop by the percentage of responsibility the jury assigns, and the instruction notes that the court, not the jury, performs the actual arithmetic. The directions for use add that the instruction should not be given at all without substantial evidence of the plaintiff's negligence. A claim letter asserting that you were partly at fault is an argument, not a finding, and the difference is worth holding onto.</p>
<h2>Where the rule came from and why it replaced an older one</h2>
<p>The instruction traces to <em>Li v. Yellow Cab Co.</em>, decided in 1975, where the court concluded that comparative negligence was preferable to the all-or-nothing contributory negligence doctrine from the standpoint of logic, practical experience and fundamental justice. Before that decision a sliver of carelessness on the injured person's part ended the case entirely. The doctrine that replaced it is described in the same authorities as flexible and commonsense, letting the trier of fact weigh the relative responsibility of everyone involved, with responsibility resting on negligence, strict liability or other theories. That flexibility is precisely why the number the other side proposes first is usually the number most favorable to the other side.</p>
<h2>Several liability changes who pays what</h2>
<p>Two vehicles at fault does not mean two full recoveries. Civil Code 1431.2 splits the analysis in half. Economic losses, meaning medical charges, lost earnings and out-of-pocket costs, remain jointly recoverable. Non-economic losses, meaning pain, suffering, inconvenience, emotional distress, loss of enjoyment of life and disfigurement, are several only, and each defendant is liable for that category solely in direct proportion to that defendant's own percentage of fault. A separate judgment is entered against each. In a High Desert pileup with four drivers involved, that provision decides how much of the human side of a loss any single insurer can be made to pay, no matter how deep that insurer's limits are.</p>
<h2>The arguments used on open highway crashes</h2>
<p>Down in a valley grid the percentage fight is usually about a signal or a driveway. Up here it is about speed, distance and reaction. Expect the file to raise how fast you were going for the conditions, whether you were following far enough back on an unlit two-lane stretch, whether you tried to steer around something instead of braking straight, and whether your headlights, tires or windshield were in the condition a careful driver would maintain before crossing forty miles of open highway. Vehicle Code 22350 gives that argument a hook, because it forbids any speed greater than is reasonable or prudent given weather, visibility, traffic and the surface, regardless of the posted number. On a two-lane undivided highway the ceiling drops anyway: Vehicle Code 22349(b) sets fifty-five.</p>
<h2>Proposition 213 sits underneath the percentage question</h2>
<p>Before percentages are even reached, one statute can remove an entire category of damages. Civil Code 3333.4 bars recovery of non-economic losses by an injured person who owned an uninsured vehicle involved in the crash, by an operator who cannot establish financial responsibility, and by a driver convicted of driving under the influence in that collision. Economic losses survive the bar. Subdivision (c) gives the non-economic damages back to an uninsured owner injured by a driver later convicted of drunk driving. Where a household keeps a second vehicle parked unregistered on a lot off Amargosa Road or Bear Valley Road, that fact belongs in the first conversation rather than the fourth, because it can matter more than any percentage argument.</p>
<h2>What actually moves a percentage</h2>
<p>Percentages move on physical detail, not on insistence. Useful items include the postmile or nearest exit rather than a street name alone, the direction of travel, the lane you occupied and whether it was the through lane, the position of the sun at that hour on that date, the surface temperature and whether the shoulder was soft sand, the condition of the centerline striping, and whether rumble strips existed where you left the lane. Dashcam files overwrite on a loop, so pull the card the same week. Where a vehicle is drivable, photograph the tires and tread before a shop rotates them, because tread condition is a favorite lever in these arguments.</p>
<h2>How it plays out in this county</h2>
<p>A file that survives negotiation and is filed at all goes to the Victorville District courthouse on Civic Drive for civil matters arising in the northern desert communities, not to the courthouse downhill in San Bernardino, and venue for any particular case still depends on the parties and the facts. Practically, the percentage discussion happens years earlier, in writing, between an adjuster and whoever is answering the letters. The useful posture is unglamorous: get the report, get the photographs, get the medical record complete, and treat every proposed percentage as a claim that has to be supported by something other than the adjuster's summary of a police narrative that nobody has read out loud.</p>

## svc_crash_reports_and_chp_investigations_lede

The collision report is the document every insurer reads first and the one most people never actually obtain. Getting it is a specific process with a form, an identification requirement and a fee, and the agency that has it depends on where the crash happened.

## svc_crash_reports_and_chp_investigations_body

<h2>Who is allowed to get a Highway Patrol report</h2>
<p>The Highway Patrol releases a collision report to a proper party of interest, which its own instructions define as a driver, a passenger, a property owner, a vehicle owner, the parent of a minor, or the legal guardian of an individual. The request form asks the applicant to check only one of those boxes. Where only property was damaged, the applicant lists their own name and address in the driver or owner field. There is a separate designation for a government entity or an insurance company. If you are unsure whether you qualify, the agency's instruction is to contact any of its area offices to verify before submitting.</p>
<h2>The mechanics of the request</h2>
<p>The process is deliberately paper-based. Print and complete the collision request form, then submit it in person or by mail to the nearest area office. Photocopy your driver license or picture identification card and attach it to the request; if you have neither, the form must be certified by a notary. Submit the applicable fees. Once the agency has received the request, verified that the requester is a proper party of interest, and received payment, a copy of the report is mailed to the address on the form. If you do not know the exact collision date or location, the instructions ask for an approximate date and as much location detail as you can provide.</p>
<h2>You do not have to travel to the office that took it</h2>
<p>One practical detail saves a great deal of driving in a county this large. Any area office can obtain a copy of a collision report the Highway Patrol investigated, or arrange for one to be mailed to your address, regardless of which office filed it. Someone injured out on a rural route who has since moved down the hill does not need to return to the desert to collect a report. The statutory basis for release rests on interest rather than curiosity: Vehicle Code 20012 keeps the reports confidential and provides for release to persons who have a proper interest in them, which is the same logic the form's party-of-interest box implements.</p>
<h2>Working out which agency has your report</h2>
<p>State highway segments are ordinarily the Highway Patrol's. City streets inside an incorporated city are ordinarily the city police department's. Unincorporated territory falls to the Sheriff, and this valley has a great deal of it. The Sheriff's Victor Valley Station, at 11613 Bartlett Avenue in Adelanto, serves Adelanto, Helendale, Oro Grande, Silver Lakes, El Mirage, Spring Valley Lake, Oak Hills, Wrightwood and the unincorporated Victor Valley areas not served by Hesperia, Victorville or Apple Valley. Ask the responding officer which agency is writing the report and get the report number at the scene if you can, because that single number removes weeks of searching later.</p>
<h2>The reports you are required to make yourself</h2>
<p>Two obligations belong to the people involved rather than to an officer. Vehicle Code 20008 requires a written report of a collision resulting in injury or death to be forwarded within twenty-four hours to the Highway Patrol, or to the police department of an incorporated city where the crash happened there. And a report to the state motor vehicles department is due within ten days when anyone is injured, no matter how minor the injury, or killed, or when property damage exceeds one thousand dollars; that department states plainly the report is required in addition to any report made to a police agency, the Highway Patrol or an insurer, and it may be completed by the driver or by an insurance agent, broker or legal representative.</p>
<h2>Exchanging information is its own statutory duty</h2>
<p>Vehicle Code 16025 requires drivers involved in a collision to exchange specified information at the scene, including evidence of financial responsibility, with a violation punishable by a fine not exceeding two hundred fifty dollars. Vehicle Code 20003 separately sets out what a driver involved in an injury crash must provide, including name, current residence address, vehicle registration information and, on request, driver license identification, together with identification to any traffic or police officer at the scene. Getting that information in writing at the roadside, rather than trusting a photograph of an insurance card you never look at again, is the cheapest evidence-gathering step in the entire file.</p>
<h2>What to do while you wait for the report</h2>
<p>A report can take weeks, and everything else can be done in the meantime. Write your own account the same day, including the route number and postmile or nearest exit, the direction of travel, the lane, the time, the light and the wind. Photograph both vehicles, all four tires, the roadway and the striping before any repair or maintenance pass. Note the tow yard from the report or the officer, and ask in writing that the vehicle be held unrepaired. When the report does arrive, read it for errors in direction, lane, vehicle position and party numbering, because those factual details drive the insurer's first evaluation.</p>

## svc_dui_caused_crash_claims_lede

When the other driver was impaired, the liability question is usually answered before the claim is opened. What remains is a criminal case running on its own clock, a damages analysis with an extra category available, and a short list of parties who may share responsibility.

## svc_dui_caused_crash_claims_body

<h2>Two separate proceedings, two separate purposes</h2>
<p>The criminal case and the civil claim are not the same matter and do not resolve together. Vehicle Code 23152 covers driving under the influence generally. Vehicle Code 23153 covers the situation where a driver under the influence commits an act forbidden by law, or neglects a duty imposed by law, and thereby proximately causes bodily injury to another person. Those provisions determine what the state does to the driver. Whether an injured person is compensated, and how much, is decided in an entirely different forum, on a different timeline, with different rules of proof. A conviction is powerful evidence in the civil file, and an acquittal or a plea to something lesser does not end the civil claim.</p>
<h2>The additional damages category impairment can open</h2>
<p>Ordinary carelessness produces compensatory damages measured by Civil Code 3333, which allows for all detriment proximately caused by the wrongful act. Impaired driving can reach further. Civil Code 3294 permits recovery of damages for the sake of example and by way of punishing the defendant where it is proven by clear and convincing evidence that the defendant is guilty of oppression, fraud or malice. That is a demanding standard, deliberately higher than the ordinary burden, and the statute also restricts when an employer can be assessed such damages for an employee's conduct. It is not automatic in every impaired-driving file, and the facts that support it are gathered early rather than argued late.</p>
<h2>Proposition 213 works in the injured person's favor here</h2>
<p>Civil Code 3333.4 normally strips non-economic damages from an injured person whose own vehicle was uninsured. Subdivision (c) reverses that outcome in one situation: those damages are not barred where the injured uninsured owner was harmed by a motorist who was later convicted of driving under the influence in that crash. The same statute independently bars non-economic recovery by any driver convicted of impaired driving in the collision. For a household with an uninsured car, the criminal outcome for the other driver can therefore change the shape of the claim, which is a reason to track the criminal case docket rather than ignore it.</p>
<h2>The narrow path to a third party's responsibility</h2>
<p>California generally does not hold a business that served alcohol responsible for what a customer later does behind the wheel. The exception is written narrowly. Business and Professions Code 25602.1 permits an action against a licensee, or a person required to be licensed, who sells or furnishes alcoholic beverages to an obviously intoxicated minor, where that service proximately causes death or injury. The elements matter, because a general observation that a bar kept serving someone does not fit the section. Where the facts might fit, the supporting evidence is receipts, register records, security footage and staff schedules, all of which are retained for a limited time and none of which will be preserved unless someone asks in writing.</p>
<h2>Coverage is often the real constraint</h2>
<p>Impaired-driving crashes tend to produce severe injuries and thin policies. The state minimum for policies issued or renewed on or after the first of January 2025 is thirty thousand dollars for injury or death to one person, sixty thousand where two or more are hurt, and fifteen thousand for property damage. A single air transport and a short admission can consume the first figure. Where a permissive driver was operating someone else's vehicle, the owner's liability under Vehicle Code 17151 is itself capped at fifteen thousand for one person, thirty thousand for more than one, and five thousand in property damage. That arithmetic is why your own uninsured and underinsured endorsement gets read in week one.</p>
<h2>What the criminal file eventually produces</h2>
<p>The criminal case generates material a civil file cannot easily create on its own: chemical test results, officer observations recorded contemporaneously, field sobriety documentation, and sometimes a recorded statement. Access and timing depend on the stage of the proceeding and on the rules governing disclosure, and criminal records are not simply handed over on request. Meanwhile the physical evidence follows the ordinary rules. Ask that both vehicles be held unrepaired, get the tow yard name from the report, and preserve any dashcam card before it loops.</p>
<h2>Reporting and deadlines still apply</h2>
<p>An obvious impairment does not suspend any of the ordinary requirements. A written report of an injury collision goes to the Highway Patrol or the city police within twenty-four hours under Vehicle Code 20008 where the section applies, and a separate report goes to the state motor vehicles department within ten days when there is any injury, however minor, a death, or property damage above one thousand dollars. The civil deadline for a personal injury claim remains two years from the injury under Code of Civil Procedure 335.1, and it does not wait for the criminal case to finish.</p>

## svc_dust_storm_and_low_visibility_crashes_lede

A wall of blowing dust can put a High Desert highway from clear to blind in seconds, and the pileups that follow involve drivers who never saw the vehicle they struck. Liability in those files turns on speed and on what each driver did once visibility failed.

## svc_dust_storm_and_low_visibility_crashes_body

<h2>What the weather service says a dust event does</h2>
<p>The National Weather Service describes a dust storm as arriving suddenly, in the form of an advancing wall of dust and debris that may be miles long and several thousand feet high, striking with little warning and usually lasting only a few minutes. Blinding, choking dust reduces visibility quickly, and the agency states plainly that reduced visibility can cause accidents, including chain collisions and massive pileups. Haboobs of this kind form out of thunderstorm outflow winds and are most common in the Southwest. The short duration is part of what makes these files difficult: by the time a report is written, the condition that caused the crash no longer exists anywhere on the road.</p>
<h2>The guidance drivers are given, and how it becomes an argument</h2>
<p>The same agency's safety guidance is specific. Do not enter a dust storm area if it can be avoided. Never stop on the traveled portion of the roadway. If a vehicle is parked off the roadway, all lights should be turned off, because in past incidents motorists who left their lights on while parked off the pavement caused approaching drivers to use those lights as a guide, inadvertently leave the roadway, and in some cases collide with the parked vehicle. Every one of those instructions can be turned into a fault argument after the fact, which is why an accurate account of where each vehicle actually came to rest matters more here than in an ordinary rear impact.</p>
<h2>The speed rule that governs a whitewash</h2>
<p>There is no posted limit for zero visibility, and there does not need to be one. Vehicle Code 22350 forbids driving at a speed greater than is reasonable or prudent having due regard for weather, visibility, and the traffic on and the surface and width of the highway, and forbids in any event a speed that endangers safety. A driver traveling at the posted limit into a visible dust wall is squarely inside that language. On a two-lane undivided highway the fixed ceiling is fifty-five under Vehicle Code 22349(b) regardless of conditions. Where the crash is a chain, Vehicle Code 21703 adds the following-distance duty, requiring a driver not to follow more closely than is reasonable and prudent with regard to speed, traffic and highway condition.</p>
<h2>Multi-vehicle chains and how the shares get divided</h2>
<p>A dust-event pileup produces the most complicated liability picture on any desert road, because a dozen drivers each contributed something and each of them was blind. California resolves this with proportional allocation rather than a single guilty party. Civil Code 1431.2 makes each defendant liable for non-economic damages only in direct proportion to that defendant's own percentage of fault, with a separate judgment entered against each, while economic losses remain jointly recoverable. That structure is why a claim against the one driver with the largest policy does not automatically reach the full human cost of a serious injury, and why identifying every vehicle in the chain early has practical value.</p>
<h2>Evidence that vanishes with the wind</h2>
<p>Nothing about a dust event is durable. The dust is gone within minutes, the tire marks blow over, and there is no photograph of the condition unless someone at the scene took one. That makes three sources unusually valuable. Dashcam footage from any vehicle in the chain shows the actual visibility, and those cards overwrite on a loop, so a request in the first week is worth more than a subpoena in the sixth month. Highway camera and traffic-management imagery may exist for the corridor and is retained only briefly. And the weather record for that hour and location, requested from the national service that issues the warnings, establishes conditions independently of anyone's memory.</p>
<h2>What to write down while it is still fresh</h2>
<p>Record the route and the postmile or last exit, the direction of travel, and the time to the minute, because a dust event's duration is measured in minutes and the timeline is the case. Note where the leading edge appeared to be when you first saw it and how much road you had. Note whether you braked, slowed gradually or left the pavement, and if you left it, how far off and whether your lights stayed on. Count the vehicles you can see, photograph their plates, and get names before people disperse, since in a chain the driver who first struck you may be four vehicles away from the driver whose insurer eventually pays.</p>
<h2>The medical and reporting sequence afterward</h2>
<p>Chain collisions in poor visibility generate a large number of moderately injured people simultaneously, which strains the local system. The county emergency medical services agency routes all field aircraft requests through a designated dispatch center that assigns the closest available aircraft using flight-following data, and applies a rotation when two aircraft sit within less than a mile of each other. Ground and air records will therefore be held by separate providers. On the reporting side, the state motor vehicles department requires its own report within ten days whenever anyone is injured, no matter how minor, or killed, or when property damage exceeds one thousand dollars, and that filing sits on top of any report the responding agency writes.</p>

## svc_fatigue_and_distracted_driving_lede

Long straight desert highway is the worst combination for an already tired driver: nothing to react to, and no forgiveness when a reaction is finally needed. Fatigue and phone use leave different evidence trails, and both are provable with the right requests made early.

## svc_fatigue_and_distracted_driving_body

<h2>What the federal data says about drowsy driving</h2>
<p>The national highway safety administration reports six hundred forty-four deaths in drowsy-driving-related crashes in 2024. Its longer-running estimate, from 2017, put ninety-one thousand police-reported crashes as involving drowsy drivers, with an estimated fifty thousand people injured and nearly eight hundred killed. The agency states directly that determining a precise number is not yet possible, because crash investigators look for clues that drowsiness contributed and those clues are not always identifiable or conclusive, and it notes broad agreement across the traffic safety, sleep science and public health communities that the published figures understate the problem. That acknowledged undercount is worth remembering when an adjuster treats the absence of a fatigue notation as proof that fatigue was not involved.</p>
<h2>Why this corridor produces the condition</h2>
<p>The agency describes fatigue as arising from sleep restriction, a late or long shift at work, staying out late, or a long and monotonous drive. Every one of those describes ordinary traffic through this valley. Commuters cross the pass before dawn and return after dark. Freight runs the corridor overnight. Weekend travelers drive four or five hours in one push. The monotony itself is a factor the agency names, and forty miles of straight two-lane highway with nothing to look at supplies it. None of that establishes fault in a particular crash. It establishes why the question belongs in the first list of things to investigate rather than the last.</p>
<h2>The evidence that shows a driver had been awake too long</h2>
<p>Fatigue leaves an indirect record. Where the other driver was working, the paper is unusually good: duty status records, dispatch messages and timestamps, fuel and toll receipts establishing how far and how long, and delivery windows showing the schedule the driver was trying to meet. Those records are kept for defined periods and then lawfully discarded, which is the reason a written preservation demand identifying the vehicle, the date and the driver goes out immediately rather than after a report arrives. Where the driver was private, the trail is receipts, phone location data where lawfully reachable, and the physical evidence at the scene itself, particularly the absence of any braking or steering input before impact.</p>
<h2>The distracted-driving statute and what it does not do</h2>
<p>Vehicle Code 23123.5 addresses driving while holding and operating a handheld wireless telephone or electronic communication device, and sets a base fine of twenty dollars for a first offense and fifty dollars for each subsequent offense. Two things follow. First, that penalty is small enough that no one should mistake it for a measure of the harm, and it is owed to a court rather than to an injured person. Second, a violation of a statute enacted for safety is evidence in a civil claim about whether a driver was behaving reasonably. The citation, if one was issued, appears on the collision report, which is one more reason to obtain the report rather than rely on a summary.</p>
<h2>Phone records exist, and they are not obtained casually</h2>
<p>Call and data records held by a carrier can establish whether a device was in use at the moment of impact, and they are not available on request from an interested member of the public. Reaching them requires a legal process appropriate to the stage of the matter, and carriers retain different categories of data for different periods. What can be done immediately is narrower and still useful: note whether the other driver was visibly holding a device, whether any witness saw it, whether the vehicle drifted before impact, and whether a passenger's account of the moments before matches the physical evidence. Written notes made the same week carry more weight than a recollection given a year later.</p>
<h2>The physical scene tells you whether anyone reacted</h2>
<p>The single most informative feature of a fatigue or inattention crash is what is missing. No tire marks. No steering input. A vehicle that tracked straight into a stationary queue or drifted gradually across a centerline at an even angle. Photograph the roadway for the full distance behind the point of impact, not just the impact itself, and do it before the next sweeping or grading pass. Event data recorders in modern vehicles capture pre-crash throttle and brake application, which is direct evidence of whether a driver reacted at all, and that module goes wherever the vehicle goes.</p>
<h2>Fault percentage and the calendar</h2>
<p>Expect the argument to run both ways, because a defense theory that you were the inattentive one is standard. Under the state's comparative fault instruction the defense carries the burden on two points, carelessness by the injured person and that carelessness operating as a substantial factor in producing the harm, after which damages are reduced by the percentage of responsibility the jury assigns and the court performs the calculation. On timing, the injury deadline is two years from the injury under Code of Civil Procedure 335.1, damage to the vehicle runs three years under Code of Civil Procedure 338, and a report to the state motor vehicles department is due within ten days when anyone was injured or killed or property damage exceeded one thousand dollars.</p>

## svc_filing_deadlines_in_california_lede

More than one clock starts on the day of a crash, they run at different speeds, and at least one of them can expire in six months rather than two years. Nothing about a cooperative adjuster pauses any of them.

## svc_filing_deadlines_in_california_body

<h2>The general injury period, and the separate one for the vehicle</h2>
<p>Code of Civil Procedure 335.1 sets two years for an action for assault, battery, or injury to or the death of an individual caused by the wrongful act or neglect of another. Code of Civil Procedure 338 sets three years for several categories, including an action for taking, detaining or injuring goods or chattels, which is where damage to the vehicle sits. Those two periods run independently from the same crash. Settling the repair claim in the first month therefore extends nothing on the injury side, and a body shop check cashed in week three has no effect on the two-year date. Diary both dates separately, in writing, on the day you learn them.</p>
<h2>The six-month clock where a public entity may be responsible</h2>
<p>Where the claim involves a city, a county, a transportation agency or the state, the ordinary periods do not govern the first step. Government Code 911.2 gives a claimant six months from accrual, and no longer, to present a claim to the entity for death or for harm to a person or to personal property, while other categories of claim get a longer period. Once the entity delivers or mails written notice rejecting the claim, Government Code 945.6 permits suit only within six months of that date. Missing the presentation step generally forecloses the claim entirely, which is why roadway condition questions get identified in week one rather than year two.</p>
<h2>Where a public entity claim comes from in a crash file</h2>
<p>Government Code 835 is the provision that makes a roadway condition actionable, providing for liability where a dangerous condition of public property existed at the time of injury, proximately caused it, created a reasonably foreseeable risk of that kind of injury, and either was created by an employee's negligent or wrongful act within the scope of employment or the entity had actual or constructive notice long enough beforehand to have protected against it. In practice that covers missing striping, a shoulder drop-off, an obscured sign, a signal malfunction, or a known hazardous curve. It is precisely the category most likely to be discovered late, and it carries the shortest deadline in the file.</p>
<h2>Minors are treated differently, with one exception</h2>
<p>Code of Civil Procedure 352 tolls the running of a limitation period while a person entitled to bring an action is a minor, so the clock on a child's injury claim generally does not begin until the disability ends. That same section withholds the tolling where the defendant is a public entity or a public employee and the claim was one that had to be presented under the government claims statutes. A child hurt by a roadway condition therefore does not get the benefit of the tolling on the presentation requirement, which makes the six-month rule the controlling date in the very files where a family is least likely to be thinking about paperwork.</p>
<h2>The uninsured motorist clock is its own animal</h2>
<p>Insurance Code 11580.2 does not borrow the two-year injury period; it creates accrual conditions. No cause of action accrues on this coverage unless, within two years of the crash, the insured has filed suit against the uninsured motorist, has agreed with the insurer on the amount due under the policy, or has formally instituted arbitration by written notice sent certified mail with return receipt requested. Where the vehicle was never identified, the statute adds requirements at the front end too: actual physical contact, a report to the police department of the city where it happened or, in unincorporated territory, to the sheriff or the local Highway Patrol headquarters, within twenty-four hours, and a sworn statement to the insurer within thirty days.</p>
<h2>The notice your insurer owes you about a deadline</h2>
<p>State claims regulations impose a warning obligation, with a limit worth knowing. Under title 10, section 2695.7(f) of the California Code of Regulations, an insurer must give written notice of any limitation period on which it may rely to deny a claim, not less than sixty days before the expiration date, or immediately if the claim is first received inside that window. For a first-party uninsured motorist claimant the notice period is at least thirty days. The subsection does not apply to a claimant represented by counsel. Nobody should organize a file around receiving that letter, but its absence in an unrepresented file is worth noting.</p>
<h2>The administrative filings that are not lawsuits</h2>
<p>Two reporting duties sit on a shorter timescale and are easy to overlook. A written report of an injury or fatal collision goes to the Highway Patrol, or to a city police department where the crash occurred in an incorporated city, within twenty-four hours under Vehicle Code 20008 where that section applies. And a report to the state motor vehicles department is due within ten days whenever anyone is injured, however minor the injury, or killed, or when property damage exceeds one thousand dollars, and that filing is required in addition to any report made to a police agency or an insurer.</p>

## svc_head_on_and_crossover_crashes_lede

Nothing about a claim changes faster than the arrival of a vehicle in the wrong lane. Closing speeds add together, injuries are severe, and the question that decides the file is almost always why the vehicle crossed at all.

## svc_head_on_and_crossover_crashes_body

<h2>Why the vehicle left its lane is the whole case</h2>
<p>A crossover collision is rarely a judgment error inside a lane. Something took the driver out of it. The candidate explanations are short and each points at different evidence: falling asleep, a medical event, impairment, a phone, an attempted pass on a two-lane road, a tire failure, a load shift, or a wheel dropping off a soft shoulder and the driver overcorrecting back across the centerline. Sorting between them is not a matter of opinion. It is scene evidence, vehicle evidence and records, and the order in which those are requested determines how much of it still exists when someone finally looks.</p>
<h2>The striping rule that frames the passing question</h2>
<p>On an undivided highway the legal question often reduces to whether a pass was permitted at all. Vehicle Code 21460 governs double parallel solid yellow lines and forbids driving to the left of them except in the limited circumstances the section describes. Where the crash happened matters as much as how, because striping changes over the length of a route. Photograph the pavement markings at the actual point of impact, not a quarter mile back, and note the direction the photograph faces. Where a driver was turning rather than passing, Vehicle Code 21801 puts the duty on the turning driver to yield to oncoming traffic close enough to constitute an immediate hazard.</p>
<h2>What the state has documented about this crash type locally</h2>
<p>Caltrans has written its own diagnosis of the problem on a High Desert route. The environmental initial study for the Route 138 median and shoulder project, covering District 08-SBD-138 from postmile 0.0 to 2.3, states the purpose as improving safety and reducing the number and severity of cross-centerline and run-off-roadway collisions on a segment experiencing a higher-than-average accident rate. The existing facility is an undivided conventional highway with one lane in each direction. The build alternative adds a four-foot median buffer with rumble strips, shoulder rumble strips on both sides, and eight-foot outside shoulders, widening only the south side, extending twelve culverts and replacing six, over three hundred forty working days.</p>
<h2>The unfinished two-lane geometry north of town</h2>
<p>The same structural problem exists on the route running north out of the city. San Bernardino County's transportation authority documented in its state baseline agreement that the US 395 Freight Mobility and Safety Project will convert a seven-mile section of state highway between Interstate 15 and Route 18 from a two-lane facility to a four-lane facility, adding a median, turning lanes, eight-foot shoulders, and pedestrian and bicycle accommodations at connecting intersections. Until that conversion is complete, opposing traffic on those seven miles is separated by paint. A crash there is not an ordinary intersection file, and the physical layout at the impact point deserves photographs from both approaches.</p>
<h2>Vehicle size decides who is hurt worse</h2>
<p>Federal data makes the mismatch explicit. In the 2023 passenger vehicle report, when a passenger car and a light truck struck each other head-on in a fatal crash, an occupant was killed nearly three times more frequently in the passenger car than in the light truck. The same report shows occupant fatality shares split forty-nine percent passenger cars and fifty-one percent light trucks overall. On a corridor carrying heavy freight and full-size pickups alongside ordinary sedans, that mismatch is a daily condition rather than an unusual one, and it shapes both the injury severity and the insurance depth on the other side.</p>
<h2>Records that answer the question of why</h2>
<p>Where the other vehicle belongs to a business, the answer usually sits in paperwork rather than at the scene: duty status records, dispatch messages, maintenance and inspection files, and any camera footage. Where the other driver was private, the answers are phone records, pharmacy and medical history where legally reachable, receipts showing where the driver had been, and the event data recorder in the vehicle. All of it is easier to obtain while the vehicles are still in a yard. Written preservation requests naming the tractor, trailer, vehicle, date and driver go out before the collision report is even typed, not after.</p>
<h2>The severity of the injuries changes the sequence</h2>
<p>Because these crashes produce critical injuries, the medical decisions happen at the roadside. In San Bernardino County the county emergency medical services agency coordinates every field aircraft request through a designated dispatch center, which assigns the closest available aircraft, and its policy names an acceptable standard of a fifteen-minute time differential in favor of air transportation. The county's designated trauma centers include two Level I facilities well down the pass. The practical consequence for a family is that the medical record begins in more than one system, and no single hospital holds the whole story. Requesting every piece early is what makes the file complete.</p>

## svc_high_speed_highway_collisions_lede

Impact energy climbs with the square of speed, which is why a crash at desert highway pace produces injuries that a city-street version of the same collision does not. The law that governs speed here is not only the number on the sign.

## svc_high_speed_highway_collisions_body

<h2>Two speed rules operate at once</h2>
<p>Drivers read the posted number and stop there. The code does not. Vehicle Code 22350 states the basic speed law: no person shall drive at a speed greater than is reasonable or prudent having due regard for weather, visibility, the traffic on and the surface and width of the highway, and in no event at a speed that endangers the safety of persons or property. That rule can be violated at a speed below the sign, which matters at four in the afternoon on a wind-blown stretch of open pavement. Layered on top of it, Vehicle Code 22349(b) caps travel at fifty-five miles an hour on a two-lane undivided highway, a description that fits a great deal of road in this valley.</p>
<h2>What the code does with very high speeds</h2>
<p>At the extreme end the legislature wrote separate consequences. Vehicle Code 22348(b) makes driving at a speed of one hundred miles an hour or more its own offense, punishable by a fine of five hundred dollars for a first violation, seven hundred fifty dollars for a second within three years, and one thousand dollars for a third within five years, with license consequences attached. Those figures are penalties owed to a court, not compensation owed to an injured person, and the two systems run on separate tracks. A citation is nonetheless evidence of the speed a responding officer believed was involved, which is one reason the collision report matters more in a high-speed file than in a parking-lot file.</p>
<h2>The federal numbers on who speeds in fatal crashes</h2>
<p>Speed shows up unevenly by vehicle type. In the federal 2023 passenger vehicle data, the share of drivers in fatal crashes who were speeding was twenty-two percent for passenger cars, sixteen percent for pickups, fourteen percent for sport utility vehicles and nine percent for vans. The same report counted 23,959 passenger vehicle occupant deaths in 2023, a six percent decrease from 25,500 the prior year, alongside an estimated 1,947,298 occupants injured. Passenger vehicles accounted for seventy-six percent of the 58,319 vehicles involved in fatal crashes that year. Those proportions describe the country, not this city, but they explain why a speed question dominates the file when the vehicles involved were traveling at highway pace.</p>
<h2>Why highway speed changes the medical picture</h2>
<p>At sixty-five or seventy-five, restraint systems reach the edge of what they are designed to manage. Airbags fire, seat belts load the chest and abdomen, and the injuries that follow tend to be structural: fractures needing fixation, internal bleeding, spinal involvement, and head injury without any external wound. That is also why the transport decision gets made at the roadside rather than in a waiting room, and why the record of a serious desert crash starts with a helicopter or an ambulance log rather than with an urgent-care note. The claim value follows the record, so a gap in the first week of documentation is expensive later.</p>
<h2>The evidence that only exists briefly</h2>
<p>High-speed collisions leave measurable physical evidence, and most of it disappears fast. Tire marks on hot asphalt fade. Gouges and fluid stains get swept or blown over. Debris fields are cleared to reopen a lane. Event data recorders inside modern vehicles hold pre-crash speed, throttle, braking and belt status, and that module leaves with the vehicle when the salvage yard sells it. Ask in writing that the vehicles be held unrepaired and unsold, and note the yard's name and the tow company from the report. Photograph the roadway itself, including the lane lines, shoulder edge and any missing delineators, before the next maintenance pass.</p>
<h2>Where high speed and geometry combine badly</h2>
<p>Two conditions in this valley multiply the risk. The first is a long straight run with nothing to look at, which encourages the speed and dulls the attention that speed demands. The second is the transition from a divided freeway to an undivided highway with no physical separation between opposing traffic. Caltrans has documented that combination locally: its initial study for the median-and-shoulder work on Route 138 describes an undivided conventional highway with one lane in each direction, a higher-than-average accident rate, and a stated purpose of reducing the number and severity of cross-centerline and run-off-roadway collisions, addressed with twelve-foot lanes, an eight-foot outside shoulder with rumble strips and a four-foot median with rumble strips.</p>
<h2>Practical steps in the first week</h2>
<p>Get the report request in, then build the physical record. Write down the route and postmile or the last exit you passed, the lane, the direction, the time and light conditions, and the wind. Photograph tread depth on all four tires before anything is rotated or replaced, because Vehicle Code 27465(b)(1) makes it unlawful to operate on a tire worn to less than one thirty-second of an inch in any two adjacent grooves, and that argument will be aimed at you if the numbers allow it. Keep a dated log of symptoms, appointments, missed shifts and mileage driven to appointments below the pass, since the distance is real and the mileage adds up.</p>

## svc_hit_and_run_and_phantom_vehicle_claims_lede

Two situations get filed under the same heading and behave very differently. In one, a driver leaves the scene and is later identified. In the other, a vehicle ran you off the road without ever touching you, and California coverage law treats that gap seriously.

## svc_hit_and_run_and_phantom_vehicle_claims_body

<h2>What the code requires of a driver who leaves</h2>
<p>Vehicle Code 20001 makes it an offense for the driver of a vehicle involved in a crash resulting in injury or death to fail to stop and comply with the statute's duties. The penalties in subdivision (b)(2), applying where the crash resulted in death or permanent serious injury, run to imprisonment for two, three or four years, or ninety days to one year in county jail, together with a fine of one thousand to ten thousand dollars. Vehicle Code 20003 sets out what the stopping driver must actually provide, including name, address, vehicle registration information and, on request, driver license identification, along with identification to any traffic or police officer at the scene.</p>
<h2>The twenty-four hour reporting requirement</h2>
<p>Vehicle Code 20008 requires a written report of a collision resulting in injury or death to be forwarded within twenty-four hours to the Highway Patrol or, where the crash occurred in an incorporated city, to that city's police department. That deadline exists independently of any claim. Separately, Vehicle Code 16025 requires drivers involved in a crash to exchange specified information at the scene, with a fine not exceeding two hundred fifty dollars for a violation. Where no other driver stayed, the reporting duty falls entirely on the person who was hit, and the timestamp on that report frequently becomes the anchor date for everything that follows.</p>
<h2>Why the phantom-vehicle version turns on physical contact</h2>
<p>Where the vehicle that caused the crash is never identified, the claim usually shifts to your own uninsured motorist coverage, and Insurance Code 11580.2 imposes conditions that do not forgive delay. The statute requires that the unidentified vehicle actually made physical contact with the insured, or with the vehicle the insured occupied. It also requires that the insured or someone on the insured's behalf report the crash within twenty-four hours to the police department of the city where it occurred, or, if it happened in unincorporated territory, to the county sheriff's office or the local headquarters of the Highway Patrol. A sworn statement then goes to the insurer within thirty days.</p>
<h2>Geography decides which agency receives that notice</h2>
<p>Much of the Victor Valley is unincorporated, so the twenty-four hour notice does not automatically go to a city police department. The Sheriff's Victor Valley Station at 11613 Bartlett Avenue in Adelanto serves Adelanto, Helendale, Oro Grande, Silver Lakes, El Mirage, Spring Valley Lake, Oak Hills, Wrightwood and the unincorporated Victor Valley areas not served by Hesperia, Victorville or Apple Valley. Getting the notice to the wrong agency is a real risk on a route that crosses jurisdictional lines several times in twenty miles. Ask the responding officer which agency is taking the report, write down the report number, and keep proof of the date the notice was given.</p>
<h2>Evidence that identifies a vehicle nobody stopped to name</h2>
<p>Fleeing vehicles leave material behind. Photograph paint transfer on your own vehicle before any repair, along with broken trim, lamp fragments and any debris in the roadway, since a lamp assembly or grille piece can identify a make, model and year range. Note anything the description can be narrowed to: color, body style, whether the vehicle was lifted, wheel type, a lettered door, a distinctive missing panel. Then canvass for footage in the first days. Commercial buildings, fuel stops and gates along desert routes overwrite recordings quickly, and a polite in-person request while the manager still remembers the day works better than a formal demand months later.</p>
<h2>The consent trap inside your own policy</h2>
<p>Insurance Code 11580.2 also withdraws this coverage where an insured, without the carrier's written consent, either settles with a party who may be legally liable or proceeds against that party to judgment. When a driver is eventually identified and a modest liability check appears, endorsing it can therefore end the coverage that was going to carry the remainder of the loss. The sequence has to be deliberate: identify every policy that might respond, read the endorsement language rather than the summary page, request consent in writing, and only then decide about the liability payment.</p>
<h2>Two calendars, neither of which pauses</h2>
<p>The deadline for suing a driver who is eventually identified is two years from the injury under Code of Civil Procedure 335.1. The uninsured motorist claim runs on its own accrual rule: under Insurance Code 11580.2 no cause of action accrues unless, within two years of the crash, suit has been filed against the uninsured motorist, agreement has been reached with the insurer on the amount due, or arbitration has been formally opened by certified written notice with return receipt requested. Those are separate calendars. Note also the state motor vehicles report within ten days where anyone was injured or killed, or property damage exceeded one thousand dollars.</p>

## svc_insurance_company_delay_tactics_lede

California regulates how quickly a claim has to be answered, in calendar days, in writing. Most of the delay people experience in a crash claim is not permitted by those rules, and knowing the actual numbers changes the tone of the correspondence.

## svc_insurance_company_delay_tactics_body

<h2>Fifteen days to answer a claimant at all</h2>
<p>The first number is the smallest and the most frequently ignored. Under title 10, section 2695.5(b) of the California Code of Regulations, upon receiving any communication from a claimant regarding a claim that reasonably suggests a response is expected, a licensee must furnish the claimant with a complete response based on the facts then known, immediately but in no event more than fifteen calendar days after receipt. The subsection stops applying once the licensee receives notice of legal action by that claimant. Section 2695.5(a) sets a parallel obligation running to the regulator: a complete written response to a Department of Insurance inquiry about a claim within twenty-one calendar days.</p>
<h2>Forty days to accept or deny</h2>
<p>Section 2695.7(b) gives an insurer that has received proof of claim forty calendar days at the outside, and requires it to act immediately where it can, either accepting or denying the claim in whole or in part and documenting the accepted and denied amounts in the file. A denial or rejection of a first-party claim must be in writing and must list all bases for the decision, including the factual and legal bases within the insurer's knowledge, and where the denial rests on a statute, law, policy provision, condition or exclusion, the written denial must reference it and explain how it applies. An insurer denying, rejecting or disputing a third-party claim, in whole or in part, must also do so in writing.</p>
<h2>The thirty-day drumbeat when more time is claimed</h2>
<p>Delay is permitted, but only on the record. Section 2695.7(c)(1) provides that where more time is needed, the insurer must give the claimant written notice of the need for additional time within the original window, specifying any additional information required and stating the continuing reasons it cannot decide. That written notice then has to be repeated at thirty-calendar-day intervals until the insurer decides or is served with notice of legal action, and where the determination depends on a future event, the insurer complies by explaining the situation and estimating when a decision can be made. A file that has gone quiet for four months without those letters is not following the rule.</p>
<h2>Payment is also on a clock</h2>
<p>Acceptance is not payment, and the regulation addresses the gap. Section 2695.7(h) requires that upon accepting a claim in whole or in part, and where necessary upon receipt of a properly executed release, the insurer immediately, and in no event more than thirty calendar days later, tender payment or otherwise act to perform its claim obligation, in the amount it accepted. Where multiple coverages are involved and the payee is known, accepted amounts must be paid within the same thirty days if payment would end the insurer's known liability under that coverage, unless doing so would impair the insured's interests.</p>
<h2>Investigation, information demands and lowball offers</h2>
<p>Three further subsections describe conduct rather than deadlines. Section 2695.7(d) requires an insurer to conduct and diligently pursue a thorough, fair and objective investigation, and forbids persisting in seeking information not reasonably required for or material to resolving a claim dispute, which is the answer to a fourth request for the same records. Section 2695.7(e) bars delaying or denying settlement of a first-party claim on the basis that responsibility should be assumed by others, except as policy provisions, statutes or regulations otherwise provide. Section 2695.7(g) prohibits attempting to settle by making an offer that is unreasonably low, and lists seven factors the Commissioner weighs in assessing that, including whether the insurer considered the claimant's own evidence of value, available legal authority, and its own adjuster's advice on the amount of damages.</p>
<h2>The statute of limitations warning letter</h2>
<p>Section 2695.7(f) requires an insurer, except where a claim has been settled by payment, to give written notice of any limitation period or other time requirement on which it may rely to deny a claim, not less than sixty days before the expiration date, or immediately where the claim first arrives inside that window. For a first-party claimant on an uninsured motorist matter the notice period is at least thirty days. The subsection expressly does not apply to a claimant represented by counsel. The statutory backdrop is Insurance Code 790.03, which defines unfair methods of competition and unfair or deceptive acts in the business of insurance, including unfair claims settlement practices.</p>
<h2>How to make the record the regulation assumes</h2>
<p>None of these rules help a file kept by telephone. Put every request in writing with a date, and keep a copy. Send records once, in an organized set, with a cover list of what is enclosed, so a repeat demand is visibly a repeat. Confirm each phone conversation by short written summary the same day. Note the date proof of claim was submitted, then count forty calendar days from it. Where the file has stalled without the written notices the regulation requires, the Department of Insurance reviews claims practices, and the regulation itself requires that a written denial tell a claimant that review is available and give the reviewing unit's address and telephone number.</p>

## svc_rollover_and_single_vehicle_crashes_lede

A report that lists one vehicle and no other driver is not a report that says nobody else is responsible. It says nobody else stopped. Roadway condition, a component failure, or a vehicle that forced the departure are all consistent with a single-vehicle designation.

## svc_rollover_and_single_vehicle_crashes_body

<h2>The federal numbers on rollover severity</h2>
<p>Rollovers are overrepresented in fatal outcomes, and the proportions vary sharply by vehicle type. Among passenger vehicle occupants killed in 2023, the national highway safety administration reports that the share who died in vehicles that rolled over was thirty-eight percent for pickups, thirty-three percent for sport utility vehicles, twenty-five percent for vans and twenty percent for passenger cars. The same report states that eighty-two percent of passenger vehicle occupants who were totally ejected from vehicles involved in fatal crashes were killed. Those figures explain why the occupant restraint question and the roof and door performance question both come up in these files, and why the vehicle itself has to be examined rather than photographed and released.</p>
<h2>What a single-vehicle classification actually means</h2>
<p>An officer writes a report from the physical scene and whatever anyone at the scene could tell him. If the vehicle that caused a departure never made contact and never stopped, there is nothing at the scene to record it. If a tire failed, the failure may not be obvious to a first responder. If the shoulder edge dropped four inches below the travel lane, that condition is not a party to the collision and does not appear as one. So the report reflects what was visible. Reopening the question means going back to the physical evidence: the vehicle, the tires, the roadway geometry at the exact point of departure, and any footage.</p>
<h2>The lane departure sequence and the countermeasures against it</h2>
<p>The mechanics are consistent enough that the state builds against them. A wheel drops onto a soft or lower shoulder, the vehicle pulls, the driver steers back hard, and the vehicle either crosses into opposing traffic or leaves the roadway on the far side and trips into a roll. Caltrans installs rumble strips on shoulders specifically to interrupt the first step of that sequence, and its Route 138 median and shoulder project in this county puts them on both shoulders while adding twelve-foot lanes, an eight-foot outside shoulder and a four-foot median buffer, on a segment the agency describes as having a higher-than-average accident rate driven by cross-centerline and run-off-roadway collisions.</p>
<h2>Where the roadway itself is the defendant</h2>
<p>Where a public agency's property contributed, a different body of law applies. Government Code 835 provides that a public entity is liable for injury caused by a dangerous condition of its property where the plaintiff establishes that the condition existed at the time of the injury, that the injury was proximately caused by it, that the condition created a reasonably foreseeable risk of the kind of injury incurred, and either that a negligent or wrongful act of an employee within the scope of employment created it, or that the entity had actual or constructive notice a sufficient time in advance to have taken protective measures. Notice is often the contested element, and it is proven with maintenance records and prior complaint history.</p>
<h2>The government claim calendar is much shorter</h2>
<p>The ordinary two-year injury deadline does not apply to a claim against a public entity. Government Code 911.2 gives a claimant six months from accrual, and no longer, to present a claim for death or for harm to a person or to personal property. Once the entity mails or delivers written notice rejecting it, Government Code 945.6 leaves six months from that date to bring suit. Code of Civil Procedure 352, which tolls limitations during a plaintiff's minority, expressly withholds that benefit where a claim had to be presented under those government provisions.</p>
<h2>The evidence list for a departure crash</h2>
<p>Photograph the point of departure from both directions, including the pavement edge, the drop to the shoulder, the shoulder material, whether rumble strips exist at that location and whether they are worn flat. Record the route number and postmile from the nearest marker. Photograph striping and delineators, and note any that were already broken or missing. Preserve the vehicle unrepaired with all four tires, and note the tow yard and the odometer. The event data recorder in a modern vehicle holds pre-crash speed, throttle, braking and belt status, and it leaves the case when the yard sells the vehicle.</p>
<h2>The injury record starts in more than one place</h2>
<p>Rollovers produce head, spine and internal injuries, so the transport decision is made at the roadside. San Bernardino County's emergency medical services agency coordinates every field aircraft request through a designated dispatch center, which assigns the closest available aircraft using automatic flight following and applies a rotation where two aircraft sit within less than a mile of each other, and its written standard treats a fifteen-minute time differential in favor of air transport as acceptable grounds for flying a patient. The consequence is a record split across a ground agency, an air operator and a receiving hospital, each requested separately, and each holding a piece of the injury picture.</p>

## svc_rural_road_and_two_lane_crashes_lede

The most dangerous roads in this valley are not the freeway. They are the two-lane routes running out to Phelan, Lucerne Valley, El Mirage and Oro Grande, where there is no median, no lighting, a soft shoulder and a fifty-five mile an hour limit that traffic treats as advisory.

## svc_rural_road_and_two_lane_crashes_body

<h2>What the code says about a two-lane undivided highway</h2>
<p>Vehicle Code 22349(b) fixes the maximum speed on a two-lane undivided highway at fifty-five miles an hour, and that ceiling stands independently of whatever sign a driver last saw. Above that sits Vehicle Code 22350, which prohibits any speed greater than is reasonable or prudent with due regard for weather, visibility, traffic and the surface and width of the highway. Read together, those two sections mean a driver can be traveling under the posted limit and still be in violation. On a narrow route with blowing sand, an unpaved shoulder and no centerline reflectors, that combination is not a technicality. It is often the core of the liability analysis.</p>
<h2>The roads are being rebuilt because of this exact problem</h2>
<p>The state's own records name the deficiency. San Bernardino County's transportation authority reported in its baseline agreement with the state that the US 395 Freight Mobility and Safety Project converts a seven-mile section between Interstate 15 and Route 18 from a two-lane facility to a four-lane facility with a median, turning lanes, eight-foot shoulders and pedestrian and bicycle accommodations at intersections. Separately, Caltrans documented a higher-than-average accident rate on Route 138 in this county and set out to reduce cross-centerline and run-off-roadway collisions with twelve-foot lanes, an eight-foot shoulder with rumble strips and a four-foot median buffer. Both documents describe the same underlying hazard.</p>
<h2>Turning movements without a turn lane</h2>
<p>Where a two-lane highway has no dedicated left-turn pocket, every vehicle waiting to turn becomes a stationary object in a fifty-five mile an hour lane. Two collision types follow. One is the rear impact into the waiting vehicle. The other is the turning vehicle crossing in front of oncoming traffic, which Vehicle Code 21801 addresses by placing the yielding duty on whoever intends to turn left or make a U-turn, measured against oncoming traffic near enough to be an immediate hazard. That duty is why the turning-lane component of the US 395 conversion is a safety item and not a convenience item, and why the presence or absence of a pocket at the impact point belongs in your photographs.</p>
<h2>Shoulders, sand and the overcorrection sequence</h2>
<p>Desert shoulders are frequently sand rather than pavement, and the drop from the travel lane edge can be abrupt. The sequence that follows a wheel leaving the lane is well understood: the vehicle pulls right, the driver steers hard left, and the vehicle crosses the centerline or leaves the road on the far side. Caltrans installs shoulder rumble strips specifically to interrupt that sequence, and the Route 138 project puts them on both the north and south shoulders. Where a crash involved a lane departure, document the shoulder condition, the height of any drop-off, whether rumble strips existed at that postmile, and whether striping was worn to the point of being invisible at night.</p>
<h2>Nobody saw it, so the physical record carries the file</h2>
<p>Witnesses are the ordinary substitute for missing evidence, and on these routes there are often none. What replaces them is measurement. Record the route number and the postmile from the nearest marker rather than a nearby address, the direction of travel, the time and the light, and the wind. Photograph tire marks, gouges and fluid trails from both directions before the next grading or sweeping pass. Note whether a delineator or guardrail was already damaged, and whether a fence line, mailbox cluster or utility pole shows fresh contact. This is the material an eventual reconstruction depends on, and almost none of it survives a month of desert wind.</p>
<h2>Response time is a factor of the geography</h2>
<p>A crash forty minutes from a hospital produces a different medical record than one four blocks from an emergency department. In this county the emergency medical services agency routes every field aircraft request through a designated dispatch center that assigns the closest available aircraft, and its written standard treats a fifteen-minute time differential in favor of air transport as acceptable grounds for flying a patient. Aircraft arrival time is measured from dispatch until the aircraft is over the scene, including crew preparation, flight planning, pre-flight and reconfiguration. For an injured person that means bills, records and reports from an air operator, a ground agency and a receiving hospital, each of which has to be requested separately.</p>
<h2>Which agency has the report, and what to do first</h2>
<p>Jurisdiction on these routes is not obvious. The Sheriff's Victor Valley Station, at 11613 Bartlett Avenue in Adelanto, serves Adelanto, Helendale, Oro Grande, Silver Lakes, El Mirage, Spring Valley Lake, Oak Hills, Wrightwood and the unincorporated Victor Valley areas not served by Hesperia, Victorville or Apple Valley. State highway segments are typically the Highway Patrol's. Ask the responding officer which agency is writing the report and get the report number at the scene if you are able. Separately, the state motor vehicles department requires a report be filed within ten days when anyone is injured, however minor, or killed, or when property damage exceeds one thousand dollars.</p>

## svc_serious_fracture_and_surgery_claims_lede

An orthopedic injury from a highway-speed crash is not one bill and one recovery. It is a surgery, hardware, a rehabilitation course, a possible second operation to remove that hardware, and a work history that changes. Settling before the medical picture is finished undervalues all of it.

## svc_serious_fracture_and_surgery_claims_body

<h2>What the damages statute actually allows</h2>
<p>Civil Code 3333 measures a tort claim of this kind by the sum that compensates every detriment proximately caused, anticipated or otherwise. That is broader than the sum of the bills received so far. It includes the surgery already performed and the one a surgeon says is likely, the physical therapy course, the assistive equipment, the earnings lost during recovery and the earning capacity lost if the injury is permanent, along with the non-economic component. An offer built from the invoices in the adjuster's file on a given afternoon is a measure of the adjuster's file, not of the detriment.</p>
<h2>Fixation hardware and the second operation nobody budgets for</h2>
<p>Fracture repair frequently involves plates, rods or screws, and a meaningful share of those cases involve a later procedure to remove or revise the hardware. That second operation carries its own costs, its own recovery period and its own time away from work. Where a treating surgeon has said such a procedure is likely, that opinion is part of the claim and needs to be in the record in writing, not mentioned in passing at an appointment. The same applies to a documented expectation of arthritis in a joint that took an intra-articular fracture, or of a permanent restriction on lifting or standing.</p>
<h2>The hospital lien attaches to your recovery, not to your bill</h2>
<p>Civil Code 3045.1 gives a hospital that furnishes emergency and ongoing services to a person injured by another's act a lien upon damages recovered, whether by settlement, compromise or judgment, for the reasonable and necessary charges of that care. That lien exists whether or not anyone explains it, and it is the reason a settlement figure and the money that reaches an injured person are two different numbers. Civil Code 3040 then limits how much of a recovery those health care liens may absorb, applying statutory caps to the share a lienholder can take. Identifying every lienholder before agreeing to any figure is basic arithmetic, not a technicality.</p>
<h2>Records at four addresses, because of where the care happens</h2>
<p>Serious orthopedic care in this valley rarely stays in one building. Trauma designations in San Bernardino County, as the state emergency medical services authority publishes them, include two Level I centers well down the pass and Level IV designations closer to the desert communities, and the county agency coordinates field aircraft requests through a designated dispatch center that assigns the closest available aircraft, applying a fifteen-minute time differential in favor of air transport as an acceptable standard. So the record is split: a ground agency, possibly an air operator, a receiving facility, a transferring facility, an orthopedic surgeon's office and a physical therapy provider. Each is a separate request, and a missing set is a discount.</p>
<h2>Documenting the part of the loss that has no invoice</h2>
<p>The economic side has paper. The functional side usually does not, unless someone creates it. Keep a dated log from the first week: what you cannot do, what you now need help with, what a shift at work involves that you can no longer perform, how far you can walk, how long you can stand, and what sleep is like. Note the mileage to every appointment, which in the High Desert is genuinely substantial when the surgeon is below the pass. Photograph the incision, the hardware imaging where you have it, and the assistive equipment. This is the material that makes a permanent restriction legible to someone who has only read a chart.</p>
<h2>Why the timing of any settlement discussion matters</h2>
<p>An early offer arrives while the medical picture is still open, and once a release is signed the claim is over regardless of what a follow-up scan shows. The useful sequence is to reach a point where the treating physicians can state whether the condition is stable, whether further surgery is expected, and whether there is a permanent restriction, and only then to evaluate. That takes longer than an adjuster prefers. It is also the difference between compensating a fracture and compensating a career change.</p>
<h2>The calendar and the percentage argument</h2>
<p>The deadline for a personal injury claim is two years from the injury under Code of Civil Procedure 335.1, and property damage to the vehicle runs three years under Code of Civil Procedure 338. Those run independently, so resolving the repair side extends nothing on the injury side. Expect a comparative fault argument as well as a damages argument: under the state's standard instruction the defense has to establish carelessness by the injured person and show it worked as a substantial factor in producing the harm, and non-economic damages are then allocated among defendants in direct proportion to each one's own percentage of fault under Civil Code 1431.2.</p>

## svc_tire_blowout_and_vehicle_defect_crashes_lede

Summer surface temperatures on High Desert pavement are punishing, and a tire that would survive a coastal commute can come apart at seventy-five miles an hour out here. When it does, the file turns into a physical evidence case with a very short shelf life.

## svc_tire_blowout_and_vehicle_defect_crashes_body

<h2>The tire is the case, so the tire has to be kept</h2>
<p>Nothing matters more in these files than physically preserving the failed component. A tread separation looks different from a road-hazard puncture, and different again from a tire run badly underinflated, and those distinctions are made by examining the actual carcass, not a photograph of the wheel well. Ask in writing that the vehicle be held unrepaired and unsold, and that every tire, including the ones that did not fail, stay with it. Get the tow yard name and address from the collision report. Photograph the sidewall markings before anything moves, since the identification code molded into the sidewall establishes the manufacturer, plant, size and week of manufacture.</p>
<h2>What the code says about worn tires, and why it cuts both ways</h2>
<p>Vehicle Code 27465 makes it unlawful to operate a vehicle on a tire in unsafe operating condition, and subdivision (b)(1) specifies less than one thirty-second of an inch of tread depth in any two adjacent major grooves at three locations around the tire. Read that as two obligations. The first is your own: the argument that a claimant drove on illegally worn rubber is standard, and photographs taken before a shop replaces anything are the only reliable rebuttal. The second is everyone else's, including the owner of the vehicle that came apart in front of you and the business that maintained it.</p>
<h2>The federal picture on tire-related crashes</h2>
<p>The national highway safety administration reports five hundred eleven traffic fatalities in tire-related crashes in 2024. Its consumer program identifies the maintenance items that determine whether a tire survives: pressure, rotation, tread depth and tire aging, together with a government grading system printed on the sidewall of every passenger tire sold in the country, rating treadwear, traction performance and temperature resistance. Temperature resistance is not an abstract category in this valley. Heat, load and underinflation act together, and a tire carrying a full family plus luggage on a summer afternoon is operating at the edge of several of those variables at once.</p>
<h2>Where the responsibility may sit besides the driver</h2>
<p>A component failure widens the list of possible parties. Depending on what the examination shows, responsibility may involve a manufacturer, a distributor, a retailer who mounted the wrong size or reinflated an already damaged tire, a shop that performed the last service, or a fleet owner whose maintenance intervals were not met. Where the vehicle that failed was commercial, the relevant material is documentary: inspection and maintenance records, work orders, purchase records for the tires, and duty status records establishing how long the equipment had been running. Those files are retained for defined periods and then discarded lawfully, which is why the preservation letter goes out before the collision report is available.</p>
<h2>The federal recall record is public and worth checking</h2>
<p>Tires and vehicles are recalled, and the national safety administration publishes recall information and accepts safety complaints from the public. Checking whether the specific tire line or vehicle was subject to a campaign is an early step, not a late one, because the answer can reshape who the claim is against. Complaints filed by other owners describing the same failure mode carry no automatic legal weight, but they point at what the examination should look for, and they are available without any formal process.</p>
<h2>What a single-vehicle failure crash looks like on paper</h2>
<p>A tire failure at speed often ends as a run-off-road or rollover event with no other vehicle involved, which means the initial report may describe it as driver error. Federal 2023 data shows how consequential that outcome is: among passenger vehicle occupants killed, the share in vehicles that rolled over was thirty-eight percent for pickups, thirty-three percent for sport utility vehicles, twenty-five percent for vans and twenty percent for passenger cars, and eighty-two percent of occupants totally ejected from vehicles in fatal crashes were killed. A single-vehicle designation on a report is a classification of the collision, not a legal conclusion about why the vehicle left the road.</p>
<h2>The clock and the first week</h2>
<p>The deadline for an injury claim is two years from the injury under Code of Civil Procedure 335.1, and a claim for damage to the vehicle itself runs three years under Code of Civil Procedure 338. Neither one waits for a laboratory examination. In week one: preserve the vehicle and all tires in writing, photograph the sidewall codes and the tread on every wheel, locate the receipt and installation date for the tires, note the odometer, and record the route, postmile, direction, time, air temperature and load in the vehicle. Then file the state motor vehicles report within ten days if anyone was injured or killed or property damage exceeded one thousand dollars.</p>

## svc_traumatic_brain_injury_claims_lede

A brain injury is the injury most likely to be missed at the roadside and most likely to be disputed later. There is often no external wound, imaging in the first hours can look unremarkable, and the symptoms that matter most are reported by the people who live with the injured person.

## svc_traumatic_brain_injury_claims_body

<h2>The scale of the problem in federal data</h2>
<p>The Centers for Disease Control and Prevention reports approximately two hundred fourteen thousand one hundred ten traumatic brain injury related hospitalizations in 2020 and sixty-nine thousand four hundred seventy-three related deaths in 2021, which the agency describes as more than five hundred eighty-six hospitalizations and more than one hundred ninety deaths a day. Critically for a crash claim, the agency states those estimates do not include brain injuries treated only in emergency departments, in primary care, in urgent care, or those that went untreated entirely. The published counts therefore describe the severe end of a much larger group, and the cases that resolve as claims very often sit in the part the data excludes.</p>
<h2>Why the injury gets missed in the first hours</h2>
<p>At a highway crash scene the visible injuries get attention, and a person who is walking and answering questions is not the priority. A brain injury can occur without any impact to the head, from acceleration forces alone, and initial imaging may be read as normal because the standard scan is looking for bleeding rather than for diffuse injury. Meanwhile the injured person is the least reliable narrator of their own condition. The practical response is to have any loss of consciousness, confusion, amnesia for the crash itself, repeated questioning, headache, nausea or light sensitivity written into a medical record on the day it happens, however minor it seems at the time.</p>
<h2>The symptoms that decide these claims</h2>
<p>The disputed ground in a brain injury file is rarely the imaging. It is function. Word-finding difficulty. Losing the thread of a conversation. Irritability that the family notices before the patient does. Sleeping too much or not at all. Sensitivity to noise in a workplace that was tolerable a month earlier. Inability to hold two tasks at once. Those are exactly the deficits that do not appear on a scan and do not appear on an invoice, and they are the ones that end careers. Documenting them means dated notes, ideally from more than one person, describing specific incidents rather than general impressions.</p>
<h2>Who should be writing things down</h2>
<p>Ask a spouse, a parent, a roommate, a supervisor and a close coworker each to keep short dated notes of what they observe. Independent observations from people with different vantage points are harder to dismiss than a single account. Keep the work record too: written performance feedback, tasks reassigned, hours reduced, mistakes that were out of character. Where school is involved, keep the grade record and any accommodation discussion. This is unglamorous paperwork that costs nothing and is impossible to reconstruct two years later, and it is frequently the difference between a claim treated as a headache and a claim treated as a permanent impairment.</p>
<h2>The damages the statute permits, and the specialists who prove them</h2>
<p>Civil Code 3333 reaches every detriment proximately caused by the wrongful act, foreseen or not, which for a brain injury reaches beyond the acute care bills into neurological and neuropsychological evaluation, cognitive rehabilitation, vocational consequences, and lost earning capacity where the impairment is permanent. Proving the functional side generally requires a formal evaluation rather than a family's account alone. Getting to a treating specialist matters medically first and evidentially second, and in this valley that specialist is often below the pass, which makes the appointment mileage and travel burden part of the loss worth logging.</p>
<h2>Where the medical record begins for a serious crash</h2>
<p>The record starts before any hospital. San Bernardino County's emergency medical services agency routes all field aircraft requests through a designated dispatch center that assigns the closest available aircraft, measuring aircraft arrival from dispatch until the aircraft is over the scene including crew preparation, flight planning, pre-flight and reconfiguration, and it treats a fifteen-minute time differential in favor of air transport as an acceptable standard. Every air transport also receives a quality improvement review. For a family, the first neurological observations may sit in a paramedic or flight record rather than a hospital chart, and those records have to be requested from the provider that created them.</p>
<h2>Deadlines, including the one that treats children differently</h2>
<p>The general deadline for a personal injury claim is two years from the injury under Code of Civil Procedure 335.1. Where the injured person was a minor at the time, Code of Civil Procedure 352 tolls the running of the period while that disability exists, though that same section withholds the tolling where the defendant is a public entity and a claim had to be presented under the government claim statutes. Where a public entity is involved, Government Code 911.2 allows six months from accrual, and no more, to present a claim for harm to a person or to personal property, and Government Code 945.6 then allows six months from a written rejection notice to file suit.</p>
