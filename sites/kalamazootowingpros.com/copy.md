## pricing_body

Towing prices in Kalamazoo are built from four parts, and once you know the parts the number stops feeling arbitrary. There is the hook, a flat fee for sending a truck and attaching the car. There is the distance, metered per mile from where the car sits to where it is going. There is the truck type, because a flatbed that carries the whole car costs more to run than a wheel-lift that carries half of it. And there is the situation: a winch line into a ditch, a night or holiday call, or a collision scene that needs the road swept.

### The hook and the miles

Local price lists posted by Kalamazoo operators put a tow inside the city and Portage in a fairly tight band, and the national 2026 rate surveys land in the same place: a fixed hook fee, then a per-mile figure that is lower for a wheel-lift and higher for a flatbed. The mistake people make is comparing hook fees alone. A cheap hook with a steep mileage rate costs more on a run to a dealer in Portage than a slightly higher hook with honest miles. Ask for both numbers, and ask what the total is to your actual destination. That total is what should be quoted before the truck leaves, and it is the figure this site expects you to be given.

### Why the flatbed costs more, and when you cannot avoid it

A flatbed lifts all four wheels off the road. It is the only correct way to move an all-wheel-drive car, an electric car, a low sports car, a motorcycle, or anything whose wheels are damaged. In Kalamazoo that describes a large share of the cars on the road, because Subarus and all-wheel-drive crossovers are what people buy for lake-effect winters. Dragging one on a wheel-lift can wreck the transfer case in a single mile, which turns a modest tow into a repair costing many times more. If a driver offers a wheel-lift for an all-wheel-drive car, that is the moment to say no.

### Roadside calls are cheaper than tows

A jump start, a lockout, a tire swap onto the spare, or a couple of gallons of fuel is a service call, not a tow, and the price reflects that. Local operators post service-call rates well under a local tow. The right sequence on a car that clicks in the cold is to try the jump first and only hook if the battery is beyond saving or the starter is the problem. You should be charged for what was done, not for the tow that was dispatched.

### Winching and recovery

A car in the ditch along M-43, buried to the axles on a Comstock side road, or off the shoulder of I-94 after a spin is a recovery before it is a tow. Recovery is billed by time on the winch line, and the range is wide because the jobs are: a straight pull from a shallow ditch is quick, a car on its side in a culvert is not. What you can expect is an honest estimate on arrival, based on what the driver can see, before the line goes on.

### Nights, holidays and whiteouts

After-hours and holiday calls carry a surcharge across the industry, and the surveys put it anywhere from a fixed add-on to a percentage of the base. On a lake-effect day like the January 2026 pile-up that closed northbound US-131 from West Main Street to Exit 38, every truck in the county is on the freeway, and the wait for a car in a driveway is real. You should be told the surcharge up front and the wait honestly, not sold a fifteen-minute promise nobody can keep.

### When the city and the state set the number

If police ordered the tow, Chapter 40 of the Kalamazoo City Code says the City Commission fixes the maximum charge by resolution or contract, and it is unlawful for a wrecker to exceed it. Storage after a police-ordered tow is governed by MCL 257.252i, which limits the fee to the lesser of the contract rate with the agency or the lot's own rate, and gives you twenty days from the notice to redeem the car or go to court. ## svc_flatbed_towing_lede

## svc_flatbed_towing_body

A flatbed carries the whole car. The bed tilts, the car is winched up or driven up, it is strapped down at four points, and it travels with nothing touching the pavement. That is the only correct way to move a large share of what drives around Kalamazoo, and the reason is mechanical, not marketing.

### Why all-wheel-drive cars cannot ride a wheel-lift

A wheel-lift picks up one end of the car and lets the other end roll. On a front-wheel-drive car with the rear wheels down, nothing in the drivetrain turns and no harm is done. On an all-wheel-drive car, the wheels on the ground are connected through the center differential or transfer case to the wheels in the air, and towing it that way for even a mile can destroy that unit. Subarus, all-wheel-drive Audis and BMWs, and most crossovers and SUVs on Kalamazoo roads fall into this group, because all-wheel-drive is what people buy for lake-effect winters. Every electric car is in the same group for a different reason: the motors are geared directly to the wheels and turn whenever the wheels do. If a driver offers a wheel-lift for any of these, decline.

### Low cars, classics and motorcycles

A car with little ground clearance can be damaged by the angle of a wheel-lift. Classic cars and anything with a custom suspension are the same. Motorcycles ride a flatbed with a wheel chock and soft straps over the bars, never a wheel-lift. If you are moving a car that does not run at all, from a driveway in Oakwood to a shop across town, a flatbed is also the practical choice, because the car does not need to steer.

### Damaged wheels and locked steering

After a collision, a wheel that is bent or folded under cannot roll, and a steering column that locked when the key came out cannot be steered. Both need a flatbed with a winch to draw the car up the bed. That overlaps with accident recovery, which has its own page on this site, but the truck is the same.

### Long runs and dealer transfers

A flatbed is also the truck for the run that is not an emergency: a car going to a dealer in Portage or Grand Rapids, a purchase coming home from a private seller in Battle Creek, a car being moved to a student's apartment near the Western Michigan University campus at the start of the semester. Those are scheduled at a normal hour, at a normal rate, and the per-mile figure is the number to compare.

### What the loading looks like

The driver positions the truck on level ground where possible, which on a Kalamazoo side street in winter can mean asking you to move a trash bin or clearing a patch of snow. The bed tilts to the ground. A running car is driven up; a dead one is winched, with the cable attached to the frame or a factory tow point, never to suspension parts. Four straps go over the tires or through the wheels, and the driver checks them before pulling away. At the other end, the car is lowered, unstrapped, and parked where you asked.

### What it costs

The hook for a flatbed runs higher than a wheel-lift, and the per-mile rate does too, because the truck weighs more and carries more. Those two numbers to your destination are stated before the truck leaves. The pricing page shows the flatbed band separately from the wheel-lift band so you can see the difference for what it is.

Michigan law, MCL 257.252i, limits the storage fee on a police-ordered tow to the lesser of the daily rate in the storage company's contract with the agency or the rate the lot actually charges, and gives you twenty days from the abandonment notice to redeem the car or petition the court before it can be sold. Bring the title or registration and photo ID, get the itemized bill, and go quickly, because days accrue whether or not the insurer has called back. Personal property inside the car can usually be collected during lot hours even if the car itself is held.

### Insurance and the paperwork

Photograph the scene, the other car's plate, and the damage before the car moves, if it is safe to do so. Get the police report number from the officer. The driver gives you a receipt showing hook, miles, recovery time and any surcharge as separate lines, which is what the insurer will ask for. If the car is a total loss, the insurer may move it again from your body shop to a salvage yard, and that second tow is on them.

### What it costs

Recovery time is the variable. A straight pull from a shallow ditch is quick; a car on its side in a culvert is not. The driver gives an estimate on arrival, before the winch line goes on, and the tow that follows is hook plus miles like any other. The pricing page lays out the recovery band and the after-hours surcharge alongside the tow rates.
