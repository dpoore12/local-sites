Carrollton draws a bright line that saves homeowners money. Replacing a door or window with one of the same size does not require a permit; changing the size of the opening does. ### Why the ranges above are ranges

Housing age sets the baseline. The 2020 to 2024 American Community Survey estimate puts the median year built in Carrollton at 1988, which means the typical door in this city is either an original builder-grade unit at the far end of its service life or the second unit installed on that opening. Two very different jobs share one description. On an original door the springs are undersized, the hinges are worn oval and the track has been shimmed at least once. On a replacement door the hardware is often sound and the fault really is one part.

Storm history sets the second variable. The city reported straight-line winds of 80 to 95 mph across all 37.1 square miles on May 28, 2024. Wind of that kind does not always break a door outright. ### What a fair quote itemizes

The spring line should carry four numbers: wire diameter, inside diameter, length and wind direction. Anything less means nobody has measured the door, and a spring chosen by eye is a spring that fails early or burns out an opener. The quote should state the door's approximate weight or the drum series it is set up for, list rollers by stem length and whether they are steel or nylon with sealed bearings, and count hinges rather than describing them.

Labor should appear as its own line, with the trip or diagnostic charge shown separately and a plain statement of whether it is credited toward the repair. On a full replacement, expect the model and gauge, the insulation value if the garage is conditioned, whether the existing opener and rail are being reused, the track configuration, new bottom weatherseal, and haul-away of the old sections. Where the opening size is not changing, the quote should say so, because that sentence is what confirms no permit is needed.

### The upsell to push back on

The pattern here is the opener sale. A door that reverses on its way down, hesitates, or runs rough is presented as a control board or motor problem, and a new opener with a bright feature list is proposed. On 1980s and 1990s housing stock the common causes are a spring that has lost tension, a photo eye knocked out of alignment by a bicycle, dry rollers, or track pinched where a car door has kissed it. Replacing the opener over any of those sets a new motor to work against the same resistance.

The tell is worth asking for. Disconnect the opener, lift the door by hand to waist height and let go. A correctly balanced door stays roughly where it is. A door that slams down or shoots up is a spring problem in an opener costume, and that test costs nothing.

## svc_garage_door_spring_repair_body

### The noise is the diagnosis and the coil confirms it

A failed torsion spring announces itself once, loudly, and then the garage goes quiet. Press the wall button afterward and the motor strains against a door that suddenly weighs everything it always weighed, minus the help. Stand well back from the opening and look at the steel shaft above the header. A broken torsion coil shows a clean interruption with both halves slightly unwound and relaxed. On the older extension layout, a slack spring sags alongside the horizontal rail, restrained only by its safety cable if one was ever fitted.

### A cycle is a round trip, and this street burns through them

ANSI/DASMA 102 counts one cycle as travel from closed to fully open and back to closed. Section 6.1 of the 2004 edition sets the design floor at a minimum of 10,000 cycles for a system that is properly selected, installed, operated and maintained. Section 2.8 defines the residential category by use rather than by building type: under 1,500 cycles in a year is the expectation. Two commuters, a trash cart and a dog on a leash clear 1,500 without trying. Hardware bought at the minimum rating is therefore roughly a six or seven year part in a household that treats the garage as its real front entrance. With the Census median build year here landing at 1988, the shaft over most of these openings is not carrying its first pair.

### Heat, grit and the things that steal cycles

The trade association data sheet on spring cycle life is specific about what shortens one. Rust reduces the effective area of the wire and lowers its strength, and corrosion pits give fatigue cracks somewhere to start and accelerate. A nick or a notch from rough handling becomes the point where the wire eventually parts. That same sheet tells owners in climates of extreme heat to keep the door closed where they can, which is worth hearing in an unconditioned Rosemeade or Josey Ranch garage in August. Fine grit blown under the bottom seal packs into bearing plates and drums, and every ounce of added drag gets charged to the counterbalance.

### Why this repair never rides on a storm claim

People ask whether a broken spring belongs on a hail claim, and the honest answer is that it does not. The state insurance department's own home guide lists wear and tear among the things most policies leave out, and a coil that reached its cycle limit is the plainest example of it. Hail can pit the skin of the same door on the same afternoon a spring lets go, and the two remain separate matters. One may be worth documenting for a carrier. The other is a repair with a measured part at the end of it.

### The winding cone is not a place for a screwdriver

Half a broken spring is still partly loaded, and on a two-spring door the second coil is fully loaded. Winding bars are the correct tool and nothing in a kitchen drawer substitutes for them; screwdrivers and cut rebar slide out of the cone under torque and travel across the garage. Until the visit, leave the door flat on the slab, pull the plug so nobody tests it, and leave the emergency release alone. A partly raised door on a dead counterbalance is held up by friction and hope.

### Measured, not guessed

A technician reads the specification off the broken part: wire gauge, the inside diameter of the coil, finished length and which direction it winds. The replacement is then matched to what the leaf weighs today rather than what the catalog assumed. That distinction has teeth. The cycle life sheet warns that paint or components added over the years can push a door past the weight its manufacturer specified, and a scale can be put under it once the counterbalance is disabled. Fitting a spring without knowing it meets the door's counterbalance requirement costs cycle life in both the door and the operator, and leaves the door unsafe to lift by hand. The standard also refuses torsion springs whose index ratio drops below six to one on a sectional door, that ratio being mean coil diameter divided by wire diameter.

### How the visit runs, and what moves the number

Both coils come off a two-spring shaft, since the second one counted the same openings in the same hot garage. Drums are reset, cables get inspected with the tension released, and the new pair is wound to the turn count the door height calls for. Too little torque there and the door drifts down out of the open position over the following months. Then the operator stays disconnected while the door is lifted by hand, and it should hold near waist height instead of sinking or climbing. Ask to watch that check happen. Spring count, wire size, cycle rating and whether cables, bearings or drums are renewed at the same time set where the total lands, and the researched local ranges are laid out on the pricing page.

### Down a few inches, then straight back up

Part 1211 of title 16 of the federal regulations covers residential operators built for sale in this country on or after January 1, 1993. Section 1211.6(a) requires primary inherent entrapment protection in the head itself. Section 1211.7(a)(1) then spells out the behavior: past the first foot of travel down from the full upmost position, a closing door has to begin reversing within two seconds of contact with an obstruction, then return to that upmost position and stop there. So a door that retreats and parks itself wide open has not failed in the ordinary sense. It is filing a report, and the work is finding out what it thinks it touched.

### Where the beam gets broken in a garage on these streets

Photo eyes are only as useful as their sight line, and that line lives about six inches off the slab. A recycling cart parked against the jamb, a bag of lawn food, a bicycle tire, a cobweb strung across a lens or a bracket knocked by a car door all read as something in the way. Low winter sun straight into a west-facing opening can wash out a receiver on its own. Look for a steady indicator lamp on both housings; one that is dark or flickering has lost its partner. Wipe the lenses with a dry cloth rather than a solvent, and square the brackets instead of bending them into place.

### The thirty-second rule, and why force settings hide problems

Federal rules also address a door that never arrives where the head expects it. Under 1211.7(g)(1), a system that is not tracking actual door position must reverse if the lower limit device has not been reached within 30 seconds of starting to close. Where the system does watch position, 1211.7(f)(1) requires it to check at increments no coarser than an inch. Both provisions mean the same thing for a homeowner. Drag anywhere in the travel eventually looks like an obstruction, so an aging door with dry rollers or a tired spring produces a symptom that appears electronic. Winding the closing force upward deletes the report instead of the resistance. Run the monthly check the required instructions describe: the door must reverse when it meets a one and a half inch object, or a two-by-four laid flat, on the floor.

### Motor noise with the door standing still

Have someone press the button while you watch the rail from the side. Chain or belt moving with the door still down means the trolley is no longer coupled to the carriage. That happens after the release cord gets pulled during a power cut, which this city knows well. NOAA's record of the May 28, 2024 bow echo notes intense winds bringing trees down across Carrollton roadways, and the survey put straight-line winds at 80 to 95 mph. A grind with the chain refusing to move usually means the nylon drive gear in an older chain head has rounded off its teeth, and that is a part rather than a purchase. A short hum that quits points instead at a capacitor, a board, or a leaf that has grown too heavy for the unit.

### Faults that follow the clock

A logic board bolted to the ceiling of an unconditioned garage spends every summer afternoon well above the temperature its capacitors prefer. That produces intermittent behavior instead of a clean failure, so the useful thing to report is the time of day. A head that answers the wall button at seven in the morning and ignores every remote at five in the afternoon is describing a thermal problem, not a mystery. Receivers, boards and capacitors are individual parts on most residential heads, so a diagnosis at that level is a component swap.

### The circuit, the permit desk and who may touch it

Hanging a new head on an existing receptacle is service work on equipment you already own. Running a fresh circuit for one is electrical work, and the city prices that in its single-trade category at $4 per $1,000 of value with a $75 floor, with a reinspection at $50. The city's remodeling guidance is direct about who does it. A homeowner may perform work on their own homestead, and anyone else doing electrical work has to hold the state license and be registered as a contractor with the city. That registration is free, renews once a year, and no permit is issued until every contractor on the job is current. An expired registration also stops further inspections from being requested, even where the work is finished.

### Repair or replace, said out loud

A head old enough to have no external sensors at all should be replaced rather than nursed. Federal rules allow three secondary arrangements under 1211.6(b): a control requiring constant pressure to lower the door, a means of connecting an external device such as photo eyes, or an inherent secondary device in the operator. A cracked housing or a board nobody stocks ends the conversation the same way. Short of that, most units above these openings are worth a part, and the answer you should get is a named fault, the price of that part, and a candid read on whether it makes sense given the age of the unit.

### Stop cycling it, then look

Whatever cable and rollers remain engaged are all that hold a derailed door up, and none of that arrangement is stable. Pressing the button again to see how bad it is can turn one corner out of the rail into a door lying across a hood. Switch the opener off at the receptacle, clear the bay, and do not lever anything back with a pry bar. Loose cable coiled at a jamb, one corner noticeably low, or a roller sitting outside the rail all mean the load has moved somewhere it was not designed to go.

### Three ways a door leaves its rail here

A cable can unspool from its drum. Each lift cable anchors at the bottom bracket and winds onto a grooved drum at the end of the torsion shaft, and when a spring lets go the door drops hard enough to jump those grooves. That side then has no lift at all and the leaf racks in the opening.

A roller can quit turning. Nylon wheels get brittle and crack, and steel ones seize once the bearings dry and dust packs in, after which the wheel slides instead of rolling and climbs out at the curve where vertical track turns horizontal.

Third, the track itself can move. A bumper touching the vertical rail at the jamb shifts it a quarter inch, which is plenty for a roller to pop out at the same height on every trip until somebody finds the bend.

### The clay under the driveway moves the opening

This corner of Dallas and Denton counties sits on the shrink-swell clay the Blackland Prairie is known for. The federal soil description for the Houston Black series records high shrink-swell potential, with cracks half an inch to four inches wide at a depth of a foot during dry spells, staying open for 90 to 150 cumulative days in most years. Ground that opens and closes on that scale carries slabs and framing with it. A garage opening that has racked even slightly holds one vertical track out of plumb, and the door then binds at the same point every cycle. Nobody calls in a panic about that one. There was no bang and no impact, just a door that has been rubbing in the same spot for two summers and a roller that is now half worn away on one side.

### Wind is the fast version of the same damage

The quick way to knock a door out of line is a storm. NOAA's storm record for the early hours of May 28, 2024 logs a 65 knot gust west of Hebron on the city's northern edge. The narrative describes intense winds bringing trees down across Carrollton roadways, and the survey placed straight-line winds at 80 to 95 mph. Hail rode the same episode, at 1.25 inches near Bouchard and 1.50 inches at Rose Hill in Dallas County and 1.25 inches at Corinth in Denton County. Pressure like that bows struts, opens up jamb brackets and pushes rails out of plumb, and none of it announces itself the way a dented skin does.

### Dented, or genuinely unable to work

These are two separate findings and they should be written up separately. The industry's guidance for building occupants sets out a list. Check jambs and header attachment, track fasteners and alignment, rollers sitting in the track without tension, brackets and hinges, cables for slack, and the sections from inside and out for cracks, dents and buckling. It flags the area around end-hardware fasteners in particular, because damage there can indicate structural damage rather than a mark. Its plainest line is the useful one: any deformation of panels, track or hardware can make a door questionable as to whether it can operate. The companion sheet for technicians goes a step further and says that where stress or fatigue has compromised the door's performance, the owner should be advised to forward the written assessment to their insurance company.

### What the repair actually involves

The door gets clamped below its lowest roller and blocked so it cannot travel, and stored load is released in the right order before anything is moved. Sections come back into line one at a time. Cracked rollers and torn hinges get replaced instead of persuaded back into shape, and a deformed length of rail is renewed rather than hammered flat. The pair of cables goes back onto the drums at equal tension, so both sides start and finish their travel together. The counterbalance gets checked at the same time, since a broken spring is often the reason the door came out rather than a coincidence. Then the door is cycled by hand with the operator still disconnected, because a remaining pinch in the rail will chew up the new parts inside a month.

### Where the money goes

Rollers, hinges and cables are inexpensive parts. Track sections cost more, and a bottom bracket or a bent shaft more again. The total really turns on three things: the distance the door traveled out of line, whether a section creased along the way, and whether the opening itself has to be corrected before a square door will run in it. A crooked opening is the one finding that turns a same-day repair into a scoped job, and it should be shown to you rather than described.

Marks in the steel skin are the least urgent item on the list. Look instead for sections that no longer meet the header evenly, an end stile pulling at fasteners, a bowed strut, a cracked hinge, distorted track or a sprung bottom bracket. Each is a loss of function, and belongs on its own photographed line rather than in a dent count. The trade's inspection sheet asks for a judgment on repair versus replacement, weighing sections, stiles, struts, hinges, rollers and fasteners.

### What this state actually excludes, and what it does not

The cosmetic exclusion prescribed by the state insurance department is narrower than its reputation. Endorsement HO-145, titled Exclusion of Cosmetic Damage to Roof Coverings Caused by Hail, took effect on May 2, 1998, with TDP-022 doing the same job on dwelling forms. Read the title: roof coverings. It defines cosmetic loss as damage that alters appearance without letting water through or causing the covering to fail at its function. It attaches only to a policy receiving a premium credit for an impact-resistant roof covering, and it is void unless the insured signed it. No equivalent state-prescribed cosmetic exclusion exists for a garage door. A carrier declining dents on your door is using its own wording, and the declarations page and endorsement list are where to find it.

### The deductible arithmetic that decides whether to file at all

Percentage deductibles are what people discover too late. The state department's consumer page does the arithmetic in public: translate the percentage into dollars first, and on a home insured for $150,000 a 5 percent deductible is $7,500. In that same example a $6,500 repair pays nothing under the percentage policy, while the identical loss under a $500 deductible pays $6,000. A garage door is a smaller ticket than a roof. Before opening a claim over a bruised door, convert the wind and hail deductible on your declarations page into dollars and hold it against what a door costs.

### The clocks that run on your insurer

Chapter 542 of the Insurance Code puts deadlines on the carrier, not only on the policyholder. Section 542.055 gives an insurer 15 days after notice of a claim to acknowledge it, start investigating and request the items it reasonably needs. Section 542.056 requires written acceptance or rejection by the 15th business day after those items are received. If it cannot decide in that window it must say why, and it then gets 45 more days. Payment on an accepted claim is due by the fifth business day under 542.057. Delay past 60 days after the requested items are in hand triggers Section 542.060: interest at 18 percent a year as damages, plus reasonable attorney's fees. Section 542.059 extends each period by 15 days after a weather-related catastrophe, the situation a metro-wide hail week creates.

### When only the amount is in dispute

Appraisal is the tool for a disagreement about amount rather than coverage. Since September 1, 2025, Chapter 1813 of the Insurance Code has required personal residential property policies to include an appraisal provision, described in the statute as a dispute resolution process solely to determine the amount of loss. An amount settled that way binds both sides, apart from fraud, accident or material mistake. Two limits matter: the commissioner sets the completion period by rule, and appraisal cannot decide whether a dent is covered at all. That is a coverage question and it lives somewhere else.

### Why replacing one section is often off the table

Two things kill the single-section repair and only one of them is money. Production is the first: a profile, stamped pattern and paint code from fifteen years ago frequently no longer exists, and a door facing full southern exposure has drifted away from anything a factory can ship. The assembly is the second. The trade association sheet on component substitution states that manufacturers design or specify every component. It warns that replacing parts on doors rated to standards such as ANSI/DASMA 102 with non-identical parts may weaken the assembly and expose whoever substituted them to liability. Differences in material thickness or strength can be impossible to see, and the sheet names extra fasteners, unusual fastener locations, longer roller stems, stronger roller bearings and heavier track as the details that carry the door. A certifying agency can invalidate a certification over a substituted part. A section that looks right, in other words, is not the same finding as a section that is right.

### Same size, or a different opening

The local rule is short and it saves real money. Replacing doors or windows with units of the same size does not require a permit, while changing the size of an opening does. Widening a single bay into a double is structural work, with a header, permit and inspections behind it. The city has run the 2024 family of international codes and the 2023 electrical code since September 1, 2025 under Ordinances 4265 and 4290, so framing work is inspected against the 2024 residential code, not the earlier cycle. Whoever does the job registers with the city through its online portal, free and renewable yearly. No state license covers this trade as such, and the Department of Public Safety says installing residential door operators needs no license from it unless the operator ties into an alarm system or logs entry and exit.

### What the written estimate has to carry

An estimate worth signing names the skin gauge and layer count, the struts per section, the insulation value if the garage shares a wall with living space, and the track configuration and radius. It specifies new springs for the leaf being hung, because a heavier insulated door will not balance on coils wound for the one being hauled away. It lists bottom weatherseal, jamb repair where framing has been wet, disposal of the old sections, and a permit line if the opening is changing. Where it is not changing, the estimate should say so plainly, since that sentence documents why no permit was pulled. Lead time belongs there too, since a specific color and window layout is built to order.
