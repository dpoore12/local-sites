Carrollton draws a bright line that saves homeowners money. Replacing a door or window with one of the same size does not require a permit; changing the size of the opening does. ### Why the ranges above are ranges

Housing age sets the baseline. The 2020 to 2024 American Community Survey estimate puts the median year built in Carrollton at 1988, which means the typical door in this city is either an original builder-grade unit at the far end of its service life or the second unit installed on that opening. Two very different jobs share one description. On an original door the springs are undersized, the hinges are worn oval and the track has been shimmed at least once. ### What a fair quote itemizes

The spring line should carry four numbers: wire diameter, inside diameter, length and wind direction. Anything less means nobody has measured the door, and a spring chosen by eye is a spring that fails early or burns out an opener. The quote should state the door's approximate weight or the drum series it is set up for, list rollers by stem length and whether they are steel or nylon with sealed bearings, and count hinges rather than describing them.

Labor should appear as its own line, with the trip or diagnostic charge shown separately and a plain statement of whether it is credited toward the repair. On a full replacement, expect the model and gauge, the insulation value if the garage is conditioned, whether the existing opener and rail are being reused, the track configuration, new bottom weatherseal, and haul-away of the old sections. Where the opening size is not changing, the quote should say so, because that sentence is what confirms no permit is needed.

### The upsell to push back on

The pattern here is the opener sale. A door that reverses on its way down, hesitates, or runs rough is presented as a control board or motor problem, and a new opener with a bright feature list is proposed. On 1980s and 1990s housing stock the common causes are a spring that has lost tension, a photo eye knocked out of alignment by a bicycle, dry rollers, or track pinched where a car door has kissed it. Replacing the opener over any of those sets a new motor to work against the same resistance.

The tell is worth asking for. Disconnect the opener, lift the door by hand to waist height and let go. A correctly balanced door stays roughly where it is. A door that slams down or shoots up is a spring problem in an opener costume, and that test costs nothing.

### The noise is the diagnosis and the coil confirms it

A failed torsion spring announces itself once, loudly, and then the garage goes quiet. Press the wall button afterward and the motor strains against a door that suddenly weighs everything it always weighed, minus the help. Stand well back from the opening and look at the steel shaft above the header. A broken torsion coil shows a clean interruption with both halves slightly unwound and relaxed. On the older extension layout, a slack spring sags alongside the horizontal rail, restrained only by its safety cable if one was ever fitted.

### A cycle is a round trip, and this street burns through them

ANSI/DASMA 102 counts one cycle as travel from closed to fully open and back to closed. Section 6.1 of the 2004 edition sets the design floor at 10,000 cycles for a properly selected system, and section 2.8 puts residential use under 1,500 cycles a year. Hardware bought at that minimum is therefore a six or seven year part in a household treating the garage as its front entrance. With the Census median build year here landing at 1988, the shaft over most of these openings is not carrying its first pair.

### Heat, grit and the things that steal cycles

The trade association data sheet on spring cycle life is specific about what shortens one. Rust reduces the effective area of the wire and lowers its strength, and corrosion pits give fatigue cracks somewhere to start and accelerate. A nick or a notch from rough handling becomes the point where the wire eventually parts. That same sheet tells owners in climates of extreme heat to keep the door closed where they can, which is worth hearing in an unconditioned Rosemeade or Josey Ranch garage in August. Fine grit blown under the bottom seal packs into bearing plates and drums, and every ounce of added drag gets charged to the counterbalance.

### The winding cone is not a place for a screwdriver

Half a broken spring is still partly loaded, and on a two-spring door the second coil is fully loaded. Winding bars are the correct tool and nothing in a kitchen drawer substitutes for them; screwdrivers and cut rebar slide out of the cone under torque and travel across the garage. Until the visit, leave the door flat on the slab, pull the plug so nobody tests it, and leave the emergency release alone. A partly raised door on a dead counterbalance is held up by friction and hope.

### Measured, not guessed

A technician reads the specification off the broken part: wire gauge, the inside diameter of the coil, finished length and which direction it winds. The replacement is then matched to what the leaf weighs today rather than what the catalog assumed. That distinction has teeth. The cycle life sheet warns that paint or components added over the years can push a door past the weight its manufacturer specified, and a scale can be put under it once the counterbalance is disabled. Fitting a spring without knowing it meets the door's counterbalance requirement costs cycle life in both the door and the operator, and leaves the door unsafe to lift by hand. The standard also refuses torsion springs whose index ratio drops below six to one on a sectional door, that ratio being mean coil diameter divided by wire diameter.

### How the visit runs, and what moves the number

Both coils come off a two-spring shaft, since the second one counted the same openings in the same hot garage. Drums are reset, cables get inspected with the tension released, and the new pair is wound to the turn count the door height calls for. Too little torque there and the door drifts down out of the open position over the following months. Then the operator stays disconnected while the door is lifted by hand, and it should hold near waist height instead of sinking or climbing. Ask to watch that check happen. Spring count, wire size, cycle rating and whether cables, bearings or drums are renewed at the same time set where the total lands, and the researched local ranges are laid out on the pricing page.

### Down a few inches, then straight back up

Part 1211 of title 16 of the federal regulations covers residential operators built for sale in this country on or after January 1, 1993. Section 1211.6(a) requires primary inherent entrapment protection in the head itself. Section 1211.7(a)(1) then spells out the behavior: past the first foot of travel down from the full upmost position, a closing door has to begin reversing within two seconds of contact with an obstruction, then return to that upmost position and stop there. So a door that retreats and parks itself wide open has not failed in the ordinary sense. It is filing a report, and the work is finding out what it thinks it touched.

### The thirty-second rule, and why force settings hide problems

Federal rules also address a door that never arrives where the head expects it. Under 1211.7(g)(1), a system that is not tracking actual door position must reverse if the lower limit device has not been reached within 30 seconds of starting to close. Where the system does watch position, 1211.7(f)(1) requires it to check at increments no coarser than an inch. Both provisions mean the same thing for a homeowner. Drag anywhere in the travel eventually looks like an obstruction, so an aging door with dry rollers or a tired spring produces a symptom that appears electronic. Winding the closing force upward deletes the report instead of the resistance. Run the monthly check the required instructions describe: the door must reverse when it meets a one and a half inch object, or a two-by-four laid flat, on the floor.

### Motor noise with the door standing still

Have someone press the button while you watch the rail from the side. Chain or belt moving with the door still down means the trolley is no longer coupled to the carriage. ## svc_off_track_garage_door_repair_body

### Stop cycling it, then look

Whatever cable and rollers remain engaged are all that hold a derailed door up, and none of that arrangement is stable. Pressing the button again to see how bad it is can turn one corner out of the rail into a door lying across a hood. Switch the opener off at the receptacle, clear the bay, and do not lever anything back with a pry bar. Loose cable coiled at a jamb, one corner noticeably low, or a roller sitting outside the rail all mean the load has moved somewhere it was not designed to go.

### Three ways a door leaves its rail here

A cable can unspool from its drum. Each lift cable anchors at the bottom bracket and winds onto a grooved drum at the end of the torsion shaft, and when a spring lets go the door drops hard enough to jump those grooves. That side then has no lift at all and the leaf racks in the opening.

A roller can quit turning. Nylon wheels get brittle and crack, and steel ones seize once the bearings dry and dust packs in, after which the wheel slides instead of rolling and climbs out at the curve where vertical track turns horizontal.

Third, the track itself can move. A bumper touching the vertical rail at the jamb shifts it a quarter inch, which is plenty for a roller to pop out at the same height on every trip until somebody finds the bend.

### The clay under the driveway moves the opening

This corner of Dallas and Denton counties sits on the shrink-swell clay the Blackland Prairie is known for. The federal soil description for the Houston Black series records high shrink-swell potential, with cracks half an inch to four inches wide at a depth of a foot during dry spells, staying open for 90 to 150 cumulative days in most years. Ground that opens and closes on that scale carries slabs and framing with it. A garage opening that has racked even slightly holds one vertical track out of plumb, and the door then binds at the same point every cycle. Nobody calls in a panic about that one. There was no bang and no impact, just a door that has been rubbing in the same spot for two summers and a roller that is now half worn away on one side.

### Dented, or genuinely unable to work

These are two separate findings and they should be written up separately. The industry's guidance for building occupants sets out a list. Check jambs and header attachment, track fasteners and alignment, rollers sitting in the track without tension, brackets and hinges, cables for slack, and the sections from inside and out for cracks, dents and buckling. It flags the area around end-hardware fasteners in particular, because damage there can indicate structural damage rather than a mark. Its plainest line is the useful one: any deformation of panels, track or hardware can make a door questionable as to whether it can operate. The companion sheet for technicians goes a step further and says that where stress or fatigue has compromised the door's performance, the owner should be advised to forward the written assessment to their insurance company.

### What the repair actually involves

The door gets clamped below its lowest roller and blocked so it cannot travel, and stored load is released in the right order before anything is moved. Sections come back into line one at a time. Cracked rollers and torn hinges get replaced instead of persuaded back into shape, and a deformed length of rail is renewed rather than hammered flat. The pair of cables goes back onto the drums at equal tension, so both sides start and finish their travel together. The counterbalance gets checked at the same time, since a broken spring is often the reason the door came out rather than a coincidence. Then the door is cycled by hand with the operator still disconnected, because a remaining pinch in the rail will chew up the new parts inside a month.

An estimate worth signing names the skin gauge and layer count, the struts per section, the insulation value if the garage shares a wall with living space, and the track configuration and radius. It specifies new springs for the leaf being hung, because a heavier insulated door will not balance on coils wound for the one being hauled away. It lists bottom weatherseal, jamb repair where framing has been wet, disposal of the old sections, and a permit line if the opening is changing. Where it is not changing, the estimate should say so plainly, since that sentence documents why no permit was pulled. Lead time belongs there too, since a specific color and window layout is built to order.

## svc_carriage_house_garage_doors_lede

Carriage house doors buy an appearance, and they charge for it in weight, glazing rules and finish maintenance. All three are manageable if they are priced into the job at the start rather than discovered afterward.

## svc_carriage_house_garage_doors_body

### What the style actually is

Most carriage house doors sold today are ordinary sectional doors wearing overlay trim, hardware and window inserts that imitate a pair of swing-out barn doors. True swing or slide-open carriage doors exist and need side room and different operators. Some doors in the city's oldest streets are still one-piece slabs, which matters because federal rules bar unattended remote closing on one-piece and swinging doors. Deciding which of the three you are buying is the first fork in the road, and the [service index](../services/) reflects it.

### Overlays are added weight, and weight has consequences

The trade association is unambiguous that garage doors are not designed to accommodate additional weight, and that adding it unbalances the door, breaks springs early, overworks the operator, and can twist horizontal track until the door falls. Factory-applied overlays are engineered into the door's rated weight; overlays glued to an existing door are not. On a door that already weighs between ninety and five hundred pounds, a full-width overlay package is a counterbalance recalculation, which is why this style so often arrives with a [door replacement](../garage-door-replacement/) conversation attached.

### Windows, and when safety glazing is required

Glazing in a garage door falls under federal glazing rules through the building code, and the trade association explains the four conditions that trigger safety glazing in a fixed or operable panel. The pane has to exceed nine square feet, its bottom edge has to sit under eighteen inches above the floor, its top edge above thirty-six inches, and a walking surface has to lie within thirty-six inches of the glazing plane. Most decorative top-section windows do not meet all four, but a large low pane can, and specifying tempered or laminated inserts up front avoids an argument at inspection.

### Wood doors and the North Texas finish cycle

Wood carriage doors need finish maintenance, not just cleaning. The guidance is to finish all six sides of each section before installation. Finishing after install can void the warranty, and the added coating weight can change how the door operates. After that, inspect and maintain the finish roughly every twelve to eighteen months. In a climate that put fifty-five days at or above one hundred degrees at the airport in 2023 and then dropped to two degrees below zero in February 2021, a south or west elevation works through that cycle faster than the interval suggests.

### Downtown, and where appearance is regulated

Carrollton's zoning ordinance includes a [historic preservation overlay district](https://www.cityofcarrollton.com/departments/departments-g-p/planning-zoning/zoning-information/zoning-ordinance), and the City Council [approved historic district zoning for Carrollton Heights on May 6, 2003](https://www.cityofcarrollton.com/departments/departments-g-p/parks-recreation/about/general-information/museum-historic-advisory-committee), providing development regulations different from other residential districts. If a door is going on a house inside such a district, confirm what the overlay requires before ordering a style change. Paint, glazing and hardware are exactly the details a design regulation addresses. Structural hardware questions raised by heavier doors are covered under [hinge and bracket repair](../garage-door-hinge-repair/).

## svc_commercial_garage_door_repair_lede

Commercial door work in this city is a different trade from residential repair. The equipment is rolling steel, sectional doors on high-lift track and dock hardware, and some of it is life-safety equipment with a testing record attached.

## svc_commercial_garage_door_repair_body

### The buildings behind the demand

Carrollton's economic development office counts [more than forty-five million square feet](https://www.carrolltontxdevelopment.com/site-selectors/business-parks) of industrial space. It sits in business parks including Valwood Industrial Park, the Belt Line Industrial Corridor, Trinity Mills Industrial, Midway South Industrial, Frankford Trade Center, [Luna Industrial Park and International Business Park](https://www.carrolltontxdevelopment.com/site-selectors/business-parks/industrial-parks). The city zones this work under Article 18, its light industrial district, in the comprehensive zoning ordinance. Those buildings run cycle counts a house never approaches, which is why the [service overview](../services/) treats commercial hardware as its own category.

### Fire doors carry an inspection record

Rolling steel fire doors are tested at installation and, under the standard the fire code adopts, inspected and tested not less than annually. The trade association's sheet on the subject sets the average closing speed limits, no more than twenty-four inches per second and no less than six, and requires the door to be drop tested twice, with repair parts obtained from the original manufacturer. Carrollton adopted the [2024 International Fire Code](https://www.cityofcarrollton.com/departments/departments-a-f/building-inspection/codes-ordinances) under ordinance 4265, effective September 1, 2025. A missing drop test record is a compliance problem before it is a maintenance problem.

### Forklift damage, and the guards that prevent the next one

Guides and bottom bars take the hits in a working dock. The recommended defenses are concrete-filled steel pipe guard posts set in front of the guides, horizontal guards where aisles turn tight, and face-mounted guides in place of between-jamb mounting so a strike deflects rather than crushes. A bent guide that still runs is a door that will jam at the worst moment, and repeated strikes at one opening usually mean the traffic pattern, not the operator, needs the attention.

### Downtime math, not part cost

On a rolling door serving a single shipping bay, the cost of the part is rarely the number that matters. Cycle counts on a warehouse door can exceed a residential door's annual total in a week, so bearings, chain hoists, operators and bottom bar astragals are wear items on a schedule. Keeping the correct curtain slats, guide sizes and spring charge on record for each opening turns a two-day part hunt into a same-day repair.

### A service program that fits the building

A useful commercial program lists every opening with its door type, size, operator, cycle estimate and inspection dates, then sets an interval per opening rather than one interval for the site. Doors on the loading side get quarterly attention; a rarely used side door gets annual. Documented testing for anything fire-rated should live with the building's other records. Fabric on the mechanical side is shared with residential work: track and roller failures are covered on the [off-track door](../off-track-garage-door-repair/) page, and the checklist approach is on the [safety inspection](../garage-door-safety-inspection/) page.

## svc_emergency_garage_door_repair_lede

An emergency is a door that cannot be secured or a vehicle that cannot get out. Both have a temporary answer you can carry out safely from inside the garage, and both have a repair that should not wait for daylight.

## svc_emergency_garage_door_repair_body

### What counts as an after-hours call

Five failures justify an evening visit: a broken counterbalance that stranded a door halfway, a section pulled off the track, a cable wrapped around a drum, a door left standing open overnight, or an opener that quit with a car boxed in. A remote that stopped working is not one of them. The distinction is whether the opening can be closed and locked without help, and the [service list](../services/) is arranged the same way, urgent failures first.

### The red handle, and the rule about where it lives

Every automatic operator has a manual release, and the safety standard requires that handle to be red and mounted no more than six feet above the floor so an adult can reach it without a ladder. Pull it with the door fully down whenever that is possible. Releasing a raised door on a broken spring drops several hundred pounds, because nothing above the header is holding the weight any more. Once the door is disconnected, lift with the handles or gripping points the manufacturer provided rather than by hooking fingers between sections.

### Power failures, and the battery you forgot about

The February 2021 event is the local benchmark for what a cold outage does. The airport recorded a low of two degrees below zero on February 16 and stayed at or below freezing for one hundred thirty-nine straight hours. Battery backup units are consumable; the trade association puts replacement at every two to five years, and a battery that age fails at the moment it is asked for current. The same sheet is blunt that an operator will not compensate for a poorly maintained door, so a marginal door and an outage tend to arrive together.

### After a storm, look past the dents

The city reported straight-line winds of eighty to ninety-five miles an hour across all thirty-seven square miles of its area on May 28, 2024. Wind that pushes a closed door inward can bend a vertical track, spring a roller out of its stile, or tweak the top fixture while leaving the skin nearly unmarked. If a door was breached, pressure inside the garage becomes a structural problem for the roof, so a door that no longer seats squarely is not a cosmetic matter. Photograph the tracks and the hinges, not only the panels.

### Holding the opening until a truck arrives

If the door is down but will not lock, park a vehicle against it from inside and keep the operator unplugged so nobody cycles it. If the door is stuck open, do not prop it with a broom handle or a jack stand; that transfers the load to something that can slide. Springs and cables are the two failures that most often force an evening call, covered on the [spring repair](../garage-door-spring-repair/) page, and a door hanging out of its rails belongs with [off-track repair](../off-track-garage-door-repair/).

## svc_garage_door_hinge_repair_lede

Hinges and brackets are the joints that let a rigid slab bend around a curve. When one tears out of a section, the door does not stop working immediately, and that grace period is when most of the real damage happens.

## svc_garage_door_hinge_repair_body

### The parts under load, and where they fail

Each pair of sections is joined by hinges at the stiles, with roller carriers on the ends and top and bottom fixtures anchoring the cable and the top roller. Failures show as a torn hinge leaf, an elongated bolt hole, a knuckle worn oval, or a stile that has pulled away from the skin. That last one is a section problem wearing a hardware costume, and the [job list](../services/) separates the two because the repairs are not close in price.

### Builder-grade stiles in a 1990s door

[Oak Creek Estates was developed between 1991 and 1994](https://www.neighborhoods.com/oak-creek-estates-carrollton-tx) with about two hundred homes, and doors of that period commonly used light-gauge end stiles. Thirty years of cycles work the fastener holes larger, so the hinge moves before the section does and the metal around each screw tears in a rough oval. Once three or four holes have opened up on one stile, replacing the hinges alone puts new hardware into failed steel. A reinforcement plate, or a section swap, is the durable answer.

### Weight the door was never designed to carry

Doors are not designed to accommodate additional weight, and the trade association lists what happens when it is added anyway. The door goes out of balance, springs break early, the operator is overworked, horizontal track twists and buckles, and the door can fall. Decorative overlays, a second coat of paint over a decade, and retrofit insulation panels all do this quietly. A door that weighs between ninety and five hundred pounds does not need much added mass before the hinges become the weakest link.

### Ground movement that puts the frame out of square

Ground movement is measurable here. The [clay under this city](https://soilseries.sc.egov.usda.gov/OSD_Docs/H/HOUSTON_BLACK.html) shrinks and swells enough to crack half an inch to four inches wide at a foot of depth, and those cracks stay open ninety to one hundred fifty cumulative days in a normal year. A jamb that racks even slightly forces the rollers to run at an angle, and the hinge that carries that roller takes the twisting load every cycle. That is why the same opening keeps breaking the same hinge, and why a hinge repair that ignores track alignment is a temporary repair.

### Fasteners belong in something structural

A code inspection checklist for sectional doors asks whether spring pads, horizontal tracks and vertical tracks are fastened to structurally sound load-bearing material. Hardware anchored into drywall or into a split jamb will keep loosening. Repairs get through-bolts and backing where the original used a short screw into pine. Noise from a worn knuckle is diagnosed on the [noisy door](../noisy-garage-door-repair/) page, and a section that has already jumped its rails belongs with [off-track repair](../off-track-garage-door-repair/).

## svc_garage_door_insulation_lede

Two numbers get printed on garage doors and they are not comparable. R-value describes a section in isolation; U-factor describes the whole installed door, and only one of them predicts what a garage will feel like in August.

## svc_garage_door_insulation_body

### R-value and U-factor measure different objects

The trade association is precise about this. U-factor is thermal transmittance in British thermal units per hour per square foot per degree Fahrenheit, tested on the entire installed assembly under the ANSI/DASMA 105 method. R-value is thermal resistance, calculated on the section alone. The two are not reciprocals and are not interchangeable, because a whole-door figure includes the thermal shortcuts at stiles, joints and the perimeter that a section calculation ignores. Everything on the [service index](../services/) that touches a door's construction ends up bumping into that distinction.

### What the local code actually regulates

Carrollton adopted the [2024 International Energy Conservation Code](https://www.cityofcarrollton.com/departments/departments-a-f/building-inspection/codes-ordinances) with the rest of the 2024 code family under ordinance 4265, effective September 1, 2025. Denton County, which covers the northern part of the city, is a [climate zone 3A](https://up.codes/s/climate-zones) location in the code's own table, a moist warm-humid designation. The energy code governs the conditioned envelope, so an unconditioned garage is not where compliance is won or lost; a door between a garage and a living space is the assembly that carries a requirement.

### Insulation changes the weight, and the weight changes the springs

An insulated section is heavier than a hollow one. Doors range from roughly ninety pounds to over five hundred, and the trade association warns plainly that doors are not designed to accommodate additional weight, that adding it can unbalance the door, break springs, overwork the operator and twist horizontal track. Retrofitting foam panels into an existing steel door is the version of this that goes wrong most often. If the counterbalance is not re-rated afterward, the new door weight is charged to hardware sized for the old one, and [spring repair](../garage-door-spring-repair/) follows within months.

### Thermal bow is the summer side effect nobody mentions

An insulated door with a big temperature difference across it bows, and the trade association notes that the effect grows with door width, to the point that a twenty-foot-wide door can bow several inches. It also notes that darker doors facing the sun bow more than white ones, and that thermally broken construction bows more, not less. In a city with fifty-five days at or above one hundred degrees at the airport in 2023, a dark bronze double door on a west elevation is the worst case. Horizontal struts are the accepted correction.

### Choosing sensibly for a garage on these streets

Decide what the room is for. A workshop or a gym behind the door justifies a two-inch polyurethane section with a good U-factor and a struts-and-seals package. Storage behind a door that faces north justifies far less. Air sealing usually returns more comfort per dollar than another inch of foam, which is why the [weather seal](../garage-door-weather-seal-replacement/) page belongs in the same conversation as the insulation catalog.

## svc_garage_door_lock_repair_lede

Manual locks matter more on this side of the Metroplex than most homeowners assume, because they are what holds a door closed during a high-wind event when the operator has nothing to do with the outcome.

## svc_garage_door_lock_repair_body

### What is actually on the door

Older sectional doors carry a center T-handle turning a pair of spring-loaded lock bars that shoot into slots in the vertical tracks, or a side-mounted slide bolt that drops through a track hole. Newer doors often carry neither, relying on the operator's drive to hold them down. Knowing which arrangement is on your opening decides whether a repair is a lock, a cable, a strike slot or a bracket, and the [service list](../services/) separates those from a door that simply will not latch because it sits crooked.

### Lock bars and openers do not mix casually

When an operator is fitted, manual locks are usually disengaged, disabled or removed, for good reason. Running the head with the bars thrown damages both the door and the operator. If a lock is restored on an opener-equipped door, the operator has to be disconnected while the lock is engaged, and the door has to be unlocked before normal automatic operation resumes. That sequence is the whole safety case for keeping a working interlock switch or simply choosing one method and sticking to it.

### The wind argument for a working lock

Guidance for high-wind events runs in four steps. Close the door and leave it closed, engage the manual locking device where the door was built with one, disconnect the operator while it is locked, then unlock before using the opener again. The reason is structural: once a garage opening is breached, pressure builds inside and can lift the roof. Carrollton reported straight-line winds of eighty to ninety-five miles an hour across all thirty-seven square miles of its area on May 28, 2024, which is exactly the case that guidance describes.

### The 1970s handle that no longer turns

Nearly three-quarters of this city's year-round housing units went up during the 1970s, the decade its population grew one hundred ninety-three percent to forty thousand five hundred ninety-five, so original T-handle hardware is still common. Typical faults are a stretched or snapped lock cable, a bar bent by a door that was cycled while locked, a corroded escutcheon, or track slots that have deformed until the bar no longer enters. Most are repairable with the correct bar length and a new cable rather than a new door.

### Security without pretending a garage is a vault

A door held only by an opener can be defeated at the release cord through the top gap. A shield over the emergency release, a properly adjusted top seal and a deadbolt on the passage door into the house all do more than a heavier padlock. Never padlock a door that anyone might operate with a remote. Where the door will not seat far enough to latch at all, start with the [safety inspection](../garage-door-safety-inspection/), and a door that cannot be secured tonight is an [emergency call](../emergency-garage-door-repair/).

## svc_garage_door_opener_installation_lede

Installing an opener is a load calculation, an electrical decision and a mounting problem before it is a product choice. A head sized for a door that is out of balance will fail early no matter which drive it uses.

## svc_garage_door_opener_installation_body

### The door decides the opener

Weigh the problem before shopping. Doors run from roughly ninety pounds to over five hundred, and the trade association warns that added trim, overlays or repainting can push a door past what its manufacturer specified. An insulated double door with a marginal spring set will overwork any head. So the sequence is balance first, then horsepower class and drive type, and the [full job list](../services/) treats the counterbalance as the prerequisite it is rather than an upsell attached to the install.

### Chain, belt or wall mount, decided by the garage

Chain drives are the cheapest to maintain and the loudest, which matters when a bedroom sits over the garage. Belt drives are quiet and cost more. Jackshaft units mount on the wall beside the door and are the answer where headroom is taken by storage racks or a low ceiling. That is a common condition in the compact garages built here during the 1970s, the decade the city grew one hundred ninety-three percent and put up nearly three-quarters of its year-round housing units.

### The circuit, and what Carrollton charges to inspect it

Hanging a head on an existing outlet is service work. Running a new dedicated circuit is electrical work, and the city prices [single-trade electrical permits](https://www.cityofcarrollton.com/departments/departments-a-f/building-inspection/fees) at four dollars per thousand dollars of value with a seventy-five dollar minimum, with a reinspection at fifty dollars. Carrollton also requires contractors to register with the city, at no cost, renewed annually, and issues no permit while any contractor on the job is unregistered. The current adopted electrical code here is the 2023 National Electrical Code, taken up with the [2024 code family under ordinance 4265](https://www.cityofcarrollton.com/departments/departments-a-f/building-inspection/codes-ordinances) effective September 1, 2025.

### Mounting into something that will hold

A head hung from ceiling drywall or a cracked truss will eventually pull free, and the trade association is direct that hardware must be fastened to structurally sound load-bearing members. The header pad above the door and the rear hanging brackets both carry cyclic load, not static load. Pre-wiring matters too: the wall control belongs at least five feet above the standing surface, out of reach of small children, and positioned where the closing door is in plain view from the button.

### Setup is not finished until it reverses

A new head is not commissioned until four things are true. Travel limits are set to the door, force is trimmed to the minimum that runs it reliably, the photoelectric pair sits no higher than six inches off the floor, and the door reverses off a two-by-four laid flat. Battery backup, if fitted, is a consumable to replace every two to five years. Keep the paperwork; the manual states the model's own force procedure. Pairing remotes, keypads and vehicle buttons afterward is covered under [remotes and keypads](../garage-door-remote-programming/), and phone control and scheduling are on the [smart opener](../smart-garage-door-opener/) page.

## svc_garage_door_remote_programming_lede

Remotes and wall keypads fail in three distinct ways: dead batteries, a receiver that has lost its rolling code table, and radio interference nobody in the house can see. The third one is the reason a remote can work at ten feet and die at forty.

## svc_garage_door_remote_programming_body

### Range that shrinks is a radio problem, not a battery

A weak battery drops out at every distance. Interference behaves differently: the remote works at the driveway apron and does nothing from the corner of the street. The trade association troubleshooting sheet for remote controls names the usual local sources, including LED and compact fluorescent lamps in the opener itself, wireless alarm equipment sharing the band, and nearby high-power broadcast transmitters. The same sheet describes a Department of Defense land mobile radio system operating near military installations that has interfered with garage door receivers, affecting as many as forty million homes. Diagnosis starts with the [full job list](../services/) and a walk test, not a parts order.

### The 1998 dividing line in a Castle Hills garage

Receiver design changed across the 1990s, and the master-planned [Castle Hills community took its first residents in 1998](https://castlehills.com/about.php), which places much of its original equipment in the fixed-code and early rolling-code era. A fixed-code receiver reads a set of dip switches; a rolling-code receiver stores a new value after every press and will not accept a transmitter it has never learned. That distinction dictates the fix. One is a switch position copied by hand, the other is a learn sequence held for a specific count of seconds at the head.

### Keypads bake, and their membranes tell on them

An exterior keypad mounted on a south or west jamb takes direct sun through a long summer, and the weather service count of fifty-five hundred-degree days at the airport in 2023 is a fair proxy for what its membrane endured. Failure shows as two or three digits that need a hard press while the rest respond normally, or a keypad that answers in the morning and ignores a code at six in the evening. Battery corrosion inside the housing is the other common finding, and it usually means the gasket has hardened rather than the electronics have died.

### When the receiver is the part that has to change

If no transmitter of any age will learn, and the wall button still runs the door, the receiver logic has stopped storing codes. On most residential heads that board is a stocked component. Replacing it means every remote, keypad and vehicle mirror button in the household is re-learned in one visit, so bring the cars into the driveway before the appointment. A vehicle mirror system that once worked and now does not is often a bridge module problem rather than a door fault.

### Deleting a code you no longer control

A lost remote, a sold vehicle or a contractor who once had the keypad code all justify clearing the receiver memory outright. Erasing the table wipes every stored transmitter, which is the point, and the household's own devices are taught again afterward. Keep the wall control out of reach of children, and treat a remote as a key. Where the underlying complaint is a motor that grinds or a head that ignores the wall button too, start at [opener repair](../garage-door-opener-repair/); if the goal is phone control and away-from-home operation, read the [smart opener](../smart-garage-door-opener/) page first.

## svc_garage_door_safety_inspection_lede

An inspection is worth having because it produces a written list of what is worn, what is missing and what does not comply, rather than an opinion. Most of the checks come from published standards you can read yourself.

## svc_garage_door_safety_inspection_body

### The four things a 1993-or-later opener must do

Residential operators manufactured on or after January 1, 1993 carry four requirements. Reverse within two seconds of sensing an obstruction. Send the door upward after thirty seconds if the closed position has not been reached. Carry a secondary entrapment protection system. Close only from a wall switch or a remote control. Federal law regulated manufacture rather than resale, so older units remain in service on plenty of openings. Establishing which side of that line a head sits on is the first entry on the report, ahead of everything else on the [service list](../services/).

### The block test, and where the eyes belong

Testing uses a two-by-four laid flat on the floor, centered under the closing door, because laid flat it is one and a half inches high. The door must contact it and reverse. Photoelectric sensors must never sit higher than six inches off the slab, so a small child cannot crawl under the beam, and the operator has to monitor any external sensor at least once per cycle and refuse to close if that sensor is faulty. Testing at additional heights is useful but does not replace the floor-level test.

### Labels, handles and heights

The residential checklist covers items an owner can verify without tools. A manual release handle must be red and no more than six feet above the floor. A wall control belongs at least five feet above the standing surface and in sight of the door. Specific warning labels should still be present, including the spring warning, the general warning and the wall-control label. Missing labels are common on doors that have been repainted, and their absence is worth noting because it usually means other original documentation is gone too.

### Structure, not just hardware

The inspection looks at what the door is bolted to. Torsion spring pads and both track sets must be fastened to structurally sound load-bearing members, the door must open and close easily by hand with the operator released, and handles or gripping points must be present. In a garage on this city's clay, checking whether the jambs still stand square is part of the same pass, since a racked frame is what turns healthy hardware into a repeat repair.

### Detached garages and the permit counter

Structural work on a detached garage is a different matter from work on the door. Carrollton requires a [permit for an accessory building](https://www.cityofcarrollton.com/departments/departments-a-f/building-inspection/my-home/accessory-buildings-storage-sheds) over one hundred twenty square feet and caps its height at fifteen feet. Spans over twenty-four feet must be engineer-sealed, and an inspection is required when the work is complete. Findings on the eyes themselves route to [sensor repair](../garage-door-sensor-repair/), and routine adjustment and lubrication belong on the [tune-up](../garage-door-tune-up/) page.

## svc_garage_door_sensor_repair_lede

A door that stops and climbs back up is usually reporting what its two photoelectric heads saw, not failing. The heads live in the last six inches above the slab, which is the most crowded strip of concrete in the garage.

## svc_garage_door_sensor_repair_body

### The six inches that decide everything

The trade association sheet on operator electronics puts the beam no higher than six inches above the floor, within six inches of the door path, with the pair usually no more than twenty feet apart. A second sheet repeats the height rule for one blunt reason: a small child must not be able to crawl under an invisible beam. That geometry is why bracket position is a repair and not a preference, and why the whole [service list](../services/) treats a misaligned eye as a door-system problem rather than an opener defect.

### A 1971 garage has nowhere to put the recycling cart

[Country Place was founded in 1971](https://www.thecountryplace.org/) and packed 742 homes onto roughly two hundred acres, so its garages were sized for the cars and the tools of that decade. Add a rolling trash cart, a bicycle and a bag of fertilizer to a floor that narrow and something ends up parked in the beam line most weeks. Look along the slab at eye height before assuming a fault. A cart handle, a folded moving blanket or a cobweb across one lens all read to the head as an obstruction.

### Heat, sun angle and faults that keep a schedule

North Texas gives the pair a hard year. The weather service count for the airport records fifty-five days at or above one hundred degrees in 2023, and an unconditioned garage runs hotter still, which loosens the plastic bracket clips that hold alignment. Late-afternoon sun straight into a west-facing opening can saturate the receiver so the door refuses to close only between four and six o'clock. When a complaint follows the clock like that, write down the hour; it is diagnostic information rather than a nuisance detail.

### One dark indicator lamp, two possible causes

Both housings carry a status lamp, and the useful reading is which lamp is out. A dark receiver with a lit sender points at the wiring: bell wire stapled through its insulation, a splice greened by humidity, or a screw terminal backed out at the motor head. A flickering receiver with a solid sender is nearly always aim, and aim is corrected by squaring the bracket rather than bending the arm. Rodent damage behind a wall-mounted freezer is common enough in older garages to be worth a flashlight before parts are ordered.

### What a finished sensor repair looks like

The brackets get set to a measured height, the wire gets run and secured so a car door cannot catch it, and the lenses get wiped dry rather than cleaned with solvent. Then the pair is proved twice, once by breaking the beam with a broom handle mid-travel and once with the door closing normally. If the head still balks with a clear beam and healthy lamps, the fault has moved to the logic board or the limits, which is [opener repair](../garage-door-opener-repair/) territory, and a door that reaches the slab and rebounds belongs on the [will not close](../garage-door-wont-close/) page instead.

## svc_garage_door_tune_up_lede

A tune-up is a measurement visit, not an oiling visit. The useful version of it counts cycles, checks balance with the operator disconnected, and proves the reversing systems still work the way the standard requires.

## svc_garage_door_tune_up_body

### Ten checks, about fifteen minutes

The trade association publishes a residential checklist that runs ten points and takes roughly fifteen minutes with three tools: a tape measure, a flashlight and a solid one and a half inch object such as a short length of two-by-four. It covers labels, the manual release, balance, hardware condition and both entrapment protection systems. That list is the backbone of an honest maintenance call, and everything on the [service index](../services/) that stops a door is easier to catch there than at the roadside.

### Cycles are the real service interval

The counterbalance standard defines one cycle as travel from closed to fully open and back, sets a design minimum of ten thousand cycles, and describes residential use as fewer than fifteen hundred cycles a year. Two commuters, a trash cart and a dog walked twice a day pass that figure without anyone noticing. A household on the high side of it is running through a minimum-rated spring set in six or seven years, which is why the count, not the calendar, decides when the shaft gets attention.

### Balance is checked with the motor out of the picture

Release the operator with the door closed, then raise the door by hand to about waist height. It should hold roughly where it is left instead of sinking or climbing, and it should move without a shove. A door that falls is under-sprung or has lost a spring; one that rises hard is over-wound. Fixing balance first is what keeps a healthy opener from being blamed for a counterbalance problem, and it is the single most useful thing a homeowner can watch a technician do.

### Lubrication, and the Rosemeade summer that undoes it

Rosemeade's single-family streets along [Rosemeade Parkway, Copperwood Drive and Standridge Drive filled in through the 1980s](https://rosemeadecarrolltonneighborhood.org/rosemeade-unfolding-the-rich-heritage-of-carrollton-s-beloved-neighborhood/), and their attached garages hold heat all afternoon. On the days the weather service counts at or above one hundred degrees at the airport, light oil migrates off hinge pins and roller stems within weeks. A tune-up uses lithium or silicone grease on hinges, roller stems, bearings, the spring coils and the opener rail, and leaves the track face bare so it does not become a dust trap.

### The two tests that end the appointment

First, place the one and a half inch block flat on the floor under the door and close it; the door must contact the block and reverse. Second, break the photoelectric beam with the door moving down and watch it return. Both are monthly homeowner checks as much as annual service items. A visit should finish with a written note of what was measured, what was adjusted and what is near end of life. Persistent grinding or rumbling is handled on the [noisy door](../noisy-garage-door-repair/) page, and a full documented review is the [safety inspection](../garage-door-safety-inspection/).

## svc_garage_door_weather_seal_replacement_lede

Most water on a garage floor did not come under the bottom rubber. It came off the face of a closed door, sat in the joints between sections, and ran inside the moment the door articulated on its way up.

## svc_garage_door_weather_seal_replacement_body

### Four separate seals, four separate failures

A door system carries a bottom astragal in a retainer, jamb weatherstrip on both sides, a header seal at the top, and in many cases a bead between sections. Each fails on its own schedule, so the first job is finding which one leaked, and the [service list](../services/) keeps that apart from a door that no longer seats squarely for mechanical reasons. Daylight along one jamb at noon and a dry floor after light rain point at very different repairs.

### The concrete matters as much as the rubber

The trade association's sheet on floor-level water infiltration describes what the slab is supposed to do. One option is a slightly inclined lip sloped three to five degrees. The other is a rectangular notch below slab grade for the door to rest in, sized to take a two-inch track and a two-inch-thick section, with a half-inch minimum dimension. Where a Carrollton driveway was poured flat to the opening, no gasket will hold back sheet flow, and the honest answer is a threshold detail rather than a thicker seal.

### Wind-driven rain arrives sideways

A companion sheet is explicit about the path. Rain accumulating in the section interfaces of a closed door wicks to the ends of the door, bypasses the perimeter seal and enters the space, and strong wind turns that into a continuous stream. It recommends sections overlapping the jambs one inch per side where the building allows, adjustable jamb brackets, and awnings or rain ledges on west-facing openings. That last point fits a city whose worst wind events blow in from the west and northwest.

### Shrink-swell clay and a bottom edge that no longer fits

The mapped [Houston Black clay](https://soilseries.sc.egov.usda.gov/OSD_Docs/H/HOUSTON_BLACK.html) under this area cracks between half an inch and four inches wide at a foot down. Those cracks stay open ninety to one hundred fifty cumulative days in a normal year. A slab that lifts on one side leaves a wedge of light under the closed door that a new gasket only partly hides. Measuring the gap at both ends and the center before ordering is what separates a seal that lasts from one that is crushed on one side and open on the other.

### Cold, and the seal that tears itself off the slab

During the February 2021 outbreak the airport sat at or below freezing for one hundred thirty-nine consecutive hours and reached two degrees below zero on February 16. A gasket frozen to concrete tears when the opener pulls, which is how a retainer ends up bent. A pliable seal in a straight retainer is the whole defense. Where the bottom section itself is rusted through behind the retainer, the fix moves to [door and panel replacement](../garage-door-replacement/), and if the goal is a warmer garage rather than a drier one, read the [insulation](../garage-door-insulation/) page.

## svc_garage_door_wont_close_lede

A door that starts down and returns is doing what federal rules built it to do. The work is finding out what convinced it something was underneath, which is a short list run in order rather than a guess at a part.

## svc_garage_door_wont_close_body

### Two federal timers behind one symptom

Residential operators built for sale in this country on or after January 1, 1993 must reverse within two seconds of meeting an obstruction. They must also carry a thirty-second timer that sends the door back up when the closed position is never reached. Position-aware systems check their progress in increments no coarser than one inch. Any of those conditions can fire from mechanical drag rather than a real obstacle, so the diagnostic order matters more than the parts list, and the [service overview](../services/) separates the electrical causes from the mechanical ones.

### Read the lamp code before touching a screw

Most heads report the reason. A steady pair of sensor lamps with a reversing door points away from the eyes and toward drag or a limit. A blinking overhead lamp or a repeating click at the wall control usually indicates the monitored safety circuit failed its self-check, which the standard requires at least once per operating cycle. Count the flashes and note whether the door reverses immediately or after several feet, because an instant reversal and a reversal near the floor come from different places.

### Where the floor moved out from under the door

Carrollton sits on shrink-swell clay. The mapped [Houston Black soils](https://soilseries.sc.egov.usda.gov/OSD_Docs/H/HOUSTON_BLACK.html) crack between half an inch and four inches wide at a foot of depth, and stay open for ninety to one hundred fifty cumulative days in a normal year. A slab edge that lifts through a dry August raises the point the bottom seal touches, so the door meets the concrete before the limit switch expects it and reads that contact as an obstruction. Josey Ranch is a fair illustration of the ground involved; its lake began as a clay pit feeding one of the city's brick factories.

### Ice, gaskets and the winter version of the same fault

Cold turns the same symptom on for a different reason. During the February 2021 outbreak the airport held at or below freezing for one hundred thirty-nine consecutive hours after an earlier ninety-three hour stretch, and it bottomed at two degrees below zero on February 16. A bottom gasket frozen to the slab, or a stiff seal that no longer compresses, stops the door short of its limit and the timer takes over. Freeing the seal, not raising the force setting, is the repair.

### Do not turn the force up to make it obey

Increasing closing force removes the report and leaves the resistance, which shortens the life of the opener, the rollers and the sections. Once the real cause is corrected, the door should reverse cleanly on a one and a half inch object laid flat on the floor under the opening, and that test is worth repeating monthly. If the reversal traces back to the eyes themselves, go to [sensor repair](../garage-door-sensor-repair/); if the door has been fighting for months, the [safety inspection](../garage-door-safety-inspection/) page covers the full checklist.

## svc_noisy_garage_door_repair_lede

Noise is a diagnosis if you can separate the sounds. A rhythmic thump repeats once per roller, a metallic screech comes from dry bearings, and a bang that shakes the ceiling joists is usually the door slamming against a bad limit setting.

## svc_noisy_garage_door_repair_body

### Match the sound to the part before buying anything

Rumble that rises with speed and repeats evenly comes from rollers, and steel rollers with exposed bearings are the common source in 1990s builds. A dry squeal that peaks as the door reaches the curve is a bearing plate or an end bearing at the torsion shaft. A single sharp clack near the top of travel points at a hinge with a worn knuckle. Cataloging the sound saves a return trip, and the [service index](../services/) splits these from failures that stop the door altogether.

### Oak Creek Estates and the 1991 hardware generation

[Oak Creek Estates was developed between 1991 and 1994](https://www.neighborhoods.com/oak-creek-estates-carrollton-tx) across roughly two hundred homes, so its original doors carry the light-gauge hardware of that period: seven-ball steel rollers, single-thickness hinges and a bearing plate pressed rather than sealed. Thirty years of cycles wear the roller stem oval, which lets the wheel skew in the track and grind. Nylon rollers with sealed bearings cut most of that noise at the source, and they change how the door loads the track, which is why a roller swap and a track alignment check belong in the same visit.

### Heat drives the lubricant out, and grit takes its place

An attached garage here runs well above outdoor temperature on the afternoons the weather service counts as hundred-degree days, and light oil left on a hinge in June is gone by August. What remains is a dry pin in a dusty knuckle, and fine grit blown under the bottom seal turns that into a lapping compound. Lithium or silicone lubricant belongs on hinge pins, roller stems, bearings and the spring coils, never on the track face itself. Oil in the track collects dirt and makes the rumble worse within weeks.

### The bang at the end of travel is a setting

A door that lands hard, then rattles, is arriving faster than the closing limit expects, and the sound is the sections stacking against the slab. Openers hide the cause in two adjustments: travel limit and force. Winding force up to stop a door reversing masks resistance and hits the sections harder every cycle. The right sequence is to release the operator, run the door by hand, feel where the drag lives, correct that, and then reset the limits to the door rather than the reverse.

### Loose hardware, and the wall it is fastened to

Vibration walks lag screws out of their holes. The trade association is explicit that door hardware must be fastened to structurally sound load-bearing material, and hardware anchored into drywall or a cracked stud will keep loosening no matter how often it is snugged. Check the opener hanging brackets, the horizontal track hangers and the header pad at the same visit. If a tune-up is what you actually want, the [tune-up page](../garage-door-tune-up/) lists what gets measured, and torn or bent brackets are covered under [hinge and bracket repair](../garage-door-hinge-repair/).

## svc_smart_garage_door_opener_lede

Phone control changes the safety rules, not just the convenience. A door closed from an app is closing with nobody in the doorway, and federal regulation writes out exactly what the equipment has to do before it moves.

## svc_smart_garage_door_opener_body

### Unattended operation is a defined mode with defined signals

Part 1211 of title 16 of the federal regulations covers closing a residential door when no one is present to watch it. The mode requires an intentional enabling action, and the operator must give an audible and a visual alarm for at least five seconds before the door moves. The audible signal sits between seven hundred and thirty-four hundred hertz at no less than forty-five decibels measured ten feet away. The visual signal is a lamp of at least forty watts or three hundred sixty lumens flashing at least once a second. Any use of a control during that warning cancels the pending movement. Nothing else on the [service list](../services/) carries requirements that specific.

### Doors this mode is not allowed on

The same regulation bars unattended operation on one-piece doors and on swinging doors, which rules out some older single-panel garages still in service around the city's 1970s streets. Sectional doors in good condition are the intended case. Before any app is set up, the photoelectric pair has to be present, working and monitored, because remote closing removes the person who would otherwise notice a bicycle in the opening.

### Retrofit module or a new head

Two paths exist. A bridge module reads the existing head's learn button and wall control, adds a position sensor and reports over the household network, and it is the cheaper route on a healthy opener. A new connected head puts the radio and the logic in one place and typically includes camera or battery options. Compatibility on the module path depends on the age of the receiver, so the model number stamped on the motor housing decides the answer, not the year the garage was built.

### Heat, Wi-Fi and where the antenna ends up

[Castle Hills took its first residents in 1998](https://castlehills.com/about.php), and like most attached garages here its ceiling cavity is unconditioned. A connected board spends the fifty-five days a year the airport reaches one hundred degrees sitting in that heat, and intermittent dropouts that cluster in the late afternoon are a thermal symptom rather than a network fault. Signal strength is the other half: a router two floors away and a steel door between them produce a device that shows offline exactly when it is needed.

### Set it up so it fails safely

Turn on movement notifications, name the device something unambiguous, and use scheduled closing rather than blind remote closing where the household has children. Keep the audible and visual warning enabled; disabling it defeats the only substitute for a person standing at the opening. Guest access should be revoked rather than shared indefinitely, the same discipline that applies to a keypad code. If the existing head is due for replacement anyway, price it against a straight [opener installation](../garage-door-opener-installation/), and if the eyes are the sticking point, start at [sensor repair](../garage-door-sensor-repair/).

## svc_torsion_spring_conversion_lede

Converting extension springs to a torsion shaft is a geometry job, not a parts swap. It moves the counterbalance from two stretched springs beside the horizontal tracks to a wound shaft above the header, and it changes how a failure behaves.

## svc_torsion_spring_conversion_body

### Why the older layout is still out there

Carrollton's population rose one hundred ninety-three percent between 1970 and 1980, reaching forty thousand five hundred ninety-five, and almost three-quarters of its year-round housing units were built in that one decade. Extension springs were the ordinary choice on those builds. Many are still in service, often on their second or third set, which is why this conversion comes up so much more here than in a city whose stock is newer. The [service overview](../services/) lists it separately from a like-for-like spring replacement for that reason.

### Containment cable is not optional on the old layout

A stretched extension spring stores energy along its length, and when it parts the pieces travel. The code inspection checklist for sectional doors asks specifically whether an extension spring containment system has been installed per the manufacturer's documentation. If your door has bare springs with no cable threaded through them, that is a finding on its own, and on some doors installing containment is the correct and much cheaper alternative to a full conversion.

### What the shaft has to be sized against

The counterbalance standard defines a cycle as one closed-open-closed trip, sets ten thousand cycles as the design minimum for a properly selected system, and puts residential use under fifteen hundred cycles a year. It also refuses torsion springs on a sectional door whose index ratio, mean coil diameter divided by wire diameter, falls below six to one. Sizing therefore starts with the door's actual weight on a scale, not the catalog weight, then picks wire gauge, coil diameter, length and wind direction to match.

### Headroom, header and the practical limits

A torsion shaft needs clear space above the opening for the shaft, drums, cable and bearing plates, and the center pad must be structurally sound load-bearing material because it carries the full wound load. Low-headroom hardware exists and costs more. Garages from the 1970s often have a shallow header and ductwork close behind it, so the site measurement is what decides whether a standard shaft, a low-clearance kit or a rear-mount arrangement is possible.

### What changes after the conversion

Balance becomes adjustable in turns rather than in hooked positions, so the door can be tuned to hold at waist height instead of drifting. Failures become contained, and a broken coil stays on the shaft. Cycle life is generally longer for the same money, and the operator sees a steadier load. Two coils on a double door share the work, so a single break leaves partial support rather than none. Ordinary replacement of an existing torsion set is on the [spring repair](../garage-door-spring-repair/) page, and the balance check afterward is part of a [tune-up](../garage-door-tune-up/).
