## pricing_lede

Two sets of numbers touch an Oceanside injury claim: the amounts the San Diego Superior Court prints on its own fee schedule, and the fee, which California regulates without ever naming a percentage. This page keeps them apart.

## pricing_body

### California writes no rate for an injury fee, and that is the finding

Start with what the state does not do. Under rule 1.5(a) of the California Rules of Professional Conduct, a fee that is unconscionable, or one that is illegal, may not be agreed to, billed, or taken in the first place. No subdivision after it turns that ban into a percentage. So a page announcing "the California limit" on a rear-end collision claim on Coast Highway is describing a custom, not a statute.

Rule 1.5(b) explains how the prohibition is enforced. Unconscionability is measured on all the facts and circumstances existing when the agreement was entered into, except where both sides expected later events to affect the fee. The rule then lists considerations without closing the list: fraud or overreaching in setting the fee, material facts left undisclosed, and the size of the fee against the value of the work performed. It also weighs how sophisticated each side was, the amount at stake and the result, and whether the fee was fixed or contingent. A percentage that looks unremarkable in a disputed-liability case with three carriers can look very different on a claim resolved with one letter.

### Because the rate is open, the contract is the document that decides it

Business and Professions Code section 6147 is where an injured person's leverage actually sits. The contract has to be written, signed by attorney and client, and the client walks away from the signing with a duplicate signed copy in hand. The rate the parties settled on has to appear. So does the treatment of disbursements and costs, and their effect on both fee and recovery, which is the clause people skip and then argue about. So does any compensation the client might owe on related matters the agreement leaves out. And unless the claim is one of the health care cases governed by section 6146, the writing has to say the rate is negotiable rather than fixed by law. When the writing misses a required element the agreement is voidable at the client's election, and the lawyer is left with a reasonable fee rather than the one in the contract.

Read the costs clause twice: the same percentage applied before costs and after costs produces two different checks.

### The court's own figures, which do not move with the facts

San Diego County publishes its civil fee schedule and the amounts are fixed. Opening an unlimited civil case, meaning any complaint pleading more than $35,000, is $435, and each other party pays the same on its first appearance. A motion or paper requiring a hearing is $60, whether it is a discovery motion, a request to move the trial date, a motion for new trial, or an ex parte application that requires notice to the other side. Summary judgment or summary adjudication is $500. Preserving a jury takes $150 in advance and the deposit is not refundable. Reporting a civil proceeding of less than an hour is $30, while anything longer is billed as a court reporter per diem of $585 for a half day and $1,170 for a full day. A complex designation adds $1,000 charged to the plaintiff side, plus $1,000 per defendant, and the complex fees stop at $18,000 for the whole case.

An Oceanside claim that reaches litigation usually files in Vista rather than downtown, and the schedule is the same either way. The courthouse changes travel time, not the price of a filing.

### What comes off a recovery before anybody sees a check

Fees are one deduction and costs are another. The larger line items in a North County file tend to be deposition transcripts, records from Tri-City and other providers, treating-physician testimony, and any retained expert. Reimbursement claims are the third deduction, and they have their own statutory limits. Where Medi-Cal paid for treatment, Welfare and Institutions Code section 14124.78 provides that the Director shall in no event recover more than the beneficiary recovers after attorney fees and litigation costs paid by the beneficiary are deducted. That is a floor under the injured person's share, and it is worth asking about early, because a claim resolved without that arithmetic done can leave a person holding very little.

### The percentages that belong to a case this is not

California caps a contingency in exactly one setting. Section 6146 limits the fee in an action for injury against a health care provider based on professional negligence to 25 percent of the recovery when every party signs a release and settles before any complaint or demand for arbitration has been filed. Once the case passes that point the figure becomes 33 percent, measured against the net sum recovered. A lawyer who tried the case may move for more, and the court decides on evidence of good cause.

Those figures have nothing to do with a truck merging off the 78 or a fall in a Mission Avenue parking lot. When they turn up on a general injury page, that page was assembled rather than checked.

### The practical version

No California law sets the rate, so it is negotiated and the contract has to say so. Costs sit separate from the fee, and the contract has to explain how they interact.

## svc_slip_and_fall_lawyer_body

### What separates a premises claim from an ordinary fall

Falling somewhere does not create a claim by itself. The questions are narrower: who controlled the walking surface, what condition existed there, and whether the party in control knew of it or should have. Control comes out of a deed, a lease, or a management contract, and on a mixed-use block along South Coast Highway the answer is often three parties at once: the building owner, the tenant running the space, and an outside cleaning company. Knowledge is the harder half. Prior complaints, work orders, sweep sheets, and inspection logs are what move a file past "the floor was wet," and none of those papers belong to the injured person.

### Notice is the argument, and inspection intervals decide it

California allows the duration of a hazard to be proved indirectly. In Ortega v. Kmart Corp. the state Supreme Court held that a plaintiff may show a dangerous condition lasted an unreasonable time with circumstantial evidence. The court wrote that ["evidence that an inspection has not been made within a particular period of time prior to an accident may warrant an inference that the defective condition existed long enough so that a person exercising reasonable care would have discovered it"](https://law.justia.com/cases/california/supreme-court/4th/26/1200.html). The court also kept the burden on the injured person to produce evidence on duration. So the sweep log with a four-hour gap in it can matter more than the photograph of the spill.

### The coastline is a legal patchwork rather than one place

Oceanside lifeguards watch 3.7 miles of beach and counted [3,714,609 beach visits, 1,500,117 water visits and 1,419 ocean rescues during 2024](https://fire.ci.oceanside.ca.us/department-overview/divisions/lifeguards). The pier that draws much of that traffic [runs 1,954 feet](https://www.ci.oceanside.ca.us/government/public-works/beaches-pier/pier). Volume does not decide the rule. Government Code section 831.2 says [neither a public entity nor a public employee is liable for an injury caused by "a natural condition of any unimproved public property," beaches included](https://law.justia.com/codes/california/code-gov/title-1/division-3-6/part-2/chapter-2/article-1/section-831-2/). A fall on sand and a fall on a concrete stairway forty feet away are analyzed under different law. On private ground, Civil Code section 846 removes the ordinary duty toward someone who entered for [a recreational purpose, subject to exceptions for a willful or malicious failure to warn, entry paid for, and a person expressly invited](https://law.justia.com/codes/california/code-civ/division-2/part-2/title-3/chapter-2/section-846/). Which foot of ground you were standing on is a legal question, not a detail.

### When the surface belongs to a public agency, the calendar shrinks

Government Code section 911.2 requires a claim for injury to a person to be [presented not later than six months after the cause of action accrues](https://codes.findlaw.com/ca/government-code/gov-sect-911-2/). Section 915 says a claim reaches a local agency by [delivery to the clerk, secretary, or auditor, by mail to the clerk or the governing body, or electronically where an ordinance allows it](https://codes.findlaw.com/ca/government-code/gov-sect-915/). The Oceanside City Clerk's office states that it [accepts liability claims along with petitions, subpoenas, and summonses](https://www.ci.oceanside.ca.us/government/city-clerk/legislative-services-public-records-request). After a rejection, section 945.6 gives [six months from the mailing or delivery of the written notice, or two years from accrual if no such notice went out](https://codes.findlaw.com/ca/government-code/gov-sect-945-6/). Identifying the owner early is not academic here. North County Transit District publishes [fencing conditions in separate segments of the Oceanside rail corridor, Morse to Cassidy, Cassidy to Vista Way, and Vista Way to Eaton](https://gonctd.com/about-nctd/safety-security/trespasser-mitigation/eee/), so ground near the tracks may answer to a transit district rather than the city.

### The seven days that decide what can still be shown

Send a written request for video naming the date, the time window, and the camera, because a small business in South Oceanside or Fire Mountain may overwrite footage in under a week. Keep the shoes unwashed in a paper bag. Photograph the mat, the drain, the handrail, the lighting, and any cone or sign. Collect phone numbers for witnesses, not just names. Keep every appointment, since a gap in treatment gets read as recovery.

### How the other side builds its file

Expect an early call for a recorded statement and a medical release drawn broadly enough to reach back years. Expect an argument over percentage rather than responsibility: CACI 405 makes the defense prove the injured person was negligent and that the negligence was [a substantial factor in the harm, after which damages drop by the share the jury assigns](https://www.justia.com/trials-litigation/docs/caci/400/405/). Where several parties are named, Civil Code section 1431.2 makes liability for [non-economic damages several only and proportionate to each defendant's own fault](https://codes.findlaw.com/ca/civil-code/civ-sect-1431-2/), which gives an owner, a tenant, and a cleaning contractor reason to point at each other.

### What follows contact, and how California sets an injury fee

Early work is inventory: the address down to the storefront, the chain of control, the records already in hand, and which deadline governs. A suit that goes forward can land at the San Diego Superior Court's [North County Regional Center at 325 South Melrose Drive in Vista, the full-service branch serving most of northern San Diego County](https://www.sdcourt.ca.gov/sdcourt/generalinformation/courtlocations/northcountycourthouse). On the fee, California prints a prohibition instead of a number: [rule 1.5(a) of the California Rules of Professional Conduct](https://www.calbar.ca.gov/Portals/0/documents/rules/Rules-of-Professional-Conduct.pdf) allows no fee that is unconscionable or illegal, and it sets out no percentage. Rule 1.5(b) judges the fee on the facts as they stood at signing, weighing the fee against the value of the services performed and how sophisticated each side was. Business and Professions Code section 6147 handles the paperwork: a written agreement, a signed duplicate for the client, how costs bear on the fee and the recovery, and [a statement that the rate is negotiable rather than fixed by law](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?sectionNum=6147.&lawCode=BPC).

## svc_medical_malpractice_lawyer_lede

## svc_medical_malpractice_lawyer_body

### Telling a treatment injury apart from a known risk

Medicine carries risk that careful practice does not remove, and a disappointing result is not a claim. What supports one is care that departed from accepted practice and caused identifiable harm, or a procedure done without the consent the situation called for. Both are shown with records rather than impressions: the order set, the timing of each test, who read an image and when, nursing notes, the operative report, the discharge summary, and the itemized bill, which often shows what was actually done. Sequence weighs as much as content, so a crash on Mission Avenue and the hospital course after it stay two files with two deadlines.

### One year from discovery, three years at the outside

Code of Civil Procedure section 340.5 sets the period for an action against a health care provider based on professional negligence at [three years after the date of injury or one year after the plaintiff discovers, or with reasonable diligence should have discovered, the injury, whichever comes first](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?sectionNum=340.5&lawCode=CCP). The three-year wall gives way only on proof of fraud, intentional concealment, or a foreign body left in the patient with no therapeutic purpose. A minor generally has three years from the alleged wrongful act, and a child hurt before the age of six has three years or until the eighth birthday, whichever is longer. Anyone who assumes the two-year period in section 335.1 covers a hospital file can be a year late already.

### A letter has to go out before the complaint

Section 364 provides that no action based on professional negligence may begin unless the defendant received [at least 90 days' prior notice of the intention to commence the action](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?sectionNum=364&lawCode=CCP). No form is prescribed, but the notice has to give the legal basis of the claim, the type of loss, and the nature of the injuries with specificity. Subdivision (d) adds the piece practitioners rely on: notice served inside the last 90 days of the limitations period extends the filing time 90 days from service. That is a narrow mechanism, not a reason to wait, since a qualified reviewer still has to read the chart first.

### The one category of damages California caps

Civil Code section 3333.2 applies to [cases filed or arbitrations demanded on or after January 1, 2023, and holds noneconomic losses against one or more health care providers, collectively, to $350,000, with separate tiers of the same size for health care institutions and for unaffiliated defendants whose negligence was separate and independent](https://codes.findlaw.com/ca/civil-code/civ-sect-3333-2/). Those figures rise $40,000 each January 1 for ten years toward a ceiling of $750,000, the wrongful death numbers climb $50,000 a year toward $1,000,000, and the amount in effect at the time of judgment, arbitration award, or settlement is the one that applies. Economic loss, meaning wages and future care, sits outside the limit entirely.

### Camp Pendleton, and why the patient's status decides the process

An active duty Marine treated at a military facility is generally outside the ordinary system. Congressional Research Service materials describe the Supreme Court's 1950 decision in Feres v. United States. That decision held the government not liable under the Federal Tort Claims Act for injuries to active duty servicemembers ["where the injuries arise out of or are in the course of activity incident to service"](https://www.congress.gov/crs-product/LSB10305). Courts have applied that bar to military medical care. Congress opened one narrow door instead. Under 10 U.S.C. 2733a a service member, or a representative for one who died or is incapacitated, may present a malpractice claim to the Department of Defense. The provider must have acted within the scope of employment at a covered military medical treatment facility, and the claim must be [presented in writing within two years after it accrues](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?req=granuleid:USC-prelim-title10-section2733a&num=0&edition=prelim). That statute also bars payment of the claimant's attorney fees and requires review by a board-certified expert.

Dependents, retirees, and civilians run on a different track: 28 U.S.C. 2401(b) bars a tort claim against the United States unless it is [presented in writing to the right federal agency within two years, with suit begun within six months after a final denial is mailed](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/28/2401). Camp Pendleton's civil law office states that claims caused by a government employee within the scope of employment may be recoverable under the Federal Tort Claims Act or the Military Claims Act, and that [claims must reach the appropriate processing agency within two years of accruing](https://www.pendleton.marines.mil/Main-Menu/Staff-Agencies/Legal-Assistance/Civil-Law/).

### Building the record while it is still whole

Ask for the complete chart in writing so the request date is fixed, then ask separately for imaging on disc and an itemized bill. Save portal messages before an account closes. Keep a dated account of symptoms, restrictions, and conversations, with description kept apart from conclusion. Continue the care recommended, and never annotate an original document.

### After contact, and how a medical fee differs

A first review runs chronologically: dates of care, every provider involved, what the records show, whether discovery or the outer limit governs, and whether the patient's status points to a federal process. This is also the single injury category where the Legislature wrote down a percentage. Section 6146 of the Business and Professions Code caps what may be taken out of a professional negligence recovery against a health care provider. The limit is [25 percent where every party signs a release before a complaint or arbitration demand is filed, and 33 percent once the case passes that point, measured against the net sum recovered](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?sectionNum=6146.&lawCode=BPC). Rule 1.5(a) still bars an [unconscionable or illegal fee](https://www.calbar.ca.gov/Portals/0/documents/rules/Rules-of-Professional-Conduct.pdf), and section 6147 still governs the writing.

## svc_product_liability_lawyer_lede

California invented the modern product rule, and it is friendlier than most people expect. The catch is proof: the case is generally decided by whether the object still exists, in the condition it failed in, with its numbers readable.

## svc_product_liability_lawyer_body

### Where the California rule came from, and what it asks

The state's own courts wrote this area of law. Greenman v. Yuba Power Products held: ["a manufacturer is strictly liable in tort when an article he places on the market, knowing that it is to be used without inspection for defects, proves to have a defect that causes injury to a human being"](https://scocal.stanford.edu/opinion/greenman-v-yuba-power-products-inc-27186/). The California Supreme Court placed the cost of such injuries on the companies that market the goods. Design is tested two ways. Barker v. Lull Engineering Co. held a design defective either where [the product failed to perform as safely as an ordinary consumer would expect in a foreseeable use, or where the defendant fails to prove the benefits of the design outweigh its inherent risk of danger](https://scocal.stanford.edu/opinion/barker-v-lull-engineering-co-30437/). Manufacturing and warning theories are separate again. Choosing among them is later work; recording facts is now.

### The object is the case

Set the item aside, stop using it, and keep every fragment, including pieces that look like trash. Photograph the data plate, serial and lot numbers, date codes, and every warning label while the printing survives. Keep the carton, the manual, the warranty card, and the receipt or order confirmation. Turn down a refund, a warranty exchange, or a repair shipment, and do not hand the item to an adjuster, because each route ends with the evidence in another building. If a landlord, an employer, or an insurer already holds it, put a written preservation demand in the file. Screenshot the listing page with its safety language and the date visible, since product pages change without notice.

### The deadline runs from the injury, and the parties multiply

Code of Civil Procedure section 335.1 gives [two years for an action for injury to an individual caused by the wrongful act or neglect of another](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?sectionNum=335.1&lawCode=CCP). Two years sounds comfortable until the inventory of parties starts: the maker, an importer, a distributor, the retailer, an installer, and anyone who serviced or modified the unit. Goods reach Oceanside through long supply chains, and tracing a foreign maker from a model number takes time. The identifying numbers, the purchase date, and the name on the invoice are worth more in week one than any theory about what went wrong.

### The product mix a coastal city produces

Failures here cluster around water and wheels. Oceanside Harbor rents [slips at 26, 34, 43, and 51 feet with side and end ties up to 85 feet](https://www.ci.oceanside.ca.us/government/public-works/harbor/boating-services), so marine hardware, hoists, fuel systems, and trailer parts are household equipment in this city, and city lifeguards logged [71 boat rescues in 2024](https://fire.ci.oceanside.ca.us/department-overview/divisions/lifeguards). Two wheels are the other pattern. The California Office of Traffic Safety ranked Oceanside [first of 62 similar cities for motorcycle victims and seventh for bicyclist victims in its 2023 data](https://www.ots.ca.gov/rankings/oceanside-2023/), so battery, brake, and frame failures on bikes and scooters turn up more often than the population suggests. A part that sat in marine air near the harbor should be photographed before anyone cleans it.

### Government equipment sends the claim somewhere else

When the item that failed belonged to a federal installation, or a federal employee was using it on duty, the route changes. Camp Pendleton's civil law office states that claims for property damage, personal injury, or wrongful death caused by a government employee acting within the scope of employment may be recoverable under the Federal Tort Claims Act or the Military Claims Act. Packages go to the Navy or Marine Corps claims office. The office also states that [every claim must be delivered to the appropriate processing agency within two years of accruing](https://www.pendleton.marines.mil/Main-Menu/Staff-Agencies/Legal-Assistance/Civil-Law/). Federal law is stricter on paperwork: 28 U.S.C. 2401(b) bars a claim [unless it is presented in writing to the right agency within two years, and suit begins within six months after a final denial is mailed](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/28/2401). Noting the equipment owner and the employer on day one keeps that door open.

### How the manufacturer's side works the file

The first move is usually a request to inspect the item, or to take it away for testing. Expect a file built around alteration, a removed guard, skipped maintenance, use outside the manual, and the claim that the risk was obvious. Expect the percentage fight too. CACI 405 requires the defense to prove the injured person was negligent and that the negligence was [a substantial factor in the harm, with damages then reduced by the percentage assigned](https://www.justia.com/trials-litigation/docs/caci/400/405/). Civil Code section 1431.2 splits [non-economic damages among defendants severally, in direct proportion to each one's share of fault](https://codes.findlaw.com/ca/civil-code/civ-sect-1431-2/), which is why a retailer and a manufacturer often blame one another rather than the design.

### After contact, and how the fee is set here

The first pass is documentary: what the product is, when and where it was bought, who holds it now, and which entities belong in the case. This work leans on experts, so costs matter as much as the fee. [Rule 1.5(a)](https://www.calbar.ca.gov/Portals/0/documents/rules/Rules-of-Professional-Conduct.pdf) rules out a fee that is unconscionable or illegal, and names no figure, while rule 1.5(b) tests one against factors including the novelty and difficulty of the questions and the skill required, which is where a defect case parts company with a fender file. Section 6147 of the Business and Professions Code requires the agreement in writing, a signed duplicate for the client, an explanation of how costs affect the fee and the recovery, and [a statement that the rate is negotiable and not fixed by law](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?sectionNum=6147.&lawCode=BPC). The 25 and 33 percent figures people repeat come from section 6146 and belong to health care claims.

A catastrophic file is not a serious file with bigger numbers. It is an arithmetic problem about decades of care, coverage layers that run out in the first week, and repayment claims that arrive before any money does.

## svc_catastrophic_injury_lawyer_body

### What makes a file catastrophic rather than serious

California writes no definition of catastrophic injury into its general damages law, so the label describes a practical situation: a spinal cord injury, an amputation, a severe burn, a brain injury, or harm that permanently changes what a person can earn and who has to help them. Everything then shifts from the past to the future. Wages already lost are arithmetic; a working life that will now be shorter is a projection built from medical opinion, employment records, and equipment cycles. That projection has to live in documents, because nobody rebuilds it convincingly two years later.

### The document set is a life, not a bill

The first invoice from an emergency department says almost nothing about a permanent injury. What describes one is duller: written restrictions and every revision to them, therapy discharge summaries, prescription changes, and durable equipment and its replacement cycle. Add quotes for widening a doorway in a Fire Mountain or Loma Alta house, transportation once driving stops, and the hours a husband, a wife, or a grown child now spend on help that used to take nobody any time. Payroll records, tax returns, and a description of the old job carry the earnings side. A dated calendar kept at home is often the most persuasive paper in the file, precisely because it was not written for a lawsuit.

### Coverage runs out before the treatment does

California's mandatory minimum liability limits are, per the Department of Motor Vehicles, [$30,000 for injury or death to one person, $60,000 where more than one person is hurt, and $15,000 for property damage](https://www.dmv.ca.gov/portal/vehicle-registration/insurance-requirements/). An intensive care admission passes that figure in days, so every additional layer is worth finding early: the at-fault driver's excess coverage, an employer's policy where someone was working, a commercial policy behind a vehicle, and the injured person's own uninsured and underinsured coverage. The Office of Traffic Safety's 2023 rankings put Oceanside [first out of 62 comparable California cities for alcohol-involved victims and for motorcycle victims, second on the composite measure, and eighth for speed-related collisions](https://www.ots.ca.gov/rankings/oceanside-2023/). Those crashes happen on corridors built for volume: SANDAG calls State Route 76 the [east-west highway running from Interstate 5 through Oceanside toward Interstate 15](https://www.sandag.org/projects-and-programs/roads-and-highways/road-and-highway-projects/sr-76-corridor-improvements) and reports 13 miles of new carpool lanes each direction on Interstate 5 [up to State Route 78 in Oceanside](https://www.sandag.org/projects-and-programs/roads-and-highways/road-and-highway-projects/north-coast-corridor).

### Two rules that quietly move the final number

Civil Code section 1431.2 makes each defendant's liability for [non-economic damages several only and proportionate to that defendant's own percentage of fault, while defining economic damages as objectively verifiable losses such as medical expenses, lost earnings, and the cost of substitute domestic services](https://codes.findlaw.com/ca/civil-code/civ-sect-1431-2/). With three defendants and a large pain and suffering component, that division decides how much of an award is collectible. The second rule surprises people. Civil Code section 3333.4 bars recovery of noneconomic losses in a motor vehicle action where the injured person was [the uninsured owner of a vehicle involved, an operator who cannot establish financial responsibility, or a driver convicted of driving under the influence](https://codes.findlaw.com/ca/civil-code/civ-sect-3333-4/). A carve-out applies where the other driver was the convicted drunk driver. Economic loss survives that bar; the intangible half does not.

### If the injured person is active duty

Camp Pendleton reports a [daytime population of 70,000 military and civilian personnel and more than 42,000 active duty members across its commands](https://www.pendleton.marines.mil/About/), so a serious injury in this market may involve someone whose legal position is nothing like a civilian's. Congressional Research Service materials describe Feres v. United States as holding that the government is not liable under the Federal Tort Claims Act for injuries to active duty servicemembers ["where the injuries arise out of or are in the course of activity incident to service"](https://www.congress.gov/crs-product/LSB10305). For a claim the doctrine does not reach, the base's civil law office sends Federal Tort Claims Act and Military Claims Act packages to the appropriate Navy or Marine Corps claims office and states that [claims must be delivered within two years of accruing](https://www.pendleton.marines.mil/Main-Menu/Staff-Agencies/Legal-Assistance/Civil-Law/). The same two-year presentment rule appears in [28 U.S.C. 2401(b), with six months to sue after a denial is mailed](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/28/2401). Duty status, location, and employer belong at the front of the file.

### Repayment claims arrive before the money

Health plans, Medi-Cal, and Medicare all reach into an injury recovery, and in a permanent injury case those claims can rival the coverage available. California puts one floor under the injured person: Welfare and Institutions Code section 14124.78 provides that the Director shall [in no event recover more than the beneficiary recovers after deducting attorney fees and litigation costs paid by the beneficiary](https://codes.findlaw.com/ca/welfare-and-institutions-code/wic-sect-14124-78/). Running that arithmetic late is how a large settlement turns into a small check, and it is why future care gets planned before anything is signed.

### After contact, and how the fee and the courthouse work

Review of a permanent injury file moves slowly on purpose: the incident facts, the prognosis, every layer of coverage, every entity claiming repayment, and whether a federal process applies. Litigation would ordinarily sit at the San Diego Superior Court's [North County Regional Center in Vista](https://www.sdcourt.ca.gov/sdcourt/generalinformation/courtlocations/northcountycourthouse). California sets no percentage for this work. [Rule 1.5(a)](https://www.calbar.ca.gov/Portals/0/documents/rules/Rules-of-Professional-Conduct.pdf) rejects a fee that is unconscionable or illegal, while rule 1.5(b) weighs the amount involved and the result obtained among its factors, and section 6147 requires the writing, the signed duplicate, the treatment of costs, and [a statement that the rate is negotiable](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?sectionNum=6147.&lawCode=BPC). The ceiling in Civil Code section 3333.2 is written for professional negligence against health care providers, so it does not reach a highway, premises, or product file.
