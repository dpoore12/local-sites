## svc_no_start_diagnosis_body

A car that will not start is the call that made mobile mechanics a business in Savannah, because there is no way to get a dead car to a shop without a tow, and the fix is usually a forty-minute job in the driveway. The trick is figuring out which of three parts failed before buying any of them.

### What the noise tells you

Turn the key and listen. One loud click and nothing else: the starter solenoid is getting some power but the motor is not turning, which is either a battery too weak to spin it or a starter that has worn out. Rapid clicking: the battery has enough to pull the solenoid in and out but not enough to crank. A slow, dragging crank: a battery on its way out, or a starter drawing too much current. Silence with the dash lit up: the starter circuit, a bad ignition switch, or on many newer cars a security system that did not recognize the key. Dash lights that flare and die: a battery with nothing left, or a terminal so corroded it cannot carry the load.

### Why Savannah eats batteries

Heat, not cold, is what ends a battery's life, and Savannah is under-hood-hot from May to October. AAA's figure is that batteries in the southern United States last roughly seventeen months less than in the cool northern states, because heat accelerates the internal corrosion and evaporates the electrolyte. A three-year-old battery here is a candidate. The salt air adds visible corrosion at the terminals, the white and green crust that can stop a healthy battery from delivering current. The first cool morning of autumn is when the weak ones show up, because a battery that scraped by all summer cannot manage the extra cranking current a cold engine wants.

### The test, in the driveway

The mechanic puts a load tester on the battery first, because it is the least expensive part and the most likely. If it holds under load, the starter draw is measured and the ground strap and terminals are checked, because a corroded ground on a Savannah car mimics a bad starter perfectly. If the car starts on a jump and then dies, or the battery light was on before it quit, the alternator output is measured with the engine running. A battery is a driveway swap in most cars; some newer ones need the new battery registered to the car's computer, which the mechanic's scan tool does. An alternator is similar, with the added step of checking the belt and tensioner, because a slipping belt shows up as a charging fault. Squealing on light braking is the wear indicator, a small metal tab that touches the rotor when the pad is nearly gone; it is a warning, not damage. ### Heat, salt and Savannah brakes

Two local factors shorten brake life here. A steady light means a fault has been logged and the car is driveable. Evaporative-emissions faults are the most common code in a humid coastal city, and the cause is frequently a loose or worn gas cap, a cracked hose, or a purge valve, none of which is the expensive part.
