### Reading the table

The ranges above come from price pages published by companies that deliver in Biloxi and along the Coast. Compare the whole bundle, not the headline number. A cheaper container with a small allowance can cost more on a roofing job than a pricier one that includes three tons.

### Weight is where heavy jobs go over

Roofing and concrete are the jobs that blow through an allowance. Asphalt shingles are dense, and a tear-off that also removes rotten decking adds wood weight on top. Concrete and brick are denser still, which is why some local price lists include a small container meant for concrete alone. Published overage rates on the Coast run from about fifty dollars a ton upward. Ask for that figure before the container lands, not after it has been weighed.

### Clean masonry has simpler places to go

Mississippi's rubbish rules separate Class I sites, which take mixed construction and demolition debris, furniture and vegetation, from Class II sites, which take only vegetation and inert material like concrete, brick, stone and asphalt. A container of nothing but broken slab qualifies for either. The moment wood, sheetrock or trash goes in, it becomes a Class I load. The last gate rate the city published for the Class I rubbish landfill on Hudson-Krohn Road was $5.25 a cubic yard with a $10 minimum, in a 2016 notice, so read it as history rather than a current figure.

### The flood-zone multiplier

The most expensive dumpster decision in Biloxi is often made at the Building Division rather than with a hauler. Under the city's floodplain rules, improvements in a flood zone that reach half the structure's market value require the whole building to meet new-construction standards, including elevation a foot above base flood. A remodel that crosses that line produces several times the debris it started with, and the container size, the rental days and the number of hauls all change with it.

### Roofs and the fortified grant

Since July 1, 2026, the Mississippi Insurance Department has offered grants of up to $10,000 toward FORTIFIED roofs for wind-pool policyholders who have been insured with the Mississippi Windstorm Underwriting Association for three consecutive policy years, with a statewide rollout expected in early 2027. A fortified roof means a full tear-off, and a full tear-off means a dumpster. The grant goes toward the roof, and the container is one line inside that roofing job, which makes the tons-included figure worth asking about.

A per-dump price suits a job that fills the container fast, like a roof torn off in a day or a slab broken out over a weekend. Roofers count in squares, a hundred square feet of roof each, and that is the right unit for the container too. Concrete is the one material where a bigger dumpster is usually the wrong dumpster. ### What fits in a concrete container

Broken slab, sidewalk, driveway, patio and footings. Brick, block, stone, mortar, pavers and old concrete steps. Asphalt from a driveway. Clean dirt and sand, if the hauler accepts them. Pieces with rebar sticking out should be cut flush so they lie flat. What does not belong: wood forms, landscape timbers, roots and trash, because the moment any of those go in, the load is no longer clean.

### Why clean matters in Mississippi

State rules divide rubbish sites into two classes. A Class II site may accept only natural vegetation, such as limbs, stumps and leaves, plus brick, mortar, concrete, stone and asphalt. A Class I site takes all of that plus construction and demolition debris, furniture, cardboard and other mixed rubbish. Harrison County has Class II rubbish sites, including one on Road 510 in north Biloxi, alongside its Class I sites. A container of nothing but masonry can go to either kind. Toss in a pallet, a bag of trash or some sheetrock and only a Class I site can take it, which narrows the options and can change the cost.

### Biloxi's slabs have a history

When Katrina's surge, about thirty feet at its peak, came over the peninsula in 2005, it left behind more unburnable debris than anything else. At the six-month mark the city's tally counted 1,625,112 cubic yards of unburnable debris out of 2,078,194 hauled from city streets and rights-of-way, against 422,696 cubic yards that could be burned. Breaking out an abandoned slab, a cracked driveway or an old patio to make way for an elevated house or a parking pad is still common work here, and that debris is almost entirely masonry.

### Loading heavy debris

Break pieces small enough to lie flat and spread them evenly across the floor of the container rather than piling them at one end. Never fill a concrete container to the top; the weight limit arrives long before the volume does. Stop at the fill line the driver marks or describes at delivery. Lay plywood under the container's rails on a driveway you want to keep, because a container full of slab concentrates a great deal of weight on two narrow steel rails.

### Placement and access

A concrete job usually means a small container placed close to the work, because every piece has to be carried or wheeled to it. Driveways are best. In East Biloxi and on Point Cadet, where lots are tight, a smaller container also leaves room for the truck to maneuver. Keep the container and any loose broken concrete out of the street-side ditch; washed-in sand and fines clog it, and the city's ordinance against dumping into ditches and streams applies.

### Dirt and fill

Soil from a pool dig, a regrade or a new foundation is as heavy as concrete and harder to estimate, because wet soil weighs much more than dry. Ask the hauler whether clean dirt is accepted at all, since not every container or site takes it. In a Biloxi flood zone, regrading or filling also needs a building permit from the city before the first load comes out.

Concrete containers are usually priced as a flat haul with a weight cap, or per dump. The questions are the same as on any other job, size, days, tons included and overage, but on concrete the tons included is nearly the whole conversation. Move refrigerators and window units out separately once the refrigerant has been recovered.

### Raised houses and stairs

Plenty of Biloxi homes are raised, and on those every piece of furniture comes down a flight of exterior stairs. Put the container as close to the foot of the stairs as the truck allows, on the driveway or a firm pad, and keep a clear path to it. Storage areas under elevated homes collect years of tools, holiday decorations and odds and ends, so check them before ordering; they are easy to forget when estimating.

### Timing against a deadline

Book the container to arrive when the sorting is finished, not when it begins. Set aside whatever goes to family, donation or consignment in a room that stays closed until the container leaves. On a military move, schedule the container to go before the movers arrive so the driveway is clear for their truck. On an estate, check with whoever holds authority over the property before anything is thrown away.

### Rentals and turnovers

Landlords and property managers often need a container more than once a year, and the pattern repeats: a tenant leaves furniture, carpet and trash, and the unit has to be ready for the next one within days. A short rental window with a fixed pickup date suits that work better than a long one. Carpet and pad are heavier than they look once they have absorbed a few years of humidity, so mention them when the container is sized.
