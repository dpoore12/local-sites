### Reading the table

The ranges above come from price pages published by companies that deliver in Biloxi and along the Coast. They overlap rather than line up, because one company's twenty-yard price includes two and a half tons and two weeks while another's includes more weight and fewer days. Compare the whole bundle, not the headline number. A cheaper container with a small allowance can cost more on a roofing job than a pricier one that includes three tons.

### Weight is where heavy jobs go over

Roofing and concrete are the jobs that blow through an allowance. Asphalt shingles are dense, and a tear-off that also removes rotten decking adds wood weight on top. Concrete and brick are denser still, which is why some local price lists include a small container meant for concrete alone. Published overage rates on the Coast run from about fifty dollars a ton upward. Ask for that figure before the container lands, not after it has been weighed.

### Clean masonry has simpler places to go

Mississippi's rubbish rules separate Class I sites, which take mixed construction and demolition debris, furniture and vegetation, from Class II sites, which take only vegetation and inert material like concrete, brick, stone and asphalt. A container of nothing but broken slab qualifies for either. The moment wood, sheetrock or trash goes in, it becomes a Class I load. The last gate rate the city published for the Class I rubbish landfill on Hudson-Krohn Road was $5.25 a cubic yard with a $10 minimum, in a 2016 notice, so read it as history rather than a current figure.

### The flood-zone multiplier

The most expensive dumpster decision in Biloxi is often made at the Building Division rather than with a hauler. Under the city's floodplain rules, improvements in a flood zone that reach half the structure's market value require the whole building to meet new-construction standards, including elevation a foot above base flood. A remodel that crosses that line produces several times the debris it started with, and the container size, the rental days and the number of hauls all change with it.

### Roofs and the fortified grant

Since July 1, 2026, the Mississippi Insurance Department has offered grants of up to $10,000 toward FORTIFIED roofs for wind-pool policyholders who have been insured with the Mississippi Windstorm Underwriting Association for three consecutive policy years, with a statewide rollout expected in early 2027. A fortified roof means a full tear-off, and a full tear-off means a dumpster. The grant goes toward the roof, and the container is one line inside that roofing job, which makes the tons-included figure worth asking about.

A per-dump price suits a job that fills the container fast, like a roof torn off in a day or a slab broken out over a weekend. A roof tear-off is the heaviest thing most Biloxi homeowners will ever put in a dumpster. ### Why the Coast tears off so many roofs

Biloxi sits in the path of the storms that matter on this coast. The city lists Camille, Frederic, Elena, Georges, Katrina, Nate and Zeta among the major flood-producing storms since 1969, and hurricane season runs from June 1 through November 30, with August and September the riskiest months. Wind damage, insurance claims and plain aging keep roofing crews busy, and every one of those jobs needs somewhere to put the old roof.

### The FORTIFIED roof grant

In 2026 the state added a new reason. Under Senate Bill 2409, the Mississippi Insurance Department began running the Strengthen Mississippi Homes grant program on July 1, 2026, for homeowners insured with the Mississippi Windstorm Underwriting Association for three consecutive policy years, with up to $10,000 per home toward a roof built to the FORTIFIED Roof standard. ### Sizing a roofing container

Roofers count in squares, a hundred square feet of roof each, and that is the right unit for the container too. Give the square count, the number of layers coming off, and whether decking is being replaced. A small or mid-size container with a generous weight allowance is often a better fit than the biggest can available, because a large container filled with shingles can end up too heavy to lift legally. On a big roof, two planned hauls cost less than one overloaded container and an argument about overage.

### Placement for throwing

Roofers want the container as close to the eave as possible, so debris goes straight from the roof into the can instead of onto the lawn and into the flower beds. That usually means the driveway, angled toward the side of the house with the most roof. On a raised house the drop from the eave is longer, and a tarp along the wall catches what misses. ### What goes where

Shingles, felt, synthetic underlayment, flashing, drip edge and decking all belong in a Class I rubbish load under Mississippi rules. Keep out what rubbish sites cannot take: tubes of sealant and cans of roof cement still holding product, paint and chemical containers, and anything liquid. Metal roofing panels are fine in the container, though some crews pull them out for scrap. A roof replaced on a schedule tied to an insurance adjuster or a grant inspection needs the container in place the day tear-off starts and gone before the final photos.

### Raised houses and steep pitches

Plenty of Biloxi homes sit high on pilings or piers, and some older ones have steep pitches. Both mean longer drops and more debris landing short of the container. Ask the roofer how they plan to get material into the can, whether by chute, by throwing from a lower section, or by carrying it down, and place the container to suit their method rather than the other way around.

## svc_concrete_dumpster_rental_body

Concrete is the one material where a bigger dumpster is usually the wrong dumpster. A single cubic yard of broken concrete can weigh more than a ton, and a roll-off truck has legal limits on what it can lift and carry. That is why some Coast companies publish a separate small container meant only for concrete and masonry, and why a concrete job is sized by weight and thickness first and by volume second.

### What fits in a concrete container

Broken slab, sidewalk, driveway, patio and footings. Brick, block, stone, mortar, pavers and old concrete steps. Asphalt from a driveway. Clean dirt and sand, if the hauler accepts them. Pieces with rebar sticking out should be cut flush so they lie flat. What does not belong: wood forms, landscape timbers, roots and trash, because the moment any of those go in, the load is no longer clean.

### Why clean matters in Mississippi

State rules divide rubbish sites into two classes. A Class II site may accept only natural vegetation, such as limbs, stumps and leaves, plus brick, mortar, concrete, stone and asphalt. A Class I site takes all of that plus construction and demolition debris, furniture, cardboard and other mixed rubbish. Harrison County has Class II rubbish sites, including one on Road 510 in north Biloxi, alongside its Class I sites. A container of nothing but masonry can go to either kind. Toss in a pallet, a bag of trash or some sheetrock and only a Class I site can take it, which narrows the options and can change the cost.

### Biloxi's slabs have a history

When Katrina's surge, about thirty feet at its peak, came over the peninsula in 2005, it left behind more unburnable debris than anything else. At the six-month mark the city's tally counted 1,625,112 cubic yards of unburnable debris out of 2,078,194 hauled from city streets and rights-of-way, against 422,696 cubic yards that could be burned. Breaking out an abandoned slab, a cracked driveway or an old patio to make way for an elevated house or a parking pad is still common work here, and that debris is almost entirely masonry.

### Loading heavy debris

Break pieces small enough to lie flat and spread them evenly across the floor of the container rather than piling them at one end. Never fill a concrete container to the top; the weight limit arrives long before the volume does. Stop at the fill line the driver marks or describes at delivery. Lay plywood under the container's rails on a driveway you want to keep, because a container full of slab concentrates a great deal of weight on two narrow steel rails.

### Placement and access

A concrete job usually means a small container placed close to the work, because every piece has to be carried or wheeled to it. Driveways are best. In East Biloxi and on Point Cadet, where lots are tight, a smaller container also leaves room for the truck to maneuver. Keep the container and any loose broken concrete out of the street-side ditch; washed-in sand and fines clog it, and the city's ordinance against dumping into ditches and streams applies.

### Dirt and fill

Soil from a pool dig, a regrade or a new foundation is as heavy as concrete and harder to estimate, because wet soil weighs much more than dry. Ask the hauler whether clean dirt is accepted at all, since not every container or site takes it. In a Biloxi flood zone, regrading or filling also needs a building permit from the city before the first load comes out.

### What it costs

Concrete containers are usually priced as a flat haul with a weight cap, or per dump. The questions are the same as on any other job, size, days, tons included and overage, but on concrete the tons included is nearly the whole conversation. Start with the biggest rooms and work down. Bedroom furniture for one room, or one packed garage bay, fits the smallest containers comfortably. A living room, two bedrooms and a garage push into the middle sizes. A full house with an attic and a shed to empty usually points to a large container, and a property packed floor to ceiling may need two hauls scheduled from the start. A walk-through description, room by room, gets the right answer faster than any guess.

### What stays out

Mississippi's rubbish sites cannot take household garbage and food, liquids, paint and chemical containers, batteries, whole tires, engines, refrigerators or air conditioners. Bag the kitchen trash and put it in the city cart. Hold paint, cleaners, oil, batteries and electronics for the household hazardous waste collection, which runs by appointment. Move refrigerators and window units out separately once the refrigerant has been recovered.

### Raised houses and stairs

Plenty of Biloxi homes are raised, and on those every piece of furniture comes down a flight of exterior stairs. Put the container as close to the foot of the stairs as the truck allows, on the driveway or a firm pad, and keep a clear path to it. Storage areas under elevated homes collect years of tools, holiday decorations and odds and ends, so check them before ordering; they are easy to forget when estimating.

### Timing against a deadline

Book the container to arrive when the sorting is finished, not when it begins. Set aside whatever goes to family, donation or consignment in a room that stays closed until the container leaves. On a military move, schedule the container to go before the movers arrive so the driveway is clear for their truck. On an estate, check with whoever holds authority over the property before anything is thrown away.

### Rentals and turnovers

Landlords and property managers often need a container more than once a year, and the pattern repeats: a tenant leaves furniture, carpet and trash, and the unit has to be ready for the next one within days. A short rental window with a fixed pickup date suits that work better than a long one. Carpet and pad are heavier than they look once they have absorbed a few years of humidity, so mention them when the container is sized.

## q_how_much_weight_can_a_dumpster_hold_lede

A dumpster can hold as much weight as the rental agreement includes, which on the Mississippi Coast runs from about one and a half tons on a 10-yard box to somewhere between two and five tons on the larger ones. The yardage tells you how much space you have; the included tons tell you how much of that space a heavy load can actually use.

## q_how_much_weight_can_a_dumpster_hold_body

### Two limits, and the one that bites first

Every roll-off carries two caps. One is volume, the cubic yards printed on the side of the box. The other is weight, the tons the rental includes before an overage applies, and beyond that the load the truck is built to lift safely. For light debris, such as furniture, carpet, cardboard and cabinets, the box fills to the rim long before weight becomes a concern. For dense debris the order flips, and the weight limit arrives while most of the box is still empty.

Posted allowances from haulers serving Biloxi, Gulfport and Ocean Springs show how much this varies. One Gulfport list includes about a ton and a half on its 10-yard box. An Ocean Springs list includes three tons on a 15-yard. Across the 20-yard lists the included weight runs from about two and a half tons to five, and on 30-yard boxes from two tons to three and a half. A bigger box does not always mean a bigger allowance, so compare the tons, not just the yards.

### Which Biloxi jobs run heavy

Three kinds of work on this coast hit the weight cap more often than anything else.

**Concrete, brick and slab removal.** Breaking up a driveway, a patio or a slab foundation produces the densest load a homeowner will ever fill. One Harrison County hauler sells a 7.5-yard box, 6 feet wide and only 3 feet tall, specifically for concrete and fill material. The low walls are the point: a box that small cannot be overloaded by volume alone. More on these jobs is on the [concrete and heavy debris dumpsters](../concrete-dumpster-rental/) page.

**Roof tear-offs.** Shingles are heavy for their bulk, and a wave of them is coming. From July 1, 2026 the Mississippi Insurance Department is funding roofs built to the FORTIFIED standard for eligible wind-pool policyholders, with grants up to $10,000, and every one of those roofs starts with stripping the old one. See [roofing dumpster rental](../roofing-dumpster-rental/) for sizing a tear-off.

**Flood-zone elevation work.** Nearly half the land in Biloxi sits in a special flood hazard area. When repairs or improvements cost half or more of a building's market value, the city requires the whole structure to meet new-construction rules, including elevation. Those jobs often mean plaster, tile, masonry and slab coming out together, which is heavy work by any measure.

### Katrina showed how much of storm debris is heavy

The city's own Katrina records make the point. Six months after the storm, 1,625,112 of the 2,078,194 cubic yards hauled from Biloxi streets and rights-of-way were unburnable debris rather than vegetation. Wrecked houses are mostly masonry, drywall and fixtures, which weigh far more per yard than limbs and leaves. A storm cleanout here should be planned around weight from the start, not volume.

### Clean loads weigh the same but cost less to place

Mississippi rubbish rules split disposal sites into two classes. A Class II site accepts only natural vegetation and inert material such as brick, mortar, concrete, stone and asphalt. A Class I site takes mixed construction and demolition debris. Biloxi has a Class II site on Road 510 and a Class I rubbish landfill on Hudson-Krohn Road. A box of nothing but broken concrete qualifies for the simpler site; the same box with a bag of drywall scraps tossed on top does not. Keeping heavy loads clean does not make them lighter, but it keeps more disposal options open.

### Loading so the truck can lift it

- Spread heavy material evenly across the floor instead of piling it at one end.
- Fill dense debris only to the line the hauler marks, even if the box looks half empty.
- Never load above the rim; the full box still has to travel on public roads.
- Keep the box off the ditch line, since dumping into ditches and streams violates city ordinance.

### What an overweight load means for the bill

Weight past the included allowance is billed per ton from the scale ticket after the box is dumped. ### Start with the project, not the box

Biloxi handles construction permits through the Community Development Department's Building Division at 676 Dr. Martin Luther King Jr. Boulevard, reachable at (228) 435-6270. Its residential building permit application covers new construction, additions and repair or renovation work, and asks for the estimated cost and the contractor's name and city license number. Staff also record the property's FEMA flood zone on the same form.

That last line is why the permit question carries so much weight here. Nearly half the land in Biloxi lies in a special flood hazard area, and under the city's floodplain rules, once improvements, additions or repairs cost at least half the structure's market value, materials and labor counted, the whole building has to meet new-construction requirements, including elevation one foot above base flood. A modest remodel and a substantial improvement can look similar from the driveway, and the permit desk is where the difference is decided. Larger jobs of this kind are described on the [construction and renovation dumpsters](../construction-dumpster-rental/) page.

### When the box lands in the street

Not every lot in Biloxi has room for a roll-off. When the only open spot is the street or the shoulder, the box sits in public right-of-way rather than on private property. Haulers serving Biloxi generally advise that permits come into play when a container goes in the right-of-way, and that the city should be asked for the specifics. Make that call before scheduling the drop, and give the city the address, the box size and how long it will stay.

### The ditch is off-limits

Roadside ditches carry street runoff, and in a city this low they matter in every heavy rain. City ordinance Sec. 9-1-15 makes dumping into ditches and streams a violation. A roll-off set across a ditch line, or debris that spills from an overloaded box into one, puts the owner on the wrong side of that rule. Choose a spot on firm, level ground clear of the ditch, and keep the load inside the walls.

### Why the curb is not a substitute

Some owners hope to avoid a container entirely by piling debris at the curb. Biloxi's trash guide closes that door: garbage, recycling and yard trash are collected under a contract through the Harrison County Utility Authority, but construction debris generated by a contractor will not be picked up, and neither will concrete, asphalt, dirt or tires. Nothing placed in the right-of-way in front of an empty lot is collected either. If the work produces debris, it needs its own container and a proper disposal site.

### After a storm, the paperwork piles up

Permits and debris arrive together after a hurricane. Following Katrina, the city had issued more than 3,984 building permits for storm repairs by August 2007, and public crews hauled more than 2.94 million cubic yards of debris from streets and rights-of-way. Public contracts clear the street. What comes out of a damaged house is the owner's or the contractor's to remove, and the same placement rules apply even when every hauler on the coast is busy. Roofs after a storm are covered on [roofing dumpster rental](../roofing-dumpster-rental/), and storm-damaged contents on [cleanout dumpster rental](../cleanout-dumpster-rental/).

### Measure the driveway first

The common roll-off sizes take up less ground than most people expect. One Harrison County hauler lists its 10, 15 and 20-yard boxes at the same exterior footprint, 8 feet wide and 14 feet long, and describes them as fitting in a standard parking spot; only the wall height changes. So the real question on most Biloxi lots is not whether the box fits but whether the truck can reach the spot: a straight run to back in, firm ground, and nothing low overhead when the bed tilts to set the container down.

If a driveway, gravel pad or side yard gives the truck that, put the box there. On private ground the box is between you and the hauler; in the street, whoever maintains the road has a say too.

### Who has a say over the street in front of you

**City streets.** Biloxi's Engineering Department reviews requests to dig in city rights-of-way under Sec. 17-2-10 of the city code, and fill permits under the flood damage prevention rules. The department says those two are the only permits it has a role in, and it sends everything else to the Community Development Department to confirm what applies before work starts on public or private property. A container that will sit on a city street belongs in that second category, so Community Development is the first call.

**Beach Boulevard and the interstate spur.** U.S. 90 along the beach and I-110 are state routes maintained by the Mississippi Department of Transportation. A roll-off parked on a state highway shoulder is not something a city office can approve on its own, and a beachfront address with no driveway space needs that question answered before booking.

**Outside the city line.** Some addresses north of the Back Bay sit inside D'Iberville or on Harrison County roads rather than Biloxi streets. Check which government maintains the road, because that is the office to ask.

### What state law says about blocking a road

Mississippi's criminal code has a short, old section on this. Under Miss. Code Sec. 97-15-37, anyone who obstructs a public highway in any manner and does not remove the obstruction immediately commits a misdemeanor, punishable by a fine of up to $50, a week in jail, or both. An approved, marked container is not a pile left in the lane, but an unapproved box blocking traffic is exactly what the statute describes.

### The city's curbside rules point the same way

Biloxi's solid waste collection guide sets placement rules for curbside pickup that make a good test for a container too. Waste should sit three to five feet from the curb and should not block the roadway. Piles should not go under power lines or tree limbs, and should stay clear of mailboxes, fire hydrants, and gas and electric meters. If a 14-foot box would cover a hydrant, crowd a meter, or put the truck's cables into a line or a live oak limb, it is in the wrong place, street or not.

### Ditches, sidewalks and storm season

Many Biloxi streets drain to an open roadside ditch rather than a curb and gutter. A box set across it can crush the edge, dam the runoff or let debris wash in, and the city treats dumping into ditches as an ordinance violation. Keep the box on the yard side, and keep sidewalks open.

Hurricane season, June 1 through November 30, adds one more reason to keep the street clear. After a storm the right-of-way is where residents stage limbs and wrecked contents for public debris crews. A private container in the lane slows that sweep for the whole block.

### If the street is the only spot

- Call Community Development before booking, and give the address, the box size and the number of days it will stay. For a state route, ask MDOT.
- Choose the shortest rental window the job allows, and schedule the haul the day the box is full.
- Keep the box clear of hydrants, meters, mailboxes, driveways and the ditch line.
- Mark it so drivers can see it at night; a dark steel box on an unlit street is easy to hit.
- Load nothing above the rim, so nothing blows into the lane.

## q_what_cant_you_put_in_a_dumpster_body

### The landfill's list becomes your list

A roll-off from a Biloxi job usually ends up at a Class I rubbish site, such as the one on Hudson Krohn Road in north Biloxi. The Mississippi Department of Environmental Quality publishes what those sites may and may not accept, and it puts the burden on the operator: anything prohibited that slips in has to be pulled out and disposed of properly at the site's expense. That is why haulers inspect loads, why a refused item can come back to you as a charge, and why the state list is the practical answer to this question.

The MDEQ list of prohibited wastes at a Class I site includes:

- household garbage, and food or drink waste;
- liquids, sludges and contaminated soils;
- paint, paint buckets, oil containers and chemical containers;
- engines and motors;
- refrigerators;
- whole tires, and cut or shredded tires without specific approval;
- batteries;
- toxic or hazardous waste;
- asbestos and anything containing it;
- medical waste.

The same list adds a few that surprise people. Metal, glass, plastic and paper containers, loose fabric and bulk paper waste are all barred unless the state's Office of Pollution Control approves them case by case. Appliances are allowed only once the motor is out, and refrigerators not at all.

### Food waste, Coast style

Biloxi's collection guide lists crawfish and shrimp hulls among the things its curbside haulers will not pick up, and they are just as unwelcome in a roll-off. Food waste is prohibited at rubbish sites, and a box of construction debris that smells like last weekend's boil after three days in August heat draws flies, gulls and complaints. Keep the shells out of the box entirely.

### Tires

State law bars landfilling waste tires in any form, whole, split or shredded, unless the environmental commission grants an exception, under Miss. Code Sec. 17-17-407. Biloxi's 2021 guide said the household hazardous waste collection took up to 25 tires per household; a D'Iberville collection day in April 2026 capped them at five per resident. Tire shops also take old tires when they sell new ones.

### Batteries

Under Miss. Code Sec. 17-17-429, a lead-acid battery from a car, boat or golf cart may not go into mixed municipal solid waste. It goes back to a retailer, a wholesaler or an authorized recycler. Lithium-ion batteries from tools, scooters and phones are a fire risk in a steel box full of dry lumber, and even the April 2026 D'Iberville collection day refused them, so ask a battery retailer or recycler instead.

### Refrigerators, freezers and window units

These carry refrigerant, and federal rules under 40 CFR 82.155 require it to be recovered before the appliance is scrapped or disposed of. Biloxi's guide says refrigerators with Freon still inside will not be collected, and that a licensed contractor must remove it and tag the unit. A rubbish site will not take a refrigerator even then, so plan on a scrap dealer or the county's hazardous waste route.

### Paint, oil and household chemicals

Harrison County runs the Rocko-McFarland Household Hazardous Waste Site at 10076 Lorraine Road in Gulfport, open the second Saturday of every month from 8 a.m. to noon, according to an April 2026 WLOX report. The city's 2021 guide listed what the county program accepted: latex and oil-based paint, used cooking and motor oil, antifreeze, car and marine batteries, aerosol cans, flammable liquids such as gasoline, fluorescent bulbs, computers, phones and televisions. Hours have changed over the years, so check before loading the car.

Even a dried-out paint can is still a container under the state list, so the collection day is the cleaner route.

### Asbestos

Old floor tile, pipe wrap, textured ceilings and some siding can contain asbestos. It is banned at both classes of rubbish site, so a remodel of an older Biloxi house should have suspect material tested before it is torn out. If it tests positive, removal and disposal follow their own rules and never involve a standard roll-off.

### Heavy but allowed

Concrete, brick, block and asphalt are welcome, and a box of nothing else can go to a Class II site as well as a Class I. They are limited by weight rather than by rules, which is covered on [How Much Weight Can a Dumpster Hold?](../how-much-weight-can-a-dumpster-hold/) and on the [concrete and heavy debris dumpsters](../concrete-dumpster-rental/) page.
