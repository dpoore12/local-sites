# Colorado Springs emergency plumbing research — 2026-08-22

1. **Four-service municipal utility.** Colorado Springs Utilities says it delivers electricity, natural gas, water, and wastewater services to the Pikes Peak region. This helps distinguish private plumbing work from utility-side service conditions. Source: https://www.csu.org/about/

2. **Elevation and daily freeze-thaw.** FAA lists COS field elevation at 6,147 feet MSL. NWS 1991–2020 normals list January high/low at 45.0°F / 18.5°F, evidence that typical winter days cross freezing. A gas-water-heater manufacturer's high-altitude manual identifies 5,300–10,100 feet as a high-altitude model range for that product, so model-specific altitude instructions matter. Sources: https://www.faa.gov/cos ; https://www.weather.gov/pub/climateCosDailyNormalsRecords ; https://assets.hotwater.com/damroot/Original/1000/100282340.pdf

3. **Water-heater installation rule.** Pikes Peak Regional Building Department's water-heater handout says permits and inspections are required for gas and electric water-heater installations and lists application, venting, combustion-air, shutoff, relief-discharge, and expansion-control checks. Source: https://www.pprbd.org/File/Resources/Downloads/ResidentialHandout/Water%20heater%20installation%20ACCESS.pdf

Neighborhood names come from the City of Colorado Springs neighborhood framework map: Downtown, Old Colorado City, Patty Jewett, Rockrimmon, Briargate, and Old North End. Source: https://mayor.coloradosprings.gov/system/files/plancos_ch2_neighborhoods_framework_map.pdf
