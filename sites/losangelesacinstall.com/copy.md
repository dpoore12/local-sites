## pricing_body

### Why two bids on the same house differ by thousands

Tonnage moves first: posted Los Angeles ranges climb roughly a thousand to two thousand dollars per ton across the two to five ton spread most houses need. Efficiency moves second, and a high-efficiency inverter model adds a four-figure sum by itself. Third is what else gets touched. A condenser and matched coil on a furnace that stays put is one project. A condenser, an air handler, a new line set, pad, disconnect and circuit is another, and that is what most twenty-year-old systems need. Bids diverge on scope, not markup: one defers the furnace, the ducts and the testing while another quotes all of it.

### The permit is small, closing it is not

A mechanical permit is required for any installation or modification of a heating or cooling system. The fee is modest. The LADBS mechanical and HVAC permit fee schedule prices a condensing unit under 25 horsepower at $24.00 and an air handling unit at $24.00. A $90.00 minimum applies once two items share one permit. Surcharges of 3 and 6 percent then land a normal changeout permit near $98.

Verification costs more. Replacing equipment on a ducted system triggers duct leakage testing, and inland it adds refrigerant charge verification and an airflow check. An independent rater performs them, not the installer, and the paperwork is required to close the permit even where a test is waived. As of January 1, 2026 that field verification sits inside the state's Energy Code compliance program under renamed certificates.

### Coast, Valley, and the zone line

One city sits in more than one design climate; the state's reference places the airport area in Climate Zone 6 and Burbank in Climate Zone 9. Charge verification follows that zone list, so the same changeout carries a longer test scope inland than near the water. Attic access, side-yard clearance and spare breaker space decide the rest.

### Refrigerant rules, and what incentives do

Since January 1, 2025 new residential systems must use a refrigerant below 700 global warming potential, while pre-2025 equipment may still be installed and repair parts are unrestricted. That mixed shelf weighs on repair-or-replace math. Incentives change the net, not the bid, and equipment type drives the spread. A straight air conditioner at 15.2 SEER2 draws $100 per ton, against $1,000 to $2,500 per ton for a qualifying heat pump. Claims need a paid itemized invoice, a matching equipment certificate and a final approved permit.

### Reading a changeout estimate

A complete estimate names both units by model as a matched certified pair, gives tonnage, efficiency and refrigerant, and lists the line set as new or reused along with the pad, disconnect, circuit and condensate route. It carries separate lines for the permit and for third-party energy code testing, and says who schedules the rater. National cost pages price a box and a day of labor; they omit the surcharges, the rater visit, the registry fee and a panel upgrade.

## svc_air_conditioner_installation_body

### What the load calculation has to settle

Room-by-room heat gain sets the tonnage, not floor area. The calculation weighs glass orientation and shading, attic insulation, ceiling height, air leakage and how each room is used, then compares the cooling total against the heating total. The address matters more than the city name. State design-day data assigns the airport area a cooling design temperature of 84 degrees at the 0.5 percent condition, and lists Burbank at 96. Twelve degrees of design spread means different tonnage, different duct sizing and a different four o'clock in July for two houses inside the same municipal boundary.

### The efficiency floor is regional, and this is the strict side

Federal standards divide the country, and this state sits in the hot-dry Southwest group alongside Arizona, Nevada and New Mexico. A split-system air conditioner under 45,000 Btu per hour has to reach 14.3 SEER2 here, against 13.4 in the northern band. It also has to clear a separate full-load figure: 11.7 EER2 for a model certified below 15.2 SEER2, or 9.8 at or above that mark. Units of 45,000 Btu per hour and up answer to 13.8 SEER2. The Department of Energy applies the southern rule by installation date rather than manufacture date, and states plainly that fitting a noncompliant unit in this region is illegal.

### Capacity is capped unless somebody measures the air

Sizing is not left to habit. Where measured airflow reaches at least 350 cubic feet per minute per ton, there is no ceiling on system capacity. Where nobody verifies it, state tables limit capacity according to whether the house is heating dominated or cooling dominated, a line drawn at 12,000 Btu per hour of difference between the two design loads. Federal research explains the caution. Equipment oversized by half or more used roughly 9 percent more cooling electricity across a year in one study, and correlated with summer peak demand 13 percent higher, while the short run cycles wear components and leave moisture in the house.

### Who is allowed to do this, and what a contract may ask of you

Cooling work falls under the C-20 classification, defined in state regulation as warm-air heating, ventilating and air-conditioning: systems complete with their appliances, blowers, plenum chambers, ducts, registers, flues, humidity and thermostatic controls, and air filters. Since January 2025 the unlicensed minor-work ceiling has been $1,000, and a proper classification is required below that figure anyway once a permit is needed or a worker is hired. The building department points homeowners straight at the state license lookup. On a home improvement contract the deposit may not exceed $1,000 or 10 percent of the price, whichever is smaller. A contract signed away from the contractor's own premises carries three days to cancel in writing, and five days for a signer aged 65 or over.

### The permit, then the rater who does not work for the installer

A mechanical permit covers any installation or modification of heating and cooling equipment inside city limits, and a homeowner may apply for mechanical e-permits directly. Nothing may be covered or concealed before it is inspected, and permitted work is not approved until inspection staff accept it. On top of that, the energy code sends a certified rater to test what was installed. Certificates are registered with an approved compliance provider, and the permit file does not close without them.

### What the visit looks like, in order

The first appointment is measurement, not equipment selection. Rooms and windows are recorded, attic and crawlspace access is checked, the panel and available breaker space are read, and outdoor unit locations are weighed against clearance, noise and the line-set route. Condensate has to reach a legal termination. The written scope that follows should separate equipment, distribution work, electrical work, permit handling, the rater visit and any patching. Ordering is the last step, not the first.

### What moves the number

Tonnage, efficiency tier and the amount of supporting work drive the spread. A house with usable attic runs, a modern panel and clear side-yard access sits low in the market range. Plaster ceilings, a full panel, a long line set or a duct system that fails its leakage test all push upward. Ask which line covers the permit, and which line covers the independent test, before comparing two bids.

## svc_ac_replacement_lede

Pulling the old condenser off the pad sounds like a parts job. State rules treat it as an alteration, and that single act drags duct leakage testing, charge verification and a thermostat upgrade into the scope of work.

## svc_ac_replacement_body

### How you know replacement is the honest call

A failed component is not automatically a changeout. The useful comparison is the cost of the current repair against the condition of the matched indoor coil, the state of the line set, and whether the tonnage still suits the house. Rooms added, a garage converted to living space, west glass left unshaded or attic insulation flattened over decades all change the load the original installer sized for. Measure the rooms again before agreeing that the answer is a larger cabinet. Two houses of the same footprint, one near the water and one over the hills, do not need the same machine.

### One replaced component, several new obligations

The energy code lists what counts. Replacing an air handler, installing or replacing the outdoor condensing unit of a split air conditioner or heat pump, completely replacing a packaged system, or installing or replacing a cooling or heating coil each trigger duct sealing and leakage testing. Any of those also forces the thermostat to be upgraded to a digital setback type if it is not one already. That is why a bid describing only a condenser and a coil is incomplete: the obligations attach to the whole distribution system the new equipment will breathe through.

### The duct test is where cheap bids come apart

Measured leakage has to land at or below 10 percent of system airflow, or at or below 7 percent when measured to outside. Miss both and every accessible leak must be sealed, then confirmed by visual inspection and a smoke test performed by the rater on that specific house. Sampling across houses is not allowed. Ducts, coils or plenums in a garage face a 6 percent target instead. Two exemptions matter locally: a duct system totaling under 40 feet, and one already sealed, tested and certified. Brittle flex duct in a hot attic frequently fails the first attempt, which is why sealing labor belongs in the scope from the start.

### Charge verification depends on which side of the hills you live on

Refrigerant charge is field verified when a refrigerant-containing component is installed or replaced. For an air conditioner that duty applies in a specific list of climate zones, and the San Fernando Valley side of the city, zone 9, is inside it while the coastal zone 6 side is not. For a heat pump the verification applies in every zone, coast included. Where charge verification is required, the system also has to move at least 300 cubic feet per minute per ton, or 250 on small-duct high-velocity equipment. Same house, same brochure, different test list.

### What the incentive spread says about equipment choice

The municipal utility pays about $100 to $120 per ton on a straight central or split air conditioner meeting 15.2 or 16.0 SEER2. A qualifying ducted heat pump earns $1,000 to $1,250 per ton on the same schedule, and a ductless heat pump reaches as high as $2,500 per ton. Claims want a paid itemized invoice, matching equipment certification and the final approved permit, so the paperwork and the incentive are the same conversation. The federal side has closed: the home improvement credit is not allowed for property placed in service after December 31, 2025. New equipment has also moved to refrigerants under the 700 GWP limit, which shifted what distributors stock, but that is a footnote beside the testing regime.

### The paperwork outlives the equipment

Registered compliance certificates, the closed permit and the rater's results are the record that the system a buyer inherits is the system that was sold. An open permit surfaces at resale, at refinance and after any insurance question about unpermitted work. Keep the invoice, the equipment certification and the final sign-off together. A future technician reads that file to know what charge and airflow the machine was commissioned at.

### What moves the number

Tonnage sets the base, efficiency tier adds to it, and everything the changeout touches decides the rest. A same-capacity swap on sound ducts with an accessible pad is the low end. A new air handler, replacement line set, sealing work after a failed leakage test, a panel with no spare capacity, or a coil buried in a tight closet all move the total. Every published range should show the permit and the independent test as their own lines.

## svc_central_air_installation_lede

Adding central air to a house that never had it is mostly attic work. A brand-new duct system answers to a tighter leakage number than a patched one, and the attic itself picks up requirements the day equipment moves into it.

## svc_central_air_installation_body

### When whole-house ducted cooling is the right answer

Central air earns its keep when the house has a usable attic or crawlspace route, rooms that should behave alike, and an electrical service that can take the load. It struggles where ceilings are plaster, the attic pinches to nothing at the eaves, or a hillside floor plan stacks rooms in ways no trunk line can serve without soffits. The honest test is whether supply and return can both reach every room. A design that gets supply air into a bedroom and leaves no return path has built its own hot room.

### A new duct system answers to a tighter figure

Where the ducts form an entirely new system, measured leakage must come in at or below 5 percent of system airflow. The code defines an entirely new system as at least 75 percent new duct material, with any reused parts accessible. Extend or partly replace an existing system with more than 25 feet of new duct instead, and the target becomes 10 percent total leakage or 7 percent to outside. A brand-new layout also has to satisfy filtration, static-pressure probe, airflow and fan efficacy requirements. Those numbers reward planned trunk-and-branch layouts over long flexible runs pulled around framing.

### The attic package that rarely appears on a first bid

Put an air handler and ducts in a vented attic and a second set of rules arrives with them. The ceiling between conditioned rooms and the attic must be air sealed. The attic floor over conditioned space is insulated to R-49. Recessed can lights that are not rated for insulation contact have to be swapped for rated fixtures or covered with a fire-rated enclosure so insulation can pass over them. Attic ventilation has to meet residential code. In a 1920s bungalow in Highland Park or West Adams that is real carpentry and real electrical work, not an afterthought, and it belongs in writing before the crew arrives.

### Insulation on the runs depends on your side of the hills

Duct insulation in unconditioned space is set by climate zone. Coastal parts of the city sit in zone 6, where R-6 is the minimum on new duct. Cross into the Valley and zone 9 requires R-8. That is not a preference an installer gets to trade away, and it interacts with attic temperatures: a run baking above a Sherman Oaks ceiling loses far more capacity than the same run in Echo Park. Design temperature moves with it, from 84 degrees at the airport station to 96 in Burbank, so the Valley house needs both the heavier insulation and more capacity behind it.

### Sizing, oversizing and the airflow proof

Capacity limits apply unless airflow is field verified at 350 cubic feet per minute per ton or better. Federal guidance puts normal cooling airflow around 400 to 500 cubic feet per minute per ton, and warns that poor airflow can cost 5 percent or more of a system's potential. Oversizing is the common error: research on units oversized by half or more found roughly 9 percent higher annual cooling electricity and 13 percent higher summer peak demand, with short cycles that wear equipment and never dry the air. A correctly sized system with generous return area beats a larger one choked by a single undersized grille.

### Filtration, smoke season and the return you actually need

Regional air quality guidance for smoke events recommends filters rated MERV13 or higher, closing any fresh-air intake so the system recirculates, keeping a clean spare filter on hand, and adding a portable HEPA cleaner for one room. It also advises against whole-house fans and evaporative coolers that pull outdoor air during smoke. Dense filter media only works if the return was designed for it, which is a design decision made during installation, not a filter you buy later. Say at the start that you want smoke-capable filtration so the return and filter rack get sized for it.

### What the visit and the number look like

Expect measurement first: rooms, glass, attic clearance, joist depth, panel capacity, condensate route and outdoor unit placement. A serious proposal shows the duct layout, register and return locations, insulation values, the sealing and testing plan, the permit, the attic work and the rater visit. Cost tracks house size, register count, attic access and how much of the attic package the job triggers. A house needing new ducts, attic insulation and a service upgrade lands in a different range than a straightforward equipment change.

## svc_ductless_mini_split_installation_body

### The housing stock argument for going ductless

City planning figures put nearly half the housing stock as built before 1960, with about one in five units dating to 1939 or earlier, and roughly 565,000 detached houses making up around 38 percent of all units. Those bungalows and cottages were heated by floor furnaces, wall units and gravity systems, so there is no trunk line to inherit. Cutting duct chases through plaster and lath in Echo Park or Koreatown costs more than the equipment. A ductless system puts the capacity in the room that needs it and leaves the ceilings alone.

### No ducts means no leakage test, but the permit still applies

Duct sealing and leakage verification are not required where there is no duct system to test, and the code also exempts systems totaling under 40 feet of duct. That removes one line item, not the permit. A mechanical permit covers any installation or modification of heating and cooling equipment in the city, work may not be concealed before inspection, and nothing is approved until an inspector accepts it. Refrigerant line penetrations, condensate routing and the new circuit are exactly what an inspector looks at, so the wall covers and chases should be planned as inspectable work.

### A ductless heat pump gets verified everywhere

Most mini-splits are heat pumps, and that decides the testing. Refrigerant charge field verification applies to a heat pump in every climate zone, so a Silver Lake install and a Sherman Oaks install both get it, while a straight air conditioner is only tested in the inland zone list. Adding or replacing a refrigerant-containing component also requires the thermostat or control to be a digital setback type. Because these systems are heat pumps rather than split air conditioners, they follow the national efficiency standard by manufacture date, currently 14.3 SEER2 and 7.5 HSPF2, instead of the regional install-date rule that governs a straight cooling changeout.

### Heating rules make the heat pump the electric option

When a space heating system is replaced, the replacement generally may not use electric resistance as its primary heat source, with narrow exceptions including a ductless replacement for an existing electric resistance system. Prescriptively the new heating unit is natural gas fueled or a heat pump, and the only electric route is the heat pump. For a house with wall heaters and no gas line to the room, that points at ductless equipment providing both heating and cooling from one outdoor unit. It also means the heating load, not just the cooling load, has to be calculated per zone.

### The electrical panel is the real constraint

An older service is where these projects stall. Each outdoor unit needs its own dedicated circuit and disconnect, and a house with a full panel and no spare spaces has to be evaluated before equipment is ordered rather than after it arrives. Multi-zone systems concentrate that load on one outdoor unit, which is sometimes kinder to a tight panel than several single-zone systems. Have the service capacity, breaker space and wire route confirmed in the written scope, with any panel work priced as its own line.

### Placement decisions you live with for years

Indoor heads should throw air across the occupied part of the room without aiming at a pillow or a desk. Line sets want the shortest sane route, in a proper cover or chase rather than draped down stucco. Condensate needs a gravity fall to a legal termination, or a pump if it cannot get one. Outdoor units need service clearance, a stable base and a spot that does not turn a narrow side yard into a noise channel between two houses. Walk the wall lines with the installer before drilling starts.

### Rebates, per zone, and what moves the number

The municipal utility rebate schedule favors this equipment class heavily: ductless and multi-split heat pumps run from about $1,500 up to $2,500 per ton by efficiency tier, against roughly $100 per ton for a straight air conditioner. New construction and accessory dwelling units are excluded, and claims want the paid invoice, matching equipment certification and the final approved permit. There is no federal credit to add now, since that credit is unavailable for property placed in service after December 31, 2025. Cost tracks zone count, head type, line-set length, wall construction and whatever the panel needs.
