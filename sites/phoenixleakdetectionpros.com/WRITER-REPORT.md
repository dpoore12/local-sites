# Phoenix leak-detection research notes

1. Phoenix’s 2025 Water Quality Report lists total hardness at 172–302 ppm (10–17.6 grains per gallon) and says hard water can cause scaling in pipes, water heaters, and plumbing fixtures. For this site, hard-water scale is a reason to inspect older copper thoughtfully; it is not proof of a leak or the sole cause of a pinhole failure.
   - https://www.phoenix.gov/content/dam/phoenix/waterservicessite/documents/wsdprimarywqr.pdf

2. Phoenix Water Services allows a Sewer Fee Review when higher-than-usual water use is caused by broken plumbing or outdoor irrigation equipment. For the 2026 cycle, requests more than 60 days after the July bill date are ineligible; prior years cannot be adjusted.
   - https://www.phoenix.gov/administration/departments/waterservices/city-services-bill/submit-a-sewer-fee-review.html

3. The Arizona Geological Survey says groundwater withdrawal can drive land subsidence and earth-fissure formation; Maricopa County is among the counties with earth fissures. Its page notes fissures can damage utility lines, roads, and canals. This supports a careful check of buried piping where ground movement is relevant, without diagnosing fissures from a household leak.
   - https://azgs.arizona.edu/earth-fissures-ground-subsidence/more-arizonas-earth-fissures

Neighborhoods selected: Arcadia, Ahwatukee Foothills, Encanto, Desert Ridge, Sunnyslope, Roosevelt Row.
