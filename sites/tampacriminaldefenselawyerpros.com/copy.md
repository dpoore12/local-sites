## qa_1_question

How quickly should an arrested person see a judge in Tampa?

## qa_1_answer

Florida Rule of Criminal Procedure 3.130 says that an arrested person who has not already been released in a lawful manner must be taken before a judicial officer within 24 hours of arrest. At that hearing the judge gives the charge, provides a complaint copy, and advises the person about silence and counsel. Rule 3.133 addresses a separate nonadversary probable-cause determination for a person held in custody, generally within 48 hours. In Hillsborough, the duty-judge administrative order assigns first appearances and bond motions by audiovisual device from Courtroom 17 in the Courthouse Annex; weekday proceedings commence at 9:00 a.m., with weekend and holiday proceedings following juvenile detention and shelter hearings.

## qa_2_question

Why might no bond be available from booking before first appearance?

## qa_2_answer

Hillsborough’s 2026 administrative order adopts the statewide bond schedule without change, but the schedule applies to the booking officer rather than to a judge. It excludes numerous arrest categories, including capital, life, first-degree, and second-degree felonies; robbery, burglary, carjacking, kidnapping, trafficking, felony battery, domestic battery by strangulation, stalking, injunction violations, and several supervision-status situations. A person excluded from the schedule may not be released before first appearance under that order. When a judge addresses release, the order calls for an individualized inquiry under Florida statutes and Rule 3.131 rather than a mechanical schedule amount.

## qa_3_question

Does silence after an arrest mean formal charges will never be filed?

## qa_3_answer

No. The arrest paperwork and an information filed by a prosecutor are separate steps. The Hillsborough Clerk explains that felony arrest paperwork goes from the jail to the Clerk’s felony department, which prepares the official court file and forwards it to the State Attorney for a filing decision. When formal charges are filed, the Clerk sends an arraignment notice to the address supplied at arrest and also notifies a bond agent when applicable. Florida Statute 775.15 generally sets four years for a first-degree felony, three years for another felony, two years for a first-degree misdemeanor, and one year for a second-degree misdemeanor, subject to statutory exceptions.

## svc_felony_defense_lawyer_lede

Felony procedure in Tampa usually turns first on custody, first appearance, the bond order, and the later filing decision. An arrest affidavit is an important record, but it is not the same document as a prosecutor’s formal charge.

## svc_felony_defense_lawyer_body

### Start with the degree and the exact statute

Florida calls an offense a felony when it is punishable by death or imprisonment in a state penitentiary; a misdemeanor is ordinarily punishable by county incarceration not exceeding one year. Write down the statute subsection and degree exactly as printed. The word “felony” alone does not identify the conduct alleged, the evidence listed, or the court division that will later handle the proceeding.

### The first 24 hours and first appearance

Florida Rule of Criminal Procedure 3.130 generally requires an arrested person who has not been lawfully released to appear before a judicial officer within 24 hours. The judge states the charge, provides a complaint copy, and advises the person about silence and counsel.

Hillsborough duty judges conduct first appearances and bond motions by audiovisual device from Courtroom 17 in the Courthouse Annex.

### Booking bond and a judge’s release decision are separate

Hillsborough’s 2026 order adopts the statewide uniform bond schedule without change. The order says plainly that the schedule applies to a booking officer, not to a judge, and that it does not bind a first-appearance or bail-bond hearing. A judge considers an individualized release question under the cited statutes and criminal rule.

The order also excludes many arrests from pre-first-appearance release under the schedule. Examples include capital, life, first-degree, and second-degree felonies; robbery; burglary; carjacking; kidnapping; trafficking; felony battery; domestic battery by strangulation; stalking; protective-injunction violations; and specified prior-supervision or recent-arrest histories. The list is important because an amount associated with a different charge does not mean the booking officer can release every person. Read the current order rather than relying on a bond amount repeated online.

### An arrest does not settle the filing question

For a Hillsborough felony arrest, the Clerk describes a sequence in which jail paperwork reaches the felony department, an official court file is prepared, and the file is forwarded to the State Attorney for a filing decision. The formal charging document may differ from the initial arrest paperwork, may include more than one count, or may not be filed. Keep every version of the paperwork; replacing the arrest affidavit with a verbal retelling loses dates, witnesses, statute sections, and property descriptions that can matter later.

When charges are filed, the Clerk says an arraignment notice is mailed to the address provided at arrest, with notice also sent to a bond agent where applicable. Check that address. A temporary address, a move from Seminole Heights to another part of town, or mail left at a former residence can create a practical problem even though the courthouse record continues.

### Conditions are active until a court changes them

Release paperwork may restrict contact, travel, substances, possession of weapons, or entry to an address. The local bond order says a person released before trial must refrain from criminal activity and from contact with a victim except through pretrial discovery; it also states that a violation may lead to revocation of bond. Read the written terms as they stand. A text, invitation, apology, or informal agreement with another person is not a court modification.

This is especially important where the allegation involves a household member, a co-worker, or a nearby address. Ordinary daily movement can overlap with a stay-away term. Questions about the scope of an order call for direct legal advice and, where appropriate, a court filing through counsel—not private workarounds.

Make one chronological folder with the booking sheet, arrest affidavit, complaint copy, bond receipt, release conditions, property receipt, notices from the Clerk, and any DUI suspension notice. Do not alter photographs, messages, location records, or other material that might later be relevant. This is a guide to Florida and Hillsborough procedure, not a determination about evidence or an outcome.

## svc_misdemeanor_defense_lawyer_lede

A misdemeanor charge can involve a notice to appear, a short booking period, or bond paperwork, then a court date that arrives later. The lower degree does not make the mail, release conditions, or statutory timing rules optional.

## svc_misdemeanor_defense_lawyer_body

### “Misdemeanor” still needs a statute and degree

Florida defines a misdemeanor as an offense punishable by county incarceration that does not exceed one year, apart from an extended term. A first-degree misdemeanor and a second-degree misdemeanor carry different general limitation periods, and specific statutes may make conduct a felony when a prior conviction, injury, protected person, amount, or another stated fact is alleged. Read the statute number, subsection, degree, count, and arrest date from the paper itself.

### Notice to appear versus custody paperwork

Some misdemeanor allegations begin with a notice to appear rather than a jail stay. Others involve booking, release, a written promise to appear, or a bond form. Each produces documents that should be saved. Put them in date order and look for the court division, location, statute subsection, and return date.

### The arraignment notice is an event, not a conclusion

An arraignment is a procedural hearing at which a charge is addressed and a plea is entered. It is not a finding that the arrest affidavit was accurate and it is not the end of the proceeding. A person should not miss it because a friend says the matter is “only a misdemeanor” or because nothing arrived immediately after arrest. Court notices and docket entries have their own schedule.

Florida Statute 775.15 generally allows two years to commence prosecution for a first-degree misdemeanor and one year for a second-degree misdemeanor. Those are outer statutory rules, not permission to skip a current hearing or summons.

### Release terms deserve plain reading

Misdemeanor release paperwork can include no-contact or stay-away terms. Read the judge’s language and the written form. Another person’s message does not itself remove a court term.

Florida’s Hillsborough bond order emphasizes that a judge’s decision is individualized and that release conditions can be enforced through revocation of bond. The person involved should retain every condition sheet and obtain direct legal advice before trying to change a condition. This resource does not decide whether an individual term applies or whether a court would modify it.

### DUI is often both a court and a driving matter

A standard Florida DUI allegation arises when a person drives or is in actual physical control of a vehicle while normal faculties are impaired, with a blood-alcohol level of 0.08 or more, or with a breath-alcohol level of 0.08 or more. The criminal proceeding is not the only moving part. Under Statute 322.2615, an officer may suspend the driving privilege for a qualifying 0.08 result or refusal and issue an otherwise-eligible ten-day temporary permit with a notice of suspension.

The person has ten days from the notice date to request a formal or informal departmental review, or to request a review of eligibility for a restricted driving privilege as provided by statute. The temporary permit generally expires at midnight on the tenth day. That administrative deadline is separate from any criminal court date. Do not wait for a later notice from the Clerk before reading the suspension notice and seeking direct legal advice.

### Records relief is not automatic

Florida has separate statutes for sealing and expunction. Statute 943.059 requires a certificate of eligibility before a sealing petition and excludes records made ineligible by Statute 943.0584. Expunction under Statute 943.0585 has different disposition and eligibility requirements and also requires a certificate. A dismissal, withheld adjudication, old charge, or completed sentence does not itself answer eligibility.

## svc_drug_crime_defense_lawyer_lede

Drug allegations often involve more than a substance name. The paperwork may raise questions about a stop, a search, possession, intent, statutory schedules, weight, laboratory reporting, and the written location of the alleged recovery.

## svc_drug_crime_defense_lawyer_body

### Preserve the documents before drawing conclusions

Begin with documents rather than a label such as “drug charge.” Keep the arrest affidavit, charging paper when available, property receipt, tow or inventory paperwork, search-warrant material if provided, body-camera or vehicle-video preservation information, and later laboratory records. Write down the statute section and subsection. The allegations may concern actual possession, constructive possession, possession with intent, sale, delivery, trafficking, paraphernalia, or a different offense entirely. Those are distinct statutory paths.

### Ordinary possession is not a single category

Florida Statute 893.13 makes actual or constructive possession of a controlled substance, except as authorized, a third-degree felony under subsection 6(a). The statute treats some substances and some conduct differently. Possession of 20 grams or less of cannabis under that subsection is a first-degree misdemeanor, while other provisions address listed Schedule V substances and more serious conduct. Do not assume that all cannabis, all prescription medication, or every substance found in a vehicle carries the same classification.

The statute’s words “actual or constructive possession” are important legal terms. Whether those terms fit particular facts is an individualized legal question. This page does not make that determination. Its practical instruction is to keep the property receipt and original allegation wording, rather than filling gaps with a summary that omits where an item was said to be found.

### Intent and trafficking are different allegations

Statute 893.13 separately addresses sale, manufacture, delivery, and possession with intent to sell, manufacture, or deliver. The controlled-substance schedule can change the stated felony degree. Trafficking appears in a different statute and uses substance-specific thresholds. Neither an officer’s casual phrase nor a social-media account substitutes for the charge actually filed.

Weight can matter, but a quantity alone does not answer every legal question. The documents may identify a gross weight, a mixture, a laboratory result, a count, or a statutory classification. Keep the laboratory report when it arrives and compare the wording to the property record. A person should obtain direct legal advice before assuming that a booking description will remain the final charge.

### A stop, consent, inventory, and warrant are different records

Drug allegations often arise after one of several encounters: a traffic stop, a consensual encounter, an arrest, a vehicle inventory, a claimed consent search, a search warrant, or a response to a residence call. Each route generates different records. Preserve dispatch times, tow records, citation copies, consent forms, warrant returns, and any paperwork left at the location. If video exists, note the agency, date, approximate time, and report number.

Do not alter a device, discard material, contact a witness about the facts, or post a narrative online. Those actions can create independent problems and can make it harder to reconstruct the sequence. A person who wants tailored advice should provide original records to a Florida lawyer of that person’s choosing rather than discussing the event on a recorded jail line.

### First appearance and bond may be affected by charge level

The 2026 Hillsborough bond schedule excludes trafficking in a controlled substance, first-degree felonies, and second-degree felonies from pre-first-appearance release under the schedule.

The local order says the schedule is for the booking officer and does not bind a judge, who addresses release through an individualized inquiry. Keep any bond form and every written condition.

### The filing step can change the paper trail

In a Hillsborough felony arrest, the Clerk says initial jail paperwork is assembled into an official court file and sent to the State Attorney for a filing decision. The later charging document may not use the same wording as an arrest affidavit. Preserve both, and keep the booking address current because the Clerk says arraignment notice is mailed there once charges are filed.

## svc_assault_charge_defense_lede

Florida statutes distinguish assault and battery and may change the level based on alleged harm, a prior conviction, a protected person, or the setting. Release conditions can become the first practical issue before later court notices arrive.

## svc_assault_charge_defense_body

### Read the statute before using the everyday label

In ordinary conversation, “assault” and “battery” may describe the same incident. Florida law separates them. Battery under Statute 784.03 generally means intentionally touching or striking another person against that person’s will, or intentionally causing bodily harm; absent another provision, it is a first-degree misdemeanor. A second or later battery after a listed prior conviction can be a third-degree felony under the statute. Other allegations—such as aggravated battery, felony battery, domestic battery by strangulation, or an offense involving a protected person—have their own statutory elements and degrees.

### First appearance may control the immediate conditions

When a person remains in custody, Rule 3.130 generally requires first appearance within 24 hours. Hillsborough duty judges conduct first appearances and bond motions by audiovisual device from Courtroom 17 at the Courthouse Annex. The initial hearing can address release and conditions before a prosecutor reaches a formal filing decision.

Hillsborough’s 2026 bond order excludes felony battery, domestic battery by strangulation, domestic violence, stalking, and first-degree and second-degree felonies from pre-first-appearance release under the booking schedule. These exclusions do not predict a judge’s release decision.

### A no-contact term is a court condition

No-contact and stay-away terms should be read literally until changed by a court. They can cover direct messages, phone calls, third-party communication, a residence, work, school, or a specified distance. The Hillsborough order says people released before trial must refrain from criminal activity and from contact with a victim except through pretrial discovery; it says a violation may subject the person to revocation of bond.

An invitation, apology, social-media message, or later change of mind by another person does not independently erase a term set by a judge. If a shared residence or workplace creates a practical conflict, the safe public-information answer is to comply with the written order and seek direct legal advice about the lawful method for requesting a change. Private workarounds can create a new allegation.

### Preserve competing records without trying to manage witnesses

A charge involving an encounter between people can produce 911 audio, dispatch logs, body-camera footage, photographs, medical records, neighborhood cameras, messages, and witness names. Preserve what already exists. Save copies of messages without editing them, record the date and location of any video source, and keep the arrest affidavit with later paperwork.

Do not ask others to change a statement, delete material, coordinate accounts, or discuss the facts through a recorded jail line. A person seeking individual advice should bring the records to a Florida lawyer chosen independently. This site does not collect facts or decide what the records mean.

### Filing is separate from the arrest record

An arrest affidavit explains an officer’s initial allegation. It does not itself constitute the formal filing decision. For Hillsborough felony arrests, the Clerk says jail paperwork goes to the felony department, which prepares the official court file and forwards it to the State Attorney. The later charging document may use different statute language or counts. Once charges are filed, the Clerk says an arraignment notice is mailed to the booking address.

Save mail even if it arrives at a former address in Palma Ceia, Hyde Park, or elsewhere in Tampa. A date on a notice can create an immediate obligation, while general statutory limitation periods are much longer outer limits. Under Statute 775.15, first-degree misdemeanors generally carry a two-year limitation period, other felonies three years, and first-degree felonies four years, subject to exceptions.

### A focused checklist after release

Keep the arrest affidavit, booking record, bond form, release conditions, notice to appear or arraignment notice, and any protective-order paperwork. Make a dated list of places the person must avoid and deadlines that appear in writing. This page provides general Florida procedure, not individualized legal advice.

## pricing_lede

Criminal defense in Florida is billed as a flat fee or by the hour, never as a share of the result. This page explains why, what the written agreement has to spell out, and which amounts Florida statutes and Hillsborough County records fix regardless of who is hired.

## pricing_body

### A defense fee cannot be tied to the outcome

Start with the rule that shapes every fee conversation in a Tampa criminal courtroom. Rule 4-1.5(f)(3)(B) of the Rules Regulating The Florida Bar says a lawyer must not arrange for, charge, or collect a contingency fee for representing a defendant in a criminal matter. Florida's rule tracks ABA Model Rule 1.5(d), which uses nearly identical wording, so the prohibition is not a local quirk.

That means the two lawful structures in a defense matter are a fixed sum for a defined scope of work and an hourly rate against a deposit. A percentage of a dismissal, an acquittal, or a reduced charge is not a lawful arrangement in Florida, and a promise built around one is a reason to walk away rather than a bargain.

### What the written agreement has to put in plain words

Rule 4-1.5(e)(1) requires that the basis or rate of the fee and costs be communicated before or within a reasonable time after the work begins, preferably in writing, when the lawyer has not represented the person before. If any part of the fee is nonrefundable, the rule requires that part to be confirmed in writing with an explanation of what the parties intended by it. Rule 4-1.5(b) lists what a reasonable fee is measured against, including difficulty, time demands, and the rate customarily charged locally for comparable work.

Read the paper for scope, not just the number. A fixed sum that ends at arraignment is a different agreement from one that carries through motion hearings, a plea negotiation, or a jury trial in the Hillsborough courthouse on Twiggs Street. Ask where the line sits, what happens if the State files a charge different from the arrest paperwork, and whether a violation of probation falls inside the scope.

### Costs sit on a separate line from the fee

Costs are money paid to other people. In a Hillsborough felony matter that can include an investigator to interview witnesses, a court reporter for depositions, an expert on toxicology or digital forensics, certified records, subpoena service, transcripts, and copying on a large discovery production. Rule 4-1.5(b)(2) treats costs as subject to their own reasonableness test and asks what disclosure was made about them and what third parties actually charged. A written agreement should say who advances each cost and whether an estimate is required before an expert is retained.

### What moves the size of a defense fee

Four things do most of the work. Charge level, because a second-degree felony filed in circuit court carries exposure and paperwork a second-degree misdemeanor in county court does not. Whether the matter resolves or is tried, since a trial adds jury selection, witness preparation, and full days in the courtroom. Motion practice, because a written motion to suppress a search or a stand-alone dismissal motion involves records, a hearing, and often testimony. And discovery volume, because body-camera footage from several officers, cell-site records, and laboratory reports take hours to review before anyone can advise on options.

### Who qualifies for a public defender in Hillsborough County

Fla. Stat. 27.52 puts the indigency determination with the Clerk, not the judge. An applicant is indigent when household income is at or below 200 percent of the current federal poverty guideline for that household size, which for a one-person household in 2026 works out to roughly $31,920 against the $15,960 guideline published by the U.S. Department of Health and Human Services. The statute also presumes an applicant is not indigent where net equity reaches $2,500 outside a homestead and one vehicle under $5,000, or where bail of $5,000 or more was posted.

Appointed counsel is not free of charge. The application itself costs $50 under Fla. Stat. 27.52(1)(b), and the Clerk applies the first $50 of anything paid toward it. Nobody found indigent may be refused counsel for not paying. On a conviction after appointed representation, Fla. Stat. 938.29(1)(a) puts a statutory judgment for attorney fees and costs at no less than $100 in a felony matter and no less than $50 in a misdemeanor or criminal traffic matter, imposed whether or not there is present ability to pay.

### Fines and court costs land on top of any fee

Fla. Stat. 775.083 caps a fine at $15,000 for a life felony, $10,000 for a first- or second-degree felony, $5,000 for a third-degree felony, $1,000 for a first-degree misdemeanor, and $500 for a second-degree misdemeanor or a noncriminal violation, with a higher figure available when it doubles the gain or the loss. Fla. Stat. 938.05 then adds court costs of $225 on a felony and $60 on a misdemeanor or criminal traffic offense, collected by the Hillsborough Clerk. Because withheld adjudication still counts as a conviction there, avoiding a formal adjudication does not by itself avoid the money.

## svc_arraignment_and_first_appearance_lede

First appearance and arraignment are two different hearings with two different purposes. One happens within a day of arrest; the other happens only after a prosecutor files a formal charging document.

## svc_arraignment_and_first_appearance_body

### First appearance: the 24-hour hearing

Florida Rule of Criminal Procedure 3.130 requires that every arrested person not otherwise released be taken before a judicial officer, in person or by electronic audiovisual device at the court's discretion, within 24 hours of arrest. The rule directs the judge to inform the defendant of the charge, provide a copy of the complaint, and advise the defendant of the right to remain silent, that anything said may be used against the defendant, and that the defendant has a right to counsel who will be appointed where the defendant is unable to pay.

### The separate 48-hour probable cause determination

Florida Rule of Criminal Procedure 3.133(a)(1) provides that in all matters where the defendant is in custody, a nonadversary probable cause determination must be held before a judge within 48 hours from the time of arrest, unless such a determination was previously made by a judge and an arrest warrant issued for the specific offense charged. On a showing of extraordinary circumstance the judge may continue the proceeding for not more than 24 hours beyond that period, and on a further showing that an extraordinary circumstance still exists, for not more than 24 additional hours. The rule states the determination may be made at first appearance where the necessary proof is available, without changing its nonadversary character.

### Nothing has been formally charged yet

An arrest is not a charge. The Hillsborough County Clerk of the Circuit Court describes the felony sequence: the jail sends initial paperwork, the Clerk's felony department prepares the official court file, and the file is forwarded to the State Attorney, which decides whether to file formal charges. The Clerk states that when charges are filed the defendant is notified by mail of an arraignment date, that a bail bond agent is notified where applicable, and that all notices go to the address given at arrest. The Clerk states plainly that the department cannot change a court date, and publishes forms for designating a current mailing and e-mail address.

### Arraignment: the plea hearing

Florida Rule of Criminal Procedure 3.160(a) provides that arraignment is conducted in open court or by audiovisual device at the court's discretion, and consists of the judge, clerk, or prosecuting attorney reading the indictment or information to the defendant, or stating orally the substance of the charge, and calling on the defendant to plead. The reading may be waived. Where the defendant is represented, the rule allows counsel to file a written not-guilty plea at or before the hearing, in which event the arraignment is treated as waived. Subdivision (b) provides that neither a failure to arraign nor an irregularity in the arraignment affects the validity of any proceeding where the defendant pleads to the charging document or proceeds to trial without objection.

Florida Rule of Criminal Procedure 3.125 governs the notice to appear, defined as a written order issued by a law enforcement officer in lieu of physical arrest requiring a person to appear at a designated court or governmental office at a specified date and time. The rule permits an arresting officer to issue one for a misdemeanor of the first or second degree, a violation, or a county or municipal ordinance triable in the county, unless one of the listed exceptions applies, such as a refusal to sign, insufficient identification, or a prior failure to respond to a notice or summons.

This page is general information about Florida rules of criminal procedure and local practice. It is not legal advice and evaluates no charge. A person with a first appearance or arraignment ahead should speak with a licensed Florida attorney before that hearing.

## svc_bond_hearings_and_pretrial_release_lede

Release before trial in Florida is governed by three layers at once: a constitutional right with exceptions, statutory criteria a judge must weigh, and conditions that attach the moment a person walks out.

## svc_bond_hearings_and_pretrial_release_body

### The presumption and its exception

Florida Rule of Criminal Procedure 3.131(a) states that unless charged with a capital offense or an offense punishable by life imprisonment where the proof of guilt is evident or the presumption is great, every person charged with a crime or violation of a municipal or county ordinance is entitled to pretrial release on reasonable conditions. The same subdivision provides that where no conditions of release can reasonably protect the community from risk of physical harm to persons, assure the presence of the accused at trial, or assure the integrity of the judicial process, the accused may be detained.

### What the judge weighs

Florida Statute 903.046(1) states the purpose of a bail determination: to ensure the appearance of the defendant at subsequent proceedings and to protect the community against unreasonable danger from the defendant. Subsection (2) lists the criteria, including the nature and circumstances of the offense charged, the weight of the evidence, family ties, length of residence in the community, immigration status, employment history, financial resources and mental condition, and past and present conduct including any record of convictions, previous flight to avoid prosecution, or failure to appear.

That paragraph also carries a specific consequence for a missed court date. A defendant who failed to appear in the matter at issue but later voluntarily appeared or surrendered is not eligible for a recognizance bond. A defendant who failed to appear and was later arrested is not eligible for a recognizance bond or for any bond without a monetary undertaking of at least $2,000, or twice the value of the original bond, whichever is greater.

### Conditions attach automatically, and release can be revoked

Florida Statute 903.047(1) provides that as a condition of pretrial release, in whatever form, the defendant must refrain from criminal activity of any kind and, where the court issues an order of no contact, must refrain from any contact of any type with the victim except through pretrial discovery. That order is effective immediately, enforceable until modified by the court, and the defendant must be informed in writing of the specific prohibited acts before release.

Florida Statute 903.0471 provides that notwithstanding 907.041, a court may on its own motion revoke pretrial release and order detention where it finds probable cause that the defendant committed a new crime while on release, or materially violated a condition of release.

### The booking schedule is not the judge's decision

Administrative Order S-2025-063 of the Thirteenth Judicial Circuit adopts the statewide uniform bond schedule for Hillsborough County without change for the 2026 calendar year, and states that the schedule applies only to the booking officer and does not bind a judge. It excludes a long list of arrests from booking-officer release, including capital, life, first-degree and second-degree felonies, homicide, felony battery, domestic battery by strangulation, and domestic violence as defined in Florida Statute 741.28, along with stalking, robbery, burglary, carjacking, kidnapping, trafficking in a controlled substance, and violation of a protective injunction.

Administrative Order S-2024-030, effective February 9, 2024, establishes Criminal Division "O" for preliminary proceedings. The presiding judge conducts first appearance hearings on all criminal matters, hears motions concerning bond and release on recognizance in felony matters not yet assigned to a division, conducts adversary preliminary proceedings, and conducts all pretrial detention hearings, including mandatory hearings under Florida Statute 907.041(4)(d) and discretionary hearings under 907.041(4)(c). All Division "O" proceedings are conducted through audiovisual devices and electronically recorded, and misdemeanor bond and release motions are handled by the county criminal divisions.

This page is general information about Florida pretrial release statutes and local administrative orders. It is not legal advice and predicts no result. Anyone seeking release or a change in release conditions should have a licensed Florida attorney address the court, since only a court modifies a condition.

## svc_burglary_defense_lede

Burglary in Florida is not a synonym for theft. Florida Statute 810.02 defines it around entry and intent, and the degree turns on the kind of place entered, whether a person was inside, and whether a weapon or an assault was involved.

## svc_burglary_defense_body

### Entry and intent, not taking

Florida Statute 810.02(1)(b) defines burglary as entering a dwelling, structure, or conveyance with intent to commit an offense therein, unless the premises are at the time open to the public or the person is licensed or invited to enter. The same subsection reaches a person who remains after permission to remain has been withdrawn, or who remains surreptitiously with intent to commit an offense, or who remains with intent to commit a forcible felony as defined in Florida Statute 776.08. Nothing in the section requires that anything be taken.

Florida Statute 810.011 supplies the definitions that set the degree. A dwelling is a building or conveyance of any kind with a roof over it, designed to be occupied by people lodging therein at night, together with the enclosed space of ground and outbuildings immediately surrounding it. A structure is a building of any kind, temporary or permanent, with a roof over it, together with the curtilage. A conveyance includes a motor vehicle, ship, vessel, railroad car, trailer, aircraft, or sleeping car, and the statute provides that entering a conveyance includes taking it. Whether a screened porch, a fenced yard, or a detached shed falls inside one of those definitions changes the exposure.

### The degree structure

Subsection (2) makes burglary a felony of the first degree, punishable by a term of years not exceeding life imprisonment, where in the course of the offense the offender makes an assault or battery, or is or becomes armed within the premises with explosives or a dangerous weapon, or uses a motor vehicle as an instrumentality other than merely as a getaway vehicle and thereby damages the dwelling or structure, or causes damage to the premises or property within them exceeding $1,000.

Subsection (3) sets out the second-degree felony forms, including entering a dwelling whether occupied or not, an occupied structure, an occupied conveyance, an authorized emergency vehicle, or a structure or conveyance where the offense is theft of a controlled substance. Subsection (4) makes the offense a felony of the third degree where the offender makes no assault or battery, is not and does not become armed with a dangerous weapon or explosive, and enters a structure or a conveyance with no other person inside.

### The weapon enhancement

Florida Statute 775.087 reclassifies certain felonies where a weapon or firearm was carried, displayed, used, threatened, or attempted to be used during commission of the offense, moving a first-degree felony to a life felony, a second-degree felony to the first degree, and a third-degree felony to the second degree. The same statute imposes a mandatory minimum term of 10 years for actual possession of a firearm or destructive device during commission of listed felonies, with a 3-year minimum specified for burglary of a conveyance. A weapon allegation is therefore not an aggravating detail; it can change both the degree and the floor of a sentence.

### Local process

Administrative Order S-2024-030 of the Thirteenth Judicial Circuit assigns first appearance hearings, felony bond and release motions in matters not yet assigned to a division, adversary preliminary proceedings, and all pretrial detention hearings to Criminal Division "O," conducted by audiovisual device and electronically recorded. Administrative Order S-2025-063 excludes burglary arrests from booking-officer release under the uniform bond schedule and states that the schedule binds only the booking officer, not a judge.

This page is general information about Florida burglary statutes and local court procedure. It is not legal advice and analyzes no set of facts. A person facing a burglary charge should speak with a licensed Florida attorney about the specific subsection of 810.02 alleged.

## svc_domestic_violence_defense_lede

Florida treats an allegation between household members differently from an ordinary battery charge, and the difference is written into the statutes: a defined category of offense, mandatory sentence components, and release conditions that begin before any finding of fact.

## svc_domestic_violence_defense_body

### The definition decides which statutes attach

Florida Statute 741.28 defines domestic violence as any of a listed group of offenses committed by one family or household member against another. The list runs through assault and aggravated assault, battery and aggravated battery, sexual assault and sexual battery, stalking and aggravated stalking, kidnapping, and false imprisonment, and it closes with any criminal offense resulting in physical injury or death of a family or household member. The same statute defines "family or household member" to reach spouses, former spouses, persons related by blood or marriage, persons living together as if a family or who have done so in the past, and persons who are parents of a child in common regardless of marriage. Except for parents of a common child, the statute requires present or past residence together in the same single dwelling unit.

### The underlying offense and the strangulation statute

Florida Statute 784.03 defines battery as actually and intentionally touching or striking another person against that person's will, or intentionally causing bodily harm. A first battery is a misdemeanor of the first degree. The same statute makes a second battery a felony of the third degree where the person has one prior battery conviction, and it defines conviction to include a determination of guilt regardless of whether adjudication was withheld.

Florida Statute 784.041 covers domestic battery by strangulation, which reaches knowingly and intentionally impeding normal breathing or blood circulation of a family or household member, or a person in a dating relationship, by applying pressure to the throat or neck or blocking the nose or mouth, creating a risk of or causing great bodily harm. Subsection (3) makes it a felony of the third degree. No visible-injury threshold appears in that definition.

### Mandatory sentencing components

Two statutes remove discretion from part of a sentence. Florida Statute 741.283 requires, where a person is adjudicated guilty of a crime of domestic violence and intentionally caused bodily harm, a minimum term of 10 days in the county jail for a first offense, 15 days for a second, and 20 days for a third or subsequent offense, unless the court imposes a nonsuspended state prison sentence. Those minimums increase where the offense was committed in the presence of a child under 16 who is a family or household member. Florida Statute 741.281 requires a minimum of one year of probation with completion of a batterers' intervention program. Florida Statute 921.0024 adds a sentencing multiplier on the Criminal Punishment Code worksheet in the same child-present circumstance.

### Conditions apply immediately, and only a court changes them

Florida Statute 903.047 requires a person on pretrial release to refrain from criminal activity of any kind and, where the court issues an order of no contact, from any contact of any type with the alleged victim except through pretrial discovery. The statute makes that order effective immediately, enforceable until modified by the court, and requires written notice of the prohibited acts before release. The defined term includes indirect contact as well as oral and written communication. An invitation or a reconciliation from the other person does not lift it.

Administrative Order S-2025-063 of the Thirteenth Judicial Circuit excludes domestic violence arrests under 741.28 from booking-officer release under the uniform bond schedule, which is why release often waits for a judge. Florida Statute 741.30 governs injunctions for protection, a civil proceeding that can produce a second order binding the same person at the same time.

This page presents general information about Florida statutes and local procedure. It is not legal advice and reaches no conclusion about any allegation. A person facing this kind of charge, or subject to a no-contact order, should obtain advice from a licensed Florida attorney before taking any step.

## svc_drug_trafficking_defense_lede

In Florida, trafficking is a weight threshold rather than a description of selling. Florida Statute 893.135 attaches mandatory minimum terms of imprisonment and fixed fines once an alleged quantity crosses a line printed in the statute.

## svc_drug_trafficking_defense_body

### Weight decides the charge

Florida Statute 893.13(6)(a) makes simple possession of a controlled substance a felony of the third degree, and subsection (6)(b) makes possession of 20 grams or less of cannabis a misdemeanor of the first degree. Florida Statute 893.135 sits above that structure and operates on quantity. A person who knowingly sells, purchases, manufactures, delivers, or brings into this state, or who is knowingly in actual or constructive possession of, a listed substance at or above a stated threshold commits trafficking, which the statute designates a felony of the first degree.

The cannabis thresholds are stated in weight and plant count. In excess of 25 pounds, or 300 or more plants, carries a mandatory minimum term of 3 years and a fine of $25,000. In excess of 2,000 pounds, or 2,000 or more plants, carries a mandatory minimum of 7 years and a $50,000 fine. In excess of 10,000 pounds, or 10,000 or more plants, carries a mandatory minimum of 15 calendar years and a $200,000 fine.

The cocaine thresholds follow the same pattern. Twenty-eight grams or more but less than 200 grams carries a mandatory minimum of 3 years and a $50,000 fine. Two hundred grams or more but less than 400 grams carries 7 years and a $100,000 fine. Four hundred grams or more but less than 150 kilograms carries 15 calendar years and a $250,000 fine. At 150 kilograms or more the statute provides for imprisonment for life, with no eligibility for discretionary early release except as the statute allows. Further schedules in the same section cover other substances, each with its own numbers.

### Why a mandatory minimum changes the analysis

A mandatory minimum is a floor. Mitigation and the guideline calculation under Florida Statute 921.0024 operate above it rather than below it unless a statutory mechanism applies. Florida Statute 893.135 does contain a provision allowing the state attorney to move the sentencing court to reduce or suspend a sentence where substantial assistance is rendered, and 921.0024 notes a discretionary multiplier where the primary offense is drug trafficking at specified severity levels. Those are statutory exceptions with their own requirements, and neither is automatic.

### Quantity, mixture, and possession

Because the charge is defined by quantity, how quantity was measured is central. The statute speaks of the substance or a mixture containing it, so packaging material, moisture, and the composition of a mixture can all be contested, as can laboratory chain of custody and scale calibration. A difference of a gram is meaningless in ordinary speech and decisive at a statutory boundary.

The statute also reaches actual or constructive possession. Constructive possession is not a synonym for proximity; it involves knowledge of the presence of the substance and the ability to exercise dominion and control over it. In a shared vehicle, a shared residence, or a delivered package, that element is frequently the contested one.

### Search issues come first, and the local sequence

Administrative Order S-2025-063 of the Thirteenth Judicial Circuit adopts the statewide uniform bond schedule for Hillsborough County and excludes trafficking in a controlled substance from booking-officer release under it, while stating that the schedule does not bind a judge. Administrative Order S-2024-030 places first appearance hearings and all pretrial detention hearings in Criminal Division "O," conducted by audiovisual device. The Hillsborough County Clerk of the Circuit Court prepares the official court file after arrest and forwards it to the State Attorney, which decides whether formal charges are filed and on what counts.

This page is general information about Florida drug statutes and local procedure. It is not legal advice and assesses no allegation, quantity, or defense. Because 893.135 carries mandatory minimum terms, anyone facing such a charge should speak with a licensed Florida attorney without delay.

## svc_dui_defense_lede

A Tampa DUI arrest opens two separate clocks: a criminal charge under Florida Statute 316.193 and an administrative driver license action under Florida Statute 322.2615. They are decided by different bodies on different schedules, and one of them moves in days.

## svc_dui_defense_body

### What 316.193 requires the state to prove

Florida Statute 316.193(1) sets out the offense in parts. A person must be driving or in actual physical control of a vehicle within this state, and must either be under the influence of alcohol or a chemical or controlled substance to the extent that normal faculties are impaired, or register an unlawful alcohol level. The statute fixes that level at 0.08 or more grams of alcohol per 100 milliliters of blood, and, for breath, at 0.08 or more grams per 210 liters. "Actual physical control" is not a synonym for driving, and "impaired" is tied by the statute to normal faculties rather than to any single roadside observation.

### The penalty ranges written into the statute

For a first conviction, 316.193 sets a fine in the range of $500 to $1,000, and imprisonment that may not exceed 6 months. A second conviction carries a fine of not less than $1,000 and not more than $2,000, with imprisonment not exceeding 9 months, and requires an ignition interlock device for at least one year at the convicted person's own expense. Subsection (4) creates a higher band where the alcohol level was 0.15 or higher, or a person under 18 was in the vehicle: on a first conviction the fine becomes not less than $1,000 and not more than $2,000, with imprisonment up to 9 months. A fourth or subsequent violation is a felony of the third degree with a fine of not less than $2,000. DUI manslaughter carries a mandatory minimum term of 4 years. Subsection (6)(a) limits a first-conviction probation term to one year and requires a minimum of 50 hours of community service.

### The 10-day license clock

Florida Statute 322.2615 runs on its own track. An officer who lawfully arrests a driver and finds an unlawful alcohol level, or encounters a refusal, takes the license and issues a notice of suspension that also serves as a temporary permit for an eligible driver. The statute states that the permit expires at midnight of the tenth day after issue, and that a review may be requested from the department within that same 10-day window. Blood results, when they arrive later, are to be transmitted within 5 days. Suspension periods are fixed: 1 year for a first refusal and 18 months where the privilege was previously suspended for refusal; 6 months for a first unlawful alcohol level and 1 year where previously suspended on that basis. Day eleven closes the review avenue but does nothing to the criminal charge.

### Refusal as its own charge

Florida Statute 316.1939 makes a refusal a separate crime where probable cause, a lawful arrest, and a proper warning are established and the driver then refuses. A first refusal is a misdemeanor of the second degree; a second or subsequent refusal is a misdemeanor of the first degree. The statute also provides that the administrative license proceeding does not determine the criminal charge.

Documents issued at the roadside and at booking carry the dates that matter: the citation with its statute subsection, the notice of suspension and temporary permit, any breath result printout with time stamps, and the bond paperwork with its conditions.

This page is general information about Florida law and Hillsborough County procedure. It is not legal advice and evaluates no individual charge. Anyone arrested for driving under the influence should speak with a licensed Florida attorney promptly, because the license deadline is measured in days.

## svc_expungement_and_record_sealing_lede

Sealing and expunction in Florida are two different remedies with two different eligibility statutes, one shared application process through the Department of Law Enforcement, and a statutory limit of one court-ordered outcome in a lifetime.

## svc_expungement_and_record_sealing_body

### Which statute applies depends on the disposition

Florida Statute 943.0585 governs court-ordered expunction. A person is eligible to petition where no indictment, information, or other charging document was filed or issued, or where such a document was filed but was dismissed or nolle prosequi by the state attorney or statewide prosecutor, or dismissed by a court of competent jurisdiction, or where a judgment of acquittal was rendered by a judge or a verdict of not guilty was rendered by a judge or jury. The statute also requires that the record not be ineligible under Florida Statute 943.0584, and that the person has never been adjudicated guilty in this state of a criminal offense, or adjudicated delinquent in this state for a felony or one of the listed misdemeanors, unless that delinquency record was expunged under Florida Statute 943.0515.

Florida Statute 943.059 governs court-ordered sealing. It applies to records not ineligible under 943.0584 where the person has never, before the date the application for a certificate of eligibility is filed, been adjudicated guilty in this state of a criminal offense or adjudicated delinquent for a felony or one of the enumerated misdemeanors. Those enumerated offenses include assault, battery, carrying a concealed weapon under Florida Statute 790.01(2), and open carrying of a weapon under Florida Statute 790.053, among others.

Florida Statute 943.0584 lists records ineligible for either remedy and defines conviction to include a plea of guilty or nolo contendere regardless of whether adjudication was withheld.

### Automatic sealing exists but is narrow

Florida Statute 943.0595 provides for automatic sealing of certain records. This is not the same relief as a court-ordered sealing, and it does not consume the one court-ordered outcome described below.

### The certificate of eligibility comes first

The Florida Department of Law Enforcement describes the certificate of eligibility as the first step for either court-ordered remedy, and states that a person is entitled to one court-ordered sealing or expungement in a lifetime. The application must be notarized or signed before a deputy clerk. It requires a certified disposition for each charge, a completed fingerprint form or card bearing the agency identifier, and, for an expunction application, a certified written statement from the appropriate state attorney or statewide prosecutor confirming the record meets the statutory criteria.

The department states a nonrefundable processing fee of $75.00, payable by money order, cashier's check, or personal check made out to the Florida Department of Law Enforcement, matching the $75 processing fee written into 943.0585. It states that processing typically takes 12 weeks from receipt of a complete package and cannot be expedited, that a record is not relieved until the department receives a certified copy of the court order, and that granting a petition remains at the sole discretion of the court even where a certificate has issued.

### Where the petition is filed

Administrative Order S-2024-030 of the Thirteenth Judicial Circuit assigns petitions to seal and expunge in matters with no division assignment to Criminal Division "O." Where a division was assigned, that division ordinarily handles the petition. The Hillsborough County Clerk of the Circuit Court maintains the court file, and its published guidance notes that on-site file retrieval can take several business days, which matters when certified dispositions are needed.

A sequence follows: confirm the disposition of every charge, check the ineligibility list, obtain certified dispositions and the prosecutor's statement where expunction is sought, complete the fingerprint requirement, submit the application with the fee, wait for the certificate, then file the petition in the correct division.

This page is general information about Florida record relief statutes and Department of Law Enforcement procedure. It is not legal advice and determines no eligibility. Anyone seeking to seal or expunge a Florida record should consult a licensed Florida attorney, since the statute allows only one court-ordered outcome in a lifetime.

## svc_felony_vs_misdemeanor_in_florida_lede

The difference between a felony and a misdemeanor in Florida is defined by where a sentence may be served, and that single distinction drives the penalty ceiling, the limitation period, and which department of the court handles the file.

## svc_felony_vs_misdemeanor_in_florida_body

### The statutory definitions

Florida Statute 775.08(1) defines a felony as any criminal offense punishable under Florida law, or that would be punishable if committed in this state, by death or by imprisonment in a state penitentiary, and states that a sentence imposed on conviction of a felony does not exceed one year unless served in a state penitentiary. Subsection (2) defines a misdemeanor as any criminal offense punishable by a term of imprisonment in a county correctional facility not in excess of one year. Subsection (3) defines a noncriminal violation as an offense for which the only penalty may be a fine, forfeiture, or other civil penalty, and states that it does not mean any conviction for a criminal offense.

### The ceilings

Florida Statute 775.082 sets the terms of imprisonment. A felony of the first degree carries a term not exceeding 30 years, or, where specifically provided by the statute defining the offense, a term of years not exceeding life imprisonment. A felony of the second degree carries a term not exceeding 15 years, and a felony of the third degree a term not exceeding 5 years. A misdemeanor of the first degree carries a definite term not exceeding 1 year, and a misdemeanor of the second degree a definite term not exceeding 60 days.

Florida Statute 775.083 sets the fine ceilings, which apply in addition to any term of imprisonment unless otherwise provided: $15,000 for a life felony or a felony of the first degree in the circumstances stated, $10,000 for a felony of the first or second degree, $5,000 for a felony of the third degree, $1,000 for a misdemeanor of the first degree, and $500 for a misdemeanor of the second degree or a noncriminal violation.

### Sentencing is calculated

Florida Statute 921.0024 sets out the Criminal Punishment Code worksheet. Sentence points are assigned for the primary offense by severity level, for additional offenses by level and count, and for victim injury at the graduated levels the worksheet lists, with a separate prior record score. The worksheet adds points where the offender possessed a firearm during a qualifying felony, and applies multipliers in circumstances the statute names, including domestic violence in the presence of a child under 16 who is a family or household member as defined in Florida Statute 741.28.

### Limitation periods and lasting consequences

Florida Statute 775.15 provides that a prosecution for a capital felony, a life felony, or a felony that resulted in a death may be commenced at any time. Otherwise, and except as the statute provides elsewhere, a prosecution for a felony of the first degree must be commenced within 4 years, a prosecution for any other felony within 3 years, a prosecution for a misdemeanor of the first degree within 2 years, and a prosecution for a misdemeanor of the second degree or a noncriminal violation within 1 year. The statute states that an offense is committed when every element has occurred, or, where a legislative purpose to prohibit a continuing course of conduct plainly appears, when that course of conduct is terminated, and that time starts to run on the day after the offense is committed.

Classification also reaches collateral matters. Florida Statute 790.23 makes possession of a firearm or ammunition unlawful for a person convicted of a Florida felony.

This page offers general information about Florida sentencing classification statutes. It is not legal advice and predicts nothing about any charge or sentence. Anyone facing a Florida charge should have a licensed Florida attorney review the specific degree and subsection alleged.

## svc_juvenile_defense_lede

Delinquency proceedings in Florida run under chapter 985, which has its own detention rules, its own hearing clocks, and its own routes by which a child can instead be prosecuted as an adult.

## svc_juvenile_defense_body

### Detention rests on statutory findings and a risk instrument

Florida Statute 985.24 states that all determinations and court orders regarding the use of detention care must be based primarily on findings that the child presents a substantial risk of not appearing at a subsequent hearing, presents a substantial risk of inflicting bodily harm on others as evidenced by recent behavior including the illegal possession or use of a firearm, presents a history of committing a property offense before adjudication, disposition, or placement, or has committed contempt of court in the ways the statute lists.

Statute 985.25 describes detention intake: the department receives custody, reviews the law enforcement report or probable cause affidavit, and makes the initial placement decision under that instrument. The same statute requires secure detention until the detention hearing where the child meets the prolific juvenile offender criteria in Florida Statute 985.255(1)(f) or is charged with an offense involving possession or use of a firearm.

### The detention clocks

Florida Statute 985.26(1) provides that a child may not be placed into or held in detention care longer than 24 hours unless the court orders it, and the order must include specific instructions directing release in accordance with Florida Statute 985.255.

Subsection (2) adds limits on supervised release detention care. A court may order it until an adjudicatory hearing is completed, but where a child has served 60 days on supervised release detention care, the court must hold a hearing within 15 days after the 60th day to determine the need for continuing it, and continuation requires good cause shown.

### Adjudicatory hearings and adult prosecution

Florida Statute 985.35 provides that the adjudicatory hearing be held as soon as practicable after the petition is filed, in accordance with the Florida Rules of Juvenile Procedure, with reasonable delay granted for investigation, discovery, or procuring counsel or witnesses, and with the time limits of 985.26 applying to a detained child. Where the child is a prolific juvenile offender, the hearing must be held within 45 days after the child is taken into custody unless the child requests a delay. Subsection (2) states that these hearings are conducted without a jury, applying the rules of evidence used in criminal proceedings.

Florida Statute 985.557 permits discretionary direct filing of an information. For a child who was 14 or 15 at the time of the alleged offense, the state attorney may file an information where, in the state attorney's judgment and discretion, the public interest requires that adult sanctions be considered or imposed, and where the offense falls within an enumerated list including arson, sexual battery, robbery, kidnapping, aggravated child abuse, aggravated assault, aggravated stalking, murder, manslaughter, and specified armed burglary offenses under Florida Statute 810.02. Once an information is filed, adult penalty statutes apply.

### Records: fingerprints and diversion expunction

Florida Statute 985.11 requires that a child charged with or found to have committed an offense that would be a felony if committed by an adult be fingerprinted, with the prints submitted to the Department of Law Enforcement, and it lists further offenses requiring fingerprinting unless a prearrest delinquency citation was issued.

Florida Statute 943.0582 provides for diversion program expunction, directing the Department of Law Enforcement to adopt rules for expunging a nonjudicial arrest record of a minor who has successfully completed a qualifying diversion program under the listed sections of chapter 985 or a program to which a state attorney referred the minor.

This page presents general information about Florida chapter 985 procedure. It is not legal advice and reaches no conclusion about any child. A parent or guardian of a child accused of a delinquent act should consult a licensed Florida attorney promptly, particularly where direct filing is possible.

## svc_probation_violation_defense_lede

A violation of probation proceeding is not a fresh prosecution with the same protections. Florida Statute 948.06 allows arrest without a warrant on reasonable grounds, and release is not guaranteed.

## svc_probation_violation_defense_body

### How the proceeding starts

Florida Statute 948.06 provides that where a probation officer, any law enforcement officer, or any other person has reasonable grounds to believe a probationer or offender in community control has violated the terms and conditions of supervision in a material respect, that officer may arrest, or request a county or municipal law enforcement officer to arrest, the probationer without warrant, wherever found, and return the person to the court that granted supervision. The statute also allows the committing judge, on receipt of an affidavit setting forth the violation, to issue a warrant or, alternatively, a notice to appear.

### First appearance on a new-law violation

The same statute addresses first appearance where the violation rests on a new criminal offense. The court is required to inform the person of the violation. Where the person admits it, the court may order the person transferred to the court that granted supervision. Where the person does not admit it, the court may commit the person or release the person with or without bail to await further hearing, notwithstanding Florida Statute 907.041, and the statute permits the court to consider whether the person is more likely than not to receive a prison sanction.

The phrase "notwithstanding s. 907.041" is the important part. The ordinary presumption favoring pretrial release does not operate the same way, which is why release in a violation proceeding is often harder than release on the original charge.

### Pretrial release can be revoked on a separate track

Florida Statute 903.0471 provides that notwithstanding 907.041, a court may, on its own motion, revoke pretrial release and order pretrial detention where it finds probable cause to believe the defendant committed a new crime while on release, or materially violated a condition of release. Florida Statute 903.047 sets the conditions attaching to release itself, including refraining from criminal activity of any kind and complying with any order of no contact, which is effective immediately and enforceable until modified by the court. A person may therefore be exposed on two tracks at once, each decided in its own proceeding.

### The conditions themselves, and the records that answer them

Florida Statute 948.03 provides that the court determines the terms and conditions of probation, and it lists conditions treated as standard that do not require oral pronouncement at sentencing. The list begins with reporting to the probation officer as directed, and the statute notes that reporting may be fulfilled remotely where approved by the relevant probation officer or county probation authority.

Because a technical violation and a new-law violation are handled differently, the wording of the affidavit is the starting point. A missed appointment, a failed test, an unpaid cost, a change of address, or a program not completed each tie back to a specific written condition, and the condition as ordered governs rather than a recollection of instructions.

Administrative Order S-2024-030 of the Thirteenth Judicial Circuit assigns first appearance hearings on all criminal matters to Criminal Division "O," conducted by audiovisual device and electronically recorded, along with bond and release motions in felony matters not yet assigned to a division. Where supervision was granted by an assigned division, that division ordinarily retains the matter. The Hillsborough County Clerk of the Circuit Court mails notices to the address on file, which is why its forms for designating a current mailing and e-mail address matter to anyone under supervision who has moved.

This page provides general information about Florida probation statutes and local court procedure. It is not legal advice and evaluates no alleged violation. Anyone facing a violation of probation or community control should obtain advice from a licensed Florida attorney before making any statement about the allegation.

## svc_robbery_defense_lede

Robbery under Florida Statute 812.13 is a taking accomplished by force, violence, assault, or putting in fear. Every degree in that statute is a felony, and the presence or absence of a weapon moves the exposure dramatically.

## svc_robbery_defense_body

### The definition and its timing rules

Florida Statute 812.13(1) defines robbery as taking money or other property which may be the subject of larceny from the person or custody of another, with intent to permanently or temporarily deprive, when in the course of the taking there is use of force, violence, assault, or putting in fear. Subsection (3) then defines when an act counts. An act is in the course of committing the robbery if it occurs in an attempt to commit robbery or in flight after the attempt or commission. An act is in the course of the taking if it occurs before, at the same time as, or after the taking, provided it and the taking form a continuous series of acts or events.

That timing language explains why a struggle occurring after property has already changed hands can be charged as robbery rather than theft, and why the minute-by-minute sequence matters more here than in a simple theft allegation.

### The three degrees

Subsection (2)(a) provides that carrying a firearm or other deadly weapon in the course of the robbery makes the offense a felony of the first degree, punishable by imprisonment for a term of years not exceeding life. Subsection (2)(b) provides that carrying a weapon makes it a felony of the first degree. Subsection (2)(c) provides that carrying no firearm, deadly weapon, or other weapon makes it a felony of the second degree. Under Florida Statute 775.082, a first-degree felony carries a term not exceeding 30 years, or a term of years not exceeding life where specifically provided, and a second-degree felony a term not exceeding 15 years. The distinction between deadly weapon, weapon, and no weapon is therefore the difference between three statutory outcomes.

### Sudden snatching and home-invasion robbery

Florida Statute 812.131 defines robbery by sudden snatching as taking property from the victim's person, with the same intent element, where in the course of the taking the victim was or became aware of it. That statute expressly states it is not necessary to show force beyond the effort needed to obtain possession, or any resistance offered or injury inflicted.

Florida Statute 812.135 defines home-invasion robbery as entering a dwelling with intent to commit a robbery and committing a robbery of the occupants therein, and makes it a felony of the first degree punishable by a term of years not exceeding life where the offender carried a firearm or other deadly weapon. These are separate statutes with separate elements, and the charging document shows which one the state selected.

### The enhancement layer

Florida Statute 775.087 operates on top of the robbery statute, reclassifying a first-degree felony to a life felony, a second-degree felony to the first degree, and a third-degree felony to the second degree where a weapon or firearm was carried, displayed, used, threatened, or attempted to be used during commission of a felony, unless such use is already an essential element. The same statute sets mandatory minimum terms for actual possession of a firearm or destructive device during commission of enumerated felonies, including robbery, and a longer minimum where a firearm was discharged.

Administrative Order S-2024-030 of the Thirteenth Judicial Circuit places first appearance hearings and all pretrial detention hearings, including mandatory hearings under Florida Statute 907.041(4)(d), in Criminal Division "O." Administrative Order S-2025-063 excludes robbery and carjacking arrests from booking-officer release under the uniform bond schedule.

This page provides general information about Florida robbery statutes and local procedure. It is not legal advice and offers no evaluation of any allegation. A person facing a robbery charge should speak with a licensed Florida attorney, particularly where a firearm allegation triggers a mandatory minimum.

## svc_sex_crime_defense_lede

Florida's sexual offense statutes carry the harshest penalty structures in the criminal code, along with registration duties that begin quickly and record consequences the statutes make permanent.

## svc_sex_crime_defense_body

### The statutes and their degrees

Florida Statute 794.011 governs sexual battery, defined by reference to specified acts committed without consent. Subsection (3) provides that a person who commits sexual battery upon a person 12 years of age or older, without consent, and in the process uses or threatens to use a deadly weapon, or uses actual physical force likely to cause serious personal injury, commits a life felony. Subsection (5)(a) provides that a person 18 or older who commits sexual battery upon a person 12 or older but younger than 18, without consent, and who does not use physical force and violence likely to cause serious personal injury, commits a felony of the first degree. Subsection (5)(b) makes the same conduct against a person 18 or older a felony of the second degree.

Florida Statute 800.04 addresses lewd or lascivious offenses committed upon or in the presence of persons less than 16 years of age, separated into defined categories. Lewd or lascivious battery under subsection (4) covers engaging in sexual activity with a person 12 or older but less than 16, and is ordinarily a felony of the second degree, with a first-degree felony provision in the circumstances stated in paragraph (c). Lewd or lascivious molestation under subsection (5) covers intentional touching in a lewd or lascivious manner of specified areas, or the clothing covering them, of a person less than 16; where an offender 18 or older commits it against a victim less than 12, the statute makes it a life felony.

Subsection (3) of that statute states a rule that surprises people: the perpetrator's ignorance of the victim's age, the victim's misrepresentation of age, and the perpetrator's bona fide belief about age cannot be raised as a defense in a prosecution under the section.

### Registration runs on a 48-hour clock

Florida Statute 943.0435 sets registration requirements for persons meeting the statutory definition of a sexual offender. On initial registration, the statute requires reporting in person at the sheriff's office in the county where the person establishes or maintains a permanent, temporary, or transient residence within 48 hours after establishing residence in this state or after release from the custody, control, or supervision of the Department of Corrections, or in the county of conviction within 48 hours after conviction for a qualifying offense where the person is not in custody or under supervision. The statute then specifies the information required and the schedule for later reporting and for reporting changes. These obligations are statutory and run independently of any supervision imposed at sentencing.

### Record relief is closed by statute

Florida Statute 943.0584 lists criminal history records ineligible for court-ordered expunction or court-ordered sealing, and the list includes sexual misconduct as defined in the referenced statutes along with a series of other offenses. The same statute defines conviction for this purpose as a determination of guilt resulting from trial or from a plea of guilty or nolo contendere, regardless of whether adjudication is withheld, and, for a minor, a finding of a delinquent act regardless of whether adjudication of delinquency is withheld. A withheld adjudication therefore does not restore eligibility.

### Pretrial posture

Administrative Order S-2024-030 of the Thirteenth Judicial Circuit assigns all pretrial detention hearings, including mandatory hearings under Florida Statute 907.041(4)(d) and discretionary hearings under 907.041(4)(c), to Criminal Division "O," with overflow capital and life dangerous-crime detention hearings assigned among the felony trial divisions.

This page contains general information about Florida statutes and local court procedure. It is not legal advice, evaluates no allegation, and does not state that any statute applies to any person. Anyone under investigation for or charged with a sexual offense in Florida should obtain advice from a licensed Florida attorney immediately and make no statement before doing so.

## svc_theft_and_shoplifting_defense_lede

Florida theft charges turn on a dollar figure, a property type, and a prior record. The same physical act can be a second-degree misdemeanor or a felony depending on which line of Florida Statute 812.014 the alleged value falls into.

## svc_theft_and_shoplifting_defense_body

### The value tiers in 812.014

Theft of property not otherwise specified is petit theft of the second degree, a misdemeanor of the second degree. Where the property is valued at $100 or more but less than $750, the offense becomes petit theft of the first degree, a misdemeanor of the first degree. Grand theft of the third degree, a felony of the third degree, begins at $750 and runs to $5,000, with further bands at $5,000 to $10,000 and $10,000 to $20,000. Grand theft of the first degree, a felony of the first degree, applies where the property is valued at $100,000 or more, where cargo valued at $50,000 or more has entered the stream of commerce, or where the offender used a motor vehicle as an instrumentality other than merely as a getaway vehicle and damaged real property, or caused damage to property of another exceeding $1,000.

### Prior convictions change the degree

Under 812.014(3)(b), a person who commits petit theft with one prior theft conviction commits a misdemeanor of the first degree. Under 812.014(3)(c), two or more prior theft convictions make a petit theft a felony of the third degree. The statute says "any theft," without requiring that the prior offense be similar in kind or value. That is the mechanism by which a low-value allegation reaches a felony docket, and it is why the accuracy of an alleged prior record is worth checking against certified dispositions rather than a summary.

### Retail theft is its own statute

Florida Statute 812.015 governs retail and farm theft and supplies its own definitions, including retail theft, merchandise, and antishoplifting or inventory control device. Subsection (8) makes retail theft a felony of the third degree where a person coordinates the activities of one or more individuals in committing the offense, which may occur through multiple acts, and the amount of each individual theft is aggregated within a 120-day period to a value of $750 or more. The same subsection reaches a conspiracy to commit retail theft with intent to sell the property, using the same aggregation window. The statute also provides that a second or subsequent petit theft from a merchant is punished under 812.014(3) and carries a fine of not less than $50 and not more than $1,000, or public service instead of the fine. Florida Statute 812.0155 permits driver license suspension as an alternative sentence for a person under 18.

### An arrest is not a filed charge

The Hillsborough County Clerk of the Circuit Court describes a sequence in which the jail forwards initial paperwork, the Clerk prepares the official court file, and the State Attorney then decides whether to file formal charges. The Clerk states that notice of an arraignment date is mailed to the address given at arrest, that a bail bond agent is notified where applicable, and that the department cannot change a court date. Forms are published for designating a current mailing and e-mail address. Petit theft appears among the examples of matters handled by the Clerk's county criminal department, where an arraignment date may instead appear on a notice to appear.

Because value is an element, the documents supporting it are evidence: the arrest affidavit with the alleged value and subsection, any loss prevention report, price documentation, the property receipt, and written video preservation requests.

This page offers general information about Florida theft statutes and local filing procedure. It is not legal advice and assesses no allegation. Anyone charged with theft or retail theft should consult a licensed Florida attorney about the exact subsection charged.

## svc_warrants_and_turning_yourself_in_lede

A Florida arrest warrant is a written command signed by a judge. Knowing what it must contain, and what a failure to appear adds to an existing charge, is the starting point for anyone who believes one exists.

## svc_warrants_and_turning_yourself_in_body

### What a warrant must contain

Florida Rule of Criminal Procedure 3.121(a) sets the requirements. An arrest warrant must be in writing and in the name of the State of Florida, set forth substantially the nature of the offense, command that the person against whom the complaint was made be arrested and brought before a judge, and specify the name of the person to be arrested or, where the name is unknown, designate the person by any name or description permitting identification with reasonable certainty, including a photograph where reasonably available. It must state the date when issued and the county where issued, and be signed by the judge with the title of office, with electronic signature permitted in the circumstances the rule describes. Subdivision (a)(7) requires that for offenses where a right to bail exists, the warrant set the amount of bail or other conditions of release, and the return date.

Subdivision (b) provides that no arrest warrant is dismissed, and no person in custody discharged, because of a defect as to form, and that the judge may amend the warrant to remedy such a defect.

### Arrest without a warrant

Florida Statute 901.15 lists the circumstances in which a law enforcement officer may arrest without a warrant. Florida Rule of Criminal Procedure 3.125 addresses the alternative, a notice to appear issued in lieu of physical arrest for a misdemeanor of the first or second degree, a violation, or an ordinance triable in the county, subject to the exceptions in the rule. Where an arrest occurs without a warrant, the nonadversary probable cause determination in Florida Rule of Criminal Procedure 3.133 applies, requiring that determination before a judge within 48 hours from the time of arrest for a defendant in custody, with only the limited extraordinary-circumstance continuances the rule permits.

### Failing to appear is a separate offense

Florida Statute 843.15 provides that a person released under chapter 903 who willfully fails to appear before any court or judicial officer as required forfeits any security given, and commits a felony of the third degree where released in connection with a felony charge or pending appeal after conviction of any offense, or a misdemeanor of the first degree where released in connection with a misdemeanor charge. Florida Statute 901.31 addresses failure to obey a written promise to appear: a person who willfully fails to appear as required by a written notice to appear may be fined not more than the fine of the principal charge or imprisoned up to the maximum sentence of imprisonment of the principal charge, or both, regardless of the disposition of the charge on which the person was originally arrested.

### The first hours, and verifying what exists

Administrative Order S-2024-030 of the Thirteenth Judicial Circuit assigns first appearance hearings for all criminal matters to Criminal Division "O," conducted through audiovisual devices and electronically recorded, along with felony bond and release motions in matters not yet assigned to a division.

The Hillsborough County Sheriff's Office publishes arrest records online and states that the data reflects only the circumstances of an arrest, bears no connection to guilt, innocence, or dismissal, and may not reflect charging decisions by the State Attorney. It provides an error correction form for factual errors and a separate process requiring fingerprinting where identity theft is claimed, and it states that final dispositions come from the Clerk of the Circuit Court. An online listing is a starting point, not a legal record.

This page presents general information about Florida warrant and failure-to-appear statutes and local administrative orders. It is not legal advice and assesses no warrant. Anyone who believes a warrant exists should consult a licensed Florida attorney before taking any action.

## svc_weapons_charge_defense_lede

Florida weapons charges are spread across chapter 790, and they are not interchangeable. Carrying, open carrying, exhibition, possession during a felony, and possession after a felony conviction are separate statutes with separate degrees.

## svc_weapons_charge_defense_body

### Carrying under 790.01 and 790.053

Florida Statute 790.01 sets the framework for concealed carry. Subsection (2) makes carrying a concealed weapon or electric weapon or device a misdemeanor of the first degree where the statute's conditions are not met, and subsection (3) makes carrying a concealed firearm, as defined in Florida Statute 790.001, a felony of the third degree in the circumstances described. Subsection (4) matters to the prosecution's burden: in any prosecution under either subsection, the state bears the burden of proving, as an element, both that the person is not licensed under Florida Statute 790.06 and that the person is ineligible to receive and maintain such a license under the criteria listed there.

Florida Statute 790.053(1) makes it unlawful, except as otherwise provided by law and in subsection (2), for any person to openly carry on or about the person any firearm or electric weapon or device. The same subsection states that briefly and openly displaying a firearm to the ordinary sight of another person is not a violation for someone carrying as authorized in 790.01(1), unless the display is intentional and made in an angry or threatening manner and not in necessary self-defense.

### Exhibition and possession during a felony

Florida Statute 790.07 addresses persons engaged in a criminal offense while having weapons. Subsection (1) makes it a felony of the third degree for a person, while committing or attempting to commit any felony or while under indictment, to display, use, threaten, or attempt to use any weapon or electric weapon or device, or to carry a concealed weapon. Subsection (2) makes it a felony of the second degree where a firearm is involved instead. Subsection (3) lists offenses excluded from the section.

### Possession after a felony conviction

Florida Statute 790.23 makes it unlawful for a person to own, or to have in the person's care, custody, possession, or control, any firearm, ammunition, or electric weapon or device, or to carry a concealed weapon, where the person has been convicted of a felony in Florida, along with the other categories the statute lists, including a person found in Florida to have committed a delinquent act that would be a felony if committed by an adult and who was under 24 at the time of the finding. Subsection (3) makes a violation a felony of the second degree. Subsection (4) raises it to a felony of the first degree, punishable by a term of years not exceeding life, for a person who has previously qualified or currently qualifies for the penalty enhancements in Florida Statute 874.04.

### The enhancement layer and the paperwork

Florida Statute 775.087 reclassifies felonies upward where a weapon or firearm was carried, displayed, used, threatened, or attempted to be used during commission of a felony, except where such use is already an essential element, and it sets mandatory minimum terms for actual possession of a firearm during listed felonies, specifying a 3-year minimum for possession of a firearm by a person convicted of a felony.

Because these statutes turn on definitions, the details recorded at the scene are the substance of the charge: the arrest affidavit with the precise subsection, the property receipt describing the item, where the item was at the time of the encounter, license documentation and its dates, and any recorded statement about ownership. Administrative Order S-2024-030 of the Thirteenth Judicial Circuit assigns motions for return of a firearm where no charges have been filed to Criminal Division "O."

This page contains general information about Florida weapons statutes. It is not legal advice and does not assess whether any statute applies to any set of facts. A person charged under chapter 790 should consult a licensed Florida attorney about the exact subsection alleged.

## svc_white_collar_and_fraud_defense_lede

Florida fraud charges are usually built by aggregation. Amounts, victims, and transactions are added together under statutes that convert a series of smaller allegations into a first-degree felony.

## svc_white_collar_and_fraud_defense_body

### Organized fraud and its value tiers

Florida Statute 817.034, the Florida Communications Fraud Act, sets out organized fraud. A person who engages in a scheme to defraud and obtains property thereby commits organized fraud, punishable as a felony of the first degree where the property obtained has an aggregate value of $50,000 or more; a felony of the second degree where the aggregate value is $20,000 or more but less than $50,000; and a felony of the third degree where the aggregate value is less than $20,000. The statute separately provides that a person who engages in a scheme to defraud and, in furtherance of it, communicates with any person with intent to obtain property from that person commits an offense for each such communication.

is the operative word. Transactions that would individually be minor may be combined, and the degree follows the total the state alleges rather than any single event.

### The White Collar Crime Victim Protection Act

Florida Statute 775.0844 defines white collar crime to include commission of, or a conspiracy to commit, any felony offense specified in a list of chapters, among them chapter 560 relating to the Money Transmitters' Code, chapter 812 relating to theft and robbery, and chapter 815 relating to computer-related crimes. It then defines aggravated white collar crime as engaging in at least two white collar crimes that share intents, results, accomplices, victims, or methods of commission, or that are otherwise interrelated by distinguishing characteristics and are not isolated incidents, provided at least one occurred after the effective date of the act.

Subsection (5) provides that a person who commits an aggravated white collar crime and thereby victimizes 10 or more elderly persons as defined in Florida Statute 825.101, 10 or more veterans, 20 or more persons, or the State of Florida or one of its agencies or political subdivisions, and thereby obtains or attempts to obtain $50,000 or more, commits a felony of the first degree. Subsection (6) provides that notwithstanding chapter 921 or any other law, an aggravated white collar crime is ranked at offense severity level 9.

### Identity information charges

Florida Statute 817.568 addresses criminal use of personal identification information. Subsection (2)(a) makes it a felony of the third degree to willfully and without authorization fraudulently use, or possess with intent to fraudulently use, personal identification information concerning another person without that person's consent. Subsection (2)(b) raises the offense to a felony of the second degree where the pecuniary benefit, value of services received, payment avoided, or amount of injury or fraud is $5,000 or more, or where the number of individuals whose information was used meets the statutory threshold.

### Documents, timing, and the investigative stage

Fraud allegations are documentary by nature, which cuts both ways. The state's theory rests on records: statements, transfers, invoices, contracts, correspondence, ledgers, and access logs.

These matters often begin before an arrest. Administrative Order S-2024-030 of the Thirteenth Judicial Circuit assigns state attorney investigative subpoena matters to Criminal Division "O," along with first appearance hearings and petitions to seal and expunge in matters with no division assignment. Florida Statute 775.15 sets the limitation periods: 4 years for a felony of the first degree, 3 years for any other felony, 2 years for a misdemeanor of the first degree, and 1 year for a misdemeanor of the second degree, with time starting on the day after the offense is committed.

Anyone contacted by investigators about a fraud allegation in Florida should speak with a licensed Florida attorney before responding.
