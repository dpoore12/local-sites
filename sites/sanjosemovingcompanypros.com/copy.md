## pricing_body

### Two numbers govern a local California move

The first is the hourly maximum. Maximum Rate Tariff 4, Item 320, effective January 1, 2025, caps straight-time charges on a move of one hundred miles or less. Santa Clara County falls in Territory A, where the ceiling is $142.01 an hour for a vehicle and one person, $228.01 for a vehicle and two, and $81.80 for each additional person. Time and a half caps at $181.84 and $307.65; double time at $228.01 and $387.55.

The second is the written agreement. Before service begins the mover completes an Agreement for Moving Services showing a Not to Exceed Price, raised only by a bona fide written change order. It cannot be preprinted and cannot be waived.

Note what these rules do not do: they cap the hourly rate, not the number of hours. Set the posted market rates in the table against the $228.01 ceiling and the ceiling binds almost nothing here. Competition sets the hourly figure in this valley. The tariff's real value is the Not to Exceed Price and the definition of billable time.

### Double drive time, explained once and properly

Item 36 defines chargeable time on a local move as loading plus unloading plus double the actual driving time between origin and destination. A twenty-five-minute run from Willow Glen to Santa Clara bills as fifty minutes. It is not a mover's invention but the state's way of paying a carrier for the empty return leg.

On a short in-town hop the doubled drive is trivial. On a job to Livermore or Santa Cruz, still inside the hundred-mile band, it adds over an hour of billing, so comparing rates without asking how travel is computed misjudges the total badly.

### Why this is the most expensive of the three metros to move in

Freight and material movers in the San Jose, Sunnyvale and Santa Clara metro had a median wage of $22.63 an hour in May 2025 across 11,830 workers, per the BLS Occupational Employment and Wage Statistics, May 2025 release. That is roughly four dollars above the same occupation in Orlando or Raleigh, and it flows straight into a crew rate. On top of the wage sit state requirements: the Bureau of Household Goods and Services charges $500 to file for a household mover permit, $15 quarterly thereafter, and requires combined single limit coverage of not less than $600,000 with $20,000 of cargo protection per shipment.

Then there is access. Downtown high-rises need a service elevator reserved, and a truck in a metered or restricted space needs a tow-away permit the city advises requesting well in advance. Both burn crew hours no rate card mentions.

### The cash rate, and what it really is

Most companies here publish two hourly figures, cash and card, with the card figure five to fifteen dollars higher and sometimes a processing percentage on top. A three-hour minimum at a card rate of $129 is $387, plus five percent processing, before doubled travel time is added. The same three hours at a $119 cash rate is $357.

### Reading an agreement here

Look for the Not to Exceed Price completed in writing, the Cal-T number, crew size, the hourly rate and the payment method it assumes, the minimum hours, and how travel is calculated under Item 36. Valuation should be named: sixty cents per pound released value is the default and it is not insurance. Packing, materials, storage, specialty handling and parking permits belong on the document as separate lines rather than appearing verbally on load day.

### What the online number leaves out

Published job totals assume elevator access or a short carry, a truck that can legally park near the door, and a boxed household. They exclude packing labor, cartons billed by the piece, shrink wrap, disassembly, appliance disconnection, a storage month between a closing and a key handover, and the parking permit itself. Cross a state line and this tariff stops applying, because federal estimate rules take over.

## svc_local_moving_lede

A household move that begins and ends in California is priced under a state tariff almost nobody reads. It fixes the hourly ceiling, defines which minutes are billable, and requires a written price cap signed before the first carton leaves the room.

## svc_local_moving_body

### The agency changed, and old advice still points at the wrong one

For decades this work was licensed by the Public Utilities Commission. Senate Bill 19, signed on October 2, 2017, ended that. Oversight of household movers moved to what is now the Bureau of Household Goods and Services, inside the Department of Consumer Affairs, on [July 1, 2018](https://bhgs.dca.ca.gov/about_us/index.shtml). The commission's own notice of the handoff is still posted, and it points consumers to the bureau for anything involving a moving company ([CPUC](https://www.cpuc.ca.gov/industries-and-topics/transportation/transfer-of-authority-of-household-goods)). So a checklist telling you to look up your mover with the utilities commission is out of date by years. The bureau licenses the companies, publishes the rate tariff, and takes the complaints.

### What the hourly ceiling in Territory A actually says

Item 320 of Maximum Rate Tariff 4 caps straight-time charges on a shipment traveling one hundred miles or less. This county sits in the tariff's Territory A, where the maximum is $142.01 an hour for a vehicle and one person and $228.01 for a vehicle and two, with $81.80 for each person past the second. Overtime has its own ceilings: $307.65 for a two-person crew at time and a half and $387.55 at double time ([tariff](https://bhgs.dca.ca.gov/forms_pubs/20240101_maxtariff.pdf)). Read that as a ceiling and nothing more. Local competition sets the figure you will actually be quoted, and it usually lands well underneath.

### Double drive time, and how the minutes get rounded

Item 36 defines the billable clock as loading, plus unloading, plus twice the driving time from origin to destination. Send a crew from Rose Garden to Evergreen and the twenty-two-minute run bills as forty-four. Two exceptions matter. On a job needing more than one trip, only the first trip is doubled and later trips bill at actual time. When two households ride on one truck, each is charged twenty-five minutes of driving.

Fractions are rounded on a published table rather than by the crew chief. Anything over seven minutes but not over twenty-two becomes a quarter hour, over twenty-two becomes a half, over thirty-seven becomes three quarters, and over fifty-two rounds to the full hour. Start and stop times must be written on the shipping document to the minute.

### The document that turns a quote into a ceiling

Item 128 requires the Agreement for Moving Services to reach you no less than three days before moving day, already signed by the mover and bound to the quoted rates. The Not to Exceed Price and the mover's final signature go on no later than the day itself, but before any service begins. Item 130 forbids that figure from being preprinted and requires it to be set in good faith on your specific circumstances. Added work needs a Change Order for Services under Item 120, signed by both sides before that work happens. The bureau is blunt about the limit: a change order cannot be used simply because a mover [underestimated the job](https://bhgs.dca.ca.gov/consumers/movers.shtml).

### What can be demanded at the new address

Under Item 104 a mover may collect before releasing the load, and a credit card payment counts as cash. It also says the crew must unload once you pay the Not to Exceed Price plus any valid change orders, whether or not the company believes more is owed. On that payment the carrier's lien is extinguished and the goods must be released. No lien ever attaches to food, medicine, medical devices, items that treat or assist a person with a disability, or items used to care for a minor child. Freight bills have to be presented within seven calendar days of delivery, and anything overcollected is refundable within ten days.

### Verify the permit before the deposit clears

Item 88 requires the bureau-issued CAL-T number on stationery, shipping documents, and printed advertising, so the same number should appear on the ad, the agreement, and the freight bill. Run it through the license search on the bureau's site. The stakes are not theoretical: a company operating without a valid permit can be cited up to $5,000 per offense, and under Business and Professions Code section 19237 it cannot enforce a security interest or sue you for the money ([law book](https://bhgs.dca.ca.gov/laws/hhm_law_book_eff_1_1_26.pdf)). The tariff adds that an unlicensed mover has no right to withhold your belongings for any reason at all.

### What actually moves the total across town

Crew size, hours, the doubled drive, stairs, long carries from the truck, packing, and materials. An hourly rate is easy to compare between companies and the number of hours is not, which is why the honest conversation is about the day rather than the rate. Almaden Valley and Evergreen addresses stretch the doubled drive; a Japantown or Downtown curb adds permit work before the truck can legally stand still.

## svc_long_distance_moving_lede

Cross the state line and California's rate ceiling stops applying. The load becomes federally regulated household freight, with a different estimate form, a different rule about what can be collected on delivery day, and a claim clock that starts when the truck arrives.

## svc_long_distance_moving_body

### Two authorities, two licenses, one truck

Business and Professions Code section 19237 sets both halves of the test. Work inside California needs a permit from the state bureau; goods moving into or out of California need valid operating authority from the Federal Motor Carrier Safety Administration ([law book](https://bhgs.dca.ca.gov/laws/hhm_law_book_eff_1_1_26.pdf)). The bureau issues two different licenses to match. A General Household Mover license covers in-state work, including companies that also handle loads crossing the line, while an Interstate Mover license covers a company that only moves goods into or out of the state ([BHGS](https://bhgs.dca.ca.gov/licensee/hhm_faqs.shtml)). Ask which one is on file and whether the company brokers the job or carries it.

### Look up the USDOT number, then read the bill of lading

The federal handbook is specific about paperwork. A bill of lading has to list the names, telephone numbers, addresses, and USDOT numbers of the motor carriers known to be handling the shipment ([FMCSA](https://www.fmcsa.dot.gov/sites/fmcsa.dot.gov/files/2023-10/FMCSA_R%26R_Handbook_Web_v1.pdf)). It also walks a consumer through searching a carrier record by USDOT or MC number. That matters most when a household books through a broker, because the company answering the phone may not be the company that shows up. Save the search result next to the estimate.

### Binding or non-binding decides delivery day

A binding estimate obliges the carrier to deliver on payment of the full estimate, plus additional-service and impracticable-operations charges capped at fifteen percent of all other charges due at delivery. A non-binding estimate is a projection, and no more than one hundred ten percent of it may be required before the load comes off. The remainder is billed later. Refusing to release goods after the correct payment is described in plain federal language as holding a shipment hostage in violation of law. If items are added before loading, the parties either reaffirm the estimate in writing, replace it, or convert it.

### The charges that arrive a month after the truck leaves

Federal rules let a carrier bill unforeseen destination conditions after thirty days from delivery, and the examples given are elevators, stairs, and required parking permits. Households leaving Silicon Valley for a city they have never seen tend to plan the origin carefully and guess at the destination. Ask what the receiving address looks like before the shipment weighs in: how far the truck can park from the door, whether a full-size trailer can turn into the street, and whether a shuttle will be needed.

### The nine-month clock, and the carrier's two deadlines

A written claim for loss or damage must be filed within nine months of delivery, or from the date a shipment that never arrived should have been delivered. The carrier then has thirty days to acknowledge the claim and one hundred twenty days to issue a disposition, with sixty-day extensions available only on written notice explaining the delay. Note exceptions on the delivery paperwork while the driver is present. For a California-licensed company, the state bureau accepts complaints as well, and its consumer page repeats the same nine-month written deadline.

### Enforcement is a joint operation now

In July 2026 the state bureau reported a sweep across the Bay Area and Southern California aimed at movers working without a license or proper insurance. It produced thirty citations totaling $110,230, including two federal citations at $39,615 each ([DCA](https://www.dca.ca.gov/newsroom/2026/bhgs/20260709_bhgs_targets_unlic_movers.pdf)). The release explains why the federal figures appear on a state action: the bureau receives tips and licensing data from the federal agency and the authority to issue federal citations. A company that will not name its authority is a company two regulators can already see.

### Timing a departure from this valley

Outbound moves are not a niche here. Census QuickFacts puts 85.9 percent of Santa Clara County residents in the same house a year earlier across the 2019 to 2023 period, which leaves roughly one resident in seven having moved within twelve months. The same table shows the county estimated at 1,926,325 people on July 1, 2024, slightly below its April 2020 base of 1,936,278 ([Census](https://www.census.gov/quickfacts/fact/table/santaclaracountycalifornia/POP060210)). Long-haul capacity out of the region tightens around month ends and school calendars, so a fixed lease end deserves an early booking and a documented delivery spread.

## svc_apartment_moving_lede

An apartment job in this city is a permissions problem before it is a lifting problem. The building controls the elevator and the paperwork it wants from the crew, the city controls the pavement, and neither one grants the other's approval.

## svc_apartment_moving_body

### Three separate approvals, and the property only owns one

Managed buildings here typically want a reserved freight elevator window, protection in the shared corridors, and a certificate of insurance naming the property before a crew is admitted. Those are private rules, set by the owner, and they vary building to building. What they cannot do is reserve the street. Curb space belongs to the city, which is why a confirmed elevator slot and a legal loading space are two errands rather than one. Ask management for the current move packet, in writing, before you agree to a date with anyone.

### The pavement has a published price list

The city's fee schedule, in the resolution effective July 1, 2025, prices a Temporary Tow-Away Zone Permit under municipal code section 11.56.040C at $57.00 per permit. Metered frontage costs more: lost meter revenue runs $4.00 per day per meter or $8.00 per day per smart meter, and removing hardware to clear the space is $101.00 per meter head and $247.00 per pole. A portable storage container left in the right of way is its own permit at $219.00 per unit, and a half-street closure is $1,687.00 ([fee resolution](https://sanjose.legistar.com/View.ashx?M=F&ID=14252328&GUID=163405CC-D6A6-4B37-B767-B987BBCFF67A)). Timing is the harder constraint. The transportation department recommends submitting a tow-away application at least two weeks ahead and says permit processing takes two business days or more ([city permits](https://www.sanjoseca.gov/your-government/departments-offices/transportation/permits)).

### Why an unreserved elevator turns into a line item

Federal rules for interstate loads name elevators, stairs, and required parking permits as conditions a carrier may bill for after thirty days from delivery ([FMCSA](https://www.fmcsa.dot.gov/sites/fmcsa.dot.gov/files/2023-10/FMCSA_R%26R_Handbook_Web_v1.pdf)). On a shipment staying inside California the mechanism differs but the money moves the same way. Extra service outside the signed agreement needs a Change Order for Services, signed before the work happens, and a crew standing in a lobby is still on the clock under Item 36 of the state tariff. Waiting is billable. Getting the elevator in writing is the cheapest thing on this list.

### Measure the route, not the apartment

Tape measure work beats optimism. Check the elevator's interior depth and door opening, the corridor turns, and the stairwell width against a sectional sofa, a wardrobe, a mattress, and a desk. Downtown high-rises and the older Japantown walk-ups fail in different places, one at the elevator and the other at a landing. Where a piece will not make the turn, learn that while a different plan is still possible.

### If you are being displaced rather than choosing to move

This is where a local move stops being a logistics question. Roughly 38,000 apartments in buildings of three or more units, built and occupied before September 7, 1979, are covered by rent stabilization, which allows one increase of 5 percent in any twelve-month period ([Rent Stabilization](https://www.sanjoseca.gov/your-government/departments-offices/housing/tenants/learn-about-rent-stabilization)). Separately, the Tenant Protection Ordinance lists thirteen just causes for ending a tenancy and requires the landlord to serve written notice, include a cause, hand over the Resources and Referral Notice, and send the City a copy within three days.

Five of those causes are landlord decisions rather than tenant conduct: substantial rehabilitation, withdrawal under the Ellis Act, owner move-in, a code enforcement order to vacate, and converting an unpermitted unit. When one of those is the reason, relocation benefits are owed at $6,925 for a studio, $8,400 for a one bedroom, $10,353 for a two bedroom, and $12,414 for a three bedroom ([Notices to Vacate](https://www.sanjoseca.gov/your-government/departments-offices/housing/tenants/learn-about-notices-to-vacate)).

### Ellis Act withdrawals run on a longer calendar

Where an owner is taking the apartments off the rental market entirely, every household gets a minimum of 120 days' notice, and residents over 62, disabled residents, terminally or catastrophically ill residents, and households with school-aged children may extend that to a year. Base relocation assistance matches the figures above, with qualified assistance adding $2,770 to $4,966 depending on unit size ([Ellis Act Ordinance](https://www.sanjoseca.gov/your-government/departments-offices/housing/tenants/learn-about-notices-to-vacate/ellis-act-ordinance)). A displaced household should confirm the benefit before spending it on a mover, because the money and the moving date are usually decided by different people.

### What sets the total on a building job

Floor count, elevator or stairs, the walk from the legal parking space to the door, permitted hours, crew size, and whether a permit had to be posted at the curb. Two identical inventories, one in Willow Glen with a driveway and one in a Downtown tower with a two-hour freight window, are not the same day of work.

## svc_packing_services_lede

Packing is quoted apart from the hourly move, and the state tariff prices it two different ways. It also decides how much a broken item is worth, because the liability election and the packing plan get signed on the same afternoon.

## svc_packing_services_body

### The tariff prices packing per container or per packer

Item 340 of Maximum Rate Tariff 4 sets both methods. Per container in Territory A, a dish-pack or barrel is $55.55 with packing labor at $50.01, a three cubic foot carton is $20.69 with $18.20 of labor, a six cubic foot carton is $28.05 with $25.28, and a corrugated container built for mirrors, paintings, or a glass tabletop is $48.32 with $43.53. Unpacking is priced separately and runs lower. The alternative is hourly: $91.12 per packer per hour at straight time, $134.28 at time and a half, and $177.02 at double time ([tariff](https://bhgs.dca.ca.gov/forms_pubs/20240101_maxtariff.pdf)). Which method applies has to be agreed before the work starts.

### Two notes in that item are worth money

Note 6 says a container charge already includes the dividers, paper, tape, and labels used to pack and seal it, and that no additional charge may be made for those materials. So a separate materials line stacked on top of a per-carton rate deserves a question. Note 8 is better still: no charge is assessed for wardrobes on a shipment moving at the Item 320 hourly rates. Hanging clothes are one of the few things in a move the tariff hands you for free, and plenty of households pay for wardrobe cartons anyway.

### A price for packing cannot come off the phone

State rules are strict about how an estimate is produced. A moving company may put an estimate in writing only after a visual inspection of what is being moved, and the bureau states plainly that verbal or internet estimates are illegal and may not be enforceable ([BHGS](https://bhgs.dca.ca.gov/consumers/movers.shtml)). Where charges are weight-based, the estimator measures cubic feet from a published table and multiplies by seven pounds per cubic foot to reach an approximate weight. Kitchens, garages, and a wall of books are exactly where a sight-unseen guess goes wrong, so the inspection is in your interest more than the mover's.

### Base liability is 60 cents a pound, and that is not insurance

Item 136 bases the tariff's transportation rates on a declared value of $0.60 per pound per article. The bureau's own booklet does the arithmetic: a twenty-pound article is worth $12.00 under that basis. Unless you write a different figure in your own handwriting on the agreement, maximum liability defaults to $20,000 of actual cash value for the shipment.

Two upgrades are priced in the same item. Actual Cash Value protection costs $1.09 for each $100 of declared value and pays depreciated value. Full Value protection costs $2.14 per $100 and obliges the mover to repair, replace, or pay the replacement cost. Deductibles cut the rate: accept the first $250 of a claim and it falls to $0.65 per $100, or $0.37 per $100 at a $500 deductible. Storage in transit carries its own lower valuation rates, and no deductible applies where a documented claim establishes that articles were lost or never delivered.

### Photograph the contents, then keep the claim window in mind

Written claims for loss or damage have to be filed within nine months of delivery, and the bureau tells consumers to photograph or record their belongings before the move so a dispute has evidence behind it. Do that room by room, close in on anything valuable, and keep the images somewhere that is not in a carton. Marking a problem on the delivery paperwork while the crew is still present is worth more later than a phone call the following week.

### What stays out of the cartons entirely

Passports, immigration documents, prescriptions, jewelry, house and car keys, backup drives, tax records, and cash belong in your own vehicle. So does anything the household needs on the first night. Aerosols, propane cylinders, fuel, paint, solvents, and pool chemicals do not ride with household goods at all, so plan their disposal before packing day rather than during it.

### What packing costs in this market, and what changes it

Room count is a weak predictor. Density is the real driver: a two bedroom in the Rose Garden with a full china cabinet and a garage of tools can outrun a larger, emptier house. Fragile-only work on glass, art, lamps, and electronics costs a fraction of a full pack and removes most of the breakage risk. Whatever the scope, get the method, the container count, the labor basis, and the valuation election written on the agreement before the tape gun comes out.
