### How the work is done

The air handler is opened and a negative-air machine, essentially a powerful vacuum with its own filtration, is connected to the main trunk near it, so the whole duct system is pulled toward the machine while the work goes on. The returns get the same treatment, and they matter most, because the return side is where pollen, pet hair and attic dust collect before the filter. The air handler cabinet and the blower wheel are cleaned, and any access holes cut into the trunk are closed with metal patches and sealed. A fresh filter goes in at the end.

## svc_dryer_vent_cleaning_lede

Dryer vent cleaning in Marietta for side-wall, roof and long upstairs runs: lint cleared from the drum to the outside hood, the transition hose checked, and airflow measured when the job is done.

## svc_dryer_vent_cleaning_body

A dryer vent is not part of the heating and cooling system, and cleaning it is not about air quality. It is about drying time, energy, and the lint that packs into the run and sits next to a hot appliance. It is often booked on the same visit as a duct cleaning because the same company does both, but it is a separate job with a separate price.

### The signs

Towels that come out damp after a full cycle. A laundry room that gets noticeably hot while the dryer runs. A dryer that shuts itself off before the cycle ends. Lint collecting around the outside hood, or a hood flap that no longer opens when the dryer is on. A smell of hot lint. Any one of those means the exhaust is restricted, and the dryer is working harder to push air through a run that is partly blocked.

### Why some Marietta runs clog faster

Short, straight runs out a side wall in a first-floor laundry room stay clear the longest. The harder ones are the runs in newer two-story houses with the laundry room upstairs, where the vent travels through the attic to a roof hood, and townhouses where the only way out is up. Every elbow and every foot of length slows the air and lets lint settle. In older ranch houses the run sometimes drops into the crawlspace and sags between supports, where lint and the damp crawlspace air combine into a wet plug at the low point. Birds also like to nest in dryer hoods in spring.

### What the work involves

The dryer is pulled out and disconnected. The transition hose between the dryer and the wall is checked first; a crushed foil or vinyl hose is replaced with semi-rigid metal duct, because the flimsy kinds trap lint and do not hold up to heat. A rotary brush is run through the vent from one or both ends, depending on access, while a vacuum collects what comes loose. The outside hood is cleared and checked to make sure the flap opens freely. Screens on a dryer hood are a problem rather than a fix, because they catch lint, so a hood with a screen gets flagged. Then the dryer is reconnected and run, and the airflow at the hood is checked to confirm the run is clear.

### When the run itself is the problem

Some vents cannot be made to work well by cleaning alone: a run that is far too long, has too many turns, or was built from the wrong material. A crushed section hidden in a wall, or a run that comes apart in the attic and dumps lint and moisture into the insulation, has to be repaired or rerouted. Those are priced separately once the problem is found, and you should see the photos before agreeing.

### How often

Clean the lint screen on the dryer every load, no exceptions. A household doing several loads a week with a long run to the roof should have the vent checked about once a year, and sooner if any of the signs above show up. A short side-wall run in a small household can usually go longer. The test is simple: if drying time creeps up, the vent is the first suspect.

## svc_hvac_coil_cleaning_lede

Evaporator coil, drain pan and blower cleaning in Marietta, for the musty smell when the AC starts, the coil that ices over, and the system that runs all afternoon without catching up.

## svc_hvac_coil_cleaning_body

The evaporator coil is the coldest, wettest part of a home's air conditioning. In Marietta it spends most of the year sweating, because its job is to pull water out of humid Georgia air. Dust that slips past the filter lands on that wet surface and stays. Over a few summers it mats into a layer that chokes airflow, holds moisture and feeds the growth that people smell for the first minute after the system starts. Cleaning the coil, the drain pan under it and the blower that pushes air across it is often the job that actually fixes what a homeowner thought was a duct problem.

### Why humidity makes this a Georgia job

University of Georgia Extension's guidance on preventing mold advises keeping indoor relative humidity below 60 percent, and ideally between 30 and 50. It lists air conditioning flaws, including an oversized unit, duct leaks, dirty coils and condensation, among the sources of excess moisture in a house. A dirty coil removes less water, the house feels clammy even at the set temperature, and the extra moisture settles into everything. The same guidance recommends running the fan on auto, not on, because a fan left running blows air across a wet coil after the compressor stops and puts that water back into the house.

### The signs

A musty or damp-sock smell when the air starts. Ice on the refrigerant line or the coil itself. Water in the secondary pan under an attic air handler, or a float switch shutting the system down. Weak airflow even with a clean filter. A system that runs through a July afternoon and never reaches the thermostat setting. Any of those points at the coil and blower before the ducts.

### What gets cleaned

Panels come off the air handler or the coil cabinet above the furnace. The coil faces are treated with a coil cleaner and rinsed at low pressure, so the thin aluminum fins are not bent flat. The primary drain pan is cleaned, and the condensate line is flushed so water leaves the way it should instead of backing up. The blower wheel comes out or is cleaned in place, because its curved blades collect a surprising load of dust and it is often the dirtiest part of the system. The cabinet is wiped down and the panels are sealed back. Some badly matted coils need to come out of the cabinet for a full cleaning, which is a bigger job and is priced separately before it starts.

### The attic air handler

Many upstairs systems around Marietta have the air handler in the attic, which means a clogged condensate line can put water through a bedroom ceiling. A secondary pan and a float switch are there to catch that, and both get checked on a coil visit. If the secondary pan already holds water, the primary drain has a problem that needs fixing now.

### What sanitizers can and cannot do

Fogging a sanitizer through the ducts gets sold hard on coil and mold calls. The EPA notes that no chemical biocide is currently registered for use inside internally insulated duct systems, and that research has not shown these products to be effective in duct cleaning. Growth on a coil comes back if the coil keeps getting wet and dirty. The fix is a clean coil, a draining pan, a sealed return and controlled humidity. UGA's guidance puts it plainly: finding and fixing the causes of excess moisture is one of the best ways to prevent mold growth.

### What it costs

Atlanta price lists treat an air handler, blower and coil cleaning as a modest addition when it is done with a duct cleaning, and sanitizer fogging as a separate line that should only appear with a documented reason. The pricing page shows both bands with their sources.

## svc_duct_sealing_repair_body

For a lot of Marietta houses the most useful thing a duct visit turns up is not dirt but a leak. ### What Georgia's code says about leaks

Georgia's energy code has required duct and envelope tightness verification on new homes since 30 June 2011, whenever any part of the duct system or the air handler sits outside the insulated envelope. As the rules were written in 2011, an existing system also had to pass the same test if more than half of it was modified, mastic was required on new or modified ductwork, and a straight equipment swap needed mastic on the plenum connections but no test. Under the current amendments the leakage target is 6 CFM per 100 square feet of conditioned floor area, measured by a certified verifier such as a HERS rater or a BPI analyst. A house built before mid-2011 was never held to any of that.

### The signs of leaky ducts

Upstairs or end-of-hall rooms that never reach the setting. Dark streaks on the ceiling around supply registers, where air leaks around the register boot. Dirty, discolored patches in the attic insulation along duct runs, which act like a filter for air leaking through a joint. A whistle at a return grille. A house that stays clammy in summer. ### What the work involves

It starts with a look at every run that can be reached in the attic, crawlspace and basement, and ideally a duct leakage test with a duct blower so the before-and-after can be measured. Pulled-apart flex is reconnected to its collar, strapped and sealed. Crushed or torn sections are replaced. Register boots are sealed to the drywall or ceiling around them, which stops the streaking. Seams on the plenums and around the air handler cabinet are sealed with mastic, the paste sealant Georgia's code calls for, rather than cloth duct tape that dries out and lets go. Returns built from framing cavities, such as a closet platform or a panned joist bay, are a common leak in houses of a certain age and get sealed or rebuilt in metal.

### Crawlspace runs

In a damp Marietta crawlspace, uninsulated metal supply ducts sweat in summer and drip onto the floor, and flex duct lying on the soil picks up moisture and pests. Lifting runs onto proper supports, sealing joints and checking the insulation jacket all reduce how much crawlspace air ends up in the living space. If the crawlspace itself is very wet, that is a drainage or vapor barrier problem for a different trade, and it should be named as one.

### Sealing first, then cleaning

When a system needs both, sealing usually comes first or on the same visit. There is little point cleaning a return that is still pulling attic air through a gap, and the cleaning crew's camera is often the thing that found the gap in the first place.

### What it costs

Sealing and repair are priced by what the inspection finds: how many joints, how much replacement duct, and whether a leakage test is included. That is quoted separately, with photos of each problem, before any of it is done. The pricing page covers the cleaning side of the visit, with market ranges and their sources.

## q_is_air_duct_cleaning_worth_it_lede

Sometimes, and less often than the ads suggest: a Marietta duct cleaning earns its cost when there is something specific in the ducts to remove, and it mostly wastes money when it is sold as a yearly habit or a cure for allergies.

## q_is_air_duct_cleaning_worth_it_body

### The short verdict

The EPA does not recommend cleaning ducts routinely, only as needed, and it says plainly that duct cleaning has never been shown to prevent health problems. It also says a cleaning done properly does no harm, so paying for one is a reasonable choice when there is a real reason. Whether it is worth it in a given house depends on what is actually inside the system and on whether whatever put it there has been fixed first.

### When the money is well spent

Three situations justify the cost on the EPA's own list. The first is substantial mold you can see growing inside hard metal ducts or on other parts of the system, confirmed by someone who knows what they are looking at. The second is rodents or insects living in the ductwork. The third is ducts so clogged with dust and debris that it puffs out of the supply registers. Add to that a house where drywall was sanded with the system running, and a move into a home whose previous owners kept several shedding pets. In each case there is a load of material to take out, and taking it out changes something.

### When it is mostly wasted

A dusty return grille on its own does not mean dirty ducts; the EPA says that is normal. Neither does a spring when everyone in the house is sneezing. In March 2025 Atlanta Allergy & Asthma counted 14,801 pollen grains per cubic meter in a single day, nearly ten times the 1,500 level classed as extreme. A house cleaned in the middle of a season like that refills within weeks. In Marietta the better order is to fix the filter fit first, wait for tree pollen to fall off, and only then decide whether the ducts still need attention.

### The leak question comes before the cleaning question

Georgia has required duct leakage testing on new systems since mid-2011, when ducts or the air handler sit in an unconditioned attic or crawlspace. Houses built before that were never tested, and many of them have return ducts pulling air from the attic or crawlspace through loose joints. That air brings insulation fibers, dust and humidity with it. Cleaning a leaky return buys a few clean months before the same leak dirties it again. Paying for [duct sealing and repair](../duct-sealing-repair/) on those joints is often the better use of the same budget, and a good inspection should tell you which problem you actually have.

### Humidity changes what is worth paying for

University of Georgia Extension guidance names oversized air conditioners, duct leaks, dirty coils and condensation among the sources of excess moisture in a house, and it suggests keeping indoor humidity below 60 percent. In a long Georgia cooling season the indoor coil and its drain pan stay wet for months, and that is where musty smells usually start. If the complaint is odor rather than dust, [HVAC coil cleaning](../hvac-coil-cleaning/) addresses the likelier source, and a duct-only job can leave it untouched.

### Older Marietta houses are a different job

The city's National Register districts, among them Whitlock Avenue and Northwest Marietta, are full of houses built long before central air. Their ductwork was added later and threaded around plaster walls, through closets and under floors, often in a mix of metal and flexible duct of different ages. In houses like these, finding where every run goes is part of the work, and a quote that assumes a simple modern layout is likely to change once a crew is inside.

### How to tell if a quote is worth it

NADCA, the industry's trade association, recommends cleaning the whole system: ducts, registers and grilles, the plenum, the blower motor and assembly, the coil, the drain pan and the heat exchanger. A price that covers only the supply vents leaves the dirtiest parts behind. NADCA also tells homeowners to steer clear of bargain whole-house specials, including the ones posted in neighborhood social media groups. Be wary of chemical add-ons too: the EPA says no biocide is registered for use in fiberglass duct board or fiberglass-lined ducts, and that duct sealants should be allowed only under unusual circumstances. The [pricing page](../pricing/) lists the sourced Marietta ranges for whole-system work, so a quote can be checked against something real before anyone opens a register. When there is a genuine reason, [air duct cleaning](../air-duct-cleaning/) done to that standard is the version worth paying for.

## q_how_often_should_air_ducts_be_cleaned_lede

There is no fixed calendar for it, whatever a mailer says; the honest schedule for a Marietta house is a yearly look at the system and a cleaning only when that look finds something worth removing.

## q_how_often_should_air_ducts_be_cleaned_body

### What the two main sources actually say

The EPA does not recommend cleaning ducts on a routine schedule, only as needed, and it lists duct cleaning outside of normal yearly maintenance. NADCA, the duct cleaning trade association, recommends having the system inspected at least once a year and cleaned as needed. Those two positions fit together better than they first appear: the interval that matters is the inspection, not the cleaning. Any number of years quoted as a rule, whether two, three or five, is a rule of thumb, and the EPA tells homeowners to be wary of anyone who sells cleaning as routine maintenance.

### What a yearly check should cover

A useful annual look goes past shining a flashlight into one register. It should cover the filter and whether air slips around it, the blower and its housing, the indoor coil and drain pan, and a sample of supply and return runs, including the main return. If those come up clean, the answer for this year is no cleaning. If they show heavy buildup, debris, water damage or pests, that is the point at which [air duct cleaning](../air-duct-cleaning/) belongs on the list.

### Things that move the next cleaning closer

NADCA names several conditions that shorten the gap: smokers in the house, residents with allergies or asthma, pets that shed heavily, water or other damage, and recent renovations. The EPA adds visible mold on hard surfaces inside the system, vermin, and dust blowing out of the supply registers. Moving into a house with an unknown history is a fair reason for a first inspection, since there is no record of what the previous owners did or did not do.

### How Marietta's seasons shape the timing

Timing matters as much as frequency here. Atlanta's tree pollen season set a record on 29 March 2025, when the count reached 14,801 grains per cubic meter. Cleaning in the middle of that brings no lasting benefit, because the house is pulling pollen back in through doors, windows and any leaky return. Late spring, once counts drop, or fall before the heating season starts, are the more sensible windows. Summer brings the other local pressure: long stretches of air conditioning keep the coil and drain pan wet, and University of Georgia Extension advises keeping indoor humidity below 60 percent to discourage mold. A musty smell in July is a reason to check the coil before it is a reason to clean the ducts, and [HVAC coil cleaning](../hvac-coil-cleaning/) may be the only work that is due.

### Houses that need checking more often

Two kinds of Marietta homes deserve a closer interval for inspection, if not for cleaning. The first is a house built before Georgia began requiring duct leakage tests on new systems in mid-2011, especially one with its air handler or returns in an attic or crawlspace, where loose joints pull dusty, humid air straight into the ducts. ### What keeps the interval long

The cheapest way to need fewer cleanings is to stop dust from getting in. ## q_mold_in_air_ducts_lede

Mold in air ducts does happen in Marietta, but far less often than a sales pitch implies: it usually begins at the cold, wet indoor coil, its drain pan, or a supply duct sweating in a hot attic or damp crawlspace, and it only needs professional removal once real growth is confirmed and the moisture feeding it has been stopped.

## q_mold_in_air_ducts_body

### What counts as mold, and who gets to say so

Plenty of dark dust inside a duct only looks like mold. The EPA says only an expert can make a positive determination, that a tape sample sent to a lab can settle it for roughly $50, and that homeowners should ask any contractor claiming mold to show it to them.

NADCA's 2021 ACR standard sorts indoor mold into three conditions taken from the IICRC S520 standard. Condition 1 is the normal background of settled spores in every Cobb County house. Condition 2 is spores spread from a growth site elsewhere. Condition 3 is actual growth. Only the last two are reasons to clean.

### Where it starts in a Cobb County system

Mold needs water, and in a Marietta air conditioner the water comes from the coil, which pulls moisture out of the house all through the long humid cooling season. The EPA calls condensation near the cooling coils a major factor in moisture contamination, and it treats standing water or debris in a drain pan as a problem needing immediate attention.

The EPA adds that an oversized unit short-cycles and removes moisture poorly, and that ducts in unconditioned space must be sealed and insulated or they will sweat. That describes a lot of local houses: air handlers in vented attics, supply runs across crawlspaces, and returns that were never leak-tested because the house went up before Georgia began requiring duct tightness checks in mid-2011.

### What to do first if you suspect it

The EPA's brief guide to mold gives the first step plainly: do not run the heating and cooling system if you know or suspect it is contaminated, because the blower can carry spores through every room. If a pan overflowed or a duct got soaked, dry the wet materials within 24 to 48 hours, which is the window the EPA gives for preventing growth. Then check indoor humidity with an inexpensive meter. The EPA and University of Georgia Extension both put the target below 60 percent, and ideally between 30 and 50.

### How it gets cleaned properly

Hard metal ducts with surface growth can be cleaned under negative pressure, with the debris leaving the house through a filtered collector. NADCA's standard says remediation should follow IICRC S520 alongside its own cleaning rules.

Fiberglass duct board, internal liner and flex duct insulation are different. The EPA says wet or moldy insulation should be removed and replaced, and NADCA recommends the same for porous parts with Condition 3 growth. A quote that promises to clean moldy duct board in place deserves a hard question.

The coil gets wet cleaning. Under the ACR standard, any suspect microbial growth on a coil or drain pan calls for Type 2 wet cleaning, not just a vacuum pass, and the pan and condensate line should drain freely when the job is finished. That work is covered on the [HVAC coil cleaning](../hvac-coil-cleaning/) page, and the duct side of it under [air duct cleaning](../air-duct-cleaning/).

### Sprays, foggers and sealants

Chemical treatment is where mold jobs get padded. The EPA notes that no biocide is currently registered for use in internally insulated duct systems, and that research has not shown these products to work in duct cleaning. Any antimicrobial belongs after mechanical cleaning, never instead of it. The EPA also says duct sealants should never be sprayed over wet liner, over actively growing mold, or over debris.

NADCA's post-job cleanliness check confirms surfaces are free of loose debris, and the standard says it does not determine microbial contamination. Visibly clean is not a mold clearance; after extensive growth, an independent indoor environmental professional should confirm the result.

### Marietta houses where it tends to show up

Three patterns account for most of the real cases here. Attic air handlers on second floors, where a slow condensate drain lets the primary pan stay wet for weeks. Crawlspace ductwork, especially flex duct lying on bare soil or uninsulated metal that drips through August. And the older homes in the city's historic districts, where central air was added long after the house was built and the runs pass through crawlspaces and closets that nobody inspects. In each, the leak or drainage problem is the real fault, and [duct sealing and repair](../duct-sealing-repair/) is what stops the mold from returning.

### What to ask before paying for mold work

Ask to see the growth from inside the run, and whether a lab sample was taken. Ask the contractor to name the moisture source and how it will be fixed, which components will be replaced rather than cleaned, and whether any chemical used is EPA-registered for that surface. Then compare the total with the sourced ranges on the [pricing page](../pricing/). A same-day mold finding that multiplies the bill is a reason to slow down.
