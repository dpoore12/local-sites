The returns get the same treatment, and they matter most, because the return side is where pollen, pet hair and attic dust collect before the filter. ## svc_dryer_vent_cleaning_lede

Dryer vent cleaning in Marietta for side-wall, roof and long upstairs runs: lint cleared from the drum to the outside hood, the transition hose checked, and airflow measured when the job is done.

## svc_dryer_vent_cleaning_body

A dryer vent is not part of the heating and cooling system, and cleaning it is not about air quality. It is about drying time, energy, and the lint that packs into the run and sits next to a hot appliance. It is often booked on the same visit as a duct cleaning because the same company does both, but it is a separate job with a separate price.

### The signs

Towels that come out damp after a full cycle. A laundry room that gets noticeably hot while the dryer runs. A dryer that shuts itself off before the cycle ends. Lint collecting around the outside hood, or a hood flap that no longer opens when the dryer is on. A smell of hot lint. Any one of those means the exhaust is restricted, and the dryer is working harder to push air through a run that is partly blocked.

### Why some Marietta runs clog faster

Short, straight runs out a side wall in a first-floor laundry room stay clear the longest. The harder ones are the runs in newer two-story houses with the laundry room upstairs, where the vent travels through the attic to a roof hood, and townhouses where the only way out is up. Every elbow and every foot of length slows the air and lets lint settle. In older ranch houses the run sometimes drops into the crawlspace and sags between supports, where lint and the damp crawlspace air combine into a wet plug at the low point. Birds also like to nest in dryer hoods in spring.

### What the work involves

The dryer is pulled out and disconnected. The transition hose between the dryer and the wall is checked first; a crushed foil or vinyl hose is replaced with semi-rigid metal duct, because the flimsy kinds trap lint and do not hold up to heat. A rotary brush is run through the vent from one or both ends, depending on access, while a vacuum collects what comes loose. The outside hood is cleared and checked to make sure the flap opens freely. Screens on a dryer hood are a problem rather than a fix, because they catch lint, so a hood with a screen gets flagged. Then the dryer is reconnected and run, and the airflow at the hood is checked to confirm the run is clear.

### When the run itself is the problem

Some vents cannot be made to work well by cleaning alone: a run that is far too long, has too many turns, or was built from the wrong material. A crushed section hidden in a wall, or a run that comes apart in the attic and dumps lint and moisture into the insulation, has to be repaired or rerouted. Those are priced separately once the problem is found, and you should see the photos before agreeing.

### How often

Clean the lint screen on the dryer every load, no exceptions. A household doing several loads a week with a long run to the roof should have the vent checked about once a year, and sooner if any of the signs above show up. A short side-wall run in a small household can usually go longer. The test is simple: if drying time creeps up, the vent is the first suspect.

## svc_hvac_coil_cleaning_lede

Evaporator coil, drain pan and blower cleaning in Marietta, for the musty smell when the AC starts, the coil that ices over, and the system that runs all afternoon without catching up.

## svc_hvac_coil_cleaning_body

The evaporator coil is the coldest, wettest part of a home's air conditioning. In Marietta it spends most of the year sweating, because its job is to pull water out of humid Georgia air. Dust that slips past the filter lands on that wet surface and stays. Over a few summers it mats into a layer that chokes airflow, holds moisture and feeds the growth that people smell for the first minute after the system starts. Cleaning the coil, the drain pan under it and the blower that pushes air across it is often the job that actually fixes what a homeowner thought was a duct problem.

### Why humidity makes this a Georgia job

University of Georgia Extension's guidance on preventing mold advises keeping indoor relative humidity below 60 percent, and ideally between 30 and 50. It lists air conditioning flaws, including an oversized unit, duct leaks, dirty coils and condensation, among the sources of excess moisture in a house. A dirty coil removes less water, the house feels clammy even at the set temperature, and the extra moisture settles into everything. The same guidance recommends running the fan on auto, not on, because a fan left running blows air across a wet coil after the compressor stops and puts that water back into the house.

### The signs

A musty or damp-sock smell when the air starts. Ice on the refrigerant line or the coil itself. Water in the secondary pan under an attic air handler, or a float switch shutting the system down. Weak airflow even with a clean filter. A system that runs through a July afternoon and never reaches the thermostat setting. Any of those points at the coil and blower before the ducts.

### What gets cleaned

Panels come off the air handler or the coil cabinet above the furnace. The coil faces are treated with a coil cleaner and rinsed at low pressure, so the thin aluminum fins are not bent flat. The primary drain pan is cleaned, and the condensate line is flushed so water leaves the way it should instead of backing up. The blower wheel comes out or is cleaned in place, because its curved blades collect a surprising load of dust and it is often the dirtiest part of the system. The cabinet is wiped down and the panels are sealed back. Some badly matted coils need to come out of the cabinet for a full cleaning, which is a bigger job and is priced separately before it starts.

### The attic air handler

Many upstairs systems around Marietta have the air handler in the attic, which means a clogged condensate line can put water through a bedroom ceiling. A secondary pan and a float switch are there to catch that, and both get checked on a coil visit. If the secondary pan already holds water, the primary drain has a problem that needs fixing now.

### What sanitizers can and cannot do

Fogging a sanitizer through the ducts gets sold hard on coil and mold calls. The EPA notes that no chemical biocide is currently registered for use inside internally insulated duct systems, and that research has not shown these products to be effective in duct cleaning. Growth on a coil comes back if the coil keeps getting wet and dirty. The fix is a clean coil, a draining pan, a sealed return and controlled humidity. UGA's guidance puts it plainly: finding and fixing the causes of excess moisture is one of the best ways to prevent mold growth.

### What it costs

Atlanta price lists treat an air handler, blower and coil cleaning as a modest addition when it is done with a duct cleaning, and sanitizer fogging as a separate line that should only appear with a documented reason. The pricing page shows both bands with their sources.

## svc_duct_sealing_repair_body

For a lot of Marietta houses the most useful thing a duct visit turns up is not dirt but a leak. A return duct with a gap in a vented attic pulls in air that is hot, dusty and full of insulation fibers. One in a crawlspace pulls in damp air that smells like soil. Supply ducts that leak dump cooled air into the attic instead of the bedrooms. Cleaning a leaky system makes it clean for a few months. Sealing it is what keeps it that way, and it usually makes the far rooms more comfortable as a side effect.

### What Georgia's code says about leaks

Georgia's energy code has required duct and envelope tightness verification on new homes since 30 June 2011, whenever any part of the duct system or the air handler sits outside the insulated envelope. As the rules were written in 2011, an existing system also had to pass the same test if more than half of it was modified, mastic was required on new or modified ductwork, and a straight equipment swap needed mastic on the plenum connections but no test. Under the current amendments the leakage target is 6 CFM per 100 square feet of conditioned floor area, measured by a certified verifier such as a HERS rater or a BPI analyst. A house built before mid-2011 was never held to any of that.

### The signs of leaky ducts

Upstairs or end-of-hall rooms that never reach the setting. Dark streaks on the ceiling around supply registers, where air leaks around the register boot. Dirty, discolored patches in the attic insulation along duct runs, which act like a filter for air leaking through a joint. A whistle at a return grille. A house that stays clammy in summer. Dust that comes back quickly no matter how often the ducts are cleaned. Flex duct that is lying in a pile, sagging between supports, or flattened under storage boxes.

### What the work involves

It starts with a look at every run that can be reached in the attic, crawlspace and basement, and ideally a duct leakage test with a duct blower so the before-and-after can be measured. Pulled-apart flex is reconnected to its collar, strapped and sealed. Crushed or torn sections are replaced. Register boots are sealed to the drywall or ceiling around them, which stops the streaking. Seams on the plenums and around the air handler cabinet are sealed with mastic, the paste sealant Georgia's code calls for, rather than cloth duct tape that dries out and lets go. Returns built from framing cavities, such as a closet platform or a panned joist bay, are a common leak in houses of a certain age and get sealed or rebuilt in metal.

### Crawlspace runs

In a damp Marietta crawlspace, uninsulated metal supply ducts sweat in summer and drip onto the floor, and flex duct lying on the soil picks up moisture and pests. Lifting runs onto proper supports, sealing joints and checking the insulation jacket all reduce how much crawlspace air ends up in the living space. If the crawlspace itself is very wet, that is a drainage or vapor barrier problem for a different trade, and it should be named as one.

### Sealing first, then cleaning

When a system needs both, sealing usually comes first or on the same visit. There is little point cleaning a return that is still pulling attic air through a gap, and the cleaning crew's camera is often the thing that found the gap in the first place.

### What it costs

Sealing and repair are priced by what the inspection finds: how many joints, how much replacement duct, and whether a leakage test is included. That is quoted separately, with photos of each problem, before any of it is done. The pricing page covers the cleaning side of the visit, with market ranges and their sources.
