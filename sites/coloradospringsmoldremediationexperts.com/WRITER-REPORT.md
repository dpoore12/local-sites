# Colorado Springs Mold Remediation — research and verification

Completed: 2026-08-22

## Local facts used

1. **No statewide mold-remediator licensing or registration program.** In 2025, Colorado HB25-1202 proposed a Mold Awareness and Registration Act that would have registered mold assessment and remediation providers, but the bill was lost. This means the site directs homeowners to judge a written scope, containment plan, moisture-source plan, and training or third-party certifications rather than a nonexistent state license lookup.  
   Official source: https://leg.colorado.gov/bills/hb25-1202

2. **Semi-arid Colorado Springs climate.** National Weather Service 1991–2020 normals list 15.91 inches of annual precipitation. The site uses this to explain why dry outdoor air should not rule out a localized indoor leak, roof entry, appliance connection, or damp below-grade area.  
   Official source: https://www.weather.gov/pub/climateCosDailyNormalsRecords

3. **Housing age mix.** City housing data lists a median construction year of 1986; the 2020–2024 consolidated plan says 42% of owner-occupied and 47% of renter-occupied homes were built before 1980, totaling more than 77,000 homes. The site uses this only to explain why water paths and access conditions must be assessed at the actual property.  
   Official sources: https://coloradosprings.gov/system/files/2025-07/hna_co_springs_factsheet_2025_07_10a_lv.pdf and https://coloradosprings.gov/system/files/2024-04/colorado_springs_2020-2024_conplan_amendment_3-comp.pdf

## Additional guidance used in copy

EPA says that, in most cases, a mold area smaller than about 10 square feet (roughly 3 by 3 feet) can be handled by a homeowner; larger areas, extensive water damage, contaminated water, or HVAC involvement need a more controlled approach.  
Official source: https://www.epa.gov/mold/mold-cleanup-your-home
