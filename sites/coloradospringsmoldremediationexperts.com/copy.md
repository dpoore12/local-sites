## pricing_body

### The number that splits every job in two

Square footage of visible growth, not the smell and not the anxiety, determines what happens next. Federal guidance in EPA mold cleanup in your home puts the first line at about 10 square feet, roughly a three-foot by three-foot patch. Below it, in most cases a homeowner can do the work with detergent, protective gear, and a fix to the water source. Above that, the same agency's guidance for larger buildings recommends limited containment between 10 and 100 square feet, and full containment above 100 square feet.

Those thresholds are why the table jumps rather than climbs. Below 10 square feet somebody wipes, cuts a small patch of drywall, and dries the cavity. Between 10 and 100 square feet the job acquires poly sheeting, a negative air machine, HEPA filtration, and a decontamination path, all rented by the day. Above 100 square feet the air is exhausted outside, crews wear respirators, and removal becomes a demolition scope with a rebuild behind it. The engineering controls changed, not the growth.

### Dry air is why the bill arrives late here

Colorado Springs averages near 16 inches of precipitation a year and outdoor humidity is genuinely low, which produces a false sense of safety. Growth here rarely announces itself on a wall surface. It sits somewhere wet for a reason unrelated to weather: a supply line weeping inside a wall, a water heater pan draining nowhere, a swamp cooler line, or snowmelt driven back under shingles. Interior air stays dry while the cavity behind the drywall stays damp.

The consequence is financial. By the time growth is visible in a basement here, the affected area is often past 10 square feet, because nothing prompted a look.

### There is no state license, so verify differently

Colorado does not license or register mold remediation contractors. The most recent attempt to change that, House Bill 25-1202, the Mold Awareness and Registration Act, was lost in the 2025 session. Nothing has replaced it. Practically, a certificate on a van proves training from a private body, not state authorization, and there is no state board to complain to about workmanship.

So verify what is checkable. Ask which written standard the work follows, and for the containment plan, the air machine count, and where exhaust goes. Ask whether the firm doing removal is also selling the clearance test. Ask for liability coverage with a pollution or mold endorsement, since general policies often exclude this work.

### The disclosure duty that surprises sellers

Anyone selling a house here signs the state seller's property disclosure, form SPD19-6-23, mandatory since January 2024. It asks directly about moisture and water and about the presence of mold or other biological hazards, and the form itself warns that failing to disclose a known adverse material fact can carry legal liability. That changes the arithmetic on a small job. A documented remediation with a clearance test is an asset in a transaction file, while an undocumented cleanup is a known fact with no paperwork behind it.

### What a fair scope looks like on paper

It names the source of water and who is fixing it, because remediation without that is a repainted problem. It states the affected area in square feet and the containment level chosen for it, which ties the price to the federal thresholds. It lists what material is leaving and how far past the visible edge cuts extend. It separates drying equipment by unit and by day, and says whether HEPA vacuuming and air scrubbing are included. It puts the rebuild in its own section, because drywall, texture, paint, trim, and flooring are construction rather than remediation, and pretending otherwise makes two very different prices look comparable.

### Where the money gets wasted

Fogging a chemical over growth still fed by an active leak. Ozone generators sold instead of removal. Whole-house air testing when one wall cavity is the question. And demolition wider than the moisture map justifies, which is easy to sell and hard to argue with later.

### Not included in any of these numbers

The plumbing or roof repair that stopped the water. Rebuilding finishes. Contents that cannot be cleaned. Independent clearance testing bought separately. Utility cost while dehumidifiers run for days, billed at the residential rates Colorado Springs Utilities publishes. And time: containment, drying, lab turnaround, clearance, and rebuild run weeks.

## svc_mold_removal_body

### The dry-climate assumption is the expensive part

Nobody here expects a moisture problem. The National Weather Service normal for annual precipitation is 15.91 inches, the yard is dry most of the year, and a faint earthy smell gets blamed on an old basement. That expectation is the whole problem. Federal guidance asks that wet materials be dried inside 24 to 48 hours, and that indoor relative humidity be held between 30 and 60 percent. A pinhole in a supply line, a water heater pan that drains nowhere, or a slow roof entry does not care what the outdoor dew point is doing. It wets gypsum, framing, and the paper facing on batt insulation continuously, and the first visible sign often arrives after the affected area has crossed the size threshold that changes the entire method.

### What square footage actually changes

The federal thresholds are the most useful numbers in this trade. Under about 10 square feet, roughly a three-foot by three-foot patch, a homeowner can generally do the work. Between 10 and 100 square feet, EPA recommends limited containment: a single layer of six-mil fire-retardant polyethylene, a slit entry with a covering flap outside it, and supply and return vents sealed off along with doors, chases, and risers. The enclosure is held under negative pressure by a HEPA-filtered fan exhausted out of the building. Above 100 square feet, the recommendation moves to full containment, with double layers of sheeting and a decontamination chamber built for entry and exit.

That is a step function, not a slope. It also is not purely arithmetic. The same guidance notes that heavy growth in a small area can release more spores than light growth across a large one, so the containment decision rests on professional judgment rather than a tape measure alone.

### The water sources this market actually produces

Five patterns cover most calls. An evaporative cooler adds moisture on purpose, and federal energy guidance is direct that these units use water continuously and that humidity builds indoors when windows are not open far enough. An overflowing pan or a leaking supply line at the roof curb then drains into a ceiling. Heavy spring snow is the second: the heaviest month in the local record is April 1957 at 42.7 inches, with April 1900 at 34.5 inches, so the wettest snow of the year lands when attic temperatures swing hardest. Hail is the third, and a bruised roof rarely leaks the week it is hit. Supply line and appliance connection failures are the fourth. Basements pulled slightly negative by dryers, range hoods, and bath fans are the fifth.

### Bleach, encapsulant, and fresh paint

Three shortcuts fail predictably. Chlorine bleach as a routine cleanup practice is not recommended by EPA, and its guidance adds that dead growth can still cause allergic reactions in some people, so killing is not the same as removing. Absorbent and porous materials, carpet and ceiling tile among them, may simply have to be discarded. And the guidance on cosmetics is blunt: do not paint or caulk moldy surfaces, because paint applied over them is likely to peel. The industry standard reads the same way, discouraging spraying products onto growth before the growth is physically removed and the surfaces are cleaned.

### There is no state license here, so read the scope instead

Colorado licenses a long list of trades through its Division of Professions and Occupations, more than 60 professions and occupations, including electrical, plumbing, and radon work. Mold assessment and remediation are not on that list. The state health department states plainly that it does not regulate mold, and the 2025 bill that would have created a registration program was lost. A written scope is what replaces the missing license. It should name the water source and who repairs it, state the affected square footage and the containment level chosen for it, and say who performs verification afterward. The consensus standard for this work distinguishes the remediator's own post-remediation evaluation from verification by an independent third party, and that distinction is worth asking about before anyone signs.

### What the visit looks like

Most growth is found by looking, with a flashlight and a nose doing more work than any instrument. From there the tools are unglamorous: a moisture meter that reads carpet, wallboard, wood, brick, and concrete, a humidity gauge, and sometimes a borescope pushed through a small drilled hole to see inside a cavity without opening it. Sampling is usually unnecessary when growth is visible, and there are no federal threshold limits for airborne spores to compare a result against, which is why lab work belongs to specific situations rather than every job.

### How the cost behaves

Price tracks containment, demolition, and drying days, so it jumps at the size thresholds instead of climbing smoothly. Rebuilding finishes is construction and belongs in its own section of the paperwork. Coverage is worth checking early, because the state insurance regulator's own buyer's guide lists mold among the common exclusions in homeowners policies.

## svc_black_mold_removal_body

### What the phrase describes and what it does not

"Black mold" is a description of appearance that people reach for when a stain looks serious. Dark growth on a bathroom ceiling, on a closet wall behind a bed, or along a baseboard could be several genera, and it is not identifiable by eye. Neither the color nor a species name changes the sequence: stop the water, contain the area, remove porous material that cannot be cleaned, clean what can be, dry the assembly, and confirm the result.

### What the federal agencies actually say about testing

This is where money gets wasted first. EPA states that in most cases, where growth is visible, sampling is unnecessary, and that no federal threshold limits have been set for spores in air. Its position is explicit that there are no EPA regulations or standards for airborne contaminants of this kind. CDC goes further and says it does not recommend testing at all, noting that there are no set standards for an acceptable quantity of any given type in a home, and that whatever type is present, it needs to be removed. A test result cannot tell you whether a house is compliant with a limit that does not exist.

### Health language, kept to what the agencies publish

Both agencies describe potential effects rather than diagnoses. EPA associates exposure with allergic reactions, asthma, and other respiratory complaints. CDC lists a stuffy nose, sore throat, coughing or wheezing, burning eyes, and skin rash for some people, notes that those with asthma or an allergy may react severely, and states that immune-compromised people and people with chronic lung disease may develop lung infections. Anyone worried about symptoms should raise it with a physician, not with a remediation crew. Nothing on this page is medical advice, and no reputable scope should be sold on a diagnosis.

### If you rent, the statute names it directly

Colorado's habitability law is unusually specific. A dwelling is deemed uninhabitable where there is mold associated with dampness, or another condition making the premises damp, that would materially interfere with a tenant's health or safety if left unremedied. Minor growth on surfaces built to get wet is excluded. Once the landlord has notice, the response clock is short. Contact with the tenant is due within 24 hours. Remedial action must commence within 24 hours where the condition materially interferes with life, health, or safety, and within 72 hours where the premises are uninhabitable.

The mold provision then lists the first tasks by name. Within 72 hours of notice, the landlord must install containment, stop active sources of water feeding the growth, and install a HEPA filtration device. Containment must be maintained through the repair. Moisture sources must be eliminated or limited and wet materials dried. Damaged materials must be decontaminated or removed. The statute also calls for evaluating whether the work succeeded, including post-remediation testing, and for reassembling the unit so the dampness does not return. A tenant may request a comparable unit or a hotel room where the condition interferes with life, health, or safety. Notice counts when it is in writing, and since January 1, 2025 rental agreements have had to state where written notice may be sent.

### Sampling that is worth buying

There are narrow cases for lab work: litigation, an unclear source, occupant health concerns, and confirming that an area has been adequately cleaned. Air samples are snapshots of one moment, and the guidance is candid that a poor sampling plan produces misleading results. If a firm removing the growth also sells the clearance test, the independence that gives the test its value is gone. The consensus standard separates the remediator's internal post-remediation evaluation from verification performed by an independent third-party professional, which is the arrangement to ask for.

### The removal itself, whatever the shade

Method follows area, not color. Small isolated patches get cleaned and dried. Between 10 and 100 square feet, limited containment under negative pressure with HEPA-filtered exhaust is the recommended approach; past 100 square feet, full containment with an airlock. Porous material that stayed wet leaves the building. Framing and other hard surfaces are HEPA vacuumed and damp wiped. Then the assembly is dried and rechecked with a meter before anything is closed up.

### Paperwork, and what insurance usually does

Two documents matter later. The first is a scope naming the water source, the square footage, the containment level, and the removal boundary. The second is whatever confirms the finish, whether that is a post-remediation verification or dated moisture readings and photographs. Both are useful in a sale or a dispute. On coverage, the Colorado Division of Insurance buyer's guide lists mold among the common exclusions in homeowners policies, and its consumer material notes that a sudden interior pipe break is treated very differently from rain or irrigation water entering a house. Read the exclusion pages before assuming the work is covered.

## svc_attic_mold_remediation_body

### Reading the pattern before touching anything

Location separates the three. A roof leak makes a narrow vertical path: a wet rafter, a stained run of decking below a penetration, and matted insulation directly under it. Condensation is diffuse instead, spreading across the north-facing plane and concentrating at nail tips and the cold upper corners. A fan terminating inside the attic makes a blossom shape right above the bathroom, often with damp dust on the joists. Photograph the pattern before disturbing it, because the pattern is the diagnosis and it does not survive demolition.

### Heavy spring snow is the local test

Winter here is not the hazard people assume. The 30-year average for seasonal snowfall in Colorado Springs is 33.6 inches, spread over a long dry cold season. The heavy accumulations arrive late: the biggest single month in the record is April 1957 at 42.7 inches, second is April 1900 at 34.5 inches, and the greatest 24-hour total is 25.6 inches on March 11, 1909. Late-season snow is wet and heavy, and daytime temperatures climb above freezing while the roof surface stays cold. Warm air escaping through ceiling penetrations melts the underside of that snowpack, meltwater refreezes at the cold eave, and water backs up under the shingle course until it finds a nail hole or a seam. Federal guidance names the same physics from the prevention side, recommending added insulation to reduce condensation on cold surfaces such as roofs.

### Hail damage rarely leaks the week it happens

Severe hail in this forecast area starts at one inch in diameter, and it is the size of the largest stone that classifies a storm, not the depth of ice on the lawn. A stone that size can bruise a shingle mat without opening a visible hole. Water then enters slowly at fastener penetrations, wets sheathing from above, and produces attic staining a season or two later, long after any claim conversation ended. Where an attic problem shows up on a roof plane that took a hard storm, the roof gets evaluated as part of the job, not after it.

### The housing stock this happens in

There are 216,476 housing units here and 61 percent are single-family detached, so most affected attics are private and rarely visited. Over 77,000 of those units were built before 1980, which is the era before current attic ventilation and air sealing practice, and where retrofit insulation was often blown over the top of an unsealed ceiling plane. The two-story layouts in the northern subdivisions, Briargate and Rockrimmon among them, put bathrooms and laundry rooms directly under the deck. On the west side, in the small early blocks around Old Colorado City, where the settlement was organized in 1859, and in Ivywild near the 1916 school building, attics are shallow, unfloored, and hard to work in without disturbing the ceiling below.

### What not to do through the hatch

Do not walk outside the framing, and do not compress wet insulation to see under it. Do not run a fan across a stained area, which is exactly how spores get pushed into the rooms below. Skip the sprayed encapsulant that gets sold as a one-visit fix; the industry standard specifically discourages spraying products before growth is physically removed and surfaces are cleaned, and treats stain removers as cosmetic work permitted only after complete source removal. Investigating hidden growth spreads it when done carelessly, which is why the inspection is deliberate and the roof side gets stopped first.

### What the scope covers

Sequence matters more than product choice. The water entry is repaired or scheduled first, because an assembly that keeps getting wet cannot be finished. Wet insulation is porous material and generally leaves the attic rather than being dried in place. Sheathing and framing that can be cleaned are HEPA vacuumed and damp wiped, then dried and verified with a meter rather than by eye. Containment goes up at the access opening whenever debris could travel into finished space, with negative pressure and HEPA-filtered exhaust when the affected area justifies it. Ceiling penetrations get sealed, the bath fan gets ducted to the outdoors with a proper termination, and ventilation is checked so the fix is not undone the next time the temperature swings.

### What moves the number

Insulation volume drives it first, since removal and disposal is measured by area and depth. Access is second: a scuttle hole over a closet costs more to work through than a walk-in gable door. Whether decking can be cleaned or has to be cut out and replaced is third, and the roof repair itself is a separate trade with its own price. A quote that lists these separately is comparable. A single lump sum for an attic is not.

## svc_crawl_space_mold_remediation_body

### What the space is telling you

Start by reading the distribution of dampness rather than the worst spot. Water concentrated under one bathroom points at a drain or supply line. A damp band along one exterior wall points outside, toward grade, a downspout, or an irrigation zone. Uniform dampness across the whole footprint with condensation on cool surfaces is an air and vapor problem instead. Insulation hanging loose from the floor framing is a useful sign, because batts that got wet lose their grip and fall before anything else looks wrong.

### Cool surfaces and air that gets pulled in

Below-grade rooms stay cool through summer, and cool surfaces are where humid air gives up its water. Every exhaust device in a house, the dryer, the range hood, the bath fans, and a radon fan where one is fitted, moves air out and something has to replace it. In a house with leaky rim joists and open vents, part of that replacement air is warm outdoor air arriving in the coolest part of the building. Federal guidance puts the target at below 60 percent relative humidity, ideally 30 to 50 percent, which is a number worth measuring down there rather than assuming from the weather.

### Clay soils, irrigation, and where the water starts

Water management outside the foundation is the local variable that matters most. The Colorado Geological Survey calls expansive or swelling soil the state's most significant geologic hazard. These clays can expand up to 20 percent by volume when they take on water, and the survey puts the force they exert as high as 30,000 pounds per square foot. The same source names two recognized problems: ponding water near a foundation, and planting next to the wall so irrigation water enters the soil. It also observes that semi-arid regions with big seasonal swings in soil moisture see expansion trouble more often than wetter places. Practically, a sprinkler zone against the wall, a downspout ending two feet from the house, or negative grade off a patio does more damage here than the annual rainfall total suggests.

### The radon system in the same crawl space

Two systems often share this space, and only one of them has a state credential behind it. Colorado licenses radon professionals through its Division of Professions and Occupations; there is no equivalent state license or registration for this work, and the state health department states that it does not regulate mold. Local numbers make the pairing common. El Paso County Public Health reports that over 40 percent of homes tested in the county between 2005 and 2023 came back with high radon levels, and it sells test kits at the county public health laboratory on West Garden of the Gods Road. A sub-slab or sub-membrane system changes the pressure regime under the floor, so sealing, membrane detailing, and any mitigation work should be coordinated rather than sequenced by accident.

### Encapsulation sold too early

The common failure here is a beautiful liner installed over a wet problem. Sheeting laid across damp soil or fastened to framing that still reads high on a meter hides the condition, and the odor comes back through the floor within a season. Order of operations is the whole trick: stop the water outside, remove porous material that cannot be cleaned, clean and dry the framing to a verified reading, then detail the ground cover and the walls. A vapor barrier is a control layer for soil moisture, not a repair for a leak or a substitute for drying.

### What not to do down there

Do not enter a space with standing water, sewage, or damaged wiring, and do not crawl into a tight area alone. Leave wet insulation overhead alone; pulling it down showers the confined space you are breathing in. Do not seal vents or fog a chemical as a first move. Skip the bleach: federal guidance does not recommend biocides as routine practice, and points out that dead growth still needs to be removed rather than merely killed.

### What the visit covers, and what it costs

Expect a walk of the accessible footprint with a moisture meter and a humidity gauge, a look at the vapor barrier, the rim joist, the insulation, the ducts, and the plumbing routes, and an exterior lap for grade, downspouts, and irrigation heads. The scope that comes back should separate three things: removal and cleaning inside the space, drying with equipment counted by unit and by day, and the water management repair outside. Cost tracks the affected square footage, whether insulation and ground cover come out, access height, and how much of the fix belongs to another trade. A crawl space priced as one number usually hides which of those three is missing.
