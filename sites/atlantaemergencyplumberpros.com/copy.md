## symptom_1_title

A pipe is leaking, spraying, or soaking the ceiling

## symptom_1

First, stop adding water to the house. Shut off the nearest fixture valve if the leak is clearly at a toilet, sink, or appliance connection. If water is coming through a ceiling, from behind a wall, or from a pipe you cannot isolate, close the main shutoff if you can reach it safely. Do not stand under a sagging wet ceiling, and do not cut into drywall while water may be near light fixtures. Move electronics, rugs, and anything that can be lifted out of the wet area. Then open a cold-water faucet briefly after the main is off to relieve pressure.

The visible drip is not always the whole failure. A split supply line can run inside a cabinet. A failed water-heater connection can send water across a garage or basement floor. In older Atlanta houses, a leak may appear downstairs even though the source is a bathroom line above it; water follows framing and pipes before it finds an opening. The site visit starts by locating the source, isolating the damaged section, and checking whether surrounding fittings have been stressed or corroded.

The repair may be a new valve, connector, section of pipe, or a more involved replacement where the line is concealed. A technician can tell that only after seeing the pipe material, access, and water damage. Keep the water off until then. Drying the area matters after the leak is stopped, but stopping the active flow comes first. If the leak is near a panel, outlet, or appliance wiring, stay clear and treat it as an electrical hazard as well as a plumbing emergency.

## symptom_2_title

The sink, tub, or toilet is overflowing instead of draining

## symptom_2

An overflowing fixture is a stop-using-water problem, not a test-it-again problem. Turn off the local supply valve behind a toilet if the bowl keeps filling. Do not keep flushing. At a sink or tub, stop the faucet and keep other fixtures nearby unused until the path is identified. Chemical drain cleaners are a poor move during an overflow: they rarely clear a serious obstruction, can damage older pipe, and leave hazardous liquid for the person opening the line.

One slow bathroom sink can be a trap, hair, or a short branch-line clog. The pattern changes when the toilet bubbles as a shower drains, the tub fills when the washing machine empties, or several fixtures on the same level are affected. That points farther down the line. In Atlanta’s older neighborhoods, the technician may need to determine whether a branch drain, the building drain, or the lateral toward the sewer is holding water. The correct tool depends on that location. A cable machine may clear a reachable obstruction; a camera can show whether roots, a separated joint, grease buildup, or a damaged pipe is causing repeat blockages.

Protect the area around the overflow. Keep children and pets away, place towels only where they can safely contain clean water, and avoid putting rags or wipes into another drain. If the water is dark, smells like sewage, or has backed up through a floor drain, treat it as contamination rather than ordinary spill water. The immediate goal is to stop the overflow and restore a clear drain, then decide whether the line needs a scoped inspection before the next heavy use of the house.

## symptom_3_title

Sewage is coming up through a tub, toilet, floor drain, or cleanout

## symptom_3

A sewage backup needs a quick stop to water use. Do not flush, run the dishwasher, start laundry, or shower while waste water is returning through a lower fixture. Keep people and pets away from the affected room. If the backup is in a basement or crawlspace, do not enter standing water until it is safe to do so. Sewage can contain contamination, and the pressure behind a blocked line can spread it farther every time another fixture drains.

This is where Atlanta’s older intown plumbing deserves a different level of attention. A sewer lateral may be buried under a mature yard, and recurring backups can be caused by roots entering joints, a shifted section, collapse, or a partial blockage that a simple plunger will never reach. The City itself continues to repair aged sewer infrastructure; on Georgia Avenue SE in January 2026, its Watershed Management crews linked a sinkhole investigation to a clay combination sewer pipe. That does not diagnose any private line, but it is a reason to take a recurring backup seriously instead of treating it as a one-time toilet clog.

The first visit focuses on clearing the immediate obstruction and locating its position. If the issue appears to be on the property side, a camera inspection can show what the line actually looks like before anyone talks about excavation. That matters around established trees. Cutting roots blindly can buy a little drainage and leave the underlying pipe problem untouched; excavating near a protected tree can also involve planning beyond the plumbing work. A technician should distinguish a clearable blockage from a damaged lateral, explain the evidence, and give the repair options before work starts. Until the line is cleared, use as little water as possible.

## symptom_4_title

There is little or no running water in the house

## symptom_4

No water at one fixture is different from no water everywhere. Check whether the cold side fails at every sink, whether toilets will refill, and whether neighbors appear to have water. Do not repeatedly cycle a well pump or appliance if your property has one; most Atlanta homes are on municipal water, but the source still needs to be isolated. If only one fixture is dry, its stop valve, supply line, faucet cartridge, or a localized leak may be involved. If every fixture has lost pressure, the issue may be at the main shutoff, meter-side supply, a major internal leak, or the public system.

Start with the simplest safe checks. Make sure the main shutoff inside the home is fully open unless you deliberately closed it for a leak. Look for water around the meter area or along the path where the service line enters the house, but do not dig, remove a meter lid, or try to turn utility equipment. If the water meter is running while every fixture is off, that can point to water moving somewhere it should not. If there is a known area outage, the plumbing inside the home may be fine; if there is no outage and the meter or shutoff condition is unclear, an on-site diagnosis is the practical next step.

Loss of water can become urgent fast when it follows a leak, leaves a water heater exposed to dry firing, or affects a whole occupied house. Turn off the water heater’s energy source only if it can be done safely and according to its controls; do not relight or reset equipment until water service is restored. The technician traces the supply route, tests pressure where appropriate, and identifies whether the repair belongs inside the property, at a private service line, or with the water utility.

## qa_1_question

Why do old Atlanta sewer lines and trees matter to a backup?

## qa_1_answer

They matter because a line can be blocked, damaged, and hard to reach at the same time. Atlanta’s Department of Watershed Management says the city’s federal sewer consent-decree work has involved repairing or replacing more than 387 miles of aged sewer lines, and a January 2026 city investigation connected a Georgia Avenue SE sinkhole to a clay combination sewer pipe. Private laterals are not the city system, but the same age-of-infrastructure context is a good reason to inspect a repeating backup rather than guessing from inside the house.

Mature roots look for moisture and can enter weak joints or breaks in an older lateral. A drain machine may restore flow, but a camera is what separates a root mass from a cracked, offset, or collapsed pipe. That distinction prevents an emergency cleanup from becoming an unnecessary excavation. It matters even more when the route crosses a landscaped yard. Atlanta now requires a completed Arborist Meeting before a permit application involving potential tree impacts will be accepted. If repair planning could affect a significant tree, the line location, repair method, and tree constraints should be understood together before the yard is opened.

## qa_2_question

Do emergency plumbing repairs need a City of Atlanta permit?

## qa_2_answer

It depends on the scope, so do not accept a blanket yes or no. The City lists plumbing permits for new or replacement plumbing in one- and two-family residences as technical permits that can be submitted online without plan review. Its general permitting guidance also lists repair or replacement of existing kitchen and bathroom fixtures among defined exemptions for existing one- and two-family homes and duplexes, subject to the limits on that page. A new line, replacement section, work in a historic district, or a project that affects a site or tree can be a different situation.

For an emergency, the plumbing priority is to stop active damage safely. For the permanent repair, the technician should identify the scope and whether it calls for a permit rather than treating every leak as identical. If a sewer-line repair changes the property or could affect trees, allow for the city review steps instead of discovering them after a yard has been opened. City rules and property conditions can change, so confirm the specific job with the Office of Buildings before work that goes beyond a straightforward repair.

## qa_3_question

Is a sewer backup an after-hours emergency?

## qa_3_answer

Often, yes. Sewage entering the house, a toilet that cannot be stopped from overflowing, or a main drain that prevents normal sanitation needs attention sooner than a slow sink. The reason is practical: using another faucet, dishwasher, or shower can force more contaminated water out at the low point. Stop water use, keep people out of the affected area, and arrange a dispatch so the line can be cleared and the cause located.

Not every drain complaint needs a late-night visit. A single sink that drains slowly but is not overflowing can generally be scheduled. The same is true of a faucet drip that can be isolated at its fixture valve. The important distinction is whether water or sewage is actively escaping, whether the house can be used safely, and whether delay is likely to spread damage. An on-site assessment provides the repair price and tells you whether the immediate fix is a clearing, a targeted pipe repair, or a planned lateral project.
