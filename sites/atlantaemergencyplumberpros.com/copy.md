### Close the water before anything else gets touched

Pressurized water that keeps arriving makes every other decision worse. If the leak is clearly at one toilet, sink, or appliance connection, close the fixture stop. If water is running behind a wall, through a ceiling, or from a pipe nobody can isolate, close the house main and then open a low cold-water tap to bleed the remaining pressure. Where waste water is the problem, the instruction reverses: stop using water anywhere in the building, because every gallon sent down a working drain reappears at the lowest one. Keep clear of wet ceilings, submerged outlets, and any appliance the water has reached.

### Sort the supply side from the drain side

Water that keeps coming with every fixture idle points at the pressure side, a water heater, or an appliance connection. Water that appears only while a shower runs or a washer empties points at drainage. One sluggish lavatory is almost always a branch problem. A tub and a toilet reacting to each other is not, and neither is a floor drain that fills when nothing upstairs is running. ATL311 is explicit that some of this is not municipal work at all: its published list of private-property matters includes a burst water pipe on private property, noting the resident may have an internal issue requiring the expertise of a licensed plumber.

### Learn where the city's responsibility stops

Repairs to distribution piping running from the main to the meter are the city's responsibility, and repairs to the piping from the meter to the house are the homeowner's. Reports of a suspected main break or a public leak go to 404-546-0311, and the department's service description says a specialist investigates within 24 hours, while full resolution including street or property restoration can take 30 to 45 days. That timeline is worth knowing before a household decides to wait on a public repair. Water rising inside a house rarely waits for anybody.

### Water heaters fail in a few recognizable ways

A tank that weeps from the shell has usually lost its lining, and no fitting replacement fixes that. A drip at the temperature and pressure relief connection can mean thermal expansion, a failing valve, or a control problem, and it should never be capped. Wet connections at the top of the tank are often corroded nipples or dielectric fittings rather than the tank itself. Sediment noise, lukewarm output, and a burner or element that short cycles all belong in the same conversation. The replacement decision should account for the failure mode, the discharge routing, and the shutoff condition together.

### Georgia licenses the plumber, not the truck

Plumbing licensure in Georgia is a state matter, handled through the Secretary of State's division for master and journeyman plumbers. State law says no person may engage in the business of plumbing as a master plumber without a valid license from that division, and a company cannot run plumbing work unless a full-time licensed master plumber is regularly connected with it. The state rules split the credential in two. ### One fixture or a whole side of the house

A single slow lavatory, tub, or kitchen sink usually holds a local restriction: hair and soap at a trap, grease in a short branch, or a fouled tailpiece. The signal changes when two fixtures talk to each other. ### What older drainage here is made of

Cast iron interior drainage was standard for decades and does not fail cleanly. It scales inward, catches paper, and can develop a rough channel that reopens with a cable and closes again within months. Galvanized branch lines behave the same way at a smaller diameter. Vitrified clay was the common material for the buried run, joined in short sections that can shift as soil moves. The city's own repair work illustrates how long clay survives underground: Watershed Management tied a January 2026 sinkhole investigation on Georgia Avenue SE to a 24-inch clay combination sewer pipe roughly 18 feet down. Private laterals of similar vintage sit under yards all over the east side.

### Roots go where the water is

Mature canopy is part of what makes Virginia-Highland, Candler Park, Kirkwood, and Grant Park pleasant, and it is also why laterals close. Fine roots follow moisture and vapor to a hairline crack or an open joint, then thicken inside the pipe until paper and grease complete the plug. Cutting them restores flow without repairing the opening that let them in, so the interval between backups is the real diagnostic. Yearly is a maintenance conversation. Quarterly is a pipe conversation. Excavation near protected trees also carries its own planning steps in the city, and from late June of 2025 the Office of Buildings has required an arborist meeting before permit applications that affect trees.

### Cable, jetter, auger: matching the tool to the line

A hand auger or a small drum machine belongs on a trap and branch work. A sectional or drum cable with the correct head reaches a building drain and a lateral, and head selection matters more than motor size. Hydro jetting scours grease and soft buildup from the pipe wall rather than punching a hole through it, which suits a kitchen line or a scaled main, but pressure applied to a cracked clay joint or a corroded cast iron section can do harm. Caustic drain openers deserve a plain warning: they seldom clear a real obstruction, they attack older metal, and they leave a hazardous pool for whoever opens the line next.

### Camera and locate work before anyone digs

After the line is open, a camera answers what a cable cannot. It shows the material, the joints, standing water in a belly, root mass, scale, offsets, and any missing section. A locator paired with the camera head marks the depth and position on the surface, which turns a vague estimate into a marked point in the yard. That distinction decides everything downstream: an obstruction that clears cleanly and shows sound pipe is maintenance, while a displaced joint or a collapsed section is a repair with excavation or a lining decision behind it. Utility locating is a separate step required before digging.

### Rain-driven backups are their own category

Not every backup involves a clog. Part of Atlanta's collection system still carries sanitary flow and storm water in the same pipe, so a heavy downpour can be the trigger rather than grease. If a fixture backs up while it rains and behaves normally in dry weather, that pattern belongs in the sewer-line discussion instead of the drain-cleaning one. ### Keeping the line open afterward

An accessible cleanout is worth more than any product on a shelf. Without one, a technician works through a pulled toilet or a roof vent, which slows every future visit. Grease belongs in a container, not down a kitchen drain with hot water behind it. Wipes labeled flushable still bind to scale and roots. Where a house has repeatedly backed up, a scheduled clearing interval based on the camera evidence is cheaper than emergency work, and the interval itself tells the owner when the pipe has stopped being a maintenance item.

### Two federal decrees rebuilt the public side, not yours

Atlanta has spent decades under court supervision of its sewers. ### What a combined pipe means on a rainy night

In a combined area, roof water, street runoff, and household waste share one pipe. The city's consent decree materials explain that overflow structures exist so the treatment plants are not flooded when rain volume exceeds what the combined pipes can carry, and that captured flow is sent for treatment once the rain stops. Combined pipe was reported as roughly 15 percent of the system, with about 85 percent of the city already separated and the program aiming near 90 percent. For a homeowner, the practical reading is narrow but important: in these areas, a hard rain can pressurize the public system at the same moment a private lateral is already restricted, and the lowest opening in the building is where that shows.

### Where private laterals actually fail

Clay sections separate at joints as clay soils shrink and swell. Cast iron thins and flakes until a hole opens along the bottom. A section can settle into a belly that holds water and solids without ever cracking. Roots enter through any of those defects. Grease hardens on a rough wall and narrows a pipe that has otherwise held up. In January 2026 a sinkhole investigation on Georgia Avenue SE led the city to a 24-inch clay combination sewer about 18 feet below the surface. That is the same material, and the same age band, sitting under private yards nearby.

### Proving the problem before the shovel

Repair decisions rest on three pieces of evidence: what the camera sees, where the locator puts it, and how the line behaves in dry versus wet weather. A defect that appears every time, regardless of rain, is a pipe defect. A backup that arrives only with storms suggests a wet-weather capacity path. The city's own overflow record separates the two the same way: federal reviewers counted an average of roughly 14 rain-induced and 242 dry-weather overflows a year on the public system across a recent multi-year window. A report should state depth, distance from the cleanout, pipe material, and the specific defect, because those four facts set the method and the cost.

### Backflow protection and its limits

A backwater valve is a one-way device installed in the building drain or lateral to stop waste from returning into the lowest fixtures. It is worth discussing with a plumber for a basement bathroom, a floor drain below street grade, or a house that has flooded before. Two cautions belong with it. A valve needs an accessible cover and periodic cleaning, because debris on the flapper defeats it silently. And a valve controls the direction of flow without correcting a broken pipe, so it should never be presented as an alternative to repairing a failed lateral.

### Repair methods and the paperwork behind them

A single defect near a cleanout at moderate depth can often be handled as a spot repair. Multiple offsets, a long root-infested run, or a flattened section point toward full replacement, or toward a trenchless option where cured-in-place lining or pipe bursting can renew a run with fewer excavations. Depth, soil, driveways, existing utilities, and mature trees decide which of those is realistic. Sewer work is permitted and inspected work in the city, and where digging affects trees the arborist meeting requirement now precedes the permit application. A plan that skips the permit conversation is not a cheaper plan.

### Renters, landlords, and the night it happens

Georgia law puts repair duty on the landlord, stating that the landlord shall keep the premises in repair. Under a 2024 amendment, residential leases entered into or renewed from the first of July that year include a provision that the premises is fit for human habitation. A tenant facing waste on a floor should notify the landlord or manager in writing, keep dated photographs, and record every call. Whoever owns the pipe, the immediate steps are the same: stop all water use in the building, keep people and pets out of the affected rooms, and report the backup to 404-546-0311 so the city can determine whether the main is involved.

### Three things move an Atlanta plumbing number

Start with access. A clogged branch line with a cleanout inside the crawlspace is a straightforward visit. The same clog in a Virginia-Highland duplex, where the only way at the stack runs through a second-floor roof vent, is a longer job. Second, material age: in-town houses carry galvanized supply and cast iron or clay drainage, and old pipe rarely fails politely. Third, the clock. A leak that started at nine on a Friday night and a leak reported Monday at ten are the same repair with different labor attached.

That is also why an honest range stays wide. A cleared line and a failed line look identical from the sink. Only the camera separates them, and the gap between those answers runs from a few hundred dollars to five figures.

### Roots, clay and hills: the local reason

Atlanta's drainage problems are largely a story about trees and slope. The city's own field work makes the point: in early January 2026 the Department of Watershed Management traced a Georgia Avenue SE sinkhole to a 24-inch clay combination sewer about eighteen feet down. Clay joints and thirsty roots find each other, and Atlanta has both in abundance on the same lots where the housing stock is oldest. Hilly in-town parcels also put the house well above or well below the main, so laterals here run longer and deeper than a flat subdivision would need, and depth is the single biggest multiplier on any dig.

Trees add a procedural layer rather than a physical one. From June 2025 the city has required a completed arborist meeting before any permit application involving possible tree impacts, and applications without that documentation are not accepted. Nobody prices that as a line item, yet it lengthens the schedule on exactly the excavations that root damage causes. Meanwhile the sewer system itself is under federal consent decrees dating to 1998 and 1999, with an estimated four billion dollars of water and sewer work behind them. Public money has gone into mains. Private laterals stayed private.

### What the city's paperwork costs, and what it enforces

The fee appendix is modest on its face: a $50 minimum for a residential plumbing permit, $10 for each fixture, $10 for a house sewer newly laid or replaced. The enforcement side is where the money lives. Starting work before the permit issues doubles the fee, up to a thousand dollars, and each reinspection trip after a written correction notice adds $50. On the utility side, a single-family sewer connection carries $230 for Watershed plan review and site inspection, plus a $250 sewer inspection fee when a new tap is involved, and the department is explicit that the installation itself is the owner's cost. Work inside the public right of way can require a separate qualified contractor permit from Public Works.

### Reading an estimate like somebody who has seen a few

An estimate worth signing states the diagnosis and the evidence behind it: footage from the camera, distance and depth to the defect, and whether the pipe is clay, cast iron, Orangeburg or plastic. It puts the permit on its own line and says who applies for it. On any dig it specifies restoration in writing, which in this city means concrete or asphalt to city spec, backfill compaction, and what happens to shrubs and sod. It separates the diagnostic charge from the repair and says whether one credits against the other. If a proposal quotes lining and excavation as a single blended number, ask which one is actually planned.

### The upsell to watch for

The pressure play in this market is a full lateral replacement proposed from a single blocked line, presented while somebody is standing in an inch of water. Roots at one joint are a repair. A pipe that has lost its shape along most of its length is a replacement. Those are different findings and the camera distinguishes them, so ask to watch the footage rather than a summary. Two smaller traps: jetting sold before any inspection, which can finish off already fragile clay, and enzyme or chemical treatments billed as prevention on a line whose actual problem is a broken joint.

### What the online average never includes

The national figures skip Atlanta's specifics almost entirely. They leave out the permit and reinspection structure, the Watershed review and inspection fees, the arborist step when a protected tree is in the way, pavement restoration on a street cut, and the drywall and paint that follow an interior repair. They also assume a lateral of ordinary length at ordinary depth, which describes very few lots inside the city. Depth, distance and what is growing overhead are the local variables a national average never sees.
