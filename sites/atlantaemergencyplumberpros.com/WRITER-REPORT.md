# Atlanta Emergency Plumbing — Research Sources

1. **Federal sewer consent-decree investment** — City of Atlanta Department of Watershed Management says the CSO and SSO consent decrees were issued in 1998 and 1999, require an estimated $4 billion in water and sewer improvements, and have funded repair/replacement of over 387 miles of aged sewer lines.
   - https://atlantawatershed.org/most/

2. **Clay sewer infrastructure remains a documented local condition** — On January 3, 2026, DWM investigated a Georgia Avenue SE sinkhole associated with a 24-inch clay combination sewer pipe roughly 18 feet deep.
   - https://atlantawatershed.org/news-release-january-2026-4/

3. **Tree-impact permit applications have an added review step** — Effective June 25, 2025, an Atlanta permit application involving potential impacts to trees must include a completed formal Arborist Meeting; applications without that documentation are not accepted.
   - https://www.atlantaga.gov/government/departments/city-planning/about-dcp/office-of-buildings

Supporting permit reference used in copy: City technical permits page lists plumbing permits for new or replacement plumbing in one- and two-family residences as online submissions that do not require plan review. The general permitting page lists repair/replacement of existing kitchen and bathroom fixtures among defined exemptions, subject to its stated limits.
- https://www.atlantaga.gov/residents/city-hall/online-services/apply-for-a-technical-permit
- https://www.atlantaga.gov/government/departments/city-planning/zoning-development-permitting-services/getting-started-with-our-zd-p-services

Neighborhoods used: Virginia-Highland, Inman Park, Grant Park, Kirkwood, Candler Park, West End.
