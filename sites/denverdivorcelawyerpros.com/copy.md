## qa_1_question
Is Colorado a no-fault divorce state?

## qa_1_answer
Colorado’s Uniform Dissolution of Marriage Act makes irretrievable breakdown of the marriage relationship the sole basis for dissolution. The petition must allege that the marriage is irretrievably broken. That does not decide property, debt, parenting, support, or the language of an agreement. It means the statutory basis for the divorce is not a need to prove marital wrongdoing. Colorado Revised Statutes section 14-10-106 and section 14-10-110 set out the irretrievable-breakdown framework. If one spouse denies that the marriage is irretrievably broken, the court has a statutory process for considering that issue. The facts and legal questions in any individual Denver case still need individual analysis.

## qa_2_question
How long does a divorce take in Colorado?

## qa_2_answer
Colorado’s court instructions state that a divorce cannot be finalized until at least 91 days have passed, even if both spouses agree on every issue. The clock begins when a joint petition is filed, or when the other spouse is served or signs a waiver in a separately filed case. That is a minimum waiting period, not a promise of completion on day 91. The court’s instructions also address filing, service, a response, financial documents, an Initial Status Conference, and later case steps. A matter can take longer because of the issues involved, the information needed, the parties’ agreements or disagreements, and court scheduling.

## pricing_lede

A divorce fee in Colorado cannot be tied to getting the divorce or to the size of the maintenance, child support or property number, so it is billed by the hour or as a flat fee for defined work. This page sets out exactly how narrow that prohibition is, what the written fee terms have to say, and what the court and its mediation program charge on a Denver case.

## pricing_body

### The prohibition is real, and narrower than people repeat

The operative language is Colo. RPC 1.5(c)(4), which lists the agreements that may not be made at all. Subparagraph (i) covers representing a defendant in a criminal case. Subparagraph (ii) is the domestic relations provision: no contingent fee agreement may be made in a domestic relations matter where payment is contingent on the securing of a divorce or upon the amount of maintenance or child support, or property settlement in lieu of such amounts. Subparagraph (iii) sweeps in any proceeding where a contingency method of determining attorney fees is otherwise prohibited by law.

Read what that actually forbids. It forbids payment riding on the decree issuing, and payment scaled to the support or property figure. It is not a rule that every dollar in a dissolution has to be paid before the work happens. The comment to the rule makes the boundary explicit in the other direction. The prohibition does not preclude a contract for a contingent fee for legal representation in connection with the recovery of post-judgment balances due under support, maintenance or other financial orders. Those contracts do not implicate the same policy concerns. A parent chasing years of unpaid support is in a different position from a spouse bargaining over the decree.

### So the fee is hourly, or flat, and both have written requirements

Rule 1.5(b) requires that before or within a reasonable time after the representation starts, the lawyer communicate to the client in writing the basis or rate of the fee and the expenses the client will be responsible for, plus the scope of the representation. Any later change to the basis or rate has to be communicated in writing too. Scope is the part that decides whether a number is comparable to another number: an engagement that ends at a temporary orders hearing is not the same product as one that carries through disclosure disputes, a parental responsibilities evaluation and a contested hearing.

Money paid in advance is governed by Rule 1.5(f). Fees are not earned until the lawyer confers a benefit or performs a service, advances of unearned fees are the property of the client, and they sit in the lawyer's trust account until earned. Rule 1.5(g) prohibits nonrefundable fees and nonrefundable retainers outright, along with any agreement that unreasonably restricts the client's right to a refund of unearned or unreasonable fees. If a flat fee is used, Rule 1.5(h) requires the terms in writing, including a description of the services and the amount and timing of payment. The writing must also state what is earned at which milestone if any part is earned before the case ends, and how much is earned if the representation stops early.

### What the court charges, and what it costs to change something later

Colorado's filing fees are statutory and published by the Judicial Branch. A petition for divorce, legal separation, annulment or declaratory judgment is $260 under C.R.S. 13-32-101(1)(a) and its surcharge subsections. A response is $146. A petition for allocation of parental responsibilities filed on its own is $252. Registering a foreign decree is $201. After the case is over, a motion to modify a decree or final order filed more than sixty days after it entered costs $105, which is the amount that matters most, because a Denver decree with parenting time in it is a document people come back to.

Court-connected mediation is priced separately. The Judicial Branch Office of Dispute Resolution charges each party $75 per hour for mediation services, case preparation and preparation of the memorandum of understanding, and neutrals may collect a two-hour advance deposit on a domestic relations case. A neutral may bill up to two hours against a party who does not appear or cancels late. Private mediators set their own rates.

### One spouse can be ordered to pay the other's fees

C.R.S. 14-10-119 lets the court, after considering the financial resources of both parties, order one party to pay a reasonable amount for the other's cost of maintaining or defending the proceeding, including attorney fees. That reach extends to services rendered and costs incurred before the case was filed or after judgment. The court may order the money paid directly to the professional. In a marriage where one spouse controlled the income and the accounts, that statute is what keeps the lower-earning spouse in the case, and it is worth raising early.

### The costs that are not the fee at all

Expenses in a Denver dissolution sit outside the hourly rate: the filing fee, service on the other spouse, subpoenas for account records, a real property appraisal, a business valuation, a pension actuary, and a parental responsibilities evaluator if the parenting dispute goes that far. Ask which expenses require approval before they are incurred, what the billing increment is, and how the file transfers if the case is handed off.

## svc_contested_divorce_lawyer_lede

Contested means a judge will decide something the two of you could not. In Denver that means temporary orders, disclosure fights, and a hearing date issued out of the district court in the City and County Building at 1437 Bannock Street.

## svc_contested_divorce_lawyer_body

### How you know your case is genuinely contested

Not by how tense the conversation is. A case is contested when even one item the decree must resolve stays open: how the marital estate splits, whether maintenance is paid and for how long, child support, decision-making, or the parenting schedule. Two people who agree about the house and disagree about one retirement account have a contested case. So does a case where one spouse will not produce a Sworn Financial Statement, because a court cannot divide an estate it has not been shown. Disputes about whether the marriage is irretrievably broken are rare in Colorado. Disputes about numbers are the ordinary situation.

### What the Colorado statutes actually set

Section 14-10-106(1)(a)(I) requires that one party has been domiciled in Colorado for ninety-one days next preceding the commencement of the proceeding. Subsection (1)(a)(III) blocks a decree until ninety-one days or more have elapsed since the court acquired jurisdiction over the respondent, whether through service under Rule 4, through joining as copetitioner, or through entering an appearance some other way. Property is divided under 14-10-113 without regard to marital misconduct. The court weighs each spouse's contribution including as homemaker, the value of property set apart to each, the economic circumstances of each when division takes effect, and any increase, decrease, or depletion of separate property during the marriage. One of those economic factors is the desirability of awarding the family home to the parent the children live with most of the time.

Maintenance runs through the advisory guideline in 14-10-114(3)(b), which applies where the marriage reaches three years and combined annual adjusted gross income does not exceed $240,000. The figure is forty percent of combined monthly adjusted gross income minus the lower earner's monthly figure, taken to eighty percent of that result where combined monthly income is $10,000 or less. Child support under 14-10-115 begins with combined adjusted gross income on the statutory schedule, divided in proportion to the parents' incomes, then adjusted for extraordinary medical expenses and work-related child care.

### The first week, with an injunction already running

Under 14-10-107(4)(b) a temporary injunction takes effect against both parties once the petition is filed and the respondent is personally served or accepts service, and its terms are printed on the summons and the petition themselves. Both spouses are restrained from transferring, encumbering, concealing, or disposing of marital property outside the usual course of business or the necessities of life, and each must notify the other of proposed extraordinary expenditures and account to the court for them. Both are enjoined from disturbing the other's peace, from removing the children from Colorado without consent or a court order, and from letting health, homeowner's, renter's, auto, or life insurance covering either spouse or the children lapse without fourteen days' advance notification and written consent. Emptying a joint account the week the papers land is the quickest way to spend credibility you will need later.

### The mistake that costs the case

Treating financial disclosure as a negotiating chip. Rule 16.2(e)(1) says the parties owe each other and the court a duty of full and honest disclosure, and that a party must affirmatively disclose everything material without awaiting inquiry from the other side. The mandatory disclosures under Form 35.1, the Sworn Financial Statement as JDF 1111 with JDF 1111SS schedules where they apply, and the Certificate of Compliance as JDF 1104 are due within 42 days of service. A statement that quietly omits a bonus, a retirement plan loan, or a side business becomes the exhibit the other lawyer reads back at the hearing, and it colors every other finding the judge makes about you.

### What the other side does

Files a response, then works the calendar. Expect a motion for temporary orders deciding who stays in the residence and who pays which bill while the case is pending. Then come subpoenas to banks, lenders, and employers, written interrogatories, and a request for a parental responsibilities evaluation if parenting is truly disputed. A settlement number appears shortly before the contested hearing, exactly when preparation costs peak. Low valuations on a closely held business and on the marital home are opening positions, not findings, and they get tested with an appraiser rather than an argument.

### What happens after the case is filed

Rule 16.2(c)(1)(E) puts the Initial Status Conference, or a filed Stipulated Case Management Plan, no later than 42 days from filing. That conference runs with a Family Court Facilitator, a magistrate, or a judge, who clarifies deadlines and sets the date of the next appearance. Denver District Court also publishes its own family forms, DEN 1 through DEN 10, including DEN 2 for the Initial Status Conference notice, and the court states plainly that they were created for use in Denver District Court only and must be completed in English. Procedural questions go to the self-help center in Room 281 at 1437 Bannock Street, which cannot give legal advice.

### How fees work under Colorado's rule

Colo. RPC 1.5(c)(4)(ii) bars an agreement in a domestic relations matter where payment is contingent on the securing of a divorce or on the amount of maintenance or child support, or property settlement in lieu of those amounts. A percentage of what you keep is not a lawful way to pay for a Colorado dissolution, so this work is billed by the hour or as a flat fee for a defined scope, against an advance that stays client property in trust until earned. Comment [6] to the rule marks the one carve-out: representation to recover post-judgment balances already due under support, maintenance, or other financial orders.

## svc_uncontested_divorce_lawyer_body

### How you know it is actually uncontested

Everything the decree has to contain is settled in writing: every asset and every debt assigned, whether maintenance is paid and for how long, child support, decision-making, and a parenting schedule. Colorado asks only that the marriage be irretrievably broken, so nobody has to establish grounds against anybody. Then test the harder question, which is whether the simplified route is even open to you. Rule 16.2(e)(11) reserves reduced disclosure for parties where neither holds an interest in a pension, an interest in a trust, or separate property with net equity above $10,000. A single teaching pension takes a Denver couple out of that lane entirely.

### Two different ninety-one-day rules

Both live in 14-10-106 and they are not the same requirement. Subsection (1)(a)(I) is residency: one spouse domiciled in Colorado for ninety-one days next preceding the commencement of the proceeding. Subsection (1)(a)(III) is the waiting period: no decree until ninety-one days or more have elapsed since the court acquired jurisdiction over the respondent, by Rule 4 service, by joining the petition as copetitioner, or by entering an appearance in any other manner. Signing jointly as copetitioners starts that clock on the filing date and removes the service step, which is the single largest scheduling difference between a cooperative filing and a served one. Ninety-one days is a floor. Nothing in the statute delivers a decree on day ninety-two.

### The first week, in order

Sequence matters more than speed. Petition and case information sheet, then either a joint signature or service, then the financial paperwork. Within 42 days of filing or receiving the petition come the Sworn Financial Statement on JDF 1111, supporting schedules on JDF 1111SS, and the Certificate of Compliance on JDF 1104. Denver publishes simplified versions for its own court: DEN 1 for a petition without children, DEN 3 as a Simplified Sworn Financial Statement, DEN 4 as a Simplified Separation Agreement, DEN 5 as a Simplified Parenting Plan. Agreed terms go into the Property and Financial Agreement, JDF 1115. Where the court can enter a decree without a hearing, JDF 1018 is the affidavit that supports it. One deadline hides after the decree: a name restoration filed within sixty days of the signed decree carries no filing fee, and after that window it does.

### The mistake that costs you the agreement

Signing a document that leaves work undone. Rule 16.2(e)(1) imposes an affirmative duty of full and honest disclosure without awaiting inquiry, and a friendly spouse who forgot a deferred bonus has still created a disclosure problem the court can revisit. The other frequent error is language that reads fine and does nothing. "He keeps the house" with no refinance deadline, no deadline for the deed, and no allocation of the mortgage leaves the departing spouse on the note and on the credit report, still liable for a loan on a Park Hill property they no longer occupy. Denver's median owner-occupied home value sat at $586,700 in the Census Bureau's 2019 through 2023 estimates, so this is rarely a small mistake.

### What the other side does, even when cooperative

Cooperative spouses still act in their own interest, and usually without meaning any harm by it. Watch for a draft trading a fixed sum now against a retirement account later with no adjustment for the tax due on withdrawal, a maintenance clause written as permanent and unmodifiable, or a parenting schedule with no holiday table at all. Section 14-10-124(7) says a parenting plan must be as specific as possible, and it lists what belongs in one. That list runs to the type of decision-making awarded, a practical schedule including holidays and school vacations, and a procedure for exchanges covering location and who transports the child. It also calls for procedures on how the parents communicate about the child, and how a parent communicates with the child outside their own time.

### What happens after the petition is filed

Agreed cases still meet Rule 16.2(c)(1)(E), which sets the Initial Status Conference or a filed Stipulated Case Management Plan no later than 42 days from filing. Filing that plan together with the mandatory disclosures and the Certificate of Compliance exempts both parties from attending. Otherwise the conference happens with a Family Court Facilitator, a magistrate, or a judge, who reviews the drafted forms and clarifies what is still outstanding. Denver's DEN 2 is the local Initial Status Conference notice, and every DEN form is restricted to Denver District Court and must be completed in English.

### How fees work in a Colorado domestic case

Colo. RPC 1.5(c)(4)(ii) prohibits an agreement where payment is contingent on the securing of a divorce or on the amount of maintenance or child support, or property settlement in lieu of those amounts. That rules out any percentage of the outcome, so payment here is hourly or a flat sum for a stated scope, communicated in writing before or within a reasonable time after the work starts, with unearned advances held in trust as client property. An uncontested matter is the one place a fixed scope genuinely fits, which makes the useful question narrow: what is inside the defined number, and what event pushes the case out of it. The court's own charges are separate, and a $260 petition fee is the same $260 whether anyone disagrees or not.

High-asset in Denver rarely means cash sitting in an account. It means a house that has appreciated hard since the wedding, a pension the city itself administers, restricted stock, and a business whose books were never built to be read by a court.

## svc_high_asset_divorce_lawyer_body

### How you know this is a valuation case

The tell is not the size of the number. It is how many of your assets have no single obvious value: a closely held business, a rental with a mortgage and a depreciation history, restricted stock that vests on a schedule, a defined benefit pension. Where a spreadsheet of balances answers the question, the case is arithmetic. Where value depends on a method, a date, and an expert, the case is valuation and tracing, and every deadline is really a deadline about documents.

### What 14-10-113 does with property you brought in

Marital property under 14-10-113(2) is everything either spouse acquired after the marriage, except property acquired by gift, bequest, devise, or descent, property acquired in exchange for something owned before the marriage or received by gift, property acquired after a decree of legal separation, and property excluded by valid agreement. Subsection (3) presumes everything acquired during the marriage is marital regardless of whose name holds title, and that presumption falls only on a showing that the asset came in through one of those exceptions. Then subsection (4) does the part people miss: an asset owned before the marriage is marital property to the extent that its present value exceeds its value at the time of the marriage. Denver's median owner-occupied home value was $586,700 in the Census Bureau's 2019 through 2023 figures.

Take a Potter Highlands Victorian inside the historic district Denver designated in 1987, or a Washington Park house from the era around Reinhard Schuetze's 1899 park design. Bought before a wedding and held through the run-up, either one carries a marital component measured in hundreds of thousands of dollars. The size of that component turns entirely on a date-of-marriage valuation nobody thought to commission.

### Denver pensions do not divide the way private plans do

This is the local trap. Employees of the City and County of Denver are in the Denver Employees Retirement Plan, not PERA. DERP states that the pension benefit is marital property, and that it will not accept or honor a Qualified Domestic Relations Order under any circumstances, because as a governmental plan it falls outside the QDRO provisions of federal law. Division instead runs on a Domestic Relations Order made in accordance with Section 18-418 of the Revised Municipal Code of the City and County of Denver, on DERP's own forms, which must be used without change.

The timing is unforgiving. The order must be approved and entered by the court at the decree and permanent orders or within 180 days afterward, and must reach DERP within 90 days after entry. Where a percentage is elected against a vested member's benefit, the amount may be reduced by as much as thirty percent depending on age at retirement. State employees and school district staff sit in PERA instead, divided through JDF 1202. Sending the wrong plan the wrong instrument wastes a window that does not reopen.

### The first week

Preserve first, then inventory. Download statements in their native format rather than as screenshots, keep three years of returns with every schedule and K-1, obtain plan documents instead of balance summaries, pull the deed and a payoff letter for each property, and locate purchase records for anything owned before the wedding. The injunction under 14-10-107(4)(b) already restrains both of you from transferring, encumbering, concealing, or disposing of marital property outside the usual course of business, and requires notice of proposed extraordinary expenditures plus an accounting to the court. Selling a rental mid-case is the version of this that ends up quoted in a hearing.

### The mistake that costs the case

Accepting a balance as a value. A business operating out of Cherry Creek, a duplex in Five Points, and a block of restricted stock each need a stated method and a stated date, not a figure. A retirement account and a taxable account with identical face values are not worth the same after tax, and dividing them as if they were hands one spouse a quiet loss. Understating income on a Sworn Financial Statement runs straight into Rule 16.2(e)(1), which the court can revisit after the decree.

### What the other side does

Retains a valuation expert who reaches a conveniently low conclusion, characterizes marital appreciation as separate property, times a bonus to land outside the disclosure period, and offers you the house together with its mortgage in exchange for the liquid accounts. Section 14-10-119 matters here: after considering the financial resources of both parties the court may order one spouse to pay a reasonable amount of the other's fees and costs, which is what keeps a spouse who never controlled the accounts able to litigate at all.

### What happens after filing

Rule 16.2 sets the Initial Status Conference within 42 days of filing and the mandatory disclosures, with the Sworn Financial Statement, within 42 days of service. A valuation-heavy case usually leaves that conference with a longer case management order, room for expert disclosure, and a mediation referral before any contested hearing gets set.

### How fees work under Colorado's rule

Colo. RPC 1.5(c)(4)(ii) forbids an agreement in a domestic relations matter where payment is contingent on the securing of a divorce or on the amount of maintenance or child support, or property settlement in lieu of those amounts. A share of the estate is therefore not available as a payment method in Colorado, however large the estate is, and this is billed hourly or as a defined flat scope against an advance held in trust. Third-party costs sit outside that: a real property appraisal, a business valuator, an actuary where a pension has to be divided. Comment [6] allows one contingent arrangement in family law, for recovering post-judgment balances already due under support, maintenance, or other financial orders.

## svc_divorce_mediation_lawyer_lede

Mediation is a negotiation run by a neutral who decides nothing. In a Denver case it usually sits between the Initial Status Conference and a contested hearing, and both spouses pay for the room by the hour while it happens.

## svc_divorce_mediation_lawyer_body

### How you know mediation fits your case

It fits when the disagreement is about figures and schedules and both people can exchange information honestly. It fits badly or not at all where there is coercive control, a safety concern, or a spouse who has not produced financial disclosures, because a neutral cannot help divide an estate that one side has not shown. Colorado's own filing instructions describe mediation as something the court may order when spouses cannot agree on the property and financial agreement, so in Denver it frequently arrives as a direction out of the Initial Status Conference rather than as a choice either party made.

### What the rules put around the room

The mediator has no authority to rule on anything, and nothing said in the session binds anyone until it is written, signed, and approved. What the session produces is a memorandum of understanding, which then has to become a Property and Financial Agreement on JDF 1115 and a parenting plan a judge can approve.

Two Colorado requirements shape what is worth mediating at all. First, Rule 16.2(e)(2): mandatory disclosures on Form 35.1 and a completed Sworn Financial Statement on Form 35.2 within 42 days after service, exchanged by the time of the Initial Status Conference so far as reasonably possible. Second, the statutory frameworks are the backdrop every offer is measured against. Section 14-10-113 divides marital property without regard to marital misconduct. Section 14-10-114(3)(b) sets an advisory maintenance figure at forty percent of combined monthly adjusted gross income minus the lower earner's, for marriages of at least three years where combined annual adjusted gross income does not exceed $240,000. Its advisory term table runs from eleven months at a three-year marriage to fifty-four months at ten years. Section 14-10-115 builds child support from combined adjusted gross income on the statutory schedule, split in proportion to each parent's income. A proposal sitting far outside those frameworks needs a reason that survives being said out loud.

### Preparing, which is most of the outcome

Because the clock runs, walk in with finished work rather than intentions. Have disclosures already exchanged, a single page listing every asset and debt with a value, a date, and the document behind each, your written list of open terms in priority order, and the actual draft language instead of a description of it. For parenting, 14-10-124(7) tells you what the plan must contain: the type of decision-making awarded, a practical schedule including holidays and school vacations, and an exchange procedure covering location and transportation. It also requires procedures for the parents to communicate about the child, and for a parent to reach the child outside that parent's time. Draft those clauses beforehand. Nobody writes a good holiday schedule at hour four of a session.

### The mistake that costs the case

Signing a memorandum that reads like a summary rather than an instrument. Housing is where this bites hardest in Denver. Park Hill was first platted in 1887 and is full of homes worth far more than the loans against them. An agreement letting one spouse remain in that house, with no refinance deadline, no deed deadline, and no allocation of taxes and insurance, leaves both names on the note indefinitely. Maintenance terms drafted without saying whether they can be modified, and parenting terms with no holiday provisions, return as post-decree motions. A motion to modify a decree or final order filed more than sixty days after entry carries a $105 filing fee before anybody's time is counted.

### What the other side does

Anchors far out on the first exchange, holds one item hostage for a concession on another, and pushes for a signature in the room while everyone is tired and the meter is running. A spouse who has not fully disclosed resists putting values on paper and prefers round-number trades that cannot be checked. The reliable pressure line is that the offer expires when the session does, which is not how any of this works: an unsigned proposal has no legal life either way, and a partial agreement is a normal, useful result.

### What happens after the session

Whatever is agreed gets reduced to writing, signed, and filed for the court's approval, and the decree still cannot enter until the ninety-one days in 14-10-106(1)(a)(III) have run. Where only some terms settle, those get filed and the remainder goes to the contested hearing on the date the court set at the Initial Status Conference. Denver District Court publishes DEN 4, a Simplified Separation Agreement, and DEN 5, a Simplified Parenting Plan, both restricted to that court and both required to be completed in English.

### How fees work under Colorado's rule

Two separate costs, and people conflate them. The neutral: the Judicial Branch Office of Dispute Resolution charges each party $75 per hour for mediation services, case preparation, and preparing the memorandum of understanding. That office may also collect a two-hour advance deposit on a domestic relations case, and may bill up to two hours against a party who cancels late or fails to appear. Private mediators set their own rates and are outside that schedule. The lawyer: Colo. RPC 1.5(c)(4)(ii) prohibits an agreement where payment is contingent on the securing of a divorce or on the amount of maintenance or child support, or property settlement in lieu of those amounts. Representation here is therefore hourly, or a defined flat scope against an advance held in trust. Comment [6] permits one contingent arrangement in this field, for collecting post-judgment balances already owed under support, maintenance, or other financial orders.
