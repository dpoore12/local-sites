## symptom_1_title
A petition or summons has arrived, and the next procedural step is unclear

## symptom_1
A petition, summons, waiver, case-management order, or notice of an Initial Status Conference is not a verdict about the case. It is a document in a court process, and the wording, date, and method of service can matter. Colorado’s court materials distinguish a joint filing from a filing by one spouse. When both people file together, the petition starts the case without the separate service step. When one person files, the other person generally needs formal notice and then has a response path. The court’s current divorce instructions identify the petition, summons, case information sheet, service materials, and response as separate pieces of that sequence.

The immediate task is usually administrative: keep every page, note the date received, identify the court and case number, and read the specific order rather than relying on an internet summary. A case-management order may set expectations that are not visible from the petition alone. A person should not ignore a scheduled conference, a response deadline, or a request for documents simply because the other spouse says an agreement is likely.

Colorado does not treat the 91-day period as a deadline for finishing a divorce. It is the earliest period before a decree may enter under the conditions in the statute. A jointly filed petition, service, or a signed waiver can affect when that period begins. Cases can take longer when documents, parenting issues, property questions, court scheduling, or unresolved terms require more work. The useful question is not whether the situation is “simple”; it is what the filed papers require next and what information must be assembled to answer them accurately.

## symptom_2_title
Property, debt, or support terms are still disputed

## symptom_2
Disagreement over a home, a retirement account, credit-card debt, a business interest, maintenance, or a proposed separation agreement is not resolved by deciding whose contribution felt larger. Colorado divorce work usually needs a clear record first: what exists, what it is worth, what is owed, when it was acquired, and what documents support each point. That record can include account statements, deeds, loan records, tax returns, payroll information, benefit documents, and information about a closely held business. The relevant documents may be spread among personal files, online accounts, lenders, and employers, so starting a list early can reduce confusion later.

Colorado’s mandatory-disclosure form illustrates the range of material that may need to be exchanged. It lists a sworn financial statement, recent tax returns, debt records, real-estate documents, investment information, retirement-plan documents, account statements, and income documentation. A person should not assume that an informal spreadsheet, a single account screenshot, or a verbal description gives the same picture. Nor should a proposed agreement be treated as self-explanatory if it assigns debt, addresses a house, uses a valuation, or affects future payments.

Some cases reach agreement on all terms. Others require negotiation, mediation, additional information, or a court hearing. None of those paths establishes what a fair outcome will be in a particular matter. The practical work is to identify the items that need records, preserve documents without altering them, and obtain advice about the actual terms before signing or filing an agreement. If the household budget is changing during the case, keep a current list of recurring expenses and income rather than reconstructing it months later.

## symptom_3_title
The household includes significant or complicated financial assets

## symptom_3
A case involving a Denver home, a rental property, stock awards, retirement plans, a family business, partnership interests, bonuses, commissions, deferred compensation, or multiple debts needs more than a rough estimate of “what we have.” The issue is often the quality and timing of information. Account values can change. A business may have records separate from personal taxes. A retirement plan may require plan-specific documents. Real property has title, loan, insurance, and value records that do not fit on one summary page. Missing information can make a proposed division difficult to evaluate.

Colorado’s disclosure materials call for documents identifying investments, employment benefits, retirement plans, bank and financial-institution accounts, income, debts, and real property. For a self-employed person, the form also calls for a sworn statement addressing gross income, necessary business expenses, and net income for the period identified in the rule. This is why “high asset” does not merely mean a high number. It can mean more categories of records, more questions about valuation, and more care in reading any settlement language.

Start with preservation and organization. Download statements in their original form where possible, retain tax returns with attachments, gather plan descriptions, keep property and debt records, and make a list of any accounts or interests that are not in one person’s name alone. Whether financial disclosure can be simplified, what other information is required, and how any property term should be written depend on the individual case. ## symptom_4_title
Mediation is being considered, or an agreement needs careful review

## symptom_4
Mediation can be one way for spouses to work through unresolved terms, but it is not a label for every case and it does not make the court process disappear. Colorado’s filing instructions say that mediation may be ordered when people cannot agree on the property and financial agreement. In a mediated discussion, the practical subjects may include property, debt, maintenance, parenting responsibilities, support, and the language of a proposed agreement. The process may be useful when both people can exchange information and work on terms; it may not fit every circumstance, particularly where safety, coercion, missing disclosure, or a severe power imbalance is present.

An agreement deserves careful reading because it may connect several issues at once. A choice about the house can affect debt, refinance timing, occupancy, taxes, insurance, and other provisions. A parenting plan can interact with decision-making, schedules, support, and school or medical responsibilities. A short phrase can be carrying more weight than it appears to carry on the page. Reaching an agreement is different from understanding each term and its practical consequences.

Before mediation or before accepting a draft, collect the financial records that relate to the subject being discussed, write down the unresolved points, and bring the complete proposed language rather than a verbal summary. Denver District Court has local family-law materials for use there only, while statewide JDF forms address the broader Colorado process. That distinction is another reason to check which court is handling the case and which forms apply. Legal advice is especially important before signing a separation agreement, parenting plan, or other filing that affects rights or obligations.

## qa_1_question
Is Colorado a no-fault divorce state?

## qa_1_answer
Colorado’s Uniform Dissolution of Marriage Act makes irretrievable breakdown of the marriage relationship the sole basis for dissolution. The petition must allege that the marriage is irretrievably broken. That does not decide property, debt, parenting, support, or the language of an agreement. It means the statutory basis for the divorce is not a need to prove marital wrongdoing. Colorado Revised Statutes section 14-10-106 and section 14-10-110 set out the irretrievable-breakdown framework. If one spouse denies that the marriage is irretrievably broken, the court has a statutory process for considering that issue. The facts and legal questions in any individual Denver case still need individual analysis.

## qa_2_question
How long does a divorce take in Colorado?

## qa_2_answer
Colorado’s court instructions state that a divorce cannot be finalized until at least 91 days have passed, even if both spouses agree on every issue. The clock begins when a joint petition is filed, or when the other spouse is served or signs a waiver in a separately filed case. That is a minimum waiting period, not a promise of completion on day 91. The court’s instructions also address filing, service, a response, financial documents, an Initial Status Conference, and later case steps. A matter can take longer because of the issues involved, the information needed, the parties’ agreements or disagreements, and court scheduling.

## pricing_lede

A divorce fee in Colorado cannot be tied to getting the divorce or to the size of the maintenance, child support or property number, so it is billed by the hour or as a flat fee for defined work. This page sets out exactly how narrow that prohibition is, what the written fee terms have to say, and what the court and its mediation program charge on a Denver case.

## pricing_body

### The prohibition is real, and narrower than people repeat

The operative language is Colo. RPC 1.5(c)(4), which lists the agreements that may not be made at all. Subparagraph (i) covers representing a defendant in a criminal case. Subparagraph (ii) is the domestic relations provision: no contingent fee agreement may be made in a domestic relations matter where payment is contingent on the securing of a divorce or upon the amount of maintenance or child support, or property settlement in lieu of such amounts. Subparagraph (iii) sweeps in any proceeding where a contingency method of determining attorney fees is otherwise prohibited by law.

Read what that actually forbids. It forbids payment riding on the decree issuing, and it forbids payment scaled to the support or property figure. It is not a rule that every dollar in a dissolution has to be paid before the work happens. The comment to the rule makes the boundary explicit in the other direction: the prohibition does not preclude a contract for a contingent fee for legal representation in connection with the recovery of post-judgment balances due under support, maintenance or other financial orders, because those contracts do not implicate the same policy concerns. A parent chasing years of unpaid support is in a different position from a spouse bargaining over the decree.

### So the fee is hourly, or flat, and both have written requirements

Rule 1.5(b) requires that before or within a reasonable time after the representation starts, the lawyer communicate to the client in writing the basis or rate of the fee and the expenses the client will be responsible for, plus the scope of the representation. Any later change to the basis or rate has to be communicated in writing too. Scope is the part that decides whether a number is comparable to another number: an engagement that ends at a temporary orders hearing is not the same product as one that carries through disclosure disputes, a parental responsibilities evaluation and a contested hearing.

Money paid in advance is governed by Rule 1.5(f). Fees are not earned until the lawyer confers a benefit or performs a service, advances of unearned fees are the property of the client, and they sit in the lawyer's trust account until earned. Rule 1.5(g) prohibits nonrefundable fees and nonrefundable retainers outright, along with any agreement that unreasonably restricts the client's right to a refund of unearned or unreasonable fees. If a flat fee is used, Rule 1.5(h) requires the terms in writing, including a description of the services, the amount and timing of payment, what is earned at which milestone if any part is earned before the case ends, and how much is earned if the representation stops early.

### What the court charges, and what it costs to change something later

Colorado's filing fees are statutory and published by the Judicial Branch. A petition for divorce, legal separation, annulment or declaratory judgment is $260 under C.R.S. 13-32-101(1)(a) and its surcharge subsections. A response is $146. A petition for allocation of parental responsibilities filed on its own is $252. Registering a foreign decree or judgment is $201. After the case is over, a motion to modify a decree or final order filed more than sixty days after it entered costs $105, which is the amount that matters most, because a Denver decree with parenting time and support in it is a document people come back to.

Court-connected mediation is priced separately. The Judicial Branch Office of Dispute Resolution charges each party $75 per hour for mediation services, case preparation and preparation of the memorandum of understanding, and neutrals may collect a two-hour advance deposit on a domestic relations case. A neutral may bill up to two hours against a party who does not appear or cancels without at least seven days' notice. Private mediators set their own rates and are not bound by that schedule.

### One spouse can be ordered to pay the other's fees

C.R.S. 14-10-119 lets the court, after considering the financial resources of both parties, order one party to pay a reasonable amount for the other's cost of maintaining or defending the proceeding, including attorney or licensed legal paraprofessional fees, and including services rendered and costs incurred before the case was filed or after judgment. The court may order the money paid directly to the professional. In a marriage where one spouse controlled the income and the accounts, that statute is the mechanism that keeps the lower-earning spouse in the case, and it is worth raising early rather than at the end.

### The costs that are not the fee at all

Expenses in a Denver dissolution sit outside the hourly rate: the filing fee, service on the other spouse, subpoenas for account records, a real property appraisal, a business valuation, a pension actuary where a Public Employees' Retirement Association or federal benefit has to be divided, and a parental responsibilities evaluator if the parenting dispute goes that far. Ask which expenses require approval before they are incurred, what the billing increment is, whether paralegal time is billed at a lower rate, and how the file transfers if the case is handed off.
