## Three sourced local facts

1. **Colorado uses an irretrievable-breakdown standard.** The Uniform Dissolution of Marriage Act makes irretrievable breakdown the sole basis for dissolution, and a petition must allege that the marriage is irretrievably broken. Source: https://leg.colorado.gov/sites/default/files/images/olls/crs2024-title-14.docx

2. **Colorado has a 91-day minimum period before a divorce can be finalized, even if spouses agree on all issues.** The Colorado Judicial Branch explains that the period begins with a joint filing, or with service or waiver in a separately filed matter. Source: https://www.coloradojudicial.gov/sites/default/files/2025-07/JDF1010.pdf

3. **Denver District Court handles domestic-relations cases, including dissolution of marriage; Colorado’s instructions require each spouse to file a Sworn Financial Statement and Certificate of Compliance within 42 days after filing or receiving the petition.** Denver also publishes local family-law forms for use in Denver District Court only. Sources: https://www.coloradojudicial.gov/denver-district-court?language=en ; https://www.coloradojudicial.gov/sites/default/files/2025-07/JDF1010.pdf ; https://www.coloradojudicial.gov/courts/trial-courts/denver-district/denver-local-forms
