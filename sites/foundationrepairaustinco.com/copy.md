## what_happens_when_you_call

## what_they_will_ask

Have a few useful details ready: the neighborhood, whether the house is slab-on-grade or raised on a crawl space, the approximate build decade, and the rooms where doors began dragging or floors began to feel different. Note whether cracks are vertical, stair-stepped along brick mortar, or diagonal from a window corner. It also helps to know whether the change appeared through one dry season, after rain returned, or has continued regardless of season. Photos taken from the same spot months apart are more useful than a close-up with no scale. The goal is not to diagnose a house from a call; it is to arrive prepared to measure the right areas.

## qa_1_question

How can I tell seasonal movement from structural failure?

## qa_1_answer

Start with change, pattern, and measurement. One stable hairline crack or one humidity-sensitive door may be cosmetic. Concern rises when several symptoms appear in the same area: widening diagonal or stair-step cracks, doors that increasingly bind, persistent floor slope, exterior gaps, or a slab edge that has changed. Date and photograph the evidence from the same location. An on-site evaluation should compare those observations with a systematic floor-elevation survey and exterior conditions. In Austin, a seasonal clay response can be real, but a repeated or worsening pattern still deserves a plan rather than a guess.

## qa_2_question

What should a real engineer’s report contain that a free inspection may not?

## qa_2_answer

An engineering report should document what was observed and how the conclusion was reached. Look for property identification, accessible-condition observations, crack locations and directions, elevation data or another stated measurement method, photographs or diagrams, an explanation of the opinion, recommended action if justified, and limits on what could not be observed. A repair-company visit can be useful, but it often ends in that company’s repair proposal. ## pricing_body

### A pier price is not a job price

Homeowners misread this market by anchoring on the per-pier number. Austin companies publish per-pier figures between roughly $900 for a pressed concrete piling and $4,000 for a helical steel pier driven to load-bearing capacity. Both are real, and neither answers the question, because the total is pier price times pier count, plus mobilization, access and whatever gets put back. Eight to twelve piers along one settled side is a different project from twenty-four around a full perimeter.

Pier count is not negotiable either. It comes out of an elevation survey and a load path, so a lower count at the same coverage is a weaker repair. Compare pier type, count, depth criteria and lift target first; the dollar figure only means something once those match.

### Method drives cost more than brand does

Pressed concrete pilings are cylinders driven hydraulically in segments until refusal, and they suit a typical slab in stable material at the lower end of the band. Steel push piers go deeper and carry more. Helical piers are turned in to a measured torque where soil is inconsistent or a lift must be precise, and they top the per-pier band. Injection is a separate category: it fills voids and lifts flatwork rather than supporting the structure, so it is no substitute for underpinning.

Access decides the rest. Exterior piers along a clear side yard go quickly. Interior piers mean cutting the slab or tunneling from outside, and tunneling is priced by the foot. Add a patio to demolish or a plumbing line under the slab and the same pier count costs more.

### The Austin soil question, stated precisely

The USDA official series description for the Austin soil series puts clay at 35 to 55 percent of the particle-size control section. That single fraction is the mechanism behind nearly every slab complaint here. High-clay material expands when wet and contracts when dry, and a slab on it rides that movement seasonally. What makes the metro unusual is how sharply the ground changes across it. West of the Balcones escarpment the material is thin over limestone, so failures tend to be localized. East and southeast the clay is deep, and movement is broad and cyclical.

So two houses ten minutes apart get different diagnoses and different prices, and a standard package quoted without an elevation survey has skipped the part that decides both.

### What the engineering standard actually says

The Texas Section ASCE guidelines for the evaluation and repair of residential foundations, Version 2, state that the evaluation and repair design shall be performed by a professional engineer licensed in the State of Texas. The engineer who seals the documents is the engineer of record, with approval authority over changes. The same guidelines adopt the residential code deflection limit of L over 360 as the analogous limit for judging a foundation profile, and note that a floor slope greater than 1 percent is usually noticeable.

Read that against how repairs get sold. Drywall cracks and a sticking door are symptoms, not measurements. The engineer's question is whether the measured profile exceeds the deflection limit. That is why the evaluation row earns its cost on any job past a few piers: a sealed report sets an independent lift target and a document to hold the work against. Austin handles foundation repair through its Residential Express Building Permit, so paperwork is light but an inspection still attaches.

### Reading a fair foundation proposal

It includes the elevation readings in a diagram with low points marked. It states pier type, count, and locations, plus the refusal or torque criteria used to stop driving. It states the intended lift in inches and admits what will crack during it. It names who runs a plumbing test before and after, which matters on clay where a leaking sewer line under a slab is often the cause rather than the consequence. It separates patching, landscaping, and cosmetic repair into their own lines, and puts warranty terms and transferability in writing.

### What these numbers exclude

The plumbing repair if a hydrostatic test fails. Drainage correction, root barriers, and grading, which are what keep a fix stable. Drywall, tile, and paint after a lift. Fence sections removed for access. Engineering fees bought independently. And the second phase some houses need after a season of watching.

## svc_foundation_crack_repair_lede

A crack is evidence, not a diagnosis. The useful work starts with reading its direction, width and history, deciding whether the movement behind it has stopped, and only then closing it so the repair holds instead of reopening next August.

## svc_foundation_crack_repair_body

### Reading the crack instead of just finding it

Four things about a crack matter, and its existence is not one of them. Direction: vertical in poured concrete is common shrinkage, while a diagonal climbing away from the upper corner of a window or a door is following a support that moved. Width along its length: a break that tapers to nothing at one end is telling you which end dropped. The company it keeps: a single line in drywall is noise, while the same line matched by stair-stepped brick mortar outside and a door that no longer latches is a pattern. Time: a crack you can date is worth ten you cannot. Mark each end with a pencil tick and the month, photograph it against a coin, and look again after the next wet spell.

### The two Austins, and the two crack patterns they produce

This city is split by a fault zone, and that is why a neighbor's answer is useless. The Bureau of Economic Geology puts it bluntly: the Balcones Escarpment generally parallels MoPac, west of MoPac Austin becomes hilly, and east of MoPac it is relatively flat. The east side dropped by as much as 700 feet along that zone, which the Bureau describes as a band of faults reaching two to five miles east of the escarpment face. West of the line, in Tarrytown and out toward Circle C Ranch, the near-surface rock is Lower Cretaceous Glen Rose and Edwards limestone and dolomite. East of it, in Hyde Park, Mueller and East Cesar Chavez, the section is younger and softer. The USGS survey of the Austin quadrangle measured the Del Rio clay at 80 to 100 feet thick at Austin, and the Taylor beds, known locally as joint clays, at roughly 540 feet in the Colorado River section.

Cracks follow that difference. On deep clay the pattern is broad and cyclical, appears on the perimeter first, and often reverses part of the way when moisture comes back. Over shallow rock the pattern is localized: one corner over a fill pocket, a garage slab poured across a bench cut, a room built out onto a terrace where the soil thins to nothing.

### Why sealing it first destroys the information

Caulk and epoxy make a crack invisible without making it stop. Worse, they erase the two measurements an evaluation depends on, which are how wide it is now and where it ends. Injection is genuinely the right treatment for a structural break in concrete that has been shown to be static. It is the wrong treatment for anything still opening a fraction of an inch each dry season, because the resin becomes the new weakest line and the crack simply reappears beside it. Filling a crack that has water behind it is worse again: the pressure moves sideways and turns up in the next joint.

### The plumbing question that cancels a repair

A crack over deep east-side clay is often downstream of something plumbed. A leaking sewer branch under a slab in the older neighborhoods east of Interstate 35 will soften a bearing area for months before the floor shows it, and patching upstairs while that line keeps running guarantees a return visit. A hydrostatic test costs a fraction of the repair it can cancel. The same applies on the west side, where an irrigation zone bleeding into a fill pocket does the same job more quietly.

### What shortens the life of a repair here

Moisture whiplash, mostly. National Weather Service records for Camp Mabry put 16.07 inches of rain in 2008 and 19.68 inches in 2011 among the driest years in a period of record reaching back to 1891, and 2011 also delivered 90 days at or above 100 degrees. Then 2015 came in at 59.96 inches, the second wettest year on the list. Clay does not survive that politely. Trees add to it: the city counted 41 percent canopy cover in 2022, and a mature live oak or pecan drawing on the soil within reach of a footing is a seasonal load nobody put on the drawing.

### What the visit actually involves

Every crack gets located on a sketch with direction and width noted, inside and out. Floor elevations are read across the accessible area so the cracks can be compared against the actual profile. Drainage, grade, downspouts and irrigation are walked. Where the pattern suggests active movement, the plumbing gets tested before anything cosmetic is quoted. You should leave with a written scope that separates monitoring, sealing, and structural work into distinct decisions.

### What crack work costs in this market

Published Austin figures for sealing and injection sit low and in a fairly narrow band when no support work is involved. The number moves on crack length, whether access means moving landscaping or cutting interior finishes, and whether the same visit includes an elevation survey. A crack tied to real movement is priced as pier work instead, which is a different order of magnitude. The pricing page collects those ranges with the sources they came from.

## svc_basement_waterproofing_lede

Full basements are rare on Austin lots, so this work is usually about a below-grade room, a walk-out lower level cut into a hillside, or a crawl space that keeps taking water. The target is the water path, not the wall surface.

## svc_basement_waterproofing_body

### What counts as a basement on an Austin lot

Ask about a basement here and the honest answer is usually something adjacent to one. West of MoPac the ground is hilly, and a hillside house in Tarrytown or above Bee Creek often has a garage, a mechanical room or a finished lower level with earth against two walls and daylight on the third. East of MoPac the land flattens out, and the equivalent is a sunken den, a cellar under an older bungalow, or a raised floor whose crawl space sits lower than the yard. All three behave the same way: soil holds water against a wall, and the wall was built assuming it would not.

### Water reaches the two sides of town differently

This is the part generic advice gets wrong. In west Austin the aquifer under your street is karst. The USGS describes the Barton Springs segment as a system where most groundwater moves through voids formed by dissolution of the rock, with conduits and caves reaching a foot or more across, and recharge focused through streambed swallets and sinkholes rather than spread evenly through soil. Water there travels fast, arrives in a slug, and can appear against a wall hours after a storm that fell somewhere upstream of you.

East of the escarpment the mechanism inverts. The soils are heavy clays: the NRCS report the city commissioned for the La Loma site in East Austin maps Bergstrom, Houston Black and frequently flooded Tinn clay, and puts the clay units in hydrologic group D, the very slow infiltration and high runoff class. Water does not drain away there. It sits in the fill next to your wall, and the pressure builds until it finds a cold joint, a form-tie hole or a crack.

### The storms that actually test it

Austin's rainfall does not arrive gently. The city's own watershed report logged 2.96 inches at Camp Mabry in a single hour after 51 days without measurable rain. The 2013 Halloween morning flood, according to FEMA's account of the Onion Creek buyout, killed five people and damaged more than 500 homes, with parts of the city taking nearly 10 inches in 24 hours. Any lower-level room here has to handle a year's worth of load in an afternoon. That is also why a distinction matters early: creek flooding is a floodplain problem with its own remedies, while groundwater against a wall is a drainage problem you can engineer your way out of.

### What not to do, and the specific hazard

Paint-on masonry sealers are the standard first purchase and the standard waste of a weekend. A coating on the inside face has to resist hydrostatic pressure pushing outward through the wall, and it fails by peeling in sheets while the water simply moves along the slab edge. Two other habits cause real trouble. Cutting a trench that discharges toward the neighboring lot creates a dispute and can conflict with the city's drainage rules. Running a sump line into a sanitary cleanout is not allowed and can back sewage into the room you were drying out.

### The second visit nobody should have to pay for

The commonest version is waterproofing something that was never groundwater in the first place. A supply line weeping under a slab, an irrigation head aimed at a foundation wall, a condensate drain terminating in the flower bed, or a roof valley dumping into one corner will all read as a wet wall. Sequence matters: fix the roof and gutter path, correct the grade so it falls away from the wall, test the plumbing, and only then decide whether the water is still coming from the ground. Skipping that order is how a homeowner pays for an exterior drain and still has the puddle.

### What the work involves

Simple cases are solved outside. Downspouts get extended well clear of the wall, the grade around the perimeter is rebuilt to fall away, planting beds are pulled back, and a surface or French drain intercepts flow before it reaches the structure. Where water is already below grade, the work moves to a drain at footing level with washed stone and filter fabric. Other options are a membrane on the exterior face where the wall can be excavated, or an interior perimeter drain to a sealed sump with a discharge line that daylights somewhere safe. On a crawl space the same problem is answered with a vapor barrier, positive drainage to a low point, and a pump. Cracks in the wall itself get injected once the water is going somewhere else.

### Cost shape, and the permit line to watch

This work prices by linear foot and by how much has to be dug, so the honest range is wide and access dominates it. Hand-digging beside a mature oak, working under a deck, or trenching between a wall and a property line all cost more than an open side yard. Watch one line on the paperwork: Austin handles foundation repair through its Residential Express Building Permit only where the work does not add more than 128 square feet of impervious cover, and a plumbing permit may be required depending on what is being done. Concrete you pour for drainage counts. The pricing page lists published Austin ranges beside their sources.

## svc_house_leveling_lede

Leveling is the only one of these four jobs that changes the shape of the building. That makes measurement the whole argument: an elevation survey across the floor, a target lift stated in inches, and a published standard to judge the result against.

## svc_house_leveling_body

### The measurement that decides whether to lift at all

Cracks do not authorize a lift. A profile does. The Texas Section of ASCE, in its guidelines for evaluating and repairing residential foundations, borrows the residential code deflection limit of L over 360 as the analogous limit for a foundation profile and notes that a floor slope beyond 1 percent is usually noticeable to people living on it. That is a real threshold instead of a feeling. An evaluation worth paying for produces a grid of elevation readings across the accessible floor, marks the low areas, and shows how far out of tolerance the worst of them is. Without that grid nobody can state a target lift, and without a target lift a crew is guessing at your expense.

### Two structures, two leveling jobs

Austin hands you one of two problems depending on when the house went up. A slab-on-grade house is leveled by underpinning: piers driven or turned to a load-bearing stratum beside the beam, then jacked in a controlled sequence. Mueller, built out on the former Robert Mueller Municipal Airport with its first family arriving in 2007, is almost entirely this. A raised house is leveled from below by shimming and replacing supports, which is a different trade with different tools. Hyde Park, platted in 1891 and hitting its biggest building boom between 1924 and 1935, is full of bungalows on that kind of floor, as are the older blocks of East Cesar Chavez.

Depth to a bearing layer changes across the escarpment too. West of MoPac the USDA description of the Volente series, whose type location is in Travis County, records clayey sediments only four to six feet thick over limestone, and the Brackett series near the Hays County line reaches a paralithic contact within five to 20 inches. Piers there may refuse almost immediately. East of the line the Heiden description gives 40 to 60 percent clay weathered from mudstone with slickensides starting 10 to 30 inches down, and piers have to travel much further before they mean anything.

### The engineer rule Texas writes into statute

Most people assume residential work is exempt from engineering requirements, and for most residential work in Texas it is. Foundations on expansive soil are carved out by name. The Texas Engineering Practice Act exempts a private dwelling from its licensing requirements. The exemption does not apply to anyone providing engineering design relating to repairing, or drawing plans for, a residential dwelling slab located on expansive soil. The soil standard is the expansive soil classification provisions of the International Residential Code as applied in that jurisdiction, unless the work meets those code requirements. The same ASCE guidelines say the evaluation and repair design shall be performed by an engineer licensed in Texas, and that the engineer who seals the documents is the engineer of record with approval authority over changes. On a slab sitting in Blackland clay, a sealed design is not an upsell. It is how the work is supposed to be documented.

### What Austin's own process expects at the end

The city is explicit about where its responsibility stops. Its residential building inspection flowchart states that Austin does not conduct a physical foundation inspection, that a State of Texas licensed engineer must inspect the foundation, and that a copy of that engineer's sealed assessment is required for foundation approval. So the closing document on a job here comes from an engineer, not an inspector. Plan for it at the start rather than discovering it at the end.

### Do not put a jack under it yourself

Bottle jacks and stacked blocks under a beam are how people get hurt and how houses get broken. Lifting one point without releasing the neighboring supports concentrates the load into a few inches of wood or a short run of slab edge, and concrete has almost no tolerance for being bent upward quickly. Rapid lifting also shears cast iron drain lines passing through the beam. Controlled leveling moves in small increments across many points, watched from inside as it goes.

### Two omissions that undo a lift

The first is no plumbing test. On heavy clay a leaking under-slab line is frequently the cause of the settlement rather than a casualty of it, and a lift over a live leak is a temporary result. The second is no post-lift survey. If nobody re-shoots the elevations after jacking, nothing proves the profile actually improved, and any argument later becomes one person's memory against another's. Both belong in the scope in writing.

### What it costs, and the notice the state gives you

Underpinning is priced per pier and then multiplied, so the total is set by pier count, pier type, and access. Interior piers reached by tunneling or by cutting the slab cost far more than exterior piers along an open side yard, and landscaping, patio demolition and finish repairs are separate lines. Pier count itself falls out of the elevation survey, so a lower count for the same coverage is a weaker repair rather than a bargain. Texas also gives you a route if the contract goes wrong. The Deceptive Trade Practices Act requires written notice at least 60 days before suit, setting out the specific complaint and the amounts claimed, and it lets a court award up to three times economic damages where the conduct was knowing. Get the scope, the lift target and the pier criteria in writing, because that is the document any dispute turns on.

## svc_crawl_space_repair_lede

Under a raised Austin house the whole floor is carried by wood standing on isolated blocks. Crawl space repair replaces the members that have settled, split or rotted, and corrects the moisture that put them in that condition.

## svc_crawl_space_repair_body

### What is actually holding the floor up

A raised floor is a stack of parts, and any one of them can be the failure. Concrete or masonry blocks sit on pads in the dirt. Short posts stand on the blocks. Beams span between the posts. Joists run across the beams, and the subfloor sits on the joists. Nothing is continuous, so movement is local: one post settling an inch produces a dip in one room rather than a slope across the house. That is why the diagnosis has to happen from underneath. From upstairs, a dropped pad and a split joist look identical.

### Which Austin houses have them

Age and location decide it. The older central neighborhoods are where raised floors live. Hyde Park was platted in 1891 and built out heaviest between 1924 and 1935, mostly in bungalows, and the pre-war blocks of East Cesar Chavez east of Interstate 35 follow the same construction habits. Tarrytown, whose planning area the West Austin Neighborhood Group describes as bounded on the east by MoPac, developed in phases across the middle of the last century and mixes raised floors with later slabs. Clearance is usually the complication rather than the framing. A house built low to the ground gives 18 inches to work in, sometimes less, which changes both the method and the price of everything below.

### Moisture is usually the story

Wood under an Austin house does not fail from age. It fails from staying wet. The clay soils east of the escarpment shed rather than absorb. The city's own NRCS soil report for East Austin places the clay units in hydrologic soil group D, the very slow infiltration and high runoff class, and maps frequently flooded Tinn clay along the drainage. Water that cannot soak in runs sideways, under the skirting, and stands where nobody looks. Add a hot humid summer and a crawl space becomes a condensation chamber: 80 days at or above 100 degrees in 2023 by National Weather Service count, against a long-term average of 15, drives enormous vapor pressure toward the cool space under the floor.

That produces the classic sequence. A sill or beam end sitting on damp masonry wicks water, decay fungus gets established, the member loses stiffness, and the floor above starts to feel springy well before anything visibly collapses. Cedar posts, common under older houses here, last a long time but still rot at the bottom inch where they meet wet ground.

### What not to go under there for

A crawl space is a confined space with structure overhead that is already suspect. Do not crawl in to lift, jack or shim anything. A beam relieved at one point can drop at the next, and there is no room to get clear. Older wiring stapled to joists is often ungrounded and sometimes cloth-insulated, and standing water beneath it is a genuine electrical hazard. Gas lines run through the same tight space. Wasps, snakes and skunks take up residence under raised houses in this climate, and duct insulation in a house from the 1930s or 1940s may contain materials you should not disturb. Look in with a light from the access hatch, photograph what you can see, and stop there.

### Where a careless repair goes wrong

The classic error is shimming a rotten member. Driving a wedge under a beam whose bearing end has gone soft raises the floor for a season and crushes down again, and every repeat visit compresses the same failing wood a little further. The honest fix cuts out the decayed section, replaces the bearing with treated material on a new pad, and only then adjusts height. The second common miss is fixing the wood while leaving the water. If the grade still falls toward the house, the skirting still traps runoff, and the vents are still blocked by a flower bed, the new framing starts down the same road.

### What the visit involves

The crawl is entered and mapped: every pier, post, beam and questionable joist gets located, and suspect wood is probed with an awl to find how deep the decay goes. Floor elevations are read upstairs so the low spots can be matched to the supports underneath. Drainage, vents, ductwork and any standing water get recorded. The work then follows a fixed order. Get the water out and keep it out. Replace failed pads, posts and bearing ends with treated material. Sister or replace damaged joists. Bring the floor back to height gradually, watching the plaster and the door reveals above. Finish with a vapor barrier over the soil, clear ventilation, and skirting that lets air move instead of holding damp.

### What it costs

Raised floor work covers an unusually wide band because two jobs share a name. Releveling a house on sound framing with new shims and a few pads is a modest project. Rebuilding sills, replacing beams and posts, and doing it all in 18 inches of clearance is not, and low clearance is the single biggest multiplier because access dictates labor. Drainage correction, vapor barrier and any plumbing disturbed on the way through are separate lines. The pricing page carries published Austin ranges for raised floor releveling next to the sources they came from.

## svc_bowing_wall_repair_lede

A wall that bows inward is holding back soil and losing. The distinguishing sign is a horizontal crack partway up rather than a vertical one, and the repair has to remove the load as well as brace the wall.

## svc_bowing_wall_repair_body

### Reading the direction of the crack

Direction tells you the mechanism. A vertical crack in a foundation wall is usually shrinkage or a settlement break, and it belongs on the [foundation crack repair](../foundation-crack-repair/) page. A horizontal crack running along a course at roughly the middle third of the wall height is a bending failure: soil and water outside are pushing in, the wall is spanning between the floor below and the framing above, and it has hinged at its weakest line. On masonry that shows as a stepped horizontal joint opening, sometimes with the wall visibly out of plumb when a string line is dropped down its face. On poured concrete it is a single horizontal crack with the upper section displaced inward. Measure it: a plumb line at several points along the wall and the offset recorded in inches is the number that matters, not the crack width. [Retaining wall repair](../retaining-wall-repair/) is the same physics on a freestanding wall, and the [services index](../services/) covers the rest.

### Where Austin has walls under this kind of load

Full basements are unusual here, but soil-retaining walls that are part of the house are not. The Bureau of Economic Geology describes the ground west of MoPac as hilly, becoming relatively flat to the east, with the east side of Austin having dropped by as much as 700 feet along the Balcones fault zone. Building on those west-side slopes produces garages cut into a bank, mechanical rooms with earth against two sides, and finished lower levels that daylight on the downhill face. Each has a wall doing a retaining wall's job while also carrying the house. On the flatter east side the same situation appears as a raised foundation whose perimeter wall sits lower than the yard, retaining a few feet of clay along one side.

### Water is the variable load, and Austin delivers it abruptly

Soil pressure alone is fairly steady. Water pressure is not, and it is what pushes a wall past its capacity. The city's rainfall period-of-record study records Camp Mabry extremes of 11.52 inches in 1954 and 64.68 inches in 1919; the city's watershed report logged 2.96 inches in one hour after fifty-one consecutive days without measurable rain. A backfill of clay that has been shrinking all summer and then takes an hour like that goes from dry and cracked to saturated in one event, and the wall sees the full hydrostatic addition at once. That is why the residential code as adopted in Austin requires drains around foundations that retain earth and enclose usable space below grade, installed at or below the top of the footing, with stone extending a foot beyond the footing edge and covered by a filter membrane. A wall bowing today is usually a wall that never had that.

### Bracing without unloading is half a repair

The interior fixes for a bowing wall work by adding stiffness or tension across the bending line, and every one of them is undone by leaving the load in place. If clay is backfilled straight against the wall with no drainage layer, if the surface above still slopes toward the wall, if a downspout still discharges into that backfill, or if a parked vehicle sits over the retained soil surcharging it, then the pressure that bent the wall is still there after the bracing goes in. Sequence: get the surface water away and the subsurface water drained, reduce the surcharge, then brace. Straightening a wall that has moved significantly requires excavating the retained side to relieve pressure first, and that excavation is its own engineered operation.

### Why this one gets escalated rather than quoted quickly

A bowing wall is a wall losing capacity under load, and it is carrying structure. Do not excavate at the base on the inside to have a look, do not notch the framing bearing on the top of the wall, and do not stack storage against it. Where a wall is out of plumb by a visible amount, where the horizontal crack is wide enough to see daylight or soil, or where the top has begun to slide inward off its bearing, the space in front of it should be treated as off limits. Texas also puts a limit on who designs the fix for the most common Austin case: Section 1001.056 of the Texas Engineering Practice Act removes the residential exemption for engineering design relating to repairing a residential dwelling slab located on expansive soil meeting the code's expansive soil classification. Where a repair plan is required to come from a professional engineer licensed in Texas, that plan is a legal prerequisite and not something this site provides.

### What the assessment involves

The wall is strung and measured for maximum inward deflection and its location up the wall height, and the crack is mapped along its full length. What sits above the wall is identified, because a wall carrying a floor and a bearing partition is a different problem from a garage wall carrying only a plate. The retained side is investigated for drainage, surcharge, and the surface grade feeding it. Where the wall is stable but poorly drained, the work is drainage and grade correction. Where it is bending, the wall gets designed reinforcement, and the drainage still gets built. Cost is set by wall length, how far it has moved, whether the retained side can be excavated at all, and whether the outside face is reachable by machine.

## svc_chimney_settlement_repair_lede

A masonry chimney is a heavy, narrow column of brick sitting on its own footing, and it settles independently of the house. The gap opening between the chimney and the wall is the symptom; the footing under it is the problem.

## svc_chimney_settlement_repair_body

### Why the chimney moves on its own

A chimney concentrates a very large dead load onto a very small footprint. A full-height masonry stack with a firebox can weigh several tons, and all of it bears on a pad that may be a few feet square, while the house beside it spreads its load across a whole slab or a whole grid of piers. Different bearing pressure on the same soil means different settlement, so the two elements separate. The classic evidence is a wedge-shaped gap between the chimney face and the siding, widest at the top, with the flashing pulled and the hearth tilted away from the room inside. Record the gap width at the top and at the bottom, because the taper tells you the chimney is rotating rather than sinking straight. Related work sits on the [services index](../services/), and the profile that determines whether the house itself has also moved comes from [foundation inspection](../foundation-inspection/).

### What the code expects under a chimney, and what old Austin stacks actually have

The residential code as adopted in Austin is specific in Sections R1001.2 and R1003.2. Footings for masonry fireplaces and chimneys are constructed of concrete or solid masonry, are a minimum of twelve inches thick, extend at least six inches beyond the face of the fireplace or foundation wall on all sides, and are founded on natural undisturbed earth or engineered fill. In areas not subject to freezing, which includes Austin, the footing has to be at least twelve inches below finished grade. Now compare that to what is under an exterior chimney on a raised bungalow in Hyde Park or on one of the pre-war blocks of East Cesar Chavez: a shallow brick or rubble pad, often less than a foot thick, bearing directly on clay a few inches down, sometimes with no reinforcement at all. That is not a defect of workmanship, it is what was normal when the house was built, and it explains why the chimney is the first element on the house to move.

### The soil under that small footing

Shallow bearing on shrink-swell clay is the worst combination available, and Travis County supplies it. The USDA official series description for Houston Black, whose type location is in this county, reports high shrink-swell potential and cracks half an inch to four inches wide opening from the surface down to twelve inches or more, staying open for 90 to 150 cumulative days in most years. A footing founded twelve inches down is inside that cracking zone. An exterior chimney is also the one part of the house with soil exposed on three sides, so its footing sees a bigger moisture swing than anything under the slab. The city's period-of-record study shows only 0.05 inches of rain at Camp Mabry between June 22 and September 16 in 2011, and its watershed report logged 2.96 inches in a single hour after fifty-one dry days.

### What repair options actually exist

There are three honest categories and they are not interchangeable. Underpin the existing footing and lift the stack back into position, which means placing piers beside or beneath the footing and jacking against them. Rebuild the chimney above a defined line, which is what a stack with failed brick and mortar as well as a moved footing usually needs. Or remove the chimney and terminate it below the roof, which is the cheapest permanent answer where the fireplace is decorative and nobody wants it back. The Texas Section ASCE guidelines add a caution that applies directly to the first option: underpinning part of a structure may be specified where calculations, tests or experience show the unsupported structure can support its design loads, and the construction documents should state that underpinning will not improve the performance of the foundation in non-underpinned areas. Underpinning a chimney does nothing for the house, and a chimney lifted back against a house that is itself settling will separate again.

### Do not work on it while it is leaning

A leaning masonry stack is a stack of small units held together by mortar that has already been overstressed at one line. Do not climb it, do not tie a ladder to it, and do not load the roof beside it. Where a chimney has separated visibly from the wall above the roofline, the risk is the section above the last point of restraint. A chimney that has rotated may have opened the flue liner joints, which is a combustion products problem as well as a structural one, so the fireplace and any appliance vented into it stay out of service until it has been looked at.

### What the assessment involves and what it costs

The gap is measured top and bottom, the stack is checked for plumb on two faces, and the footing is exposed at a corner where possible to find its actual thickness, projection and bearing depth against the twelve-inch and six-inch code figures. Floor elevations are read inside so the chimney's movement can be compared against the house's own profile — if both moved, the scope is much larger than a chimney. Flashing, crown, mortar joints and the flue get recorded. Price separates along the three options: underpinning and lifting is priced per pier and by access, rebuilding by the number of courses above the roof, and removal is the least expensive of the three.

## svc_drainage_and_grading_lede

Drainage is the cheapest foundation work in Austin and the most often skipped. The goal is a measured slope and a defined discharge path, so that rainfall leaves the ground next to the structure before it can change the moisture content of the clay under it.

## svc_drainage_and_grading_body

### The two numbers that define the job

There is a code minimum and a repair standard, and they are not the same. Section R401.3 of the residential code as adopted in Austin requires lots to be graded to drain surface water away from foundation walls, with the grade falling not fewer than six inches within the first ten feet; where lot lines, walls or slopes make that impossible, drains or swales have to do the work, and impervious surfaces within ten feet of the foundation are sloped at least two percent away. The Texas Section ASCE guidelines for repairing residential foundations ask for more where it is practicable: a minimum slope of five percent, which is six inches in ten feet, for the first five feet all the way around, plus swales at a longitudinal slope of at least two percent and one percent as an absolute floor. Those are the acceptance criteria for a grading job. Anything else is landscaping. [Slab foundation repair](../slab-foundation-repair/) and [french drain installation](../french-drain-installation/) sit on either side of this work, and the [services index](../services/) lists the rest.

### Why grading carries so much weight on Austin clay

Because the mechanism is moisture, not water pressure. The clay under most of the east side changes volume with its water content, so the harm is done by where rainfall soaks in, not by how deep the puddle got. Austin's rainfall makes that violent. The city's rainfall period-of-record study puts the Camp Mabry extremes at 11.52 inches in 1954 and 64.68 inches in 1919, against a long-term mean near 33 inches, and records that in 2011 only 0.05 inches of rain fell between June 22 and September 16. The city's watershed report for 2022 logged 2.96 inches in a single hour at Camp Mabry after 51 consecutive days with no measurable rainfall. A yard that ponds against the beam turns that whiplash into a moisture gradient a few feet wide, which is exactly the loading a slab cannot absorb.

### Roof water is usually the largest single input

A roof concentrates more water in one spot than any other feature on the lot. The guidelines are specific: uncontrolled roof runoff can cause erosion and ponding near the structure, gutters and downspouts mitigate it, and downspouts should be extended well past the edge of the foundation, past the edge of abutting planting beds, and into well-drained areas. That last clause is where most installations fail. A downspout that discharges onto a bed pressed against the wall has moved the problem eighteen inches. Where surface grading alone cannot carry the volume, the guidelines call for solid pipe drainage systems, with the ground graded to slope to one or more inlets and cleanouts provided for maintenance. Solid pipe, not perforated — perforated pipe belongs to a subsurface system and should not have a downspout tied into it.

### The Austin rule that limits where the water can go

Regrading a lot is a change to drainage, and Austin regulates that. The city's drainage requirements sit in Title 25, Chapter 7 of the Land Development Code, and the operating principle it briefs to applicants is No Adverse Impact: a development cannot increase the flow or the timing of water leaving the site, cannot block flow that passes through the site under existing conditions, and has to discharge water responsibly downstream. Practically, that rules out the fix that gets attempted most — cutting a swale that dumps onto the neighbor's lot. Water leaving faster, arriving where it did not arrive before, or standing higher than it used to are all listed as adverse impacts. A grading plan that solves a foundation and creates a flooding complaint next door has not solved anything.

### The mistakes that repeat

Beds built up against the wall so the mulch line sits above the slab edge. A patio poured dead level, or sloped back at the house, adding twenty feet of impervious catchment right at the beam. Irrigation zones aimed at the foundation and run on a fixed schedule through a wet spell, which produces the same moisture gradient as bad grading. Air conditioning condensate piped into a bed at the corner of the house. And the most expensive one: fixing the grade after the structural repair rather than before, so the new piers immediately start working against the same soil moisture swings that caused the movement.

### What the work involves

The lot gets shot with a level, not eyeballed: elevations at the foundation, at five feet out, at ten feet out, and along the flow path to the discharge point. Downspout locations and volumes are noted. Beds are pulled back and lowered, and fill is placed and compacted to build the fall away from the structure across the first five feet. Swales are cut where a property line or a slope blocks a straight fall. Downspouts are extended into solid pipe with cleanouts and daylighted somewhere that can take the water. Where the site cannot shed water at the surface, the work moves to a subsurface drain, which is a different scope with different materials. Cost tracks the amount of dirt moved, the length of pipe, and whether a mature tree or a fence line means hand-digging.

## svc_foundation_inspection_lede

An inspection is a measurement, not an opinion. Its output should be floor elevations across the whole footprint, a drawing showing them, and a stated finding about whether the profile exceeds a defined limit. Anything less is a sales visit.

## svc_foundation_inspection_body

### What the recognized levels of investigation are

The Texas Section ASCE guidelines for the evaluation and repair of residential foundations set out three levels, and knowing which one you are buying prevents most disputes. Level A is first impressions — a walkthrough. Level B adds relative foundation elevations and a drawing showing them, which is the level most homeowners actually need. Level C adds soil sampling and testing, plumbing testing, material testing, a steel reinforcing survey, post-tensioning cable testing, scaled drawings, a tree survey and a detailed distress survey. A visit that produces a verbal conclusion and a price is a Level A, and it is not a basis for authorizing structural work. Other jobs are on the [services index](../services/).

### The number the profile is judged against

The guidelines adopt the residential code live-load deflection limit of L over 360 as potentially appropriate for the analogous in-service deflection of a residential foundation, and state that where a measured profile is less than that limit it is unlikely the foundation is deflected materially unless visible indications show otherwise. They add two useful calibration points: a floor slope greater than 1 percent is usually noticeable, and the Americans with Disabilities Act considers a 2 percent slope too large. Those numbers turn a subjective complaint into an arithmetic one. They also come with a caveat the guidelines state plainly — determining deflection and tilt of a slab-on-ground foundation is an approximation without an as-built or previous floor elevation survey, because the original surface configuration is unknown, and a floor elevation survey can provide valuable information but should not be the only basis for evaluating deflection and tilt.

### Why the Austin location changes what gets looked at

The city sits across a geologic boundary and the inspection has to account for which side of it the house is on. The Bureau of Economic Geology places the escarpment generally parallel to MoPac, with Austin hilly to the west and relatively flat to the east, and notes the east side moved downward by as much as 700 feet along a zone of faults extending roughly two to five miles east. West of that line the near-surface rock is Glen Rose and Edwards limestone with thin, rocky soils; east of it are the Upper Cretaceous units including the Taylor Clay, which the city's geologic appendix describes as dark gray shale weathering to a calcareous, montmorillonitic clay. On the west side an inspection is looking for localized bearing problems over shallow rock. On the east side it is looking for broad seasonal movement in deep clay, and the tree survey and drainage assessment carry real weight.

### The city's own process, and who must sign

This is the part that surprises people. In Austin's residential building inspection process, the city does not conduct a physical foundation inspection. Once the pre-foundation inspections are approved, the city's flowchart states that a State of Texas licensed engineer will need to inspect the foundation, and that a copy of their sealed assessment is required for foundation approval. Where a sealed letter is required, Austin's Building Criteria Manual section on the Structural Verification Report sets out what it must contain: the date of the site visit, the areas observed, detailed foundation and framing information, the current condition, the engineer's opinion of the adequacy of the existing structure to support anticipated loads, and a repair plan if one is required.

Texas law also decides who may design a repair. The Texas Engineering Practice Act exempts a person repairing or drawing plans for a private dwelling, but Section 1001.056 removes that exemption for anyone providing engineering design relating to repairing, or drawing plans or specifications for, a residential dwelling slab located on expansive soil that meets the expansive soil classification provisions of the residential code as applied in the jurisdiction. Austin's clay lots routinely meet that classification. The guidelines say the same thing from the profession's side: the evaluation and repair design shall be performed by a professional engineer licensed in the State of Texas. To be explicit, this site does not perform engineering services and does not issue engineering reports or sealed assessments. Where your situation requires one, it has to come from a licensed Texas professional engineer, and that is a legal requirement rather than an upsell.

### What a field measurement visit actually produces

Floor elevations read at a grid across the whole footprint, not at three convenient spots, plotted on a floor plan with the high and low points marked and the differential stated in inches. A distress survey: crack locations, widths, direction and whether they taper. Door and window operation recorded room by room, since a door binding on the latch side and one binding at the top are telling you different things. Exterior observations of the grade, the roof discharge points and the distance from significant trees. Where the finding points at water, a plumbing test is recommended before any structural scope.

### What it costs and what to do with it

A measurement visit is priced as a fixed fee. A sealed engineering assessment purchased independently from a licensed engineer costs more and is worth it on any job past a handful of piers, because it produces an independent lift target. Two surveys a season apart on the same grid tell you whether the house is moving or has moved, and on a clay lot that decides whether the correct action is repair or monitoring.

## svc_french_drain_installation_lede

A french drain is a perforated pipe in a stone-filled trench that intercepts water moving through soil and carries it to an outlet by gravity. It is the correct answer when surface grading has run out of room, and the wrong answer when the water is arriving over the top.

## svc_french_drain_installation_body

### Subsurface versus surface, and how to tell which one you need

These are two different systems and mixing them is the most common installation error in Austin yards. A surface system collects water already running across the ground and moves it in solid pipe to a discharge point. A subsurface system collects water traveling through the soil, and the Texas Section ASCE guidelines describe it as perforated pipe, with or without filter fabric, in an aggregate-filled trench, with a continuous minimum slope of half a percent to a surface outfall and cleanouts provided for maintenance. The same guidelines state flatly that downspouts should not be connected to perforated pipe subsurface drainage systems. Tie a roof into a french drain and it stops draining soil and starts injecting several hundred gallons directly into the ground next to the foundation. If the water you are chasing is sheet flow off a patio, the job is [drainage and grading](../drainage-and-grading/) instead.

### The construction detail the code actually specifies

Where a foundation retains earth and encloses habitable or usable space below grade, the residential code as adopted in Austin sets out the drain in Section R405.1, and the numbers are worth knowing because they are what separates a drain from a trench full of rock. The drainage system is installed at or below the top of the footing or below the bottom of the slab, and discharges by gravity or mechanical means into an approved drainage system. Gravel or crushed stone extends at least one foot beyond the outside edge of the footing and six inches above the top of the footing, and is covered with an approved filter membrane. Perforated pipe is placed on at least two inches of washed gravel and covered with at least six inches of the same material, with the washed rock at least one sieve size larger than the perforations so the openings do not plug. Those dimensions are why a properly built drain outlives a shallow one: the stone envelope, not the pipe, is what conducts the water.

### The filter is what decides whether it lasts

On Austin's east-side clay the failure mode is silting, not crushing. Clay particles migrating into the stone envelope fill the voids, and a drain that silted up conducts nothing while looking perfectly intact from the surface. That is what the filter membrane in the code detail is for, and it is the layer most often left out to save a few dollars on a job nobody can inspect afterward. The second life-limiting decision is the outlet. Half a percent of slope is a fall of about six inches over a hundred feet, so a long run on flat ground east of MoPac has very little room for error, and a drain that ends in a buried sock or a low spot in the lawn instead of a daylighted outfall is a storage tank. Cleanouts, which the guidelines call for, are the only way to ever flush the line.

### Where these go in Austin, and where they should not

The flat ground east of the escarpment produces the classic case: a yard with nowhere to fall, standing water along one elevation after rain, and clay that will not accept infiltration. On the hilly west side the problem inverts and a drain is more often used to intercept water moving down a slope before it reaches a lower-level wall or a garage cut into the hillside. Both need a real outfall, and on a small lot that is the constraint that kills otherwise sound designs. Austin's drainage rules do not allow a project to increase the flow or timing of water leaving the site, to block flow passing through it, or to discharge irresponsibly downstream, so a drain that solves one yard by loading the next is not a legitimate design.

### The permit item specific to trenching here

Digging a trench near a large tree is regulated in Austin, and a french drain is a trench. The city requires a Tree Ordinance Review Application for any activity that may impact a regulated tree or disturb its critical root zone, and its plumbing permit form states that installing utilities or irrigation lines within the critical root zone of a regulated tree requires that application. Any tree nineteen inches or more in diameter is regulated, and within the inner quarter of the root zone no impacts are permitted. Where the trench cannot avoid the zone, hand excavation and a permit are the path. [Root barrier installation](../root-barrier-installation/) runs into the same rules.

### What the work involves and what sets the price

The route is laid out from the wet area to a viable outfall, and the fall between the ends is measured before anything is dug, because if the fall does not exist the design has to change. Depth is set from the footing or slab elevation rather than from the lawn. The trench is cut, lined, filled with washed stone to the specified envelope, piped, wrapped, covered and brought back to grade with a soil cap that does not seal the top. Cleanouts go in at the ends and at direction changes. Cost follows length, depth, whether the spoil can be driven out or has to be barrowed, whether rock is hit — a real possibility west of MoPac on shallow soils over limestone — and whether tree roots and the ordinance force hand digging.

## svc_grade_beam_repair_lede

A grade beam is the thickened rib of concrete that stiffens a slab, at the perimeter and in lines across the interior. It is what makes a slab act as one piece over moving clay, and when it cracks the slab loses that stiffness.

## svc_grade_beam_repair_body

### What the beam does in an Austin slab

A residential slab on expansive soil is not a flat plate. It is a plate with integral downward ribs — a deep one around the perimeter and interior beams in a grid — and those ribs give it bending stiffness. On a shrink-swell profile that stiffness is the whole design premise: the soil will lift and drop unevenly, and the slab has to be rigid enough to span the difference rather than follow it. That is why the residential code requires foundations on expansive soils to be designed under the building code's expansive soil provisions, as the Texas Department of Licensing and Regulation's bulletin sets out, with expansive defined by a plasticity index of 15 or greater by ASTM D4318, more than 10 percent of particles passing a No. 200 sieve, more than 10 percent smaller than 5 micrometers, or an expansion index greater than 20 by ASTM D4829. Austin's clay lots meet those tests routinely.

### Why a beam is a different repair from a pier

A pier changes what the foundation bears on. A beam repair changes the foundation's own stiffness. They answer different findings and are not substitutes. The Texas Section ASCE guidelines make the dependency explicit from the pier side: performance of underpinning can be compromised by the integrity of existing slab components, changes in soil moisture, skin friction, point load and other factors. Read that in reverse and it says something useful — piering a slab whose beams are broken transfers load into a structure that cannot distribute it, so the repair underperforms. This is the reason a proposal should identify the condition of the beams before it counts piers. [Steel pier installation](../steel-pier-installation/) is the bearing-side job; this is the stiffness-side one. The [services index](../services/) lists both.

### What is loading the beam here

The soil, cyclically. The USDA official series descriptions for Houston Black, type-located in Travis County, and Heiden classify these soils as fine, smectitic, thermic Udic Haplusterts with high shrink-swell potential and 40 to 60 percent clay in the particle-size control section, and record cracks half an inch to four inches wide reaching twelve inches or deeper and staying open 90 to 150 cumulative days in most years. The geology beneath is the Taylor Group, which the City of Austin's geologic appendix describes as dark gray shale weathering to a greenish gray and brown calcareous, montmorillonitic clay generally referred to in the Austin area as the Taylor Clay. Loading that alternates between edge lift and center lift as the perimeter dries and rewets is exactly what a beam is designed to resist, and it is what eventually cracks one.

### Reading the evidence

You cannot see an interior beam, so the evidence is indirect and has to be assembled. Cracks in the slab surface running in a consistent direction, particularly where they cross a beam line. A step or offset across a crack, which indicates the two sides are no longer acting together — a crack with vertical displacement is a different finding from a hairline with none. Diagonal cracks at door openings and separation at the brick ledge on the outside. And a floor elevation profile whose shape is a bend concentrated at a line rather than a smooth dish, which suggests the section has hinged. The guidelines put cracking in perspective: cracks may make concrete structural members weaker, although the majority of cracks do not compromise structural integrity. That is the reason measurement rather than crack-counting drives the scope.

### What the repair consists of

Restoring the section's ability to transfer force across the break. The guidelines describe the primary tool: injecting slab cracks of about 1/32 inch and larger with epoxy repair cement is intended to restore stiffness across the injected crack. That is a specific claim with a specific threshold, and it is a stiffness repair rather than a cosmetic one. Where a beam has lost material — spalled concrete, exposed and corroded reinforcement, or a section damaged by excavation for plumbing — the repair is removal of the unsound concrete, treatment or replacement of the steel, and rebuilding the section. Where the slab is post-tensioned, tendon stressing enters the picture, and the guidelines are careful about it: it may be applicable when tests show tendon forces below those specified in the original design, and it should be performed after elevation adjustments and epoxy crack injecting if any. That sequence is not optional.

### Who designs it, and what it costs

This is engineered work. Section 1001.056 of the Texas Engineering Practice Act withdraws the residential exemption from anyone providing engineering design relating to repairing a residential dwelling slab located on expansive soil meeting the residential code's expansive soil classification, and the guidelines state that the evaluation and repair design shall be performed by a professional engineer licensed in the State of Texas. Where a plan is required it must come from a licensed Texas engineer; this site does not perform engineering services or issue engineering reports. Cost is driven by access — reaching an interior beam means cutting and restoring flooring and slab — by the linear footage of crack being injected, and by whether reinforcement has to be exposed and treated. Newer slab stock, including the post-2007 housing at Mueller, is generally engineered for these soils, which usually makes the finding localized rather than a whole-foundation scope.

## svc_mudjacking_and_slab_lifting_lede

Mudjacking pumps material under a slab through drilled ports to fill voids and adjust elevation hydraulically. It is the right tool for flatwork and for restoring support, and it is not underpinning — nothing about it reaches deeper soil.

## svc_mudjacking_and_slab_lifting_body

### Filling versus lifting, which are two different jobs

The Texas Section ASCE guidelines separate these precisely, and the distinction is what decides whether a proposal is honest. In general, grouting provides continuous slab support without lifting appreciably. Mudjacking, by contrast, is done to adjust elevations of a foundation hydraulically with continuous uniform support. So if the problem is a void under a slab that is otherwise at the right elevation, the objective is fill, and lift is neither promised nor needed. If the problem is a section of concrete that has dropped, the objective is elevation adjustment, and the material is placed to raise it while keeping support continuous underneath. Both appear under the same heading in the guidelines' list of slab-on-ground repair methods, alongside underpinning, crack injecting, tendon stressing and partial demolition and reconstruction. The other jobs are on the [services index](../services/).

### Where the voids come from in this soil

Two mechanisms produce them here and both are local. The first is shrinkage. The USDA official series description for Houston Black, whose type location is Travis County, records cracks half an inch to four inches wide extending from the surface to a depth of twelve inches or more, staying open for 90 to 150 cumulative days in most years. Clay that has pulled away downward leaves the slab above spanning air. The second is erosion, and Austin's rainfall pattern is built for it. The city's watershed report documents 2.96 inches of rain in a single hour at Camp Mabry, a one-hour record, arriving after 51 consecutive days with no measurable rainfall, and the city's period-of-record study notes rainfall exceeding five inches in several hours causing flash floods. Water at that intensity entering open shrinkage cracks beside a driveway or patio moves fines out from under the concrete and leaves a cavity behind.

### What this method is legitimately used for

Driveways, sidewalks, patios, garage floors, pool decks and porch slabs. These are non-structural or lightly loaded slabs where the concrete itself is sound and the problem is what is underneath it. A settled driveway panel that has dropped an inch at a joint, a walkway that ponds water against the house, a garage slab with a hollow ring when tapped — all of these are candidates, and all of them are far cheaper to fill and re-level than to demolish and repour. The related benefit is drainage: a patio that has tilted back toward the house is feeding water to the perimeter beam, and correcting that surface is part of the same problem addressed in [drainage and grading](../drainage-and-grading/). A trip hazard at a sidewalk joint is worth fixing on its own terms.

### The limits, stated plainly

This is not a substitute for underpinning. Injected material bears on the same soil that moved, so where the cause is deep clay cycling, filling the gap treats the gap and not the cause. The guidelines add a warning that matters most on Austin lots: grouting or mudjacking should not be performed beneath underpinned foundations if expected swelling of the soil in the injected area is sufficient to damage the structure. Read that carefully — it means injecting under a house that has already been piered can push against a foundation that is now held at fixed points, and the structure is what gives. That is a sequencing rule, and it is the reason the method and the order both belong in an engineer's plan rather than in a crew's judgment. Where a structural slab on expansive soil is involved, Texas law requires the repair design to come from a licensed professional engineer under Section 1001.056 of the Engineering Practice Act, and this site does not provide engineering services or issue engineering plans.

Two more limits are worth knowing. Injection does not repair cracked concrete; a panel broken into several pieces will lift as several pieces. And it does not restore reinforcement, so a slab that has failed because the steel corroded is a replacement candidate, not a lifting candidate.

### How the work runs

The slab is sounded to map where support has been lost, because the hollow area is usually larger than the visibly settled area. Elevations are read across the slab and target elevations set, including the direction water should run afterward. Small ports are drilled through the concrete on a grid sized to the void. Material is pumped in stages at low pressure with elevations watched continuously, moving between ports so support stays uniform rather than concentrating pressure at one point and cracking the panel. When the target profile is reached, pumping stops, the ports are patched, and the slab is left to cure before load returns. Getting this wrong looks like a slab lifted too fast at one hole and broken in the middle, which is why staged, monitored placement is the whole technique.

### Cost and what to buy with it

Pricing follows the volume of material the void takes and the number of ports, which is why the sounding step matters — a big hollow under a driveway costs more to fill than a small one under a step, regardless of how the surface looks. It is nearly always cheaper than replacing the concrete, and it keeps the existing surface. What it should be bought alongside is whatever put the water there: a downspout discharging onto the slab edge, a patio pitched toward the house, or a shrinkage crack line at the slab perimeter that lets stormwater straight into the soil.

## svc_pier_and_beam_repair_lede

Under a raised Austin house the floor rides on a grid of separate supports, and every one of them can move on its own. Pier and beam repair rebuilds that load path — pads, blocks, posts, beams and shims — and returns the floor above to a profile somebody can defend with numbers.

## svc_pier_and_beam_repair_body

### The load path, one part at a time

Start from the dirt and work up, because that is the order failures happen in. A pad bears on soil. A concrete or masonry block sits on the pad. A short post stands on the block. A beam spans post to post. Joists cross the beams, and the subfloor rides the joists. Nothing in that stack is continuous, so a defect stays local: one pad that has pressed into softened clay shows up as a dip in one room, not a tilt across the house. That is the opposite of how a slab behaves, and it is the reason a diagnosis made from the living room is worth very little. All of it has to be read from underneath. Other jobs on the [services index](../services/) start from a different structure entirely — [slab foundation repair](../slab-foundation-repair/) works on a monolithic pour, and [sagging floor repair](../sagging-floor-repair/) deals with framing that spans too far between supports that are themselves fine.

### Why the supports move in this city

The soil under the pads is the variable. In Travis County the dominant clay is the Houston Black series, and the USDA official series description is unusually direct about what it does: high shrink-swell potential, with cracks between half an inch and four inches wide opening from the surface down to a foot or more, and those cracks staying open for 90 to 150 cumulative days in most years. A pad sitting on soil that opens and closes on that schedule does not stay at one elevation. In the older raised-floor blocks of Hyde Park and East Cesar Chavez the pads are often shallow, small in plan, and were placed before anybody wrote down a bearing pressure, so they respond to every seasonal cycle rather than resisting it.

Depth to something firmer changes across town. West of MoPac the USDA description of the Volente series, whose type location is in Travis County, records clayey sediments only four to six feet thick over limestone. East of the escarpment the Heiden series carries 40 to 60 percent clay weathered from mudstone with densic material 40 to 65 inches down. A new support on the west side may reach rock in one dig. On the east side it may never reach anything but more clay, which is an argument for a deeper element rather than a wider pad.

### The four repairs the Texas standard names

The Texas Section of the American Society of Civil Engineers publishes guidelines for evaluating and repairing residential foundations, and for a raised floor it lists exactly four moves: shimming the floor framing atop the existing supports, repairing or strengthening the floor framing, replacing or adding supports, and re-establishing void space. Shims are hardwood or steel and fill a gap; they do not create bearing where bearing has been lost. Adding supports is what the guidelines call for when framing spans are too great for the design loads or when the existing supports have deteriorated. Framing repairs get treated lumber. Those four moves in that order is a scope. A crew that only owns the first one will hand you the first one.

### Void space, and the item everybody forgets

Void spaces designed under foundation elements are supposed to be reestablished as necessary — that is in the guidelines, and it is the line most often skipped. On a raised house the deliberate gap between the soil and the underside of a beam or an interior pier cap is not sloppiness. It is the allowance that lets swelling clay rise without loading a member that was never designed to take that load from below. Fill it with dirt, debris, a flower bed or the spoil from a plumbing repair, and the clay now pushes directly on framing. The floor then heaves in wet weather and drops in dry weather, and the shims get blamed.

### What the work involves

The crawl gets mapped: every pad, block, post, beam and suspect joist located, with decayed wood probed to find how deep the damage goes rather than how far the surface is stained. Elevations are read on the floor above so the low areas can be tied to specific supports below. Failed pads and bearing ends are replaced with treated material on new bearing before anything is lifted, because raising a floor on wood that has already lost section only compresses it further. Height comes back in small increments across many points at once, watched from inside for plaster and door reveals. Void space is cleared and reinstated. The guidelines are honest about the ceiling on all of this: it is unlikely that elevation adjustments will result in a level foundation, so the target is a profile inside tolerance, not a laser-flat floor.

### What sets the price

Access is the multiplier, not the parts. Clearance under the joists decides whether a crew works on its knees or on its side, and a house sitting low to the ground can double the labor for identical materials. After that it is the count: how many pads, how many posts, how many feet of beam, and whether the bearing ends have gone. Plumbing disturbed on the way through is separate.

## svc_retaining_wall_repair_lede

Retaining walls hold up the terraces that make west Austin buildable, and they fail from water pressure behind them rather than from the weight of the soil alone. Repair means restoring drainage behind the wall and restoring the wall's ability to resist a lateral push.

## svc_retaining_wall_repair_body

### What is pushing on the wall

A retaining wall carries lateral earth pressure, and that pressure roughly doubles when the soil behind it saturates, because water in the backfill adds hydrostatic load to the soil load. That is the whole story behind most failures. A wall that leans out at the top, bulges through its middle third, opens vertical joints between blocks or shows a horizontal crack partway up is telling you the push exceeded what it was built to resist. On stone and segmental block walls the give is usually at the joints; on a poured or masonry wall it concentrates at one horizontal line. Diagnosis begins with a plumb reading at several points along the length and a look at whether the wall has drainage or is holding water. [Bowing wall repair](../bowing-wall-repair/) covers the same physics applied to a wall of the house itself, and the [services index](../services/) lists the rest.

### Why Austin has so many of these walls

Terrain. The Bureau of Economic Geology describes the Balcones Escarpment as generally paralleling MoPac through Austin, with the ground becoming hilly to the west and relatively flat to the east, and the east side having moved downward by as much as 700 feet. Building on the west side means cutting benches into slope, and every bench needs something to hold the cut face. The soils there are thin over rock: the USDA description of the Brackett series, whose type location is in Hays County, puts the paralithic contact between five and twenty inches below the surface, and the Volente series records clayey sediments four to six feet thick over limestone with a type location in Travis County. So a wall on the west side is often founded on or near rock with a shallow layer of soil behind it, while a wall on the flat east side is retaining deep clay that swells. Those two walls fail differently and should not be repaired the same way.

### The Austin permit line that catches homeowners

Height is the trigger. The City of Austin's small project retaining wall instructions state that any retaining wall over four feet in height located inside the city limits also requires a building permit, under Section 25-11-34 of the Land Development Code. The small project path itself covers construction or reconstruction of a retaining wall less than 100 feet in length and eight feet in height within city jurisdiction, including the extraterritorial jurisdiction. Two more limits inside that path matter on a repair: backfill for a small project cannot be more than two feet deep, unless it occurs for no more than four feet measured horizontally behind the wall, and no more than four feet of cut is allowed in front of the wall. A professional engineer's seal, signature and date are required where the work sits within the public right of way. Rebuilding a five-foot wall is therefore permitted work, and the four-foot line is worth checking with a tape before anybody quotes it.

### Drainage is the repair, the wall is just the visible part

Rebuilding a leaning wall without changing what is behind it buys a few seasons. A functioning wall has a drainage layer of clean stone behind it, a filter separating that stone from the clay so it does not silt up, weep holes or a collector pipe at the base with a real outlet, and surface grading above that keeps runoff from pouring into the backfill. The failures are predictable: clay backfilled straight against the wall face, a collector pipe that was never daylighted or was crushed during backfill, weeps plugged with soil, and irrigation running directly into the retained mass. Any of those and the wall is holding water, which is the load it was least designed for.

### What not to do while it is moving

Do not excavate at the toe of a leaning wall to have a look. Removing soil in front of a wall removes the passive resistance holding the base in place, and that is the classic way a slow lean becomes a sudden collapse — which is part of why the city's own small project rules cap cut in front of a wall at four feet. Do not stack additional block on top to fix a wall that is already tipping, and do not park or place fill on the retained side while the wall is out of plumb. Where a wall is holding up a driveway, a neighboring structure, or anything with a person under it, treat the area below as off limits until it is assessed.

### What the work involves

The wall is measured for plumb and alignment along its length, its height above grade is recorded against the four-foot permit line, and the backfill is investigated to find out whether a drainage layer and an outlet exist. Where the wall is sound but the drainage is not, the repair is excavation behind the face, a stone drainage zone with filter fabric, a collector pipe run to a daylighted outlet, and regrading above. Where the wall itself has failed, the section is dismantled and rebuilt with drainage built in from the start rather than added later. Cost follows height, length, how much soil has to be moved and held back during the work, and access — a wall behind a house on a slope with no equipment route is a hand-labor job, and that dominates the price.

## svc_root_barrier_installation_lede

A large tree beside an Austin house is a pump. It draws water out of the clay within reach of its roots, the clay shrinks, and the foundation follows it down. A root barrier is a vertical wall in the soil that moves the tree's feeding zone away from the structure.

## svc_root_barrier_installation_body

### The mechanism is subsidence, not roots pushing concrete

The popular picture is a root levering a slab upward. That is not what usually happens here. The Lady Bird Johnson Wildflower Center, answering a question about live oaks and foundations, put the real mechanism plainly: tree roots can grow up to three times the spread of the tree, but the problem in foundations, especially in a very dry climate, is soil subsidence — the root looks for water and sucks it up, and the drying soil drops. So a root barrier is not armor against roots. It is a moisture-management device, and it belongs with [drainage and grading](../drainage-and-grading/) rather than with underpinning.

### How deep the roots that matter actually run

Shallower than most people expect, which is what makes a barrier feasible. A Texas Transportation Institute report prepared for the state highway agency on protecting trees during construction states that the fine feeder roots that take up moisture are found mostly within the top twelve inches of the surface, and that the great majority of roots are located within three feet of the surface. The same report's root barrier detail shows a trench eighteen to twenty-four inches deep backfilled with sand, and notes that barriers may be fabrics or hard barriers such as plastics or metals. Those are the working dimensions: a continuous vertical element in the top few feet of soil, between the tree and the structure, with no gaps at the ends where the moisture gradient can route around it.

### The Austin tree ordinance decides whether this is legal before it decides whether it is a good idea

In Austin the rules are unusually strict for the exact species that causes the problem. The City Arborist defines a Protected tree as any species nineteen inches or more in diameter measured at four and a half feet above the ground, and a Heritage tree as twenty-four inches or more in a listed species group — a group that expressly includes all oaks and pecan. Removal of a Heritage tree is prohibited except under Section 25-8-641 of the City Code, and a Heritage tree thirty inches or larger takes a public process for a variance. So the mature live oak drying out the clay beside a 1930s bungalow is very often a Heritage tree that cannot simply be taken down.

The barrier itself is regulated too, because a barrier is a trench. The city's residential tree rules require a Tree Ordinance Review Application for removing a regulated tree, for pruning twenty-five percent or more of the canopy, or for starting an activity that may impact a tree or disturb the critical root zone. Inside the inner quarter of the critical root zone no impacts are allowed at all. Within the half critical root zone there can be no cut or fill greater than four inches, and across the full zone fifty percent of the ground has to be preserved without impact. The city's plumbing permit form repeats it from the other direction: installing utilities or irrigation lines within the critical root zone of a regulated tree requires that application. A barrier trench between a house and a large oak will often fall inside that zone, which makes the permit part of the design rather than an afterthought.

### What a barrier does and does not accomplish

The Texas Section ASCE guidelines for repairing residential foundations are measured about it: root barriers or periodic root pruning may mitigate the effects of vegetation, and root barriers are generally not as effective as tree removal. That is the honest ceiling. A barrier reduces the tree's ability to draw from the soil under the structure; it does not restore soil that has already consolidated, and it does not lift anything. The guidelines also flag the opposite hazard: removing a tree may stop shrinkage or partially restore settled areas, but removal may cause upheaval from the resulting soil moisture increase, especially where the tree predates construction, and a suitable waiting period may be recommended to allow for that heave. Cutting down a sixty-year-old live oak next to a slab can move the house the other way.

### What the installation involves

The tree is measured at four and a half feet for diameter, because that number decides which permit path applies, and the critical root zone is laid out and the proposed trench line checked against it. Depth to rock, buried utilities and the irrigation layout get located, since hitting a lateral or a gas service in a hand trench next to a foundation is the main site risk. The trench is cut to depth in a continuous line with the ends carried past the corner of the influence zone rather than stopped short, the barrier is set with its top close enough to the surface that shallow feeder roots cannot bridge over it, and the trench is backfilled to grade. Trenching inside the root zone is done by hand or with air excavation wherever the ordinance limits ground disturbance.

### What it costs and what it is worth buying with

Barrier work prices by the linear foot and by how the trench has to be dug, and hand excavation inside a critical root zone is the multiplier. Where a tree is drawing moisture from under a slab, the same visit should correct the grade, extend the roof discharge away from the beam, and put irrigation on a schedule that keeps moisture near uniform on all four elevations. The guidelines call that uniform-moisture approach potentially beneficial while cautioning against excessive watering.

## svc_sagging_floor_repair_lede

A floor that sags, bounces or dips is telling you about the framing and the supports beneath it. It is a different measurement from foundation movement, and the two get conflated constantly.

## svc_sagging_floor_repair_body

### Framing problem or foundation problem

Both produce sloping floors, and telling them apart decides whether you buy carpentry or piers. A framing problem is local and bounded: the dip follows a joist line or centers between supports, the floor flexes underfoot when you walk across it, and the low point sits mid-span rather than at a wall. A foundation problem is continuous: the slope runs across rooms without regard to the framing direction, exterior brick and door frames move with it, and the low area corresponds to a low area in an elevation survey of the whole footprint. The residential code as adopted in Austin says where the load ends up — Section R501.2 requires floor construction to accommodate all loads per Section R301 and to transmit them to the supporting structural elements. If the supporting elements have moved, that is [house leveling](../house-leveling/). If the framing between them is under-sized or damaged, that is this page. The [services index](../services/) has both.

### The numbers a floor is judged by

The code gives allowable joist spans in tables, and Section R502.3 divides them by loading. Table R502.3.1(1) applies where the live load does not exceed 30 pounds per square foot and the dead load does not exceed 20, and Table R502.3.1(2) applies where the live load does not exceed 40 psf with the same dead load. Deflection is the other half: Section R504.1 requires floor framing to meet the joist deflection requirements of Section R301, and the limit for a floor member under live load is L over 360 — a span divided by 360. The Texas Section ASCE guidelines borrow that same figure as potentially appropriate for the analogous in-service deflection of a residential foundation, which is why the number shows up in two different arguments and gets muddled. Applied to the floor itself it is the actual code limit. Applied to a foundation profile it is an analogy. They also note that a floor slope greater than 1 percent is usually noticeable and that the Americans with Disabilities Act considers a 2 percent slope too large.

### The specific failures found under Austin raised floors

Older raised houses in Hyde Park, East Cesar Chavez and along Onion Creek share a set of them. Joists undersized for their span, or notched by a plumber or an electrician until the remaining depth cannot carry the load. Girders that have deflected between piers because the pier spacing was generous. Piers that have settled, shimmed at some point and never corrected. Sill and joist ends rotted where a crawl space stayed damp. And the one the code calls out specifically: Section R502.4 requires joists under parallel bearing partitions to be of adequate size to support the load, which means an interior wall running parallel to and between joists needs framing sized for it. Where a later remodel added a wall on a floor that was never designed to carry one, the sag traces exactly along that wall. A dip that stops abruptly at a partition line is the giveaway.

### What repair actually consists of

The Texas Section ASCE guidelines list the options for raised foundations: repairs may include shimming the floor framing atop the existing supports, repairing or strengthening the floor framing, replacing or adding supports, and re-establishing void space. Shimming is the cheapest and the most abused — the guidelines specify that hardwood or steel shims may be used to fill gaps, which rules out the scrap softwood and stacked cedar shingles found under a lot of older Austin houses, because those crush and rot. Strengthening the framing means sistering a new member to a damaged one, replacing a rotted section, or adding a beam and support where a span is simply too long. For any replacement in a crawl space, the guidelines recommend treated lumber for general use in framing repairs, which matters where ground moisture is present. And where void space was designed under foundation elements, they note it should be reestablished as necessary.

### The order the work has to happen in

Supports first, then framing, then finishes. Lifting a sagged girder before the piers under it are sound just moves the problem, and shimming a floor flat over a rotted sill hides a member that is still failing. Where the crawl space is damp, the moisture source is fixed before new lumber goes in, because replacing rot without removing the cause buys a few years. That usually means grade and roof discharge corrected outside, which is the [drainage and grading](../drainage-and-grading/) scope, and it means the ventilation and ground cover in the crawl space itself, which is [crawl space repair](../crawl-space-repair/). Lifting is done slowly and in stages regardless, because a floor pulled up quickly cracks plaster, tile and drywall above it.

### What the visit produces and what it costs

Floor elevations read across the affected rooms and plotted, with the low point located relative to the framing direction — the single measurement that separates framing from foundation. Access under the floor to identify the members, their actual dimensions, their span, and the pier or girder spacing, checked against the code span tables for the loading that applies. Notches, cuts, rot and insect damage recorded by location. A note on whether a bearing partition sits where the framing cannot carry it. Cost then follows how much of the framing is being replaced rather than shimmed, how much headroom the crawl space gives a worker, and whether finished flooring above has to come up to reach anything.

## svc_slab_foundation_repair_lede

Most Austin houses built after the middle of the last century sit on a single concrete pour with thickened ribs underneath, and that pour moves as one stiff plate. Slab repair is about restoring support under the plate, which is a different problem from patching what the plate has cracked.

## svc_slab_foundation_repair_body

### The plate, and why it cracks where it does

A slab-on-grade foundation is a thin field of concrete stiffened by deeper beams around the perimeter and across the interior. It has almost no ability to bend, so when the soil under one part of it drops away or swells upward, the concrete relieves the stress by cracking, and the cracking shows up above as diagonal breaks over door and window heads, stair-stepped mortar joints outside, and doors that stop latching along one wall. Understanding it as a plate explains the sequence: soil moves, support changes, the plate deflects, finishes tear. Treating the torn finishes is downstream work. [Foundation crack repair](../foundation-crack-repair/) is the right page for a break that has been shown to be static, and the [services index](../services/) lists the rest.

### The clay under the east side of town

East of MoPac the near-surface geology is Upper Cretaceous, and the unit that matters is the Taylor Group. A City of Austin geologic appendix describes it plainly: dark gray shale that weathers to a greenish gray and brown calcareous, montmorillonitic clay and marly clay in the Austin area, generally referred to as the Taylor Clay. Montmorillonite is the mineral behind seasonal slab movement, because its volume tracks its water content. The soil series developed on that kind of parent material carry the numbers: Houston Black at 40 to 60 percent clay with a Travis County type location, Heiden at 40 to 60 percent clay weathered from mudstone. A slab in Mueller or East Cesar Chavez is bearing on that. A slab in Circle C Ranch may be bearing on much thinner soil over rock, and the same symptom means something different there.

### The five things a repair can be

The Texas Section ASCE guidelines list the available slab repair methods and the list is short: underpinning, grouting, mudjacking, crack injecting, tendon stressing, and partial demolition and reconstruction. Each answers a different finding. Underpinning transfers load past the moving soil to a deeper stratum, and it is what a settled perimeter needs. Grouting, in the guidelines' words, generally provides continuous slab support without lifting appreciably — it fills a void rather than raising anything. [Mudjacking and slab lifting](../mudjacking-and-slab-lifting/) adjusts elevation hydraulically with continuous support. Crack injection with epoxy is aimed at restoring stiffness across a crack of about a thirty-second of an inch or wider, not at stopping movement. Tendon stressing only applies to a post-tensioned slab where testing shows the tendon forces are below what the original design specified. Reading those apart is most of the value in an evaluation.

### The Texas rule that changes who designs this

Texas exempts most residential work from engineering licensure, and then removes the exemption for exactly this job. Section 1001.056 of the Texas Engineering Practice Act exempts a person repairing or drawing plans for a private dwelling. Subsection (c) states that the exemption does not apply to anyone providing engineering design relating to repairing, or drawing plans or specifications for, a residential dwelling slab located on expansive soil that meets the expansive soil classification provisions of the International Residential Code as applied in that jurisdiction. The classification itself is numeric: a Texas Department of Licensing and Regulation bulletin on foundations on expansive soils sets out the code's four tests, including a plasticity index of 15 or greater and an expansion index above 20. On soil that meets them, the repair design belongs to a professional engineer licensed in Texas. That is a legal requirement on the design, not a service this site performs or supplies.

### What Austin asks for on paper

The city lists foundation repair among residential minor repairs, handled through its Residential Express Building Permit web form, so the paperwork is fast — but it is still permitted work with an inspection attached, and the city's own page warns that the permits listed may not cover everything a project needs. On the inspection side, the city's residential building inspection flowchart says the City of Austin does not conduct a physical foundation inspection, that a State of Texas licensed engineer will need to inspect the foundation, and that a copy of that engineer's sealed assessment is required for foundation approval. Plan for that document at the start of the job instead of discovering it at the end.

### What the work involves and what it costs

Elevations are read across the accessible floor and drawn, because without a profile nobody can say how far out of tolerance the slab is or where a repair should stop. Drainage, grade, downspouts, irrigation and trees get walked, since those are the inputs that moved the soil. Where the pattern points at water under the slab, the supply and drain lines get tested before any structural quote, and the guidelines specifically direct that water supply and sanitary drain lines be tested for leaks if jacking or lifting is part of the remedy. Pricing follows pier count, pier type and access — an interior pier reached by tunneling or by cutting the floor is a different line item from an exterior pier in an open side yard. The pricing page collects published Austin ranges with their sources.

## svc_slab_moisture_and_plumbing_leaks_lede

A leaking supply or drain line under a slab does not just waste water. It puts a continuous point source of moisture into shrink-swell clay, and the clay lifts. On a Central Texas slab the leak is frequently the cause of the movement rather than a consequence of it.

## svc_slab_moisture_and_plumbing_leaks_body

### Why a leak under a slab moves a house here

Under a slab on high-plasticity clay, a leak is a localized irrigation system. The USDA official series description for Houston Black, whose type location is Travis County, reports 40 to 60 percent clay in the particle-size control section and high shrink-swell potential, with a profile more than 80 inches deep. Add water in one spot in that material and it expands in that spot. The result is heave — a dome centered on the leak — which reads on an elevation survey as a high area rather than a low one and produces cracks that open at the top and close at the bottom, the inverse of settlement. Diagnose it backward and you get piers installed to lift a slab that is already too high in the middle. The other jobs are on the [services index](../services/).

### Which lines fail, and how the symptoms differ

Pressurized supply lines under a slab leak continuously and at volume. The tells are a water bill that stepped up without a change in habits, the sound of running water with every fixture closed, a warm spot on the floor when the leak is on the hot side, and unexplained damp flooring. Drain and sewer lines are not pressurized, so they leak only when something is being used. Their tells are slower: recurring backups, a smell in one part of the house, and soil erosion under the slab that eventually produces a void rather than heave. Both need to be separated from surface water arriving over the edge of the slab, a grading problem covered in [drainage and grading](../drainage-and-grading/), and from moisture wicking through concrete, which is a vapor problem rather than a leak.

### The testing sequence

The Texas Section ASCE guidelines make the test a required step whenever a slab is going to be moved: water supply and sanitary drain lines should be tested for leaks if jacking or lifting is included in the remedial measures, and gas service lines may require adjustment. That is why a foundation proposal without a plumbing test line item is incomplete. Supply lines are isolated at the meter and held under pressure to see whether the system holds. Drain lines are isolated at their low point and filled with water to a defined level so the surface can be watched for a drop. Where a test fails, locating the break before breaking concrete keeps the repair small, and that is done with cameras run through the line, with acoustic and pressure equipment on the supply side, and with tracing gas where a supply leak will not localize.

### What a general home inspection does and does not cover

Texas sets the scope of a standard real estate inspection in the administrative rules, and the limits matter if you are relying on an inspection report. Under 22 TAC 535.231 the inspector shall report static water pressure and the presence of active leaks, but is not required to inspect for sewer clean-outs, verify the performance of floor drains, or pressurize or test the gas system. So a clean inspection report is not a statement that the lines under the slab hold. That is a separate test, and on a house with movement on a clay lot it is the test that should be bought first, before any repair scope is written.

### The permit rules that apply to the repair

Once a leak is found, the repair falls under Austin's trade permit process, and the city is specific about who may hold the permit. Stand-alone trade permits can only be obtained by licensed professional trade contractors or by a qualified homeowner acting as their own trade contractor under the city's Homestead Permit requirements. The city's plumbing permit form adds that where a building permit applies, that permit has to be issued before the trade permit is, and that a permit expires on the 181st day if no inspection has taken place. The same form carries the tree rule: installing utilities within the critical root zone of a regulated tree requires a Tree Ordinance Review Application, a live constraint when a service line has to be dug up beside a mature oak.

### Repair options once the break is located

There are three. Spot repair through the slab: cutting an opening, excavating to the line, replacing the failed section and restoring the concrete. Tunneling from outside, which reaches the same point without cutting the interior floor and is priced by the linear foot. Or reroute, which abandons the under-slab run and carries the line overhead or around the perimeter, usually the right answer on an older galvanized or cast-iron system where the failure is age rather than one bad joint. The decision follows how many breaks the camera finds and what the pipe material is.

### What happens to the foundation afterward

Stopping the leak does not undo the movement. Clay that has been wet for a long time consolidates as it dries out again, and the shape it comes back to is not necessarily the shape it started in. So the sequence is: test, repair the plumbing, re-survey floor elevations after a drying period, and only then decide whether structural work is warranted. The measurement side is covered under [foundation inspection](../foundation-inspection/).

## svc_soil_stabilization_lede

Stabilization treats the cause instead of the symptom. Rather than supporting a foundation against soil movement, it aims to reduce how much the soil moves, by controlling the moisture it can gain and lose.

## svc_soil_stabilization_body

### What is actually moving

The mechanism is smectitic clay changing volume with water content. The USDA official series descriptions for Houston Black, whose type location is Travis County, and Heiden, its close relative on weathered mudstone, both classify the soils as fine, smectitic, thermic Udic Haplusterts with high shrink-swell potential and 40 to 60 percent clay in the particle-size control section. Houston Black is more than 80 inches deep, while Heiden reaches densic material at 40 to 65 inches. Houston Black cracks half an inch to four inches wide from the surface down to twelve inches or more when dry, and those cracks remain open for 90 to 150 cumulative days in most years. That is the machine under a slab east of the escarpment: a hundred-plus days a year of open fissures that let air and rainfall reach deep, followed by closure and expansion when the profile rewets.

### Why Austin's rainfall pattern is the aggravating factor

Volume change needs a moisture swing, and this city produces extreme ones. The City of Austin's study updating the rainfall period of record puts the Camp Mabry annual extremes at 11.52 inches in the driest year, 1954, and 64.68 inches in the wettest, 1919, against an 1856 to 2012 mean of 33.4 inches, and records that in 2011 only 0.05 inches of rain fell between June 22 and September 16. It also notes rainfall exceeding five inches in several hours causing flash floods. The city's watershed report documents the reversal in a single hour: 2.96 inches at Camp Mabry, a one-hour record, after 51 consecutive days with no measurable rainfall. A soil that cracks to twelve inches over a dry summer and then receives three inches in an hour is being cycled hard, and that cycling, not the clay by itself, is what damages foundations.

### The methods the code recognizes

The residential code requires foundations on expansive soils to be designed under the building code's expansive soil provisions, and it defines expansive by four tests, as summarized in the Texas Department of Licensing and Regulation's technical bulletin on foundations on expansive soils: a plasticity index of 15 or greater by ASTM D4318, more than 10 percent of particles passing a No. 200 sieve, more than 10 percent smaller than 5 micrometers, or an expansion index greater than 20 by ASTM D4829. The same bulletin sets out the alternatives to a complete geotechnical evaluation under Section R401.4.2: remove the top or subsoils to a depth and width sufficient to assure stable moisture content, with the caveat that soils removed may not be used as fill, or stabilize the soil by chemical dewatering or chemical presaturation. Those are the recognized categories. Note what they have in common — each one is about moisture content, not about strength.

### Moisture barriers and watering, the practical version

For an existing house, the Texas Section ASCE guidelines are the operative reference, and they are brief about it: vertical or horizontal moisture barriers may be effective to mitigate moisture migration under the foundation. A vertical barrier is a membrane installed in a trench around the perimeter to slow lateral moisture exchange between the yard and the soil under the slab. A horizontal barrier is an impervious surface extending out from the foundation to keep rainfall from entering at the edge. The guidelines pair those with grading numbers worth using: a minimum slope of 5 percent, six inches in ten feet, away from the foundation for the first five feet all around, and swales at 2 percent, six inches in 25 feet, or 1 percent at a minimum. Beyond that, they treat conscientious watering as potentially beneficial while warning against excessive watering. The point is uniformity — moisture roughly equal on all four elevations, so the slab is not being pushed up on one side and dropped on the other.

### Where the survey data comes from before anything is designed

The TDLR bulletin makes a useful, checkable statement: as a minimum, USDA Natural Resources Conservation Service soil survey reports can help to determine the presence and location of expansive soils. That is the free first step on any Austin lot, and it often explains the whole complaint. Two examples from Travis County's own series data show why the answer changes across town. Volente, with a type location in this county, formed in calcareous clayey sediments four to six feet thick over limestone with 35 to 50 percent clay, which is a shallow profile over rock. Heiden reaches densic material at 40 to 65 inches. Brackett, type location Hays County, reaches paralithic contact at five to twenty inches. Those depths determine whether stabilization is even the right lever or whether the answer is bearing on the rock that is already close to the surface — which is the [steel pier installation](../steel-pier-installation/) conversation instead.

### What stabilization can and cannot be sold as

It cannot be sold as a lift. Nothing in this category raises a foundation, and any claim that a soil treatment will bring a settled corner back up is a claim about the future rather than a description of the work. It is also not a substitute for structural repair where the structure has already been damaged. What it can do is reduce the amplitude of seasonal movement so a repair, once made, is not immediately re-loaded, which is why it belongs in the same scope as [drainage and grading](../drainage-and-grading/) and [root barrier installation](../root-barrier-installation/) rather than in place of them. On any project where the design of a repair to a slab on expansive soil is involved, Texas requires that design to come from a licensed professional engineer, and this site does not provide engineering services.

## svc_steel_pier_installation_lede

A steel pier is a structural element driven or turned into competent material and connected to the foundation so the load bypasses the soil that is moving. It goes deeper than a concrete piling and carries more, which is why it is the method of choice where the depth to bearing is uncertain.

## svc_steel_pier_installation_body

### What separates steel from the alternatives

Underpinning in this market comes in three common forms and they are not interchangeable. Pressed concrete pilings are short cylinders driven hydraulically in segments until refusal, using the building's own weight as the reaction. Steel push piers are driven the same way but go further and take more load because the section is stronger. Helical piers are screwed in to a measured installation torque that correlates with capacity, so the depth is not fixed in advance and the installer knows what the pier will carry when it stops. Where the depth to competent material varies across a lot, that measurable stopping criterion is the reason to specify steel. The Texas Section ASCE guidelines list underpinning alongside grouting, mudjacking, crack injecting, tendon stressing and partial demolition and reconstruction as slab repair methods, and each answers a different problem. [Mudjacking and slab lifting](../mudjacking-and-slab-lifting/) is on the [services index](../services/) as its own job for that reason.

### Why depth to bearing is the Austin variable

The distance a pier has to travel changes street by street here, and the soil data shows it. The USDA series description for Volente, with a type location in Travis County, describes soils formed in calcareous clayey sediments four to six feet thick over limestone. Brackett, type-located in Hays County, reaches paralithic contact at five to twenty inches. On the other side of the escarpment, Houston Black, also type-located in Travis County, runs more than 80 inches of high-shrink-swell clay, and Heiden reaches densic material only at 40 to 65 inches. The Bureau of Economic Geology explains the split: the escarpment runs roughly parallel to MoPac, west Austin is hilly with thin rocky soils over Glen Rose and Edwards limestone, and east Austin is relatively flat with deep, well-developed soils. So a pier on a west-side lot may find rock in a few feet, while the same pier east of the line has to pass through the entire active clay zone first. A fixed pier depth quoted across the metro is a quote written without reference to the ground.

### What the plan has to specify

The guidelines are specific about the documents. The plans should show or specify specific locations of underpinning elements and their sizes, depths, material types, and minimum required material strengths where appropriate. That is the checklist to hold a proposal against: not just how many piers, but where each goes, how deep it runs, what it is made of, and what strength that material has to meet. The guidelines are equally direct about the limits. Performance of underpinning can be compromised by the integrity of existing slab components, changes in soil moisture, skin friction, point load and other factors. And where only part of a structure is underpinned, the construction documents should state that underpinning will not improve the performance of the foundation in non-underpinned areas. That sentence is the honest answer to why one side of a house can be piered and the other side can still move.

### Who is legally required to design it

For the most common Austin case this is not optional. The Texas Engineering Practice Act exempts a person repairing, or drawing plans for, a private dwelling, but Section 1001.056 withdraws that exemption for anyone providing engineering design relating to repairing, or drawing plans or specifications for, a residential dwelling slab located on expansive soil that meets the expansive soil classification provisions of the residential code as applied in the jurisdiction. Austin's clay lots routinely meet that classification, which puts a licensed Texas professional engineer's design at the front of the job as a legal requirement. The guidelines state it from the other direction: the evaluation and repair design shall be performed by a professional engineer licensed in the State of Texas, and the engineer who seals the documents is the engineer of record. This site does not perform engineering services and does not issue engineering plans or sealed reports.

### How the installation runs

An elevation survey establishes the existing profile and the low areas. The engineer's plan fixes pier locations, typically at the perimeter beam and at interior load points, and interior piers mean either cutting the slab or tunneling in from outside. Access is excavated at each location, the pier is driven or turned to its stopping criterion — refusal for a driven pier, a target installation torque for a helical — and a bracket transfers the foundation load onto the pier. Lift is then taken in small increments across multiple piers at once, with elevations re-read as it goes, so the structure moves as a shape rather than being jacked at points. The guidelines set expectations honestly: it is unlikely that elevation adjustments will result in a level foundation. The target is the profile the plan calls for, not a spirit level.

### Permits, cost and what comes after

Austin lists foundation repair under residential Minor Repairs, applied for through the Residential Express Building Permit, and the city notes that the permits listed may not encompass every permit a project needs. Underpinning is not called out by name on that list, so the permit path is confirmed with the city for the specific scope rather than assumed. Cost is pier count times pier type plus access, and pier count comes out of the survey and the load path rather than out of a package. Afterward the drainage and moisture work still has to happen: piers stop the supported points from settling further, they do not stop the clay between them from cycling.

## svc_stem_wall_repair_lede

A stem wall is the continuous perimeter wall standing on a spread footing that carries the framing above a crawl space. It is not a grade beam and it is not a retaining wall, and repairing it means repairing concrete or masonry that is deteriorating in contact with soil and water.

## svc_stem_wall_repair_body

### What it is, and what it is not

Three elements get called the same thing and they behave differently. A stem wall is a short wall sitting on a footing, rising above grade, with the house's sill plate bolted to its top — the perimeter of a raised or crawl space foundation. A grade beam is a thickened rib cast integrally with a slab, covered under [grade beam repair](../grade-beam-repair/). A retaining wall is freestanding, its only job holding back soil, covered under [retaining wall repair](../retaining-wall-repair/). A stem wall's problem is usually material deterioration where soil, water and air meet, plus differential settlement of the footing beneath it. The [services index](../services/) keeps them separate.

### Where they are in this city

Raised perimeter-wall foundations belong to the older housing stock — the pre-war and mid-century blocks around East Cesar Chavez and Hyde Park, and older houses along the creek bottoms near Onion Creek — and to houses on ground too sloped for a slab. On the sloping ground west of the escarpment, which the Bureau of Economic Geology describes as hilly in contrast to the relatively flat east side, a stem wall can be a few inches tall on the uphill elevation and several feet on the downhill one, so one wall does two jobs on two sides of the same house. Where the wall retains earth and encloses usable space below grade, Section R405.1 of the residential code as adopted in Austin requires a drainage system installed at or below the top of the footing, with gravel extending at least one foot beyond the outside edge of the footing and six inches above its top, covered with an approved filter membrane.

### The failure modes

Spalling is the common one: concrete breaking away in flakes or chunks along the outside face near grade, exposing aggregate. Where reinforcement has corroded, the mechanism is expansion — rusting steel occupies more volume than sound steel and forces the cover off, which shows as a crack line following the bar and then as a piece detaching. On masonry stem walls the equivalent is mortar joints eroding out and units losing bond. Vertical cracks at corners generally point to differential settlement of the footing. The question on any of these is whether the section still has thickness to carry load, and that is answered by removing the loose material and looking, not by measuring a crack from outside.

### Why the Austin moisture cycle attacks this element specifically

A stem wall lives in the wet-dry zone. The USDA series description for Houston Black, type-located in Travis County, records shrinkage cracks half an inch to four inches wide reaching twelve inches or more below the surface and remaining open for 90 to 150 cumulative days in most years. Those cracks form against the outside face of the wall, then close and swell against it when rain returns. The city's rainfall record shows how abrupt that is — the watershed report logged 2.96 inches in one hour at Camp Mabry after 51 consecutive days with no measurable rain. Repeated wetting and drying at the same elevation is what drives spalling and corrosion, and it is why the damage is nearly always in a band near grade rather than over the whole height.

### What repair involves

Unsound concrete is removed back to sound material rather than patched over, because a patch bonded to spalling concrete fails at the same line. Exposed reinforcement is cleaned and treated, or replaced where section loss is significant. The section is rebuilt with a repair mortar or concrete appropriate to the exposure and keyed into the existing wall. Where the footing beneath has settled differentially, rebuilding the wall alone does nothing — the footing comes first, with the guidelines' caution in mind that underpinning will not improve the performance of the foundation in non-underpinned areas. Where the sill plate or the bolts at the top of the wall have corroded or rotted, that connection is repaired at the same time, since it is the load path from the framing into the wall.

### The work that has to accompany it

Removing the water. Grade corrected so the ground falls away from the wall — the guidelines call for a minimum slope of 5 percent, six inches in ten feet, for the first five feet all around, with swales at 2 percent or 1 percent as a minimum. Roof discharge carried away rather than dumped at the base. Where the wall retains earth, the code drain detail built as specified with its filter membrane, and where a below-grade run is needed, [french drain installation](../french-drain-installation/) is the mechanism. Crawl space ventilation and ground cover matter too, because a wall that stays wet on the inside face deteriorates from both directions.

### Assessment and cost

The wall is walked on all four elevations with the damaged band mapped by length and height, spalled areas sounded to find how far the unsound zone extends past what is visible, and reinforcement located where exposed. Corners are checked for vertical cracking and the top for plumb, which distinguishes deterioration from movement. The sill plate and anchor bolts are inspected from inside the crawl space. Cost follows the linear footage being rebuilt, the depth of removal, whether reinforcement work is needed, and whether footing work is part of the scope.
