### What the local lists show

Two local haulers post prices for the common sizes, and they bracket the market. One sells boxes with a fixed tonnage allowance, a single ton on its smaller boxes and two on its twenty, plus a per-ton charge for anything over. The other sells by the dump, at a higher flat price per size, with a weekly charge if the box stays past its window, and a cheaper box reserved for clean concrete alone. Neither model is better in itself. The tonnage model rewards light loads; the per-dump model is simpler to compare, though it still carries weight limits of its own. The question to ask either way is what happens to the price if the box comes back heavier than expected.

### The county scale sets the floor

Every load from a Pensacola box crosses a scale. At Escambia County's Perdido Landfill, solid waste is $45.06 a ton and yard trash is $27.62, and the county's Palafox Transfer Station charges $57.90. That gate rate is the floor under any overage charge; a hauler's per-ton rate past the allowance adds the truck, the driver and the trip on top of it. Clean concrete, dirt and fill with no rebar or wire are accepted at no fee, which is why a concrete-only box costs less than a mixed one and why one bag of trash ruins the savings.

### Inside the city, the franchise fee is part of the price

A hauler collecting inside Pensacola must hold a city franchise and pays the city $1.50 per cubic yard of container capacity on every collection, so a twenty-yard box carries a thirty-dollar city fee each time it is hauled. It is not a line the customer usually sees, but it is part of why the quote is what it is, and it is the reason a cut-rate hauler without a franchise is no bargain on a job site the building official can shut down.

### Outside the city, the county utility posts a schedule

The Emerald Coast Utilities Authority publishes a commercial roll-off schedule: a delivery charge, a monthly rental, and a per-pull charge of roughly two hundred dollars that includes the Escambia County franchise fee, with disposal billed separately at actual weight from Perdido and a requirement that the box be emptied at least every thirty days. Set aside banned items for a separate trip, and pull out anything the family might want later before it disappears under a layer of carpet.

### Rental turnovers

Landlords turning a unit over see the same load again: worn carpet and pad, a broken dresser, mattresses, whatever the last tenant left. A small box dropped with the property manager's permission clears a unit in a day, and a landlord with several units can often run one box across a week of turnovers.

### Loading so it all fits

Take furniture apart where you can, flatten boxes, lay mattresses and box springs flat along the walls, and fill the gaps with bagged items. Keep everything below the top edge of the walls; an overfilled box cannot be hauled until it is leveled, and that costs time on a move date that will not move.

### When a box is more than the job needs

A couch and a few bags are not a dumpster rental. The City of Pensacola's sanitation service handles residential collection within city limits, at 850-435-1890, and ECUA handles it in unincorporated Escambia County, at 850-476-0480. Ask either about bulky items before renting a box for a handful of pieces. A box earns its price when the pile is bigger than a couple of truckloads or the deadline cannot wait.
