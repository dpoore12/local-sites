## pricing_body


An Evansville tow bill has four ingredients. A hook charge for sending the truck and attaching the car. Mileage, metered from the pickup to the drop. And the circumstances: a winch line into a ditch, a two-in-the-morning call, or a crash scene that has to be swept before traffic can move.

### The county contract is the one public price list

Vanderburgh County publishes the fee schedule it allows for tows it orders itself, through Central Dispatch or a deputy. It sets a flat charge for a car, pickup or SUV, a lower one for motorcycles and heavy vehicles, an hourly rate for recovery work, a per-mile figure for tows that leave the county, a per-day storage charge, a fixed fee for a jump start at the scene, and it forbids any charge for a dry run or a refused service. It also requires the contractor at the scene within twenty minutes. Those numbers are low, because the county negotiates volume, and they apply only to county-ordered tows. But they are the best local anchor for what the work costs at the floor, and a private quote many times higher deserves a question.

### The hook and the miles on a tow you call yourself

No schedule governs a private tow. Local operators publish a starting figure for a basic job, and the 2026 national surveys agree on the structure: a fixed hook, then mileage that runs cheaper on a wheel-lift and dearer on a flatbed. Comparing hooks in isolation is the classic mistake. A bargain hook attached to steep mileage ends up costing more on a run to Newburgh or over the bridges to Henderson than a slightly dearer hook with fair miles. Get both figures, then get the total to the actual address. That total is the number that should be spoken before the truck leaves the yard.

### Why the flatbed costs more and when you cannot skip it


A flatbed carries the car with nothing on the pavement. That is the only safe way to move an all-wheel-drive car, an electric car, anything low, a motorcycle, or a car with a wrecked wheel. Hauling an all-wheel-drive car on a wheel-lift for a mile can ruin its transfer case, and a cheap tow becomes a four-figure repair. A driver who suggests a wheel-lift for one of those should be told no.

### Roadside calls cost less than tows


A jump, a lockout, a spare swapped on, or a can of fuel is a service call rather than a tow, and it is priced accordingly. The county contract's own figure for a jump at the scene is a small fraction of its tow charge, and private service calls track the same ratio. When a car clicks in the heat or the cold, the sensible order is jump first, hook only if the battery is beyond saving or the starter has died. ### Winching and recovery


A car in the ditch beside Highway 41, drowned in an underpass after Pigeon Creek jumps its banks, or spun off the I-69 shoulder is a recovery before it is a tow. ### Nights, holidays and the Lloyd

Loose belongings can generally be retrieved during lot hours even while the car stays.

### Insurance and the paperwork

Photograph the scene, the other plate and the damage before anything moves, if that can be done safely. Get the report number from the officer. The receipt from the driver itemizes hook, mileage, recovery minutes and any surcharge, which is exactly what the adjuster will want. A total loss may be moved a second time by the insurer to a salvage yard, and that trip is theirs to pay.

### What it costs

Recovery minutes are the variable. A clean pull out of a shallow ditch is short; a car on its roof or sitting in water is not. The driver states an estimate on arrival before the line is attached, and the tow afterward is hook plus mileage like any other. The county contract bills recovery by the hour for exactly this reason. The pricing page sets the recovery band and the after-hours surcharge next to the tow rates.
