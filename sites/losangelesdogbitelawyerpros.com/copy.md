## pricing_lede

California asks whether a fee is unconscionable rather than whether it exceeds some set percentage. The sections here lay out the thirteen factors that answer the question, the disclosures the Business and Professions Code forces into a contingency contract, and the amounts the Superior Court of Los Angeles County prints on its own civil fee schedule.

## pricing_body

### The California standard is unconscionability

Rule 1.5(a) of the California Rules of Professional Conduct draws the outer boundary of any fee arrangement: it may be neither unconscionable nor illegal. California chose that harsher word over mere unreasonableness and then supplied a long list of considerations for applying it. Neither the rule nor any statute attaches a percentage to a claim brought against a dog's owner, so a figure printed on a retainer arrived there by negotiation and not by legislation.

### Thirteen factors, and the ones that bite

Rule 1.5(b) fixes the moment of judgment at the time the bargain was struck, unless the two of them expected later events to move the fee, and then names thirteen considerations. Some look at conduct: overreaching in arriving at the number, or keeping back facts the client needed. Some look at proportion: the size of the fee set beside what the representation was actually worth, and the sum at stake measured against the outcome delivered. Some look at the two people signing: how sophisticated each was, how long and how closely they had dealt with one another, and what standing and ability counsel brought to it. The rest look at the work itself: how novel and hard the questions were, whether taking it shut out other paying clients, what deadlines pressed on it, and how many hours it swallowed. The list closes with whether payment was fixed or rode entirely on the result, and whether the client knowingly agreed to the terms after being told what they meant.

### What section 6147 puts on the page

Business and Professions Code section 6147 governs the contingency contract itself. The agreed rate has to appear in the document, and for any claim outside section 6146 the contract must carry express notice that no law fixes that rate and the client is free to bargain over it. That notice is the reason a first number is never binding on anybody. Subdivision (b) gives the requirement teeth: omit a required element and the plaintiff may void the whole agreement, dropping counsel to nothing more than a reasonable fee. Rule 1.5(c) separately bars a contingency in a family law matter tied to a dissolution or to support, and bars one for a criminal defendant.

### The exception that does not reach a bite claim

Section 6146 is California's only percentage ceiling in injury work, and it applies exclusively to claims against a health care provider based on professional negligence. There the maximum is 25 percent of the amount recovered where all parties execute a settlement and release before a complaint or arbitration demand is filed, and 33 percent where the recovery comes after filing. An attorney may move for more than 33 percent in a tried or arbitrated case on evidence of good cause. Amounts recovered are net of disbursements and costs, and the statute expressly refuses to let medical care costs or office overhead be deducted first. A claim against a dog's owner is a straightforward negligence and strict liability matter, so none of that schedule applies to it.

### Stanley Mosk and the money the clerk collects

The Superior Court of Los Angeles County publishes its civil fee schedule with the Government Code section beside each line. A complaint or other first paper in an unlimited civil case, meaning one seeking more than $35,000, is $435 under section 70611, and each responding party pays the same. Keeping a jury requires a nonrefundable advance jury fee of $150 under Code of Civil Procedure section 631(b), with later daily deposits of $15 plus mileage of thirty-four cents a mile one way for each juror. A court reporter's per diem runs $764 for a session of four hours or more, $382 for more than one hour but under four, and $30 for an hour or less under Government Code section 68086.

### When a case is designated complex

Government Code section 70616 adds a complex case designation fee of $1,000 paid once for all plaintiffs and $1,000 for each defendant, with the total for the case stopping at $18,000. Most single-dog bite claims never see that designation, though a matter drawing in a landlord, a manager, an association and several insurers occasionally does. Because these amounts are costs advanced rather than fees earned, the contract should say who fronts them and what happens to them if the claim fails.

### The city's license amounts, and why records matter

Los Angeles Municipal Code section 53.15.3 sets a dog license processing fee of $91.50, dropping to $16.50 for a spayed female, a neutered male, or a dog a veterinarian certifies cannot breed. Section 53.15 adds an annual license tax of $8.50 per dog over four months, or $3.50 for an altered dog, with a reduced rate available at half the altered tax for a qualifying owner aged sixty-two or older or receiving disability benefits. Payment later than forty-five days after the renewal date carries a late fee of 25 percent of the license cost, and field collection at the property where the dog is kept adds $25.00. Failing to license within forty-five days is a misdemeanor, and license and rabies records are ordinary evidence in a bite claim.

## svc_dog_bite_injury_claim_lede

A bite on a Los Angeles sidewalk generates three separate records inside ten days: a Public Health file, an animal control file and a medical chart. The state statute is the straightforward part. Proving where you were standing is not.

## svc_dog_bite_injury_claim_body

### The statute does not ask what the dog did last year

[Civil Code section 3342](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?sectionNum=3342.&lawCode=CIV) makes the owner of any dog liable for damages suffered by a person bitten in a public place or lawfully in a private place, the owner's own property included. The statute says this applies regardless of the former viciousness of the dog or the owner's knowledge of it. Nobody has to dig up an earlier bite. The same subdivision defines lawful presence on the owner's land: being there to perform a duty imposed by the laws of this state or by federal postal regulation, or being there on the owner's invitation, express or implied. Once ownership and a bite are settled, the argument narrows to where you stood and why.

### The City ordinance gives you a second theory

[Municipal Code section 53.06.2](https://codelibrary.amlegal.com/codes/los_angeles/latest/lamc/0-0-0-136443) keeps a dog on its keeper's premises and lets it leave only under the control of a competent person, restrained by a substantial chain or leash not exceeding six feet, or inside a dog exercise or training area established under section 63.44. [Evidence Code section 669](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?sectionNum=669.&lawCode=EVID) then presumes that a person failed to exercise due care where an ordinance was violated, the violation proximately caused the injury, the injury was of the kind the ordinance was written to prevent, and the injured person belonged to the class it protects. The presumption can be rebutted by proof that the person did what an ordinarily prudent person wanting to comply would have done, so the physical details matter: photograph the lead, measure it if you safely can, and record whether any posted exercise area covered that ground.

### The same afternoon can also be a misdemeanor

Under [section 53.34](https://codelibrary.amlegal.com/codes/los_angeles/latest/lamc/0-0-0-137068), a person who owns, controls, possesses or is in charge of a dog and permits it to be at large on public property, or on someone else's private property, is guilty of a misdemeanor if the animal bites, attacks or injures a person or another animal. A conviction bars that person from owning the same species for three years and directs the Department not to issue or renew a license for it. Ask early whether a citation exists. It is dated, official, and written by somebody other than the dog's owner.

### If the dog was working for a public agency

Subdivision (b) of section 3342 withdraws the statutory action against a governmental agency using a dog in military or police work. One case is a bite that happened while the dog defended itself from an annoying, harassing or provoking act. The other covers a dog assisting an employee in the apprehension or holding of a suspect on reasonable suspicion, the investigation of a crime or possible crime, the execution of a warrant, or the defense of a peace officer or another person.

Subdivision (c) removes that shelter where the person bitten was not a party to, a participant in, or suspected of the conduct that prompted the dog's use. Subdivision (d) allows the shelter only where the agency has adopted a written policy on necessary and appropriate use. Where any public body sits in the facts, [Government Code section 911.2](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?sectionNum=911.2.&lawCode=GOV) requires a written claim no later than six months after the cause of action accrues, and [section 945.6](https://california.public.law/codes/ca_gov't_code_section_945.6) allows six months from the day a written rejection is delivered or mailed to get a suit on file.

### The first ten days belong to Public Health

County Veterinary Public Health puts the quarantine for a biting dog or cat at a minimum of ten days and holds biting livestock for thirty. The vast majority of quarantines are served in the animal's own home, while an animal impounded by an animal control agency finishes confinement in a shelter ([Reporting Animal Bites](http://publichealth.lacounty.gov/vet/biteintro.htm)). The County rabies manual describes the alternative: strict confinement under a licensed veterinarian, with release possible after five days where the veterinarian examines the animal on the fifth day or later and certifies no clinical signs ([quarantine guidance](http://publichealth.lacounty.gov/vet/rabiesmanual/quarantines.htm)). Public Health also states that a bite report does not mean the animal has been deemed dangerous, and that anyone who believes it is dangerous has to file a separate report with the local animal control agency. Pasadena, Long Beach and Vernon take their own reports.

### Two years, and the week that decides whether you keep them

[Code of Civil Procedure section 335.1](https://law.justia.com/codes/california/code-ccp/part-2/title-2/chapter-3/section-335-1/) sets a two-year period for a claim over injury to a person caused by another's wrongful act or neglect. That looks generous beside the six-month government claim rule, which is why the public-entity question belongs in the first weeks rather than the twentieth month. Ordinary evidence ages on its own schedule anyway. Lobby and doorbell footage gets overwritten, torn clothing goes in the bin, and a puncture over a knuckle that looked shallow on Sunday can declare itself as an infection by Wednesday.

### What the first contact looks like, and how the money side is written

The productive first exchange is an inventory rather than a story: date, address and unit, the animal and its owner, the report number, treatment so far, and whether any agency touched the facts. On the fee side, California attaches no percentage to a claim against a dog's owner. [Business and Professions Code section 6147](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?sectionNum=6147.&lawCode=BPC) requires a written contract signed by both people, a duplicate handed over at signing, the agreed rate on the page, an account of how costs bear on the fee and on the client's share, and plain language that no law sets that rate. A contract missing any of it is voidable at the client's option.

## svc_child_dog_bite_claim_lede

A child's bite claim is not a smaller version of an adult's. The clock is different, the defense is different, and the injury is not finished being an injury for months or years after the wound closes.

## svc_child_dog_bite_claim_body

### One bite, two claims, two clocks

[Code of Civil Procedure section 352](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?sectionNum=352.&lawCode=CCP) provides that where the person entitled to bring an action was under the age of majority when the cause of action accrued, the time of that disability is not part of the time limited for starting it. The child's own injury claim therefore has room to run past the eighteenth birthday. What a parent spent does not get the same treatment, and evidence keeps aging regardless. Subdivision (b) is the part families are never told: the tolling rule does not apply to an action against a public entity or public employee where a claim has to be presented under the Government Code. A bite at a public facility puts the family back on the six-month schedule in [Government Code section 911.2](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?sectionNum=911.2.&lawCode=GOV) no matter how young the child is.

### The provocation argument, and what the law expects of a child

Someone will suggest the child started it. [Evidence Code section 669](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?sectionNum=669.&lawCode=EVID) measures a child's conduct against the care ordinarily exercised by people of that maturity, intelligence and capacity, and it closes off even that argument where the violation occurred during an activity normally engaged in only by adults. Provocation shows up on the City's side of the file too. When the Department weighs whether to declare an animal dangerous, the presence or absence of provocation is one of the eleven categories of evidence it considers, alongside the place where the injury happened and the number of victims involved ([section 53.34.4](https://codelibrary.amlegal.com/codes/los_angeles/latest/lamc/0-0-0-137100)). Whatever a child says in week one tends to become the version everyone works from afterward, so take the account down once, carefully, and stop asking for it.

### Where children in this city meet dogs

Los Angeles is a renter's city built mostly before the current rules. Census figures put the owner-occupied share of housing units at 36.0 percent for 2020 through 2024 ([QuickFacts](https://www.census.gov/quickfacts/fact/table/losangelescitycalifornia/HSG445221)), and the Housing Department counts roughly 624,000 units across about 118,000 properties under the Rent Stabilization Ordinance, which generally reaches buildings first built on or before October 1, 1978 ([RSO coverage](https://housing.lacity.gov/residents/what-is-covered-under-the-rso)). In practice that means courtyard apartments, duplexes and bungalow courts in Echo Park, West Adams, Highland Park and Boyle Heights, and in Koreatown a residential density the Times measured at 42,611 people per square mile ([Mapping L.A.](https://maps.latimes.com/neighborhoods/neighborhood/koreatown/)). Children in buildings like those meet dogs on interior stairs, in laundry corridors and at gates rather than in fenced back yards. Photograph the route the child took and the sight lines from where the adults were standing.

### The dog may be quarantined down the hall

County Veterinary Public Health says most quarantines are performed in the biting animal's home and that dogs and cats are held for a minimum of ten days ([Reporting Animal Bites](http://publichealth.lacounty.gov/vet/biteintro.htm)). In a shared building that means the animal spends a week and a half behind a door on the same landing. Ask the agency in writing where confinement is being served and on what conditions, keep the answer, and route anything further through the agency rather than through the hallway.

### What the pediatric record has to contain

The provider's own words on body area, depth, repair method and any note about nerve, tendon or infection concern. Referrals for scar management, surgical revision or counseling. Dated photographs taken in consistent light as the swelling drops. The hours, mileage and missed school days that appointments consume. Ask the treating clinic which agency it notified, because reporting duties reach beyond the family, and get the report number in writing.

### The mistake that quietly shrinks a child's claim

Finishing it while the child is still growing. Revision work on a face or a hand is often deferred until growth allows it, so a figure discussed in week three is a figure set before anyone knows what the injury is. The second mistake is public. A post carrying photographs and a narrative becomes material the other side is entitled to read, and a child asked to retell the event weekly ends up remembering the retelling.

### The hearing that puts the dog on the record

If Animal Services sets a hearing, the machinery is worth knowing, because it produces sworn and transcribed material about the animal. Notice must be served at least ten days ahead and state the reason for the hearing and the remedy or penalty sought. A Hearing Examiner appointed by the General Manager presides, a recording or transcript is taken, the Department carries the burden by a preponderance, and hearsay may supplement direct evidence but cannot on its own support a finding ([hearing ordinance text](https://www.laanimalservices.com/sites/default/files/documents/PERMITS/LAMC53.18.5.pdf)). That is a public-safety proceeding about the dog's future, not a decision about a child's damages. On fees, [Business and Professions Code section 6147](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?sectionNum=6147.&lawCode=BPC) requires a written contract signed by both sides, a duplicate handed over at signing, the agreed rate on the page, a statement of how costs affect both the fee and the recovery, and language saying no law sets the rate.

## svc_landlord_dog_bite_liability_lede

A bite in the courtyard of a Los Angeles apartment building produces two claims, not one. The bite statute reaches the dog's owner. Reaching the building owner is an entirely separate proof about knowledge, authority and the condition of the property.

## svc_landlord_dog_bite_liability_body

### The deed and the lead are separate documents

[Civil Code section 3342](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?sectionNum=3342.&lawCode=CIV) fixes liability on the owner of the dog. It says nothing about whoever owns the walls around the dog. A claim aimed at a building owner, an ownership entity or a management company is ordinary negligence. Its statutory root is [Civil Code section 1714](https://law.justia.com/codes/california/code-civ/division-3/part-3/section-1714/), which makes everyone answerable for an injury occasioned to another by want of ordinary care or skill in the management of that person's property. So the questions change shape. What did the property side actually know about this particular animal before the bite, what authority did it hold over the animal or the space, and what condition of the building let the encounter happen at all.

### This is a rental city, and mostly an old one

Only 36.0 percent of Los Angeles housing units were owner occupied over 2020 through 2024 ([QuickFacts](https://www.census.gov/quickfacts/fact/table/losangelescitycalifornia/HSG445221)). The Housing Department reports approximately 624,000 units in about 118,000 properties covered by the Rent Stabilization Ordinance, which generally applies where the property was first built on or before October 1, 1978 ([RSO coverage](https://housing.lacity.gov/residents/what-is-covered-under-the-rso)). Pre-1978 stock is why so many bites here happen in space the tenant does not control. Interior stairwells and light wells serve Koreatown, whose density the Times put at 42,611 people per square mile ([Mapping L.A.](https://maps.latimes.com/neighborhoods/neighborhood/koreatown/)), and bungalow courts and side driveways do the same work in West Adams and Highland Park. Boyle Heights has shared front steps on its older lots, and hillside parcels in Echo Park and Silver Lake attach public stairways and retaining walls to the building rather than to any one unit.

### When a manager becomes the person in charge

The City ordinances are not written around registered ownership. Section 53.06.2 speaks to every person owning or having charge, care, custody or control of a dog, and [section 53.34](https://codelibrary.amlegal.com/codes/los_angeles/latest/lamc/0-0-0-137068) reaches a person who owns or is in charge of or controls or possesses one. A resident manager who walked the animal, held it during a unit showing, or moved it between units can fall inside that language even though the bite statute never names that person. Write down who physically had the animal, on what day, and at whose instruction.

### The City hearing writes things down about the property

The dangerous animal procedure is the strongest paper a property claim can borrow. Under [section 53.34.4](https://codelibrary.amlegal.com/codes/los_angeles/latest/lamc/0-0-0-137100), the Department may declare a dog dangerous after a hearing whenever it has bitten, attacked or caused injury. The evidence it weighs includes the place where the injury occurred, the manner in which the animal had been maintained by its owner or custodian, and whether public safety can be protected in future if the animal stays in the City. The remedies are physical and property-shaped.

A license can be issued or reissued on conditions. Those conditions can select locations on the property where the dog may not be kept, set the size, construction or design of an enclosure, require removal of one or more dogs from the premises, or require restraint, muzzling, identification or sterilization ([hearing ordinance text](https://www.laanimalservices.com/sites/default/files/documents/PERMITS/LAMC53.18.5.pdf)). The Department has run these hearings under ordinances adopted in July 1987 ([Administrative Hearing Guide](https://www.laanimalservices.com/administrative-hearing-guide)). A finding there is an animal control decision, not a damages decision, but it is dated, on the record, and frequently describes the gate, the yard or the enclosure that a landlord will later say was fine.

### The wrong agency wastes weeks

All six neighborhoods named on this site sit inside City of Los Angeles limits, so the Municipal Code track above is the one that applies. The County chapter is different machinery: outside the City, the Director may petition the Superior Court or serve a petition for an administrative hearing, and the hearing is held promptly between five and ten working days after service, before a neutral hearing officer, with no jury ([County Code 10.37.110](http://lacounty-ca.elaws.us/code/coor_title10_div1_ch10.37_sec10.37.110)). Two adjoining addresses can run on the two systems, so pin the exact address and unit before requesting anything. Where the property or the operator is public, [Government Code section 911.2](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?sectionNum=911.2.&lawCode=GOV) puts a written claim inside six months of accrual and [section 945.6](https://california.public.law/codes/ca_gov't_code_section_945.6) allows six months after a written rejection is mailed or delivered to file suit.

### The paper that leaves when the tenant leaves

Collect the lease and any pet addendum, the community rules, every message to the manager or owner in original form with its date and recipient visible, portal tickets, maintenance requests about the fence or the gate, and any notice served on the tenant. Photograph the latch, the hinge, the gap the animal used, the posted signs and the common area from several distances. In an older courtyard building the repair is often the first thing done after the ambulance pulls away, and a complaint written by neighbors after the attack proves nothing about the week before it.

### What the other side leads with, and what the contract must say

A property defense usually opens on duty rather than on the wound, arguing that nobody on the ownership side knew anything about this animal and that the bite happened inside space the tenant possessed exclusively. Expect the tenant's own renters coverage to go unmentioned while the building's carrier does the talking. On fees, no California statute fixes a percentage for this work. [Section 6147](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?sectionNum=6147.&lawCode=BPC) instead requires a written contract signed by both people with a duplicate given at signing, the agreed rate stated, an explanation of how costs bear on the fee and on the client's recovery, and a statement that the rate is negotiable rather than set by law.

## svc_dog_bite_insurance_claim_lede

Almost every dog bite payment in this city comes out of a residential liability policy. That makes the policy wording and the dated City and County records the real subject matter, long before anyone discusses a number.

## svc_dog_bite_insurance_claim_body

### What the adjuster is actually arguing about

Because [Civil Code section 3342](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?sectionNum=3342.&lawCode=CIV) removes the question of the dog's former viciousness and the owner's knowledge of it, a residential liability file rarely turns on whether the animal had a reputation. It turns on four narrower things: who owned the dog, whether the policy answers for that person at that address, whether the person bitten was in a public place or lawfully in a private one, and what the injury is worth once treatment finishes. Work out which of the four a letter is really about before answering it, because a paragraph about coverage and a paragraph about liability call for completely different responses.

### Three dated files the insurer did not write

The first is the Public Health bite record, which fixes the date, the location, the animal and the owner. The second is the animal control file, including anything generated by a hearing. The third is the licensing and rabies record for the dog. None of them was drafted by a party to your claim, which is precisely their value. Ask each agency for its number in writing and keep the request alongside the answer, because a records request with a date on it is itself evidence of when you asked.

### Why the hearing record carries weight

City hearing procedure is built to produce a usable record. A Hearing Examiner appointed by the General Manager presides. Relevant evidence is admitted if it is the sort responsible people rely on in serious affairs, hearsay may supplement direct evidence but cannot alone support a finding, oral evidence is taken only on oath or affirmation, and a recording or transcript of the proceeding is made. The Department carries the burden of showing its allegations true by a preponderance.

The Examiner then reports to the General Manager within fifteen days with a summary of the evidence including oral testimony, plus findings and recommendations, and that report is a public record served on the dog's owner. The General Manager's written decision follows within fifteen days of receiving it, becomes final when served, and may be appealed to the Board of Commissioners within fifteen days on a form the Department supplies ([hearing ordinance text](https://www.laanimalservices.com/sites/default/files/documents/PERMITS/LAMC53.18.5.pdf)). Reports of dangerous animals go to the nearest shelter, and officers investigating menacing behavior may impound an animal for observation ([Administrative Hearing Guide](https://www.laanimalservices.com/administrative-hearing-guide)).

### The paperwork attached to the animal

Licensing is not housekeeping trivia. [Municipal Code section 53.15.3](https://codelibrary.amlegal.com/codes/los_angeles/latest/lamc/0-0-0-136780) charges a $91.50 processing fee for a dog license, dropping to $16.50 for a spayed female, a neutered male, or a dog a veterinarian certifies cannot breed. Failing to pay the license tax and fee within forty-five days of acquiring a dog four months or older, of the dog reaching four months, of a license expiring, of a notice being mailed, or of an anti-rabies vaccination expiring is a misdemeanor. A lapsed license or an expired vaccination is therefore a dated public fact about how the animal was kept, and it sits in a file the owner cannot revise after the fact.

### What a bite report does not decide

County Veterinary Public Health is explicit that a report of a bite does not mean the animal has been deemed dangerous, and that a resident who believes an animal is dangerous must file a separate report with the local animal control agency ([Reporting Animal Bites](http://publichealth.lacounty.gov/vet/biteintro.htm)). Expect that sentence to be used in both directions: the existence of a report offered as proof of a vicious animal, or the absence of a dangerous declaration offered as proof of a harmless one. Neither is what the record says. The report establishes an event, a date and an animal, and the quarantine file records where the animal spent the following ten days.

### Read before signing, and send records once

A medical authorization can be drafted broadly enough to open years of unrelated history. A statement recorded in week one becomes the account the file treats as settled. A release can close more than the amount printed on the draft attached to it. If a letter reproduces only part of an exclusion, ask for the whole form, name the endorsement by number, and ask whether every page of the policy has been produced. Send treatment records when the treatment is documented rather than in installments, because a partial chart invites a valuation built on the emergency room bill alone.

### Two calendars that do not pause while you negotiate

Section 335.1 of the [Code of Civil Procedure](https://law.justia.com/codes/california/code-ccp/part-2/title-2/chapter-3/section-335-1/) sets the ordinary two-year period for a personal injury claim. If any public agency is in the facts, [Government Code section 911.2](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?sectionNum=911.2.&lawCode=GOV) requires the written claim within six months of accrual and [section 945.6](https://california.public.law/codes/ca_gov't_code_section_945.6) then allows six months after a written rejection is mailed or delivered. [Code of Civil Procedure section 352](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?sectionNum=352.&lawCode=CCP) tolls the period during a claimant's minority but expressly withholds that tolling from claims that must be presented to a public entity. Correspondence with an adjuster moves none of these dates.

### What the first contact covers, and how the fee agreement reads

Bring the declarations page, the letters with their envelopes or headers, the report numbers, the medical file, the photographs and a dated contact log. On fees, [Business and Professions Code section 6147](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?sectionNum=6147.&lawCode=BPC) requires the contract in writing, signed by both, with a duplicate handed over at signing. It must also state the agreed rate, how disbursements and costs will affect the fee and the client's recovery, and that the fee is negotiable and not fixed by law. Where any of that is missing, the plaintiff may void the agreement.

## svc_animal_control_reports_lede

Los Angeles produces several different bite files, held by different agencies, under different rules. Knowing which one records what is the difference between a documented incident and a remembered one.

## svc_animal_control_reports_body

### The health report, and who must make it

County Veterinary Public Health administers animal bite reporting. Its guidance states that all persons with knowledge of a bite are required to report it, that all mammal bites are reportable other than bites from small rodents and rabbits, and that the current location of the biting animal is one of the most important parts of the report because it determines whether the animal can be confined and observed. It also states that a report of a bite does not mean the animal has been deemed dangerous, and that a resident who believes an animal is dangerous must file a separate report with the local animal control agency. Certain cities take their own reports, including Pasadena, Long Beach and Vernon, each at its own number, and the program lists its own office address on North Figueroa Street with a telephone number for inquiries ([Reporting Animal Bites](http://publichealth.lacounty.gov/vet/biteintro.htm)).

### What the quarantine file records

The County rabies manual describes the confinement rules. County Code section 11.04.210 requires quarantine of at least fourteen days, reduced to ten days for dogs and cats, with release possible after five days where a veterinarian examines the animal on the fifth day or later and certifies that no clinical signs of rabies are present. Section 11.04.220 permits the confinement at the owner's home or in a licensed boarding kennel. Section 11.04.225 sets a fifty-dollar confinement fee, recovered where the bitten person was neither the owner nor the custodian of the animal and was not engaged in illegal activity at the time, and not assessed for police service dogs or guide dogs ([quarantine provisions](http://publichealth.lacounty.gov/vet/rabiesmanual/quarantines.htm)). County Animal Care and Control adds that quarantine is usually ten days and may occur at home, at the agency, or at an approved boarding facility, with the county health officer observing the animal at the start and end of the period ([Protecting People](https://animalcare.lacounty.gov/protecting-people/)).

### The dangerous-animal file is a different filing

That second report starts a public-safety process rather than a health one. County Animal Care and Control describes a Dangerous Dog Investigations Unit that investigates reported bites and attacks and may pursue potentially dangerous or vicious declarations, along with a Safe Neighborhoods Program that inspects property for safety. Inside the City of Los Angeles, [Municipal Code section 53.34.4](https://codelibrary.amlegal.com/codes/los_angeles/latest/lamc/0-0-0-137100) sets out the dangerous-animal procedure and lists eleven categories of evidence the Department may consider, and Los Angeles Animal Services states that reports of dangerous animals should go to the nearest shelter and that an animal may be impounded for observation ([Administrative Hearing Guide](https://www.laanimalservices.com/administrative-hearing-guide)).

### Which agency, and why the address decides

Jurisdiction follows the address where the animal is kept, not the address of the person bitten. City incidents run through Los Angeles Animal Services under the Municipal Code, including the hearing procedure in [section 53.18.5](https://www.laanimalservices.com/sites/default/files/documents/PERMITS/LAMC53.18.5.pdf). Unincorporated areas and contract cities run through County Animal Care and Control under County Code section 10.37.110, which allows the Director to petition the Superior Court or to serve a petition for an administrative hearing, sets the hearing between five and ten working days after service, provides that there is no jury and the standard is a preponderance, and requires a hearing officer who neither signed the petition nor is junior in rank to the person who did ([County Code 10.37.110](http://lacounty-ca.elaws.us/code/coor_title10_div1_ch10.37_sec10.37.110)). The state framework behind both appears at [Food and Agricultural Code section 31621](https://law.justia.com/codes/california/code-fac/division-14/chapter-9/article-2/section-31621/), which also permits a city or county to establish its own administrative hearing procedure.

### The ordinary City rules that generate paperwork

Routine ordinances often supply the dated detail in a file. Los Angeles Animal Services publishes a summary of important animal laws stating that dogs must be leashed under Municipal Code section 53.06, that a household may keep up to three dogs and three cats under sections 53.00 and 53.50, that tethering a dog for more than three cumulative hours in a twenty-four hour period is unlawful under section 53.70, and that owners must clean up after their animals under section 53.49 ([animal laws handout](https://www.laanimalservices.com/sites/default/files/2022-10/LAASAnimalLawsHandout.pdf)). Animals at large are addressed separately in [Municipal Code section 53.34](https://codelibrary.amlegal.com/codes/los_angeles/latest/lamc/0-0-0-137068).

### Asking for records, and the statute that supports it

Agency files are public records subject to the usual exemptions. [Government Code section 7922.530](https://codes.findlaw.com/ca/government-code/gov-sect-7922-530/) requires each state or local agency, upon a request that reasonably describes an identifiable record, to make the records promptly available on payment of fees covering direct costs of duplication or an applicable statutory fee, and provides that a requester inspecting a disclosable record on the agency's premises may use the requester's own equipment to photograph or copy it without charge, subject to stated limits. Requests should name the agency, the date, the address and the animal, and should be kept alongside the response.

### Why an ordinance violation matters to a report

Where a report documents a violation of a restraint ordinance such as [Municipal Code section 53.06.2](https://codelibrary.amlegal.com/codes/los_angeles/latest/lamc/0-0-0-136443), [Evidence Code section 669](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?sectionNum=669.&lawCode=EVID) presumes a failure to exercise due care once its four conditions are met. That is a large consequence to hang on whether a responding officer wrote down the leash length.

### Scope

General information about published agency guidance, ordinances and statutes. Not legal advice, and not a description of any particular report. Questions belong with the attorney who advertises on this site.

## svc_apartment_and_landlord_liability_lede

Most Los Angeles bite accounts involve somebody else's building. The bite statute does not reach a building owner, so an apartment claim is built on a different duty and a different paper trail.

## svc_apartment_and_landlord_liability_body

### Why the statute stops at the dog's owner

[Civil Code section 3342](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?sectionNum=3342.&lawCode=CIV) addresses the owner of the dog. A property owner, a management company and an on-site manager are not the owner of the tenant's dog, so the strict rule that removes former viciousness from the discussion does not apply to them. What applies instead is the general duty in [Civil Code section 1714](https://codes.findlaw.com/ca/civil-code/civ-sect-1714/): a person is responsible for injury caused to another by want of ordinary care in the management of property. That change of statute changes the whole subject matter of the claim. Knowledge, control and what was done after learning something become the questions, rather than the fact of the bite.

### The size of the local rental picture

This is not a marginal scenario in Los Angeles. Census QuickFacts puts the City's owner-occupied housing unit rate at 36.3 percent for 2019 to 2023, with 1,419,663 households and a median gross rent of $1,879 ([QuickFacts, Los Angeles city](https://www.census.gov/quickfacts/losangelescitycalifornia)). The Los Angeles Housing Department describes the Rent Stabilization Ordinance as covering approximately 624,000 units in 118,000 properties, generally rental units in buildings with two or more units built on or before October 1, 1978 ([What is covered under the RSO](https://housing.lacity.gov/residents/what-is-covered-under-the-rso)). Older multi-unit stock means shared hallways, interior courtyards, laundry rooms, parking structures and controlled-access gates, which is exactly the geography in which a resident, a guest or a worker meets a neighbor's dog.

### Common areas are off the keeper's premises

The restraint rule in [Municipal Code section 53.06.2](https://codelibrary.amlegal.com/codes/los_angeles/latest/lamc/0-0-0-136443) reaches a dog when it is off the premises of its keeper, on a chain or leash no longer than six feet under the control of a competent person. In a multi-unit building, the tenant's premises is the unit, which means a shared corridor, an elevator lobby, a stairwell or a courtyard is generally not the place where an unrestrained dog belongs. Where that ordinance is violated, [Evidence Code section 669](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?sectionNum=669.&lawCode=EVID) supplies a presumption of failure to exercise due care, provided the violation proximately caused the injury, the injury was of the kind the enactment was designed to prevent, and the injured person belonged to the protected class. That analysis is about the dog's keeper. It also tends to describe the exact space a landlord controls, which is why the location of a bite matters twice over.

### What a management file may already contain

Building records are usually written before anyone contemplates a claim, which makes them useful. Maintenance tickets describing a gate that does not latch. Complaint emails from other residents about a dog in the courtyard. Notices posted about pets in common areas. Pet addenda naming the animal. Correspondence with a resident about a prior incident. Security camera retention schedules, which are frequently short and worth asking about in writing on the first day rather than the fourth week. Requests should be dated and kept alongside the answers, because a dated request is itself evidence of when the question was raised.

### Agency files that reach the building, not just the animal

Two separate public processes may generate documents about the same dog. County Veterinary Public Health handles bite reports and quarantine, and states plainly that a bite report does not mean an animal has been deemed dangerous, so a resident who believes an animal is dangerous must file a separate report with the local animal control agency ([Reporting Animal Bites](http://publichealth.lacounty.gov/vet/biteintro.htm)). City animal control runs the dangerous-animal procedure, and [Municipal Code section 53.34.4](https://codelibrary.amlegal.com/codes/los_angeles/latest/lamc/0-0-0-137100) lists categories of evidence the Department may consider, so a prior hearing file can document what was known about the animal at an address before the incident under discussion.

### Where the quarantine record sits

The County rabies manual describes County Code section 11.04.210 as requiring quarantine of at least fourteen days, reduced to ten for dogs and cats, with earlier release possible after five days where a veterinarian examines the animal on the fifth day or later and certifies no clinical signs, and section 11.04.220 allows that confinement at home or in a licensed boarding kennel ([quarantine provisions](http://publichealth.lacounty.gov/vet/rabiesmanual/quarantines.htm)). For an apartment claim, the value of that file is locational: it records where the animal was kept, which unit, and under whose custody.

### Divided fault, and two categories of loss

Where a dog owner and a property owner are both said to be at fault, [Civil Code section 1431.2](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?sectionNum=1431.2.&lawCode=CIV) makes liability for non-economic damages several only, each defendant answering in proportion to that defendant's own percentage of fault, while treating medical expenses and lost earnings as economic damages under its definitions. Two policies may respond, on different terms, to the same incident.

### Practical notes and the limits of this page

Photograph the space as well as the injury: the gate, the latch, the sight lines, the signage, the corridor. Note who employed the person who responded. Keep the tenancy documents. This page describes general California and Los Angeles rules and is not legal advice about any particular building or incident; specific questions belong with the attorney who advertises on this site.

## svc_child_facial_injuries_lede

A bite to a child's face raises two questions at once: what the injury will require over years of growth, and what a court must approve before any of it is resolved.

## svc_child_facial_injuries_body

### Why the wound description matters beyond medicine

State law contains a definition that turns a clinical description into a legal category. [Food and Agricultural Code section 31604](https://law.justia.com/codes/california/code-fac/division-14/chapter-9/article-1/section-31604/) defines severe injury as any physical injury to a human being that results in muscle tears or disfiguring lacerations, or requires multiple sutures or corrective or cosmetic surgery. Facial bites frequently meet that description on the first emergency-department note. The definition is used to separate the categories in the dangerous-dog chapter, since [section 31602](https://law.justia.com/codes/california/code-fac/division-14/chapter-9/article-1/section-31602/) defines a potentially dangerous dog partly by reference to a bite causing a less severe injury than section 31604 describes. So the words a treating clinician chooses on the first day may end up quoted in an administrative or judicial proceeding months later, which is a reason to obtain the full chart rather than a discharge summary.

### The liability rule does not soften for a child, and does not harden either

[Civil Code section 3342](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?sectionNum=3342.&lawCode=CIV) applies the same way: the owner answers for a bite suffered in a public place or while the person is lawfully in a private place, regardless of the former viciousness of the animal or the owner's knowledge of it. Where an ordinance violation is in the facts, [Evidence Code section 669](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?sectionNum=669.&lawCode=EVID) presumes failure to exercise due care on the four stated conditions. That section also contains a subdivision written for young people: under subdivision (b)(2), the presumption may be rebutted by proof that the person who violated the enactment was a child and exercised the degree of care ordinarily exercised by persons of that maturity, intelligence and capacity under similar circumstances, though not where the violation occurred during an activity normally engaged in only by adults and requiring adult qualifications.

### The clock, and the one exception that closes early

[Code of Civil Procedure section 335.1](https://law.justia.com/codes/california/code-ccp/part-2/title-2/chapter-3/section-335-1/) sets two years for an action for injury caused by the wrongful act or neglect of another. [Section 352](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?sectionNum=352.&lawCode=CCP) provides that where a person entitled to bring an action is under the age of majority, the time of that disability is not part of the limitations period. Subdivision (b) then withholds that tolling from an action against a public entity or public employee for which a claim must first be presented. Where a public agency is anywhere in the facts, the [six-month presentation rule](https://codes.findlaw.com/ca/government-code/gov-sect-911-2/) in Government Code section 911.2 governs the written claim for a personal injury cause of action, and the [suit deadline](https://codes.findlaw.com/ca/government-code/gov-sect-945-6/) in section 945.6 measures six months from the day a written rejection is mailed or delivered. A family relying on a child's tolling can lose the public-entity part of a case while the private part is still open.

### Nobody settles a child's claim alone

Procedure requires an adult and a judge. [Code of Civil Procedure section 372](https://codes.findlaw.com/ca/code-of-civil-procedure/ccp-sect-372/) requires a minor who is a party to appear by a guardian or conservator of the estate or by a guardian ad litem appointed by the court, and provides that the person so appearing has power, with the approval of the court, to compromise the action, agree to the judgment, and release the claim pursuant to that compromise. Money or property to be paid for the benefit of a minor is then handled under the Probate Code chapter beginning at section 3600.

### Where the money goes afterward

[Probate Code section 3611](https://codes.findlaw.com/ca/probate-code/prob-sect-3611/) lists what the court may order for the remaining balance. A guardian or conservator of the estate may be appointed and the balance delivered to that person. The balance may be deposited in an insured account at a financial institution in this state, or in a single-premium deferred annuity, subject to withdrawal only on court authorization. It may go to a special needs trust established under section 3604 after a hearing, with notice mailed to named state directors at least fifteen days beforehand. Where the balance does not exceed twenty thousand dollars, the court may hold it on other conditions it determines to be in the minor's best interest, and where it does not exceed five thousand dollars for a minor, it may be paid to a parent without bond on stated terms. Families are often surprised that a settlement does not arrive as a check; the statute explains why.

### Facial injuries and the timing problem

The measure of compensation is broad. [Civil Code section 3333](https://law.justia.com/codes/california/code-civ/division-4/part-1/title-2/chapter-2/article-2/section-3333/) allows all the detriment proximately caused, whether or not it could have been anticipated. That breadth is precisely why a facial claim for a growing child sits awkwardly with early resolution: the surgical plan, the revision schedule and the eventual appearance are opinions that treating specialists form over time. Photographs at fixed intervals, in consistent light, are the cheapest record a family can keep, and the operative reports and clinic notes are the documents that later carry the discussion.

### The public files that run in parallel

A bite report and a dangerous-animal report are different filings. County Veterinary Public Health states that a bite report does not mean the animal has been deemed dangerous and that a separate report must go to the local animal control agency ([Reporting Animal Bites](http://publichealth.lacounty.gov/vet/biteintro.htm)). Inside the City, dangerous-animal procedure appears at [Municipal Code section 53.34.4](https://codelibrary.amlegal.com/codes/los_angeles/latest/lamc/0-0-0-137100), with hearing procedure at [section 53.18.5](https://www.laanimalservices.com/sites/default/files/documents/PERMITS/LAMC53.18.5.pdf).

### Scope

General information about California statutes and Los Angeles procedures, not legal advice, and not a description of any child's case. Questions about a specific injury belong with the attorney who advertises on this site.

## svc_dangerous_dog_hearings_los_angeles_lede

Two different dangerous-dog systems operate across this county, one built by City ordinance and one built by state statute. Which one applies depends on the address where the dog is kept.

## svc_dangerous_dog_hearings_los_angeles_body

### The state framework, and the permission it grants

State law defines the categories. [Food and Agricultural Code section 31602](https://law.justia.com/codes/california/code-fac/division-14/chapter-9/article-1/section-31602/) describes a potentially dangerous dog as one that, unprovoked, on two separate occasions within the prior 36-month period required defensive action by a person off the owner's property, or bit a person causing a less severe injury than the statute's severe-injury definition, or twice within that period killed or injured a domestic animal off the owner's property. [Section 31604](https://law.justia.com/codes/california/code-fac/division-14/chapter-9/article-1/section-31604/) defines severe injury as muscle tears or disfiguring lacerations, or injuries requiring multiple sutures or corrective or cosmetic surgery, and [section 31603](https://law.justia.com/codes/california/code-fac/division-14/chapter-9/article-1/section-31603/) reserves the vicious label for aggravated cases. [Section 31683](https://law.justia.com/codes/california/code-fac/division-14/chapter-9/article-5/section-31683/) then allows a city or county to run its own program, including more restrictive terms, while providing that no program shall be specific as to breed.

### The judicial route and its clock

Under [section 31621](https://law.justia.com/codes/california/code-fac/division-14/chapter-9/article-2/section-31621/), where an officer has determined probable cause, the chief officer of the shelter or animal control department, that officer's immediate supervisor, or the head of the local law enforcement agency petitions the superior court of the county where the dog is owned or kept, and the proceeding is a limited civil case. The same section lets a city or county establish an administrative hearing procedure to hear those petitions instead. Notice and a copy of the petition are served personally or by first-class mail with return receipt requested, the hearing is held within no less than five working days nor more than ten working days after service, and it is open to the public.

### The County's version of the same procedure

Los Angeles County has taken up that option. County Code section 10.37.110 permits the Director to petition the Superior Court or to serve a petition for an administrative hearing, sets the hearing between five and ten working days after service, provides that there is no jury and that the standard is a preponderance of the evidence, and requires a neutral hearing officer who neither signed the petition nor is junior in rank to the person who did ([County Code 10.37.110](http://lacounty-ca.elaws.us/code/coor_title10_div1_ch10.37_sec10.37.110)). County Animal Care and Control describes a Dangerous Dog Investigations Unit that investigates reported bites and attacks and may seek those declarations, alongside a Safe Neighborhoods Program property inspection ([Protecting People](https://animalcare.lacounty.gov/protecting-people/)).

### The City procedure is a different document

Inside the City of Los Angeles, [Municipal Code section 53.34.4](https://codelibrary.amlegal.com/codes/los_angeles/latest/lamc/0-0-0-137100) governs. It lists eleven categories of evidence the Department may consider in deciding whether an animal is dangerous, and it sets out what may follow a finding, including conditions on keeping the animal and, in stated circumstances, humane destruction. Where a hearing produces a finding that an animal is not dangerous, the section still contemplates that a license may be revoked or reissued with conditions. The order timing is written into subdivision (d): the General Manager signs an order within two days after the time for appeal has run, or within five days after service on the Board.

### How the City hearing is actually conducted

[Municipal Code section 53.18.5](https://www.laanimalservices.com/sites/default/files/documents/PERMITS/LAMC53.18.5.pdf) supplies the procedure. A Hearing Examiner appointed by the General Manager presides. Written notice is given at least ten days before the hearing. Subpoenas are available through the authority in Charter section 217. Relevant evidence is admitted if it is the sort responsible persons rely on in serious affairs, hearsay may supplement but cannot by itself support a finding, and a recording or transcript is made. The Department carries the burden of proving its allegations by a preponderance, and the Examiner reports to the General Manager within fifteen days with a summary of the evidence and recommendations. Los Angeles Animal Services notes that ordinances adopted in July 1987 authorized these administrative hearings, that reports of dangerous animals go to the nearest shelter, and that an animal may be impounded for observation ([Administrative Hearing Guide](https://www.laanimalservices.com/administrative-hearing-guide)).

### What a declaration changes for the owner

The state consequences are concrete. [Section 31641](https://california.public.law/codes/food_and_agricultural_code_section_31641) requires licensing and vaccination and a notation of the designation in the registration records, with an additional fee permitted. [Section 31642](https://law.justia.com/codes/california/code-fac/division-14/chapter-9/article-3/section-31642/) requires the dog to be kept indoors or in a securely fenced yard and, off the premises, on a substantial leash under the control of a responsible adult. [Section 31643](https://law.justia.com/codes/california/code-fac/division-14/chapter-9/article-3/section-31643/) requires written notice to animal control within two working days of the animal's death, sale or removal from the jurisdiction. [Section 31645](https://california.public.law/codes/food_and_agricultural_code_section_31645) allows destruction of a dog declared vicious where public safety cannot otherwise be protected, and [section 31646](https://california.public.law/codes/food_and_agricultural_code_section_31646) allows a prohibition on owning any dog for up to three years.

### Why the record outlives the hearing

A hearing is a public-safety proceeding, not a compensation proceeding, and nothing decided in it awards anyone anything. Its value afterward is documentary: sworn complaints, incident reports, a summary of testimony, findings, and a dated order, all produced by people who were not parties to any later civil dispute. County Veterinary Public Health is explicit that a bite report is not itself a dangerous-animal finding and that a separate report to local animal control is required ([Reporting Animal Bites](http://publichealth.lacounty.gov/vet/biteintro.htm)).

## svc_delivery_driver_and_postal_bites_lede

Couriers, carriers and meter readers are named in the bite statute itself, not by argument. When the route is the job, a second system of rules also applies before any of it reaches a homeowner's policy.

## svc_delivery_driver_and_postal_bites_body

### The statute says who is lawfully there

[Civil Code section 3342](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?sectionNum=3342.&lawCode=CIV) attaches liability to the owner of a dog for a bite suffered in a public place or while the person is lawfully in a private place, and it then defines lawful presence to include a person performing a duty imposed by the laws of this state or by the laws or postal regulations of the United States, as well as a person on the property by invitation express or implied. For someone delivering to a porch, reading a meter or serving papers, that clause moves the ordinary entry dispute out of the way. What remains is ownership of the animal, the location, and the injury.

### Gates, lobbies and courtyards change the location analysis

Delivery in this city rarely stops at a single front door. Packages travel through controlled-access gates, interior courtyards, subterranean parking, mail rooms and long exterior corridors, a consequence of the housing stock: the Los Angeles Housing Department describes rent stabilization as covering approximately 624,000 units in 118,000 properties, generally buildings of two or more units built on or before October 1, 1978 ([RSO coverage](https://housing.lacity.gov/residents/what-is-covered-under-the-rso)). Inside the City, [Municipal Code section 53.06.2](https://codelibrary.amlegal.com/codes/los_angeles/latest/lamc/0-0-0-136443) requires a dog off its keeper's premises to be on a substantial chain or leash no longer than six feet under a competent person's control, and a shared corridor or courtyard is not the unit. [Municipal Code section 53.34](https://codelibrary.amlegal.com/codes/los_angeles/latest/lamc/0-0-0-137068) treats animals at large separately and makes the violation a misdemeanor where the animal bites, with an ownership prohibition of up to three years available following conviction.

### The presumption a restraint violation creates

Where an ordinance was violated, [Evidence Code section 669](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?sectionNum=669.&lawCode=EVID) presumes failure to exercise due care if the violation proximately caused the injury, the injury resulted from the nature of occurrence the enactment was designed to prevent, and the injured person belonged to the class the enactment protected. Subdivision (b)(1) allows rebuttal by proof that the person did what might reasonably be expected of a person of ordinary prudence acting under similar circumstances who desired to comply with the law. Whether the dog was loose in a gated area, tied outside a lobby, or held on a long retractable line is therefore a factual question with a legal consequence, and it is answered by photographs and the responding officer's notes rather than recollection.

### The second system: work injuries

An injury on the route also runs through workers' compensation. [Labor Code section 3600](https://codes.findlaw.com/ca/labor-code/lab-sect-3600/) sets the conditions under which compensation is the exclusive remedy against an employer. That exclusivity is directed at the employer, not at the dog's owner. [Labor Code section 3852](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?sectionNum=3852.&lawCode=LAB) provides that a compensation claim does not affect the injured worker's right of action against a third party who caused the injury, and it also allows the employer to claim against that third party for amounts paid. So a delivery worker bitten by a resident's dog may have a compensation claim and a separate claim against the animal's owner running at the same time, on separate paperwork.

### How the two recoveries interact

[Labor Code section 3856](https://codes.findlaw.com/ca/labor-code/lab-sect-3856/) addresses the order of payment where there is a suit against the third party. Where the employee prosecutes the action alone, the court first orders paid from any judgment the reasonable litigation expenses and a reasonable attorney's fee based solely on services rendered in effecting the recovery for both the employee and the employer, and then, on the employer's application, allows as a first lien the amount of the employer's expenditure for compensation together with amounts to which the employer may be entitled as special damages under section 3852. Subdivisions (a) and (c) set out the parallel sequences when the employer sues alone or the two proceed together. The practical point is that the compensation carrier's interest is dealt with inside the third-party recovery, in a defined order, rather than being ignored.

### Reporting duties do not depend on the employer

County Veterinary Public Health states that all persons with knowledge of a bite are required to report it, that all mammal bites are reportable other than those from small rodents and rabbits, that the current location of the biting animal is one of the most important parts of the report, and that Pasadena, Long Beach and Vernon take reports for their own jurisdictions at their own numbers ([Reporting Animal Bites](http://publichealth.lacounty.gov/vet/biteintro.htm)). A route worker who reports through an employer app has usually not made that report. The County rabies manual describes quarantine under County Code section 11.04.210 of at least fourteen days, reduced to ten for dogs and cats, with a confinement fee under section 11.04.225 recovered where the bitten person was neither the owner nor the custodian of the animal and was not engaged in illegal activity ([quarantine provisions](http://publichealth.lacounty.gov/vet/rabiesmanual/quarantines.htm)).

### Documents worth collecting on the day

The address and unit, not the block. Photographs of the gate, the latch and the sight line from the entry point. The scan or timestamp record showing when the stop occurred. Names of any building staff who responded. Whether animal control or police attended and under what report number. The rabies license and vaccination status of the dog, which [Health and Safety Code section 121690](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?sectionNum=121690.&lawCode=HSC) requires in rabies areas after the dog reaches four months for licensing and three months for vaccination.

### What the money is measured against

Compensation for the third-party claim is measured under [Civil Code section 3333](https://law.justia.com/codes/california/code-civ/division-4/part-1/title-2/chapter-2/article-2/section-3333/) as all the detriment proximately caused, whether or not it could have been anticipated. Where fault is divided among several people, [Civil Code section 1431.2](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?sectionNum=1431.2.&lawCode=CIV) makes liability for non-economic damages several only and proportionate to each defendant's own share of fault.

## svc_dog_attack_wrongful_death_lede

A death changes which statutes apply and which family members may act. Two related actions exist, they recover different things, and one of them lost a category of damages for cases filed after the start of 2026.

## svc_dog_attack_wrongful_death_body

### Who may bring the action

[Code of Civil Procedure section 377.60](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?sectionNum=377.60.&lawCode=CCP) provides that a cause of action for the death of a person caused by the wrongful act or neglect of another may be asserted by listed persons or by the decedent's personal representative on their behalf. Subdivision (a) names the surviving spouse, domestic partner, children and issue of deceased children, or, where there is no surviving issue, the persons who would be entitled to the decedent's property by intestate succession, and it addresses legal guardians where the parents who would otherwise be entitled are deceased. Subdivision (b) reaches a putative spouse, children of a putative spouse, stepchildren, parents or legal guardians who were dependent on the decedent, and it defines a putative spouse by reference to a void or voidable marriage where the court finds the survivor held a good faith belief in its validity. Subdivision (c) then reaches a minor who had lived in the decedent's household throughout the previous 180 days and looked to the decedent for at least half of that minor's support.

### What the death action pays, and what it does not

[Section 377.61](https://california.public.law/codes/ca_civ_proc_code_section_377.61) provides that damages may be awarded that, under all the circumstances of the case, may be just, but may not include damages recoverable under section 377.34, and it directs the court to determine the respective rights in an award of the persons entitled to assert the cause of action. That last sentence matters in families with several claimants: the statute contemplates apportionment by the court rather than by agreement among relatives.

### The survival action, and the window that closed

The second action belongs to the decedent's own claim. [Section 377.34](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?sectionNum=377.34.&lawCode=CCP) limits damages in an action by a personal representative or successor in interest on the decedent's cause of action to the loss or damage the decedent sustained or incurred before death, including penalties or punitive damages the decedent would have been entitled to recover, and states that they do not include damages for pain, suffering or disfigurement. Subdivision (b) creates an exception: those damages may be included where the action was granted a preference under section 36 before January 1, 2022, or was filed on or after January 1, 2022, and before January 1, 2026. On the face of that subdivision, an action filed after that date falls back to the general rule in subdivision (a). Subdivision (c) required plaintiffs recovering under subdivision (b) between January 1, 2022, and January 1, 2025, to report specified information to the Judicial Council within sixty days.

### Which liability rule applies

[Civil Code section 3342](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?sectionNum=3342.&lawCode=CIV) addresses damages suffered by a person bitten in a public place or while lawfully in a private place, regardless of the former viciousness of the animal or the owner's knowledge of it. A fatal attack may also involve conduct the statute does not describe, and there the general duty in [Civil Code section 1714](https://codes.findlaw.com/ca/civil-code/civ-sect-1714/) governs, since it makes everyone responsible for injury caused to another by want of ordinary care in the management of property or person. A restraint violation may add the presumption in [Evidence Code section 669](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?sectionNum=669.&lawCode=EVID) where the four listed conditions are met, and inside the City the relevant enactments are commonly [Municipal Code section 53.06.2](https://codelibrary.amlegal.com/codes/los_angeles/latest/lamc/0-0-0-136443) on restraint and [section 53.34](https://codelibrary.amlegal.com/codes/los_angeles/latest/lamc/0-0-0-137068) on animals at large, which makes the offense a misdemeanor where the animal bites and allows an ownership prohibition of up to three years after conviction.

### The state categories, and what follows a finding

[Food and Agricultural Code section 31604](https://law.justia.com/codes/california/code-fac/division-14/chapter-9/article-1/section-31604/) defines severe injury by reference to muscle tears or disfiguring lacerations, or injuries requiring multiple sutures or corrective or cosmetic surgery, and [section 31603](https://law.justia.com/codes/california/code-fac/division-14/chapter-9/article-1/section-31603/) defines the vicious category. Where a dog is declared vicious, [section 31645](https://california.public.law/codes/food_and_agricultural_code_section_31645) allows destruction where public safety cannot otherwise be protected, and [section 31646](https://california.public.law/codes/food_and_agricultural_code_section_31646) allows the owner to be prohibited from owning any dog for up to three years. [Civil Code section 3342.5](https://california.public.law/codes/ca_civ_code_section_3342.5) separately places a duty on an owner whose dog has bitten a human being to take reasonable steps to remove any danger from bites by that animal.

### The public files in a fatal case

The same agencies remain involved. County Veterinary Public Health handles the bite report and quarantine and states that a bite report does not itself deem an animal dangerous ([Reporting Animal Bites](http://publichealth.lacounty.gov/vet/biteintro.htm)). The County rabies manual describes quarantine under County Code section 11.04.210 and the confinement options in section 11.04.220 ([quarantine provisions](http://publichealth.lacounty.gov/vet/rabiesmanual/quarantines.htm)). County Animal Care and Control describes its Dangerous Dog Investigations Unit and its property inspection program ([Protecting People](https://animalcare.lacounty.gov/protecting-people/)). A City dangerous-animal proceeding follows [Municipal Code section 53.34.4](https://codelibrary.amlegal.com/codes/los_angeles/latest/lamc/0-0-0-137100) with hearing procedure in [section 53.18.5](https://www.laanimalservices.com/sites/default/files/documents/PERMITS/LAMC53.18.5.pdf), which requires notice at least ten days beforehand, provides that hearsay may supplement but cannot alone support a finding, and requires a recording or transcript.

### Deadlines, and the additional one for agencies

[Code of Civil Procedure section 335.1](https://law.justia.com/codes/california/code-ccp/part-2/title-2/chapter-3/section-335-1/) covers an action for the death of an individual caused by the wrongful act or neglect of another. Where a public entity is involved, the [presentation deadline](https://codes.findlaw.com/ca/government-code/gov-sect-911-2/) in Government Code section 911.2 calls for a written claim inside six months of accrual, [section 912.4](https://codes.findlaw.com/ca/government-code/gov-sect-912-4/) requires board action within forty-five days and deems inaction a rejection, and the [suit-filing provision](https://codes.findlaw.com/ca/government-code/gov-sect-945-6/) numbered 945.6 allows six months measured from the mailing or delivery of a rejection notice.

## svc_hoa_and_common_area_attacks_lede

In a condominium or a planned development, the space where most encounters happen belongs to nobody in particular and to everybody at once. California statute assigns responsibility for that space by name.

## svc_hoa_and_common_area_attacks_body

### The statute that names the common area

[Civil Code section 4775](https://codes.findlaw.com/ca/civil-code/civ-sect-4775/), part of the Davis-Stirling Common Interest Development Act, provides that unless otherwise stated in the declaration, the association is responsible for repairing, replacing and maintaining the common area, while the owner of each separate interest is responsible for that separate interest. It also allocates exclusive use common area: unless the declaration provides otherwise, the owner maintains the exclusive use common area appurtenant to that separate interest and the association repairs and replaces it. Those allocations are the starting point for any question about a gate that does not close, a fence panel missing from a pool enclosure, a broken latch on a courtyard door, or lighting in a corridor where an encounter happened.

### The declaration is a document, not a summary

Because section 4775 begins with the phrase about what the declaration provides, the recorded declaration and the association's rules can shift responsibility for particular components. That makes the recorded documents primary evidence rather than background reading: the declaration, any amendments, the rules on animals, and the minutes or notices in which the board addressed a dog complaint. Requests for those documents should be dated and kept with the response.

### The dog's owner is governed by a different statute again

None of this displaces the rule that governs the animal. [Civil Code section 3342](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?sectionNum=3342.&lawCode=CIV) makes the owner of the dog liable for damages suffered by a person bitten in a public place or while lawfully in a private place, including the dog owner's own property, regardless of the former viciousness of the animal or the owner's knowledge of it. An association is not the owner of a resident's dog. Its potential exposure rests on the ordinary duty in [Civil Code section 1714](https://codes.findlaw.com/ca/civil-code/civ-sect-1714/) to avoid want of ordinary care in managing property, read together with what section 4775 says it was responsible for maintaining.

### Why a hallway is treated as off the premises

Inside the City, [Municipal Code section 53.06.2](https://codelibrary.amlegal.com/codes/los_angeles/latest/lamc/0-0-0-136443) requires a dog off its keeper's premises to be under a competent person's control on a substantial chain or leash no longer than six feet. A resident's premises in a common interest development is the unit, so a lobby, an elevator, a stairwell, a shared garage or a landscaped courtyard is generally not it. Where that ordinance is violated, [Evidence Code section 669](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?sectionNum=669.&lawCode=EVID) presumes failure to exercise due care where the violation proximately caused the injury, the injury resulted from the nature of occurrence the enactment was designed to prevent, and the injured person was within the protected class. Subdivision (b)(1) allows rebuttal by proof of what a person of ordinary prudence desiring to comply would have done in similar circumstances.

### Notice, and where notice is written down

An association's file is often the only place a prior incident appears. Board minutes recording a complaint about a dog in the pool area. Violation letters sent to an owner. Work orders about a gate closer. Vendor invoices for fence repair. Correspondence with a property manager. Two public files may also bear on notice: County Veterinary Public Health bite reports, which the agency says do not themselves deem an animal dangerous and must be supplemented by a separate report to local animal control if a resident believes it is dangerous ([Reporting Animal Bites](http://publichealth.lacounty.gov/vet/biteintro.htm)), and City dangerous-animal proceedings under [Municipal Code section 53.34.4](https://codelibrary.amlegal.com/codes/los_angeles/latest/lamc/0-0-0-137100), which lists eleven evidence categories the Department may consider.

### Where the property sits decides the animal-control agency

City addresses run through Los Angeles Animal Services and its hearing procedure in [Municipal Code section 53.18.5](https://www.laanimalservices.com/sites/default/files/documents/PERMITS/LAMC53.18.5.pdf). Developments in unincorporated areas and contract cities run through County Animal Care and Control, whose administrative hearings follow County Code section 10.37.110, with the hearing set between five and ten working days after service, no jury, a preponderance standard, and a neutral hearing officer ([County Code 10.37.110](http://lacounty-ca.elaws.us/code/coor_title10_div1_ch10.37_sec10.37.110)). The state framework permitting either route appears at [Food and Agricultural Code section 31621](https://law.justia.com/codes/california/code-fac/division-14/chapter-9/article-2/section-31621/).

### Several defendants, and two categories of loss

Where an association, a property manager and a dog owner are all said to have contributed, [Civil Code section 1431.2](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?sectionNum=1431.2.&lawCode=CIV) makes liability for non-economic damages several only and proportionate to each defendant's own percentage of fault, while treating medical expenses and lost earnings as economic damages under its definitions. The measure of loss itself remains [Civil Code section 3333](https://law.justia.com/codes/california/code-civ/division-4/part-1/title-2/chapter-2/article-2/section-3333/), covering all the detriment proximately caused whether or not it could have been anticipated.

### Practical steps in a shared-space incident

Photograph the component as well as the injury: the gate, the latch, the hinge, the fence line, the lighting, the signage. Note the unit numbers involved rather than the building address alone. Ask in writing whether camera footage exists and how long it is retained. Identify who employs the person who responded, since a management company, a security vendor and the association may be three different entities.

## svc_homeowners_and_renters_insurance_claims_lede

Residential liability coverage is where most Los Angeles bite payments come from, and state regulation puts calendar dates on the insurer's side of the file. Those dates are published, and they are worth knowing before the first letter arrives.

## svc_homeowners_and_renters_insurance_claims_body

### Two different policies, one incident

In a city where the owner-occupied housing unit rate stands at 36.3 percent for 2019 to 2023 with 1,419,663 households ([QuickFacts, Los Angeles city](https://www.census.gov/quickfacts/losangelescitycalifornia)), the dog and the building frequently belong to different people, each with separate insurance. A resident's renters policy may respond for the animal's owner. A building owner's policy responds for the building owner, on the different duty stated in [Civil Code section 1714](https://codes.findlaw.com/ca/civil-code/civ-sect-1714/) rather than the statutory rule in [Civil Code section 3342](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?sectionNum=3342.&lawCode=CIV), which speaks only to the dog's owner. Identifying which carrier is answering for which person, in writing, prevents a single conversation from being treated as coverage for everyone involved.

### What the regulations require in the first weeks

California's Fair Claims Settlement Practices Regulations set deadlines. Under [title 10, section 2695.5](https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/california/10-CCR-2695.5) of the California Code of Regulations, an insurer receiving notice of claim must, immediately but in no event more than fifteen calendar days later, acknowledge receipt unless payment is made in that period, provide necessary forms, instructions and reasonable assistance including specifying what is required for proof of claim, and begin any necessary investigation. The same section requires a licensee receiving a communication from a claimant that reasonably suggests a response is expected to furnish a complete response within fifteen calendar days, and it provides that an insurer may not require written notice of claim unless the policy or an endorsement says so.

### The forty-day decision, and the thirty-day updates

[Section 2695.7](https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/california/10-CCR-2695.7) requires that, upon receiving proof of claim, an insurer accept or deny the claim in whole or in part immediately but in no event more than forty calendar days later, with the amounts accepted or denied documented in the file unless the claim is denied entirely. Where more time is needed, written notice of the need for additional time must go out within that same period, specifying the additional information required and any continuing reasons for the inability to decide, and that written notice must then be provided every thirty calendar days until a determination is made or notice of legal action is served. A denial or rejection of a third party claim, or a dispute about liability or damages, must be in writing.

### Payment timing after acceptance

The same section addresses what happens after a claim is accepted. Upon acceptance in whole or in part and, when necessary, upon receipt of a properly executed release, the insurer must tender payment or otherwise act to perform its claim obligation immediately but in no event more than thirty calendar days later, and the amount tendered is the amount the insurer accepted. Where multiple coverage is involved and the payee is known, accepted amounts are to be paid within the same thirty days if payment would terminate the insurer's known liability under that coverage, unless impairment of the insured's interests would result.

### The regulation on offers that are too low

Subdivision (g) of section 2695.7 states that no insurer shall attempt to settle a claim by making a settlement offer that is unreasonably low, and lists factors considered in that assessment: the extent to which the insurer considered evidence submitted by the claimant to support the value of the claim, legal authority or evidence made known to it or reasonably available, the advice of its claims adjuster as to the amount of damages, the advice of counsel about a substantial likelihood of recovery in excess of policy limits, the procedures used to determine property damage, and the probable liability of the insured together with the likely jury verdict or other final determination. The statutory backdrop is [Insurance Code section 790.03](https://codes.findlaw.com/ca/insurance-code/ins-sect-790-03/), which defines unfair methods of competition and deceptive acts in the business of insurance.

### Records the carrier did not create

Three dated agency records commonly exist alongside a residential claim, and they were written by people with no stake in it. The County bite report, which County Veterinary Public Health explains does not itself deem an animal dangerous and must be supplemented by a separate report to local animal control if a resident believes the animal is dangerous ([Reporting Animal Bites](http://publichealth.lacounty.gov/vet/biteintro.htm)). The quarantine record, described in the County rabies manual under County Code section 11.04.210 as at least fourteen days and ten for dogs and cats, with home confinement or a licensed boarding kennel permitted under section 11.04.220 ([quarantine provisions](http://publichealth.lacounty.gov/vet/rabiesmanual/quarantines.htm)). And any City dangerous-animal file generated under [Municipal Code section 53.34.4](https://codelibrary.amlegal.com/codes/los_angeles/latest/lamc/0-0-0-137100).

### Two ideas that do not follow from a policy letter

First, an insurer's evaluation is not the legal measure of loss. That measure is in [Civil Code section 3333](https://law.justia.com/codes/california/code-civ/division-4/part-1/title-2/chapter-2/article-2/section-3333/), covering all the detriment proximately caused whether or not it could have been anticipated. Second, where several parties are blamed, [Civil Code section 1431.2](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?sectionNum=1431.2.&lawCode=CIV) confines each defendant's share of the non-economic damages to that defendant's own allocated percentage of fault, so two carriers evaluating one incident are not evaluating identical exposure.

### Deadlines that regulation does not move

Regulatory timelines govern insurer conduct. They do not extend the time to file suit. [Code of Civil Procedure section 335.1](https://law.justia.com/codes/california/code-ccp/part-2/title-2/chapter-3/section-335-1/) sets two years for a personal injury action, and where a public agency is involved, the [claim-presentation rule](https://codes.findlaw.com/ca/government-code/gov-sect-911-2/) numbered 911.2 in the Government Code calls for a written claim inside six months of accrual.

### Scope

This page summarizes published regulations, statutes and agency guidance for general information. It is not legal advice and does not interpret any policy. Questions about specific correspondence belong with the attorney who advertises on this site.

## svc_medical_bills_and_liens_lede

A settlement figure is not the amount anyone takes home. California statutes cap what hospitals, health plans and public programs may take out of a recovery, and the caps differ by the kind of payer.

## svc_medical_bills_and_liens_body

### The hospital lien, and what it attaches to

[Civil Code section 3045.1](https://codes.findlaw.com/ca/civil-code/civ-sect-3045-1/) gives a hospital licensed under the laws of this state, which furnishes emergency and ongoing medical or other services to a person injured by reason of an accident or a negligent or other wrongful act not covered by the workers' compensation divisions of the Labor Code, a lien upon the damages recovered or to be recovered by that person, to the extent of the reasonable and necessary charges of the hospital and any affiliated health facility for treatment, care and maintenance resulting from that act. Two limits sit inside that sentence: the charges must be reasonable and necessary, and the lien attaches to the recovery rather than to the patient generally.

### The fifty percent ceiling on hospital liens

The [enforcement provision](https://codes.findlaw.com/ca/civil-code/civ-sect-3045-4/) numbered 3045.4 in the Civil Code addresses what happens when a payer, including an insurance carrier, pays the injured person or that person's attorney after receiving the notice described in section 3045.3 without paying the hospital. Liability runs to the amount of the lien claimed in the notice, or so much of it as can be satisfied out of 50 percent of the moneys due under any final judgment, compromise or settlement agreement after paying any prior liens. That percentage is the practical ceiling that keeps an emergency-department charge from consuming an entire recovery.

### Health plan liens are capped differently, and reduced twice

[Civil Code section 3040](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?sectionNum=3040.&lawCode=CIV) governs liens asserted by licensees of the Department of Managed Health Care or the Department of Insurance, and by medical groups and independent practice associations, for money paid on behalf of an enrollee or insured. Such a lien may not exceed the sum of the reasonable costs actually paid to perfect it plus, for services not provided on a capitated basis, the amount actually paid to treating providers, or for capitated services, an amount equal to 80 percent of the usual and customary charge for the same services in the geographic region. Subdivision (c) adds that where the enrollee engaged an attorney, the lien may not exceed the lesser of that maximum or one-third of the moneys due under any final judgment, compromise or settlement agreement, and subdivision (d) sets the comparable figure at one-half where no attorney was engaged. Subdivision (e) requires the lien to be reduced by the same comparative fault percentage by which the recovery was reduced where a judge, jury or arbitrator makes a special finding of partial fault, and subdivision (f) subjects it to pro rata reduction commensurate with reasonable attorney's fees and costs under the common fund doctrine.

### What section 3040 does not touch

Subdivision (g) excludes three categories from its limits: a lien made against a workers' compensation claim, a lien for Medi-Cal benefits under the Welfare and Institutions Code article beginning at section 14124.70, and a lien for hospital services under the chapter beginning at Civil Code section 3045.1. Those three are governed by their own provisions, which is why a single sentence about liens is usually wrong.

### The Medi-Cal limit

For Medi-Cal, [Welfare and Institutions Code section 14124.78](https://codes.findlaw.com/ca/welfare-and-institutions-code/wic-sect-14124-78/) provides that in no event shall the director recover more than the beneficiary recovers after deducting, from the settlement, judgment or award, attorney's fees and litigation costs paid for by the beneficiary, and that where recovery is determined under that section the reductions in subdivision (d) of section 14124.72 do not apply. The comparison is made after fees and costs, not before.

### Where a work injury changes the order

If the bite happened on the job, a compensation carrier's interest is handled inside the third-party recovery. [Labor Code section 3856](https://codes.findlaw.com/ca/labor-code/lab-sect-3856/) provides that where the employee prosecutes the action alone, the court first orders paid from the judgment the reasonable litigation expenses and a reasonable attorney's fee based solely on services rendered in effecting recovery for both employee and employer, and then, on the employer's application, allows as a first lien the employer's expenditure for compensation together with amounts to which the employer may be entitled as special damages under section 3852. [Section 3852](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?sectionNum=3852.&lawCode=LAB) preserves the injured worker's claim against the third party while allowing the employer its own claim.

### Why the treatment record still governs the number

Every one of these provisions measures a share of something. The something is the recovery, and the recovery is measured under [Civil Code section 3333](https://law.justia.com/codes/california/code-civ/division-4/part-1/title-2/chapter-2/article-2/section-3333/) as all the detriment proximately caused, whether or not it could have been anticipated. Where multiple parties share fault, [Civil Code section 1431.2](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?sectionNum=1431.2.&lawCode=CIV) makes non-economic damages several only in proportion to each defendant's fault, which affects the size of the pool from which every lien is later calculated.

### A minor's recovery is held, not handed over

Where the injured person is a child, [Probate Code section 3611](https://codes.findlaw.com/ca/probate-code/prob-sect-3611/) lists the ways the balance may be held after fees, costs and liens: delivery to a guardian or conservator of the estate, deposit in an insured account or a single-premium deferred annuity subject to withdrawal only on court authorization, a special needs trust under section 3604 after a hearing, other conditions where the balance does not exceed twenty thousand dollars, or payment to a parent without bond where it does not exceed five thousand dollars.

### Practical steps

Ask each provider in writing whether a lien has been asserted and under which statute. Keep the itemized statements rather than balance letters. Note the dates any lien notice was received.

When more than one dog is involved, the questions multiply rather than combine: how many animals a household may keep, who owned which, and how fault is divided among several people.

## svc_multi_dog_and_pack_attacks_body

### The City limit on animals per household

Los Angeles Animal Services publishes a summary of important animal laws stating that a household may keep up to three dogs and three cats, citing Municipal Code sections 53.00 and 53.50, and that dogs must be leashed under section 53.06. ### Each dog has an owner, and owners may differ

[Civil Code section 3342](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?sectionNum=3342.&lawCode=CIV) directs liability to the owner of a dog for damages suffered by a person bitten in a public place or while lawfully in a private place, regardless of the former viciousness of the animal or the owner's knowledge of it. In a multi-animal incident, that requires an inventory rather than a summary: which animals were present, which are licensed to which person, which unit each lives in, and who was in control of each at the time. Where a person handling a dog is not its owner, the claim against that handler runs on the ordinary-care duty in [Civil Code section 1714](https://codes.findlaw.com/ca/civil-code/civ-sect-1714/). Licensing records answer ownership better than a conversation at the scene, and in rabies areas [Health and Safety Code section 121690](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?sectionNum=121690.&lawCode=HSC) requires licensing after a dog reaches four months and vaccination after three months.

### How fault divides across several people

The division is not uniform across damage types. [Civil Code section 1431.2](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?sectionNum=1431.2.&lawCode=CIV) provides that in an action for personal injury based on principles of comparative fault, liability for non-economic damages is several only and not joint, with each defendant liable only for the amount allocated in direct proportion to that defendant's percentage of fault and a separate judgment rendered accordingly. The same section defines economic damages as objectively verifiable monetary losses including medical expenses, loss of earnings, costs of obtaining substitute domestic services and loss of employment opportunities, and non-economic damages as subjective losses including pain, suffering, inconvenience, mental suffering and emotional distress. Where two households each owned an animal, that allocation is the mechanism through which a single injury becomes two exposures of different sizes.

[Food and Agricultural Code section 31602](https://law.justia.com/codes/california/code-fac/division-14/chapter-9/article-1/section-31602/) defines a potentially dangerous dog to include one that, unprovoked, on two separate occasions within the prior 36-month period engaged in behavior requiring defensive action by a person off the owner's property, one that unprovoked bit a person causing a less severe injury than the severe-injury definition, and one that, unprovoked, on two separate occasions within that period killed, seriously bit, inflicted injury or otherwise caused injury attacking a domestic animal off the owner's property. [Section 31604](https://law.justia.com/codes/california/code-fac/division-14/chapter-9/article-1/section-31604/) supplies that severe-injury definition, and [section 31603](https://law.justia.com/codes/california/code-fac/division-14/chapter-9/article-1/section-31603/) defines the vicious category. Because the definitions run animal by animal, a proceeding may reach one dog in a household and not another.

### Conditions that follow a declaration, animal by animal

[Section 31641](https://california.public.law/codes/food_and_agricultural_code_section_31641) requires licensing and vaccination with the designation noted in the registration records and permits an additional fee. [Section 31642](https://law.justia.com/codes/california/code-fac/division-14/chapter-9/article-3/section-31642/) requires the dog to be kept indoors or in a securely fenced yard and, off the premises, on a substantial leash under the control of a responsible adult. [Section 31643](https://law.justia.com/codes/california/code-fac/division-14/chapter-9/article-3/section-31643/) requires written notice to animal control within two working days of the animal's death, sale or removal from the jurisdiction. [Section 31605](https://law.justia.com/codes/california/code-fac/division-14/chapter-9/article-1/section-31605/) addresses enclosure requirements.

### Restraint rules and the presumption

Inside the City, [Municipal Code section 53.06.2](https://codelibrary.amlegal.com/codes/los_angeles/latest/lamc/0-0-0-136443) requires a six-foot leash off the keeper's premises and [section 53.34](https://codelibrary.amlegal.com/codes/los_angeles/latest/lamc/0-0-0-137068) addresses animals at large, making the offense a misdemeanor where the animal bites. With several animals, the presumption is assessed against each keeper separately, which is another reason the inventory matters.

The bite report, which County Veterinary Public Health says should identify the current location of the biting animal as one of its most important elements ([Reporting Animal Bites](http://publichealth.lacounty.gov/vet/biteintro.htm)). Quarantine records for each animal confined, under the County provisions described in the rabies manual ([quarantine provisions](http://publichealth.lacounty.gov/vet/rabiesmanual/quarantines.htm)). Any dangerous-animal file under [Municipal Code section 53.34.4](https://codelibrary.amlegal.com/codes/los_angeles/latest/lamc/0-0-0-137100) or the County process in County Code section 10.37.110 ([County Code 10.37.110](http://lacounty-ca.elaws.us/code/coor_title10_div1_ch10.37_sec10.37.110)).

## svc_nerve_damage_and_infection_lede

The complications that matter most after a bite often appear after the wound looks closed. Two federal health agencies publish the timelines, and those timelines explain why the medical file and the quarantine file are read together.

## svc_nerve_damage_and_infection_body

### What the wound can carry

The Centers for Disease Control and Prevention describes Capnocytophaga bacteria as commonly found in the mouths of dogs and cats and as capable of causing a rare but potentially serious infection, usually after a bite or after saliva enters an open wound or sore. Its guidance identifies the people at greater risk, including those with a weakened immune system from conditions such as cancer, diabetes or HIV, those taking medicines such as chemotherapy that weaken immune response, those without a spleen, and those with alcohol use disorders. It lists possible complications including sepsis, heart attack, kidney failure and gangrene, and states that some people may need to have fingers, toes or limbs amputated. It also names other organisms associated with bites, including Pasteurella and Bartonella ([About Capnocytophaga](https://www.cdc.gov/capnocytophaga/about/index.html)).

### The fourteen-day instruction

The same guidance sets a specific window. If symptoms appear within fourteen days of being bitten by a dog or cat, the guidance directs contacting a healthcare provider immediately and stating that a bite occurred recently. The symptoms listed include blisters around the bite area, redness, swelling, draining pus or pain at the site, fever, diarrhea or stomach pain, vomiting, headache or confusion, and muscle or joint pain. That fourteen-day window is a documentation instruction as much as a medical one: symptoms reported on a dated visit and connected in the chart to the bite are recorded in a way that later reading can follow, while the same symptoms described from memory are not.

### Rabies protocol, and why the quarantine file matters

CDC guidance on rabies prevention states that wounds should be washed immediately with soap and water for fifteen minutes to flush out virus, and that post-exposure prophylaxis consists of wound washing, human rabies immune globulin and rabies vaccine, and is highly effective when given as soon as possible after exposure. It also states that more than four million Americans report being bitten by animals each year, that there are more than 100,000 potential rabies exposures annually with about 100,000 people vaccinated after a potential exposure, and that fewer than ten human deaths occur each year in the United States because of prevention efforts and access to that prophylaxis ([Rabies prevention](https://www.cdc.gov/rabies/prevention/index.html)). Locally, that risk is managed by confinement of the animal. The County rabies manual describes County Code section 11.04.210 as requiring quarantine of at least fourteen days, reduced to ten days for dogs and cats, with release possible after five days where a veterinarian examines the animal on the fifth day or later and certifies no clinical signs of rabies, and section 11.04.220 permitting home confinement or a licensed boarding kennel ([quarantine provisions](http://publichealth.lacounty.gov/vet/rabiesmanual/quarantines.htm)).

### Vaccination status is a licensing record, not a rumor

In rabies areas, [Health and Safety Code section 121690](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?sectionNum=121690.&lawCode=HSC) requires an owner to license a dog no less than once every two years after it reaches four months of age, and to have it vaccinated by a licensed veterinarian after it reaches three months, at intervals prescribed by the department, unless a veterinarian determines annually that vaccination would endanger the dog's life and an exemption is issued by the local health officer. The statute provides that an exempt dog is considered unvaccinated. Whether the specific animal was current is therefore answerable from records held by an agency, which is a better foundation than an owner's assurance at the scene.

### Reporting is a duty, and it reaches beyond the person bitten

County Veterinary Public Health states that all persons with knowledge of a bite are required to report, that all mammal bites are reportable other than those from small rodents and rabbits, and that the current location of the biting animal is among the most important parts of the report. It also lists jurisdictions that handle their own reports, including Pasadena, Long Beach and Vernon, and gives its own office location and number ([Reporting Animal Bites](http://publichealth.lacounty.gov/vet/biteintro.htm)).

### Nerve involvement is proved by follow-up, not by the first note

Numbness, weakness, altered sensation and grip loss are typically evaluated after the acute visit, by a different clinician, sometimes with testing. The record that supports such a claim is a sequence: the emergency note, the wound-check visits, the referral, the specialist's findings, and any therapy notes describing function over time. Under [Civil Code section 3333](https://law.justia.com/codes/california/code-civ/division-4/part-1/title-2/chapter-2/article-2/section-3333/), compensation covers all the detriment proximately caused, whether or not it could have been anticipated, and [Civil Code section 1431.2](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?sectionNum=1431.2.&lawCode=CIV) separates objectively verifiable monetary losses from subjective losses and makes liability for the latter several only.

### Practical file-keeping

Keep discharge instructions, prescription records including antibiotics, every wound-check visit, and any communication with a provider about worsening symptoms. Record the dates the bite report and any animal control report were made, and ask each agency in writing for its file number.

A dog off leash anywhere in Silver Lake Recreation Center other than the fenced area is outside the exception by the ordinance's own words. So the first useful record from a park incident is not a description of the dog. It is a location: the park, the named area, the side of the fence, the entrance, the trail, the time on the phone that took the photograph.

Beyond parks, [Municipal Code section 53.06.2](https://codelibrary.amlegal.com/codes/los_angeles/latest/lamc/0-0-0-136443) requires a dog off its keeper's premises to be under a competent person's control on a substantial chain or leash no longer than six feet, with an exception referencing the park exercise areas. Witnesses in a park are strangers; a phone number written down on the spot is often the only version of that record that will ever exist.

## svc_scarring_and_reconstructive_surgery_lede

A scar is valued on paper, and the paper is written by treating specialists over a period of months. That timing, not the intensity of the first week, is what usually decides whether a disfigurement claim is documented or merely described.

## svc_scarring_and_reconstructive_surgery_body

### The measure the law actually uses

[Civil Code section 3333](https://law.justia.com/codes/california/code-civ/division-4/part-1/title-2/chapter-2/article-2/section-3333/) provides that for a breach of an obligation not arising from contract, the measure of damages, except where otherwise expressly provided, is the amount which will compensate for all the detriment proximately caused, whether it could have been anticipated or not. Nothing in that sentence is limited to bills already paid. It is the statutory basis on which future surgical cost, permanent appearance change and the consequences that follow from it are discussed at all, and it is why a file closed at the end of wound care is frequently closed too early.

### Two damage categories, split two different ways

[Civil Code section 1431.2](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?sectionNum=1431.2.&lawCode=CIV) defines economic damages as objectively verifiable monetary losses including medical expenses, loss of earnings, costs of obtaining substitute domestic services, and loss of employment or business opportunities, and defines non-economic damages as subjective, non-monetary losses including pain, suffering, inconvenience, mental suffering, emotional distress, loss of society and companionship, injury to reputation and humiliation. The same section makes liability for non-economic damages several only, so each defendant answers in direct proportion to that defendant's own percentage of fault. Disfigurement claims live substantially in the second category, which is exactly the category that gets divided when more than one person is blamed.

### The statutory description of a serious wound

State law already has language for the kinds of injuries reconstructive work addresses. [Food and Agricultural Code section 31604](https://law.justia.com/codes/california/code-fac/division-14/chapter-9/article-1/section-31604/) defines severe injury as physical injury resulting in muscle tears or disfiguring lacerations, or requiring multiple sutures or corrective or cosmetic surgery. That definition exists for the dangerous-dog chapter rather than for compensation, but it is useful vocabulary: it shows that the distinction between a wound that closes and a wound that requires revision is one the Legislature has already drawn in writing.

### Why an early number is usually a partial number

Insurance regulation puts dates on an adjuster's file, and those dates arrive well before a surgical plan is settled. Under [title 10, section 2695.7](https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/california/10-CCR-2695.7) of the California Code of Regulations, an insurer must accept or deny a claim immediately but in no event more than forty calendar days after receiving proof of claim, must give written notice of the need for additional time within that period and then every thirty calendar days until a determination is made, and, upon acceptance and where necessary receipt of an executed release, must tender payment within thirty calendar days. Subdivision (g) states that no insurer shall attempt to settle a claim by making a settlement offer that is unreasonably low, and lists factors the Commissioner considers, among them the extent to which the insurer considered evidence submitted by the claimant to support the value of the claim and the advice of its own claims adjuster as to the amount of damages. A claimant who has not yet obtained the specialist's written plan has not yet submitted the evidence that provision contemplates.

### The record that carries a disfigurement discussion

Operative reports rather than discharge instructions. Clinic notes describing contracture, pigmentation change, sensation and function. A written plan naming the procedures under consideration, their sequence, and the interval between them. Cost estimates on facility letterhead. Photographs taken at fixed intervals from the same distance and angle, in similar light, beginning as early as possible and continuing after each stage. Where scarring affects work, documentation of the work itself: shift records, customer-facing duties, and any accommodation requested or refused.

### Children are on a different schedule again

Where the injured person is a minor, [Code of Civil Procedure section 352](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?sectionNum=352.&lawCode=CCP) excludes the period of minority from the limitations period of [section 335.1](https://law.justia.com/codes/california/code-ccp/part-2/title-2/chapter-3/section-335-1/), with subdivision (b) withholding that tolling from claims that must be presented to a public entity. Resolution also requires court involvement: [Code of Civil Procedure section 372](https://codes.findlaw.com/ca/code-of-civil-procedure/ccp-sect-372/) requires appearance by a guardian or guardian ad litem with power to compromise only with court approval, and [Probate Code section 3611](https://codes.findlaw.com/ca/probate-code/prob-sect-3611/) governs where the balance is deposited afterward, including blocked accounts subject to withdrawal only on court authorization.

### Local records that fix the incident in place

A scarring claim still rests on an identified animal and an identified location. County Veterinary Public Health records the bite, the animal and the quarantine, and states that the current location of the biting animal is one of the most important parts of a report ([Reporting Animal Bites](http://publichealth.lacounty.gov/vet/biteintro.htm)). Inside the City, [Municipal Code section 53.06.2](https://codelibrary.amlegal.com/codes/los_angeles/latest/lamc/0-0-0-136443) supplies the six-foot restraint rule that often frames how the encounter happened, and [section 53.34.4](https://codelibrary.amlegal.com/codes/los_angeles/latest/lamc/0-0-0-137100) supplies the dangerous-animal procedure that may generate a further file.

This page is a general explanation of California damages provisions, claim-handling regulations and Los Angeles records, not legal or medical advice about any injury. Specific questions belong with the attorney who advertises on this site.

## svc_service_animal_and_leash_violations_lede

Two sets of rules meet here. One protects a handler's right to be accompanied by a trained dog. The other requires every dog off its keeper's premises to be restrained. Neither cancels the other.

## svc_service_animal_and_leash_violations_body

### The access right, and the condition attached to it

[Civil Code section 54.2](https://codes.findlaw.com/ca/civil-code/civ-sect-54-2/) provides that every individual with a disability has the right to be accompanied by a guide dog, signal dog or service dog, especially trained for the purpose, in the places specified in section 54.1, without being required to pay an extra charge or security deposit, while also providing that the individual is liable for any damage done to the premises or facilities by the dog. Subdivision (b) extends comparable rights to authorized trainers and states that those persons shall ensure the dog is on a leash and tagged as a guide, signal or service dog by an identification tag issued by the county clerk, animal control department or other authorized agency. Subdivision (c) provides that a violation of a right under the Americans with Disabilities Act also violates the section.

### The criminal provision behind access disputes

[Penal Code section 365.5](https://codes.findlaw.com/ca/penal-code/pen-sect-365-5/) entitles a blind, deaf or disabled person who is a passenger on a common carrier or other public conveyance to have a specially trained guide, signal or service dog, and prohibits denial of admittance to accommodations, facilities, medical facilities including hospitals and clinics, private schools, hotels, lodging places and places of public accommodation because of that dog. Subdivision (c) makes interference with those rights a misdemeanor punishable by a fine not exceeding two thousand five hundred dollars, and subdivisions (d) through (f) define guide dog, signal dog and service dog, the last as any dog individually trained to do work or perform tasks for the benefit of an individual with a disability.

### Where park rules make the distinction explicit

[Municipal Code section 63.44](https://codelibrary.amlegal.com/codes/los_angeles/latest/lamc/0-0-0-159459) bars animals from City parks except in listed cases, and one of those cases is dogs which have been specially trained and are being used by blind or otherwise disabled persons to aid and guide them in their movements. A separate exception covers licensed dogs led by a leash not more than six feet long, and another covers licensed dogs under the control of a competent person inside designated dog exercise and training areas, which the ordinance identifies by location, including the fenced area at Silver Lake Recreation Center, portions of Runyon Canyon Park and the Sepulveda Basin Recreation Area, and a 1.4-acre fenced portion of Barrington Park. The ordinance draws those categories separately, which means a trained working dog and a pet under the off-leash exception are not being treated the same way.

### The general restraint rule

Outside those exceptions, [Municipal Code section 53.06.2](https://codelibrary.amlegal.com/codes/los_angeles/latest/lamc/0-0-0-136443) requires a dog off its keeper's premises to be under a competent person's control on a substantial chain or leash no longer than six feet. [Municipal Code section 53.34](https://codelibrary.amlegal.com/codes/los_angeles/latest/lamc/0-0-0-137068) addresses animals at large and makes the offense a misdemeanor where the animal bites, with an ownership prohibition of up to three years available after conviction. Where an ordinance is violated, [Evidence Code section 669](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?sectionNum=669.&lawCode=EVID) presumes failure to exercise due care where the violation proximately caused the injury, the injury resulted from the nature of occurrence the enactment was designed to prevent, and the injured person was in the protected class.

### The vaccination rule that includes its own leash term

[Health and Safety Code section 121690](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?sectionNum=121690.&lawCode=HSC) requires, in rabies areas, licensing after a dog attains four months of age and vaccination after three months, and allows an exemption where a licensed veterinarian determines annually that vaccination would endanger the dog's life, submitted on a departmental form with statements from the veterinarian and the owner and issued by the local health officer. The statute provides that an exempt dog is considered unvaccinated and, at the discretion of the local health officer or designee, is subject to stated conditions when off the owner's premises, including being on a leash not exceeding six feet under the direct physical control of an adult. Dogs under four months are addressed separately.

### When a working dog is the animal injured

An attack on a service animal raises the handler's own losses as well as the animal's care. The measure of compensation in [Civil Code section 3333](https://law.justia.com/codes/california/code-civ/division-4/part-1/title-2/chapter-2/article-2/section-3333/) is all the detriment proximately caused, whether or not it could have been anticipated. Where a person is bitten, [Civil Code section 3342](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?sectionNum=3342.&lawCode=CIV) directs liability to the owner of the offending dog regardless of former viciousness or knowledge, and [Food and Agricultural Code section 31602](https://law.justia.com/codes/california/code-fac/division-14/chapter-9/article-1/section-31602/) includes, in its potentially dangerous definition, a dog that unprovoked on two separate occasions within the prior 36-month period killed, seriously bit, inflicted injury or otherwise caused injury attacking a domestic animal off the owner's property.

### The reporting and confinement files

County Veterinary Public Health takes bite reports, states that all persons with knowledge of a bite must report, and notes that the current location of the biting animal is among the most important parts of the report ([Reporting Animal Bites](http://publichealth.lacounty.gov/vet/biteintro.htm)). The County rabies manual describes the confinement fee under County Code section 11.04.225 as not assessed for police service dogs or guide dogs ([quarantine provisions](http://publichealth.lacounty.gov/vet/rabiesmanual/quarantines.htm)). A dangerous-animal report is separate and follows [Municipal Code section 53.34.4](https://codelibrary.amlegal.com/codes/los_angeles/latest/lamc/0-0-0-137100) inside the City.

### Scope

General information about published statutes, ordinances and agency material. It is not legal advice about any access dispute or attack. Questions belong with the attorney who advertises on this site.

## svc_statute_of_limitations_california_lede

Two years is the number most people have heard. In Los Angeles it is often not the operative one, because a public agency in the facts can shorten the useful window to six months.

## svc_statute_of_limitations_california_body

### The ordinary period

[Code of Civil Procedure section 335.1](https://law.justia.com/codes/california/code-ccp/part-2/title-2/chapter-3/section-335-1/) provides two years for an action for assault, battery, or injury to or for the death of an individual caused by the wrongful act or neglect of another. A dog bite claim brought under [Civil Code section 3342](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?sectionNum=3342.&lawCode=CIV) or on the ordinary-care duty in [Civil Code section 1714](https://codes.findlaw.com/ca/civil-code/civ-sect-1714/) sits inside that period. Nothing in negotiation, correspondence or an insurer's own regulatory timeline changes it.

### The public-entity track, and why it is measured in months

Where a public agency, a public employee or public property appears anywhere in the account, a separate sequence applies first. the [presentation statute](https://codes.findlaw.com/ca/government-code/gov-sect-911-2/) numbered 911.2 in the Government Code requires a claim relating to a cause of action for death or injury to person to be presented not later than six months after accrual, with claims relating to other causes of action allowed one year, and it addresses the effect of a filing fee and fee waiver on the presentation date for claims to the Department of General Services. [Section 912.4](https://codes.findlaw.com/ca/government-code/gov-sect-912-4/) requires the board to act within forty-five days after presentation, allows the claimant and board to extend that period by written agreement, and provides that failure to act within the time prescribed means the claim is deemed rejected on the last day of the period.

### What the rejection notice starts

[Government Code section 913](https://codes.findlaw.com/ca/government-code/gov-sect-913/) requires written notice of the action taken or of inaction deemed rejection, and prescribes a warning where a claim is rejected in whole or in part, stating that subject to certain exceptions the claimant has only six months from the date the notice was personally delivered or deposited in the mail to file a court action, with a reference to section 945.6. The [companion provision](https://codes.findlaw.com/ca/government-code/gov-sect-945-6/) numbered 945.6 then provides that suit must be commenced not later than six months after that notice is personally delivered or deposited in the mail, and where written notice is not given in accordance with section 913, within two years of accrual, with an extension for a person unable to sue because of imprisonment in state prison.

### The minority rule, and the hole in it

[Code of Civil Procedure section 352](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?sectionNum=352.&lawCode=CCP) provides that where a person entitled to bring an action is under the age of majority at the time the cause of action accrued, the time of that disability is not part of the limitations period. Subdivision (b) removes that tolling for an action against a public entity or public employee upon a cause of action for which a claim is required to be presented. A family that assumes a child's claim can wait may find that the private part of the claim is intact and the public-entity part is gone.

### Death claims run on their own provisions

Where a bite results in death, [Code of Civil Procedure section 377.60](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?sectionNum=377.60.&lawCode=CCP) identifies who may bring the action, and [section 377.61](https://california.public.law/codes/ca_civ_proc_code_section_377.61) provides that damages may be awarded that under all the circumstances may be just but may not include damages recoverable under section 377.34. [Section 377.34](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?sectionNum=377.34.&lawCode=CCP) limits a survival action to loss or damage the decedent sustained before death and excludes damages for pain, suffering or disfigurement, with subdivision (b) allowing those damages only where the action was granted a preference under section 36 before January 1, 2022, or was filed on or after January 1, 2022, and before January 1, 2026. Filing dates therefore carry consequences beyond timeliness.

### Related actions with their own procedures

[Civil Code section 3342.5](https://california.public.law/codes/ca_civ_code_section_3342.5) allows an action, where a dog has bitten a human being on at least two separate occasions, brought by any person, the district attorney or a city attorney in the county where a bite occurred, to determine whether conditions of treatment or confinement have changed enough to remove the danger. The dangerous-dog process moves faster still: under [Food and Agricultural Code section 31621](https://law.justia.com/codes/california/code-fac/division-14/chapter-9/article-2/section-31621/), the hearing on a petition is held within no less than five working days nor more than ten working days after service of notice, and County Code section 10.37.110 sets the same five-to-ten working day window for County administrative hearings ([County Code 10.37.110](http://lacounty-ca.elaws.us/code/coor_title10_div1_ch10.37_sec10.37.110)). Those proceedings are not compensation claims, but they are where much of the useful record is created, and they can conclude long before a civil claim is prepared.

### Why local records have their own informal clock

Evidence in this city expires for practical reasons rather than statutory ones. Building camera footage is often overwritten. Witnesses in apartment buildings and parks move. Quarantine ends in ten days for dogs and cats under the County provisions described in the rabies manual, and the animal's location afterward may change ([quarantine provisions](http://publichealth.lacounty.gov/vet/rabiesmanual/quarantines.htm)). County Veterinary Public Health notes the importance of recording the current location of the biting animal in a report ([Reporting Animal Bites](http://publichealth.lacounty.gov/vet/biteintro.htm)).

### Scope

This page summarizes published limitations statutes and claim-presentation provisions for general information. It is not legal advice, and no date on this page should be applied to a particular set of facts without professional review. Questions belong with the attorney who advertises on this site.

## svc_strict_liability_in_california_lede

California does not ask whether a dog had a reputation before it bit someone. The statute asks two narrower questions, and most Los Angeles disputes are really arguments about the second one.

## svc_strict_liability_in_california_body

### The two conditions the statute actually sets

[Civil Code section 3342](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?sectionNum=3342.&lawCode=CIV) makes the owner of a dog liable for damages suffered by a person bitten in a public place or while lawfully in a private place, including the dog owner's own property, and it says that liability attaches regardless of the former viciousness of the animal or the owner's knowledge of it. Everything else follows from those two conditions. There must be an owner, and the bitten person must have been somewhere the law entitled that person to be. A file that spends pages on whether the dog had ever growled at a neighbor is usually a file that has lost track of what the statute measures.

### Owner is a narrower word than it sounds in a rental city

The statute speaks to the owner, not to whoever happened to be holding the leash. In a city where households share courtyards, stairwells and gates, the person controlling the dog at the moment of a bite is frequently a roommate, a partner, a dog walker or a relative watching the animal for the weekend. A claim against that person does not run on section 3342 at all. It runs on the general duty in [Civil Code section 1714](https://codes.findlaw.com/ca/civil-code/civ-sect-1714/), which makes everyone responsible for injury caused to another by want of ordinary care in managing property or person. One incident can therefore generate a statutory claim against an owner and a separate negligence claim against a handler, resting on different proof and sometimes reaching different insurance policies. Establishing ownership early, in writing, from a licensing record or an agency file rather than from a conversation, is what keeps the two theories from being confused.

### Lawful presence, and the delivery exception written into the text

Section 3342 does not leave lawful presence to argument. It states that a person is lawfully on private property of the owner when performing a duty imposed by the laws of this state, by the laws or postal regulations of the United States, or when on the property by invitation express or implied. That single sentence is why mail carriers, meter readers and process servers are treated as lawfully present without further discussion, and why an implied invitation to a front walkway matters in a duplex or a fourplex. The same sentence marks the boundary: a trespasser is outside the statute, which is where a negligence analysis and the facts about fences, gates and signs return to the center of the discussion.

### Subjects the statute never mentions

Breed appears nowhere in the section. Neither does a warning sign, a prior complaint, or the length of time an owner had kept the animal. The statute does carve out working animals: subdivisions (b) through (d) exclude bites by dogs assisting peace officers or military personnel in specified duties, and they condition that exclusion on the agency having adopted a written policy on the necessary and appropriate use of the dog. The carve-out is narrow and agency-specific, and its written-policy requirement is itself a records question rather than an assumption.

### The companion section that survives the first bite

[Civil Code section 3342.5](https://california.public.law/codes/ca_civ_code_section_3342.5) sits directly beside the liability rule and does something different. Subdivision (a) places on the owner of a dog that has bitten a human being the duty to take reasonable steps to remove any danger presented to others from bites by that animal. Subdivision (b) provides that when a dog has bitten a human being on at least two separate occasions, any person, the district attorney or a city attorney may bring an action against the owner, in the county where a bite occurred, to determine whether conditions of treatment or confinement have changed enough to remove the danger, and the court after hearing may make any order it considers appropriate, including removal of the animal from the area or its destruction. Subdivision (d) withholds that action for bites on a trespasser and for military or police dogs actually performing in that capacity.

### The ordinance route running alongside the statute

A restraint violation is a separate lever. [Evidence Code section 669](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?sectionNum=669.&lawCode=EVID) creates a presumption of failure to exercise due care when a person violates a statute or ordinance, the violation proximately caused the injury, the injury resulted from the kind of occurrence the enactment was designed to prevent, and the injured person was within the class the enactment protected. Inside the City, [Municipal Code section 53.06.2](https://codelibrary.amlegal.com/codes/los_angeles/latest/lamc/0-0-0-136443) supplies the enactment by requiring a dog off its keeper's premises to be controlled by a competent person on a substantial chain or leash no longer than six feet.

### Two kinds of damages, divided two different ways

Where more than one person is at fault, the split is not uniform. Under [Civil Code section 1431.2](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?sectionNum=1431.2.&lawCode=CIV), liability for non-economic damages is several only and each defendant answers in direct proportion to that defendant's percentage of fault, while the same section defines economic damages as objectively verifiable monetary losses such as medical expenses and lost earnings. The measure itself comes from [Civil Code section 3333](https://law.justia.com/codes/california/code-civ/division-4/part-1/title-2/chapter-2/article-2/section-3333/), which allows compensation for all the detriment proximately caused, whether or not it could have been anticipated.
