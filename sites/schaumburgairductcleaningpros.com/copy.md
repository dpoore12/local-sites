Duct cleaning prices in the northwest suburbs follow a fairly simple structure once it is laid out. Most companies here price per furnace, not per vent: one figure for the furnace and every duct run it feeds. A few still price by the opening. On top of that sit separate jobs that often ride along on the same visit, like a dryer vent or a deeper furnace cleaning. And there is what the inspection finds, which can range from ordinary dust to a mouse nest in a basement trunk line.

### One furnace, every run

Chicagoland companies that name Schaumburg in their service areas post a single-furnace cleaning at a few hundred dollars, and other metro operators run higher, up to around a thousand for a large or complicated system. The difference is mostly scope. Some entry packages cover the supply vents only, without the returns or the main trunk lines, and without negative air pressure. A full cleaning puts the whole system under negative pressure, works every supply and return run back to the trunk, and cleans the furnace cabinet and blower. When comparing quotes, line them up by what they include, not by the headline figure.

Larger houses and some additions run two systems. Each one is its own duct network. Posted prices around Chicagoland reflect that: one list goes from $550 for one furnace to $950 for two, and others simply quote each furnace as its own system. Where a company prices per opening, the count matters. The furnace is shut down and its cabinet opened. A negative-air machine, a large filtered vacuum, is connected to the main trunk so the entire system is pulled toward it while the work goes on. ## svc_furnace_blower_cleaning_lede

Furnace and blower cleaning in Schaumburg: the blower wheel, the cabinet and the burner area cleaned before the heating season, so the first cold night does not smell scorched and the furnace moves the air it was built to move.

## svc_furnace_blower_cleaning_body

In a heating-heavy climate the dirtiest part of the system is rarely the duct runs. It is the furnace blower. O'Hare's 1991-2020 normals put the year at 5,974 heating degree days against 1,003 cooling degree days, so the blower in a Schaumburg furnace spends long stretches of every winter pulling air across the filter and pushing it into the ducts. Whatever gets past the filter lands on the blower wheel's curved blades and on the inside of the cabinet. After a few seasons the wheel can carry a thick felt of dust that cuts airflow, makes the motor work harder and gives off a scorched smell when the burners first light.

### The smell on the first cold night

A brief burning-dust smell the first time the heat runs in fall is normal. Dust settles on the heat exchanger and burners over summer and cooks off within the first hour or so. A smell that returns every time the furnace starts, lasts, or smells like hot plastic or wiring is not normal. Shut the furnace off at its switch and have it looked at. A dirty blower and cabinet are the common cause of a repeating dust smell; anything electrical is a repair for a heating technician.

### What a furnace cleaning covers

The power and gas are shut off and the furnace panels come off. The blower assembly is removed or cleaned in place, the wheel's blades scraped and vacuumed blade by blade, and the motor wiped down. The cabinet, the area around the burners and the return plenum are vacuumed. The flame sensor, a small rod that tells the control board the burner has lit, is worth cleaning on the same visit when the company also services furnaces, because a coated sensor makes a furnace shut off a few seconds after lighting. If there is a cased cooling coil above the furnace, its upstream face is checked and cleaned. The filter slot gets a fresh filter of the right size, and the furnace is run through a full cycle to confirm it lights and the blower runs smoothly.

### Why the wheel matters for the ducts

A clogged blower moves less air, so rooms at the end of long runs get less heat and the furnace runs longer to satisfy the thermostat. It also throws dust off its blades into the supply ducts every time it starts. Cleaning ducts without cleaning the blower means the clean ducts start collecting that dust again the next morning. That is why a duct cleaning should always include the blower, and why a furnace cleaning on its own is sometimes all a system needs.

### High-efficiency furnaces and snow

Many furnaces put in during the last few decades are high-efficiency models that vent through plastic pipes out a side wall or the roof. In a snowy Schaumburg winter, with a normal year bringing 38.4 inches, a drift over a low side-wall intake or exhaust can shut the furnace down. Keeping those pipes clear is a homeowner job, but the furnace cleaning visit is a good time to point out where they are and how close they sit to grade.

### The humidifier and the drain

A whole-house humidifier on the furnace and the condensate drain on a high-efficiency unit both carry water, and both can clog. Changing the humidifier pad, checking that its drain runs, and making sure the condensate trap and line are clear all belong on the same visit if the scope includes them.

### When to schedule it

Late summer or early fall, before the furnace is needed every day, is the best window. Spring, after the heat goes off, is the second-best. Mid-January is the worst, when a furnace taken apart for an hour is a cold house.

## svc_dryer_vent_cleaning_lede

## svc_dryer_vent_cleaning_body

A dryer vent has nothing to do with the furnace or the ducts, but it is usually cleaned by the same companies and often on the same visit. It is a separate job for a separate reason. Lint that builds up in the run between the dryer and the outside hood slows drying, wastes energy and sits right behind a hot appliance.

### The signs

Clothes that need a second cycle. A laundry room that turns hot and humid while the dryer runs. A dryer that stops before the timer ends because it is overheating. A hot-lint smell on the clothes or in the room. Lint piling up around the outside hood, or a hood flap that stays shut when the dryer is running. Any one of these means air is not getting out.

### Schaumburg runs that clog

A short run straight out the wall from a basement or first-floor laundry stays clear the longest. Longer runs are the problem: a laundry room moved upstairs in a remodel, a townhouse where the vent goes up through the building to the roof, or a run that crosses a basement ceiling with several elbows before it reaches the outside wall. Every turn and every extra foot slows the air and gives lint a place to settle.

### Winter and spring at the hood

Schaumburg's winters add a problem warmer places do not have. A dryer hood near the ground can be buried by a drift, and a flap that is wet from exhaust can freeze shut on a cold night. Both choke the run. In spring, birds nest in hoods that do not close properly. A hood that sits well above the snow line, with a flap that opens freely and no screen across the opening, avoids most of it. Screens on a dryer hood catch lint and should come off.

### What the work involves

The dryer is pulled out and unplugged, or its gas supply is shut off. The transition hose behind it is checked; crushed foil and vinyl hoses get replaced with semi-rigid metal, which keeps its shape and holds up to heat. A spinning brush on a flexible rod works through the vent, from the hood end, the dryer end or both, while a vacuum pulls out what it loosens. The outside hood is cleared, the flap is checked, and anything nesting there is removed. Then the dryer goes back, runs, and the airflow at the hood is checked to confirm the whole run is open.

### When cleaning is not enough

Some runs are simply too long or too twisted to work well, or they were built from the wrong material. A section crushed inside a wall, or a run that has come apart in a basement ceiling or attic and is dumping lint and moist air into the house, has to be repaired or rerouted. That is priced separately after it is found, with a photo of the problem.

### How often

Clean the dryer's lint screen after every load. A busy household with a long run should have the vent checked about once a year; a short side-wall run in a small household can go longer. If drying times start creeping up, the vent is the first thing to check.

### How construction dust gets into the system

Cutting, sanding and demolition throw fine dust into the air, and the return grilles on that floor pull it in every time the furnace or air conditioning runs. The best time to protect the ducts is before the sanding starts. After the dust-making work is completely done, including the final sanding and the trim. Cleaning halfway through a project means doing it twice. ### Where the "waste of money" reputation comes from

The skepticism is earned. ### The four ways the money gets thrown away

## q_how_does_duct_cleaning_work_lede

A proper duct cleaning puts the whole heating and cooling system under suction, knocks the dirt loose with brushes or air tools, and pulls it out of the house, starting at the furnace and working outward through every supply and return run.

## q_how_does_duct_cleaning_work_body

### The principle: source removal

NADCA, the duct cleaning trade association, calls the method source removal. Two things happen at once. A powerful vacuum holds the system under continuous negative pressure so that loosened dust is drawn toward the collection hose instead of drifting into the rooms, and agitation tools break the dust free from the inside surfaces of the ducts. NADCA lists brushes, air whips, compressed air nozzles and so-called skipper balls as agitation devices, along with hand brushing and contact vacuuming. Without both halves the job does not work: suction alone leaves stuck dust behind, and agitation without suction blows it into the house.

### Step one: look before anything is opened

A sound job starts with an inspection of the furnace, the coil, the main trunks and a sample of branch runs, ideally with a camera so the homeowner can see what is there. The EPA also says the service provider should check for asbestos in the system before cleaning, which matters in older houses. This is also the moment to agree on scope and price. In Illinois, the Home Repair and Remodeling Act requires a written contract or work order with the total cost before home repair work over $1,000 begins.

### Step two: connect the vacuum and protect the house

The vacuum may be truck-mounted or portable; NADCA says either can be used to contain the dust. The EPA says vacuums that exhaust indoors should use HEPA filtration, and that carpets and furnishings should be protected. The vacuum is usually connected to the main supply or return trunk near the furnace, and registers are covered so the suction concentrates on the ducts being cleaned.

### Step three: open access points

Technicians use existing openings such as registers, grilles and service panels wherever they can. Sometimes they need to cut access holes in the ductwork to reach inside. NADCA notes that making and closing those openings takes real skill, and the EPA says any holes cut should be sealed and insulated again afterward. Ask where holes will go and how they will be closed.

### Step four: agitate run by run

With suction pulling, each supply branch and return is worked with brushes or whips until the inside surfaces are clean. Flexible duct and fiberglass-lined duct need gentler tools than bare metal; the EPA recommends soft-bristled brushes for fiberglass to avoid tearing it. Returns matter as much as supplies, because they carry the household's dust back to the furnace.

### Step five: clean the equipment, not just the ducts

NADCA recommends cleaning the entire system: ducts, registers and grilles, the plenum, the coil, the drain pan, the heat exchanger, the air cleaner and the blower motor and assembly. In a Schaumburg house that last item deserves particular attention. O'Hare averages close to six thousand heating degree days a year, so the furnace blower moves air through the house for most of the cold season, and its wheel collects dust faster than most duct runs. Pulling and cleaning that wheel is a job of its own, covered in [furnace blower cleaning](../furnace-blower-cleaning/).

### Step six: chemicals only after cleaning, if at all

NADCA says antimicrobial products should be considered only after mechanical cleaning and only if registered with the EPA. The EPA adds that no biocides are registered for fiberglass duct board or fiberglass-lined ducts. A cleaning does not need a spray to be complete.

### Step seven: close up and check

Access holes get patched and sealed, registers go back, a fresh filter goes in, and the system is run to confirm it heats and cools normally. The EPA's checklist says every cleaned surface should be free of debris you can see, the coil should be clean, and the drain pan should drain. Before-and-after photos from inside the same runs are the easiest proof.

## q_can_dirty_air_ducts_make_you_sick_body

### What the evidence actually shows

The EPA says duct cleaning has never been shown to prevent health problems, and that studies have not conclusively shown that dust levels in a home rise because the ducts are dirty or fall after they are cleaned. Much duct dirt simply sticks to the inside surfaces. The EPA also points out that a light layer of household dust in ducts is normal, and that everyday sources such as cooking, cleaning, smoking and outdoor pollution can account for more of what a family breathes than a dusty supply run does.

### When the system can be part of the problem

**Mold growing inside the system.** The EPA's mold guidance says breathing or touching mold and its spores can trigger allergic reactions in sensitive people and asthma attacks in people with asthma who are allergic to mold, and that it can irritate the eyes, nose, throat and lungs even in people without allergies. A system with actual growth on the coil, in the drain pan or on hard duct surfaces can spread that through every room. The EPA's advice is not to run the system at all if you know or suspect it is contaminated, until it has been dealt with.

**Rodents and insects.** Mice nesting in a basement trunk or return cavity leave droppings in the airstream, which is a removal job.

**Construction dust.** Sanding drywall or cutting tile with the furnace running sends fine dust through the returns and into every branch. For a household with asthma, that dust is worth getting out, which is what [post-renovation duct cleaning](../post-renovation-duct-cleaning/) is for.

### The furnace matters more than the ducts

In this climate the appliance at the center of the system deserves more attention than the sheet metal. National Weather Service normals for O'Hare show close to 6,000 heating degree days a year, roughly six times the cooling load, so a gas furnace in a Schaumburg basement may run for most of seven months. The Illinois State Fire Marshal describes carbon monoxide as an odorless, colorless gas produced when fuels such as natural gas burn incompletely, and names heating equipment as a potential source. Unlike dust, CO can make people seriously ill.

Illinois has required carbon monoxide alarms since 1 January 2007. Under the Carbon Monoxide Alarm Detector Act, a home that burns fossil fuel for heat or hot water, or has an attached garage, needs a working, approved alarm within 15 feet of every room used for sleeping. If an alarm sounds, get everyone outside and call for help before thinking about the ducts.

The burning-dust smell on the first cold night is different: summer dust on the heat exchanger and blower, usually gone after a few cycles. A smell that lingers for days, or one that comes with headaches, is a reason to have the furnace checked. From October to April most Schaumburg homes stay sealed, which makes the filter the key part of the system for anyone with allergies.
