## pricing_body

### The tile is not the roof

Tile sheds most of the water, but the layer keeping a Tampa house dry is the underlayment on the deck beneath it. Tile lasts decades; underlayment does not, and Gulf coast heat and ultraviolet exposure age it long before the tile above shows anything. That explains the shape of the table. Replacing a few cracked pieces is some tile and an hour of careful walking. Replacing the membrane under them means inventorying, lifting and stacking every tile in the work area, dry-in, then setting it all back, and the number moves by a factor of ten. The diagnosis drives the price and it is not visible from the driveway.

### What the 25 percent rule says now

Florida Building Code Existing Building section 706.1.1 holds that not more than 25 percent of a total roof area or roof section may be repaired, replaced or recovered in any 12-month period unless the covering on the entire existing roofing system or section is brought to current code.

The carve-out sits in statute rather than local ordinance. Florida Statutes section 553.844 provides an exception for a system or section built, repaired or replaced in compliance with the 2007 Florida Building Code or any later edition. On such a roof, when 25 percent or more is repaired, replaced or recovered, only that portion must meet current code. A local government may not amend the exception. So the age of the last permitted roof work becomes a pricing input. A roof permitted under the 2007 code or later can absorb a large repair without a whole-system upgrade. Repairs should be measured as they happen, because two visits to one section within a year still add up.

### City fees and the 500 square foot line

Tampa exempts roof covering work of 500 square feet or less from a building permit and requires one above that area. Once a permit is in play, the City of Tampa trade permit fee schedule lists residential roofing at $177.00 and commercial roofing at $220.00. Small against a five-figure project, noticeable on a small repair, which is why measured area matters. Tile also carries its own sequence: a dry-in inspection before tile is set, then a final, and tile cannot go back down until the new underlayment has been looked at. City guidance further directs homes permitted before March 1, 2002 to hurricane mitigation retrofit requirements, adding secondary water barrier and fastening work no national estimate carries.

### Wind, rain, and the storm scope

Bay area roofs live under uplift and windborne debris requirements for a reason. When a named storm passes, tile rarely fails in one spot; it lets go along the rake, hip and ridge where uplift concentrates, and displaced tile leaves underlayment exposed to weeks of ordinary afternoon rain. A sound scope covers the surrounding field, the flashings and the roof edge rather than the piece spotted from the yard. It should separate the tarp from the permanent repair, say whether an emergency dry-in is credited against the finished work, and measure the area against the 25 percent threshold.

### Reading a tile estimate

A trustworthy estimate states tile type, profile and color, and whether replacement pieces are new, salvaged or reclaimed from elsewhere on the roof. It names the attachment method, mortar set, foam adhesive or mechanical fastening. It names the underlayment product and the number of layers, and gives the measured repair area in square feet, since that decides permitting and the 25 percent question. It lists flashing, valley metal, pipe boots and ridge treatment as included or excluded, and prices a breakage allowance instead of pretending nothing snaps when tile is lifted. National cost pages average shingle work into tile work: they omit the permit, the dry-in hold, retrofit requirements on older homes, and mortar-set ridge that must be rebuilt.

### When patching stops being the honest answer

Two very different situations arrive under the same words. One is a single split piece over a dry deck, which is an afternoon of careful work. The other is a slope that has taken three or four visits since the last storm season, where every visit found the same brittle felt under a different set of tiles. The second is not a larger version of the first. It is a decision about whether the water-control layer still has service life left, and in this state that decision drags code and insurance consequences along with it.

### The 25 percent line, and what actually changed in 2022

Florida Building Code Existing Building section 706.1.1 still reads that not more than 25 percent of the total roof area or roof section may be repaired, replaced or recovered in any 12-month period unless the covering on the entire roof system or section is brought up to current code. What changed is the exception printed underneath it. Senate Bill 4-D, effective when it became law in May 2022, wrote that exception into section 553.844. Where the existing roofing system or roof section was built, repaired or replaced in compliance with the 2007 Florida Building Code or any later edition, crossing 25 percent obliges only the repaired portion to meet current code. A local government may not amend that exception by ordinance. So the date on the last permitted roof job is a genuine fork in the road. A concrete S-tile slope on a subdivision house permitted after 2007 can absorb a large section repair. A clay barrel roof whose last permit is far older cannot, and once a year's repairs cross the line, the conversation changes shape.

### Tampa's own line sits at 500 square feet

City roofing guidance exempts roof covering installation, recovering, reroofing or repair of 500 square feet or less from a building permit and requires one above that area. On tile the City also requires a dry-in inspection before tile is installed, then a final. That inspection is a scheduling fact rather than paperwork: once old felt is off, the deck stays open until an inspector has looked at the new dry-in, so any repair reaching the underlayment needs a dry weather window and not merely an available crew. Guidance also directs single-family homes permitted before March 1, 2002 to the hurricane mitigation retrofit requirements, which is where secondary water barrier and deck fastening enter a job that started as a leak.

### Which Tampa tile roof you have

Tile arrived here in two eras and they fail differently. Clay barrel and Mediterranean Revival work belongs to the 1920s: the Hyde Park historic district carries a period of significance of 1886 to 1933, West Tampa's runs 1893 to 1933, and D. P. Davis began assembling his dredge-and-fill islands in 1924. Those roofs have mortar-set hip and ridge, layers of old repairs, and profiles nobody presses anymore. Concrete tile belongs to the stucco subdivisions; the City's housing assessment counts 48,131 structures built in 2000 or later against 23,236 from before 1950. On those newer roofs the original felt tends to run out across a whole slope at once, which is why the third leak in a year is rarely a coincidence.

### What your insurance file already says about the roof

Florida statute 627.7011 says an insurer may not refuse to issue or renew a policy on a home with a roof less than 15 years old solely because of roof age. At 15 years or more, the homeowner may have an authorized inspector look at it, and if the inspection shows five or more years of useful life remaining, roof age alone is not grounds for refusal. The same subsection calculates roof age from the last date 100 percent of the surface area was built or replaced, so a run of partial repairs does not restart the clock. Citizens requires documentation of full replacement for tile, clay, concrete, slate or metal roofs more than 50 years old, with remaining-useful-life documentation from a roof inspection form or the four-point inspection form as the alternative, capped at five years. Whatever gets written on that form outlasts the repair.

### What the visit looks like

Measurement comes first: where the roof section boundaries fall, the square footage of the damaged area, and where earlier patches sit. Then tile is lifted in a few chosen spots rather than everywhere, at the leak, at the highest suspect course, and at a sound control spot, so the felt can be compared against itself. Fasteners, battens and deck get read while the tile is up. The scope that comes back should name the failed layer, the measured area, whether it stays under the permit threshold, where the section stands against the 25 percent window, and what happens to tile that snaps on the way off.

### How the cost is shaped

Two variables do most of the work. The first is how much tile has to come off to reach the failure, which is why a valley or a wall transition runs above an open field of the same size. The second is whether the answer is tile work or felt work, because relaying a section over fresh dry-in sits an order of magnitude away from swapping pieces. A breakage allowance belongs in the scope. Ask for the measured area in writing, and ask what the estimate assumes about the last permitted roof date, since that assumption is quietly setting the price.

## svc_broken_roof_tile_repair_lede

Broken tile is the repair people postpone, because the ceiling stays clean and the roof still reads as orderly from the street. What sits under the split is asphalt felt, and in this climate that felt is already working harder than the tile above it.

## svc_broken_roof_tile_repair_body

### How you know that is what happened

From the ground, look for a shadow line running across the pan of a tile, a missing corner at the head lap, a piece sitting lower than its neighbors, and shards in the gutter or the shrub bed below the eave. Cracks in Tampa arrive three ways. Impact breaks are clean and often obvious: a limb down, a dropped tool, an anchor set through a tile during a solar or satellite install. Fatigue breaks start at the nail hole and travel, because a tile fastened tight to a warped deck flexes every time the sun swings across it. Slip breaks happen when a piece lets go, slides, and shatters its nose against the course below, which leaves two damaged tiles instead of one.

### Foot traffic is the usual culprit here

Most broken tile in this city was broken by a person, not by weather. Every roof-mounted install, gutter cleaning, pressure wash, satellite alignment and holiday light run puts weight on a surface that only takes weight in specific places, near the head lap where the tile is supported, never mid-pan. Concrete tile bears less than people assume and clay barrel from the 1920s bears far less than that. Notice that broken tile clusters along the paths people walk: from the ladder point to the chimney, from the roof hatch to the air handler stand.

### What is under the split

Tile is the sunshade and the first defense; the felt beneath it is the actual roof. Concrete tile is built to last the life of a house, and the manufacturer side of the trade cites a National Association of Home Builders study putting tile among the few roofing materials that last a lifetime. Underlayment does not come close. Florida roofing guidance commonly puts it at 20 to 25 years here, which is why most tile roofs in this state come off because of the layer nobody sees. A break lets ultraviolet light and driven rain onto that layer directly, and Tampa takes most of its rain between June and September, so an August split gets tested weekly.

### Fasteners and the bay

The code specification is narrow. Florida Building Code section 1507.3.6 requires tile fasteners to be corrosion resistant, not less than 11-gage with a 5/16-inch head, and long enough to penetrate the deck at least three quarters of an inch or all the way through it. It also defines the perimeter fastening areas as three tile courses, and not less than 36 inches from either side of hips or ridges and from the edges of eaves and gable rakes, which is precisely where wind pulls hardest. Salt air complicates all of it. A Florida Building Commission survey of roofing professionals found 79.5 percent had seen corrosion on the exposed portion of metal fasteners, and within roughly ten miles of a coast about 58 percent said corrosion was usually or always present against 17 percent inland. On Davis Islands and along the bayfront streets, a fastener that would age slowly twenty miles inland does not.

### What not to do from the ladder

Do not walk out on the field to reach a broken tile, do not slide a replacement under the course above without lifting that course, and do not solve the crack with a trowel of roof cement. Sealant on a cracked pan traps water on the felt and hides the break from the next person who inspects the roof. Foam and mortar are attachment systems with their own approvals, not gap filler. Wet tile after an afternoon storm is genuinely slick, and the fall hazard on a 1920s clay roof begins before anyone reaches the damage.

### The second-visit trap

Careless operators break this job two ways. The first is the match: profile, exposure and color have to agree with the surrounding courses, and a piece that sits proud channels water sideways into the head lap instead of down the pan. Discontinued profiles are common on anything built before the mid-1990s, and salvage stock is often the only correct answer. The second is skipping the layer underneath. If the felt under the break has been rained on for a season, setting a fresh tile over it produces a roof that looks repaired and leaks in the same spot in October. The courses lifted to reach the damage also need to be refastened rather than laid back loose.

### What the visit looks like

The count comes first, since one visible break usually means several. Fragments come out of the pan so water can run again. The course above is lifted, the broken piece is removed whole, the felt below it is examined and patched with a compatible self-adhering membrane if it has been exposed, and the replacement is set and fastened to specification rather than propped. Hip and ridge mortar gets checked at the same time, because a piece that slipped often started with a failed bed. Work of this size normally stays well under the City's 500 square foot permit threshold, which is worth confirming rather than assuming.

### What it costs

This is a small flat-rate visit at the low end and the range is fairly tight. What moves it is the number of tiles, roof height and access, whether the profile is still in production, and whether the felt underneath needs patching. Matching a discontinued color is the single biggest variable. Ask for the felt condition in writing even when the answer is good news, because that note is what tells you whether next year's leak was predictable.

## svc_roof_leak_repair_lede

A tile roof leak is a tracing job before it is a repair job. Water enters at one place, runs along felt, a batten or a deck seam, and appears indoors somewhere else entirely, often several feet downhill and a room over.

## svc_roof_leak_repair_body

### The stain is not the hole

Start indoors, because the indoor evidence narrows the roof. A stain that grows only in blowing rain points at a wall transition or a head lap. One that appears in any steady rain points at an open path straight down. A ring around a ceiling fixture near a bathroom is as likely to be a plumbing vent boot as anything in the tile field. Note the room, the direction the water travels, and whether the drip stops within an hour of the rain or keeps going, since a slow finish means water is sitting somewhere in the assembly.

### Where Tampa tile roofs actually leak

Rarely through unbroken tile. The recurring entry points are head laps where felt has shrunk back, valley metal that has rusted through beneath the tile ends, and pipe boots whose collars have split. Two more are mortar-set hip and ridge that has cracked and let water past the bedding, and wall or chimney transitions where the vertical turn-up was tucked behind the wrong layer. Slope matters too. The code allows tile from two and a half units in twelve, and between that and four in twelve it requires double underlayment. Shallow slopes on 1990s and 2000s Tampa houses therefore depend on a doubled layer that is now the same age as everything else.

### Wind-driven rain is a different failure

Hurricane Milton came ashore at Siesta Key as a Category 3 on October 10, 2024, and the National Hurricane Center's report puts hurricane conditions from Clearwater Beach and Tampa southward with an 83-knot gust reported at Tampa International Airport. Rain in that state does not fall on a roof, it is pushed across and up it. That is exactly what the secondary water barrier requirement exists for: the Florida Building Commission describes it as a water-resistant underlayment layer that resists intrusion when the primary covering is damaged, removed or penetrated by wind-driven rain in hurricane-force winds. Practically, a roof can pass a garden-hose test and still leak in a squall, which is why a repair scope written after a storm needs to cover the whole run of the affected slope rather than one tile.

### The mistake that costs the second visit

Sealing where the water shows. A bead of mastic at the drip point, a smear across the crack in a mortar ridge, a tarp thrown over tile and weighted with the tiles it is meant to protect. All three move the symptom rather than the water. The related error is stopping at the first thing found. If the pipe boot is split and the felt around it has been wet for two rainy seasons, replacing the boot buys one dry month. A repair scope should say what was found, what was ruled out, and how far the wet area extends past the visible damage.

### What the visit looks like

Interior first, then the attic where one exists, reading stains and framing for the direction of travel. On the roof, tile comes up above the suspected entry and works upslope in a widening pattern until dry felt is found, because the opening is always uphill of the stain. Fasteners, valley metal, boots and the mortar at hips get inspected while the field is open. Repair means patching or replacing the felt over the affected area, tying new membrane into sound material with the laps running the correct direction, replacing failed metal, then relaying tile with breakage accounted for. Where the exposed area grows past 500 square feet the City's permit and its dry-in inspection before tile goes back down enter the schedule.

### The insurance detail worth knowing before you call anyone

Two 2022 changes shape what a leak costs a Tampa homeowner. Senate Bill 2-D created the separate roof deductible now at section 627.701(10). It may not exceed the lesser of 2 percent of the Coverage A limit or 50 percent of the cost to replace the roof. It does not apply to a roof loss from a hurricane, to a tree fall or similar hazard that punctures the deck, to a total loss, or to a roof loss requiring repair of less than 50 percent of the roof. That last exclusion is why a documented, measured repair area matters on paper. Separately, section 627.7011 requires an insurer to pay at least actual cash value up front and the remainder as work is performed. A leak repair invoice that describes the failed layer and the measured area is a far more useful document than one that says roof repair.

### What it costs

Leak repairs price by how much tile must come off and what is under it, not by the size of the stain. A boot, a short valley run or a single open lap sits low. A slope where the felt has failed across a wide area moves into section work, where every tile in the area is inventoried, stacked and reset. Ask what the estimate assumes about the felt beyond the opened area, and ask what happens to the price if the crew finds wet decking, because that is the branch point on nearly every Tampa tile leak.

### What the gaps tell you

Tile leaves from the edges. Look at the rake, the hip, the first courses above the eave, and the ridge, because that is where uplift concentrates and where the code concentrates fastening in response. Section 1507.3.6 defines those perimeter fastening areas as three tile courses and not less than 36 inches from either side of hips or ridges and from the edges of eaves and gable rakes. If pieces are gone from those bands and the field is intact, the roof behaved normally under wind. If pieces are missing mid-slope, suspect prior work, foot traffic, or attachment that was never right.

### The wind zone Tampa is actually in

The strictest chapters of the Florida Building Code, the High Velocity Hurricane Zone provisions, apply only in Miami-Dade and Broward counties. Tampa is not in that zone, which sometimes surprises people who have read Miami roofing advice. The city sets its own line instead: the Tampa code of ordinances defines a basic wind speed line by contour map and allows a non-site-specific design at 120 miles per hour for areas landward of the 120 mile per hour line. Hillsborough County publishes windborne debris maps at 130, 140 and 150 miles per hour, which is how an address gets sorted. The practical consequence is that a replacement tile here has to be attached according to a statewide product approval and the wind speed applicable to your address, not according to whichever detail a crew learned on the other coast.

### Matching what is gone

This is where a replacement job succeeds or looks like a repair forever. Profile, exposure, thickness and color all have to agree with the surrounding courses. Tampa's older tile is a real sourcing problem: the Ybor City historic district covers 1886 to 1940, Tampa Heights 1890 to 1945, Seminole Heights 1912 to 1939, and clay profiles from those decades are long out of production, so salvage stock or reclaiming tile from a less visible slope is often the honest answer. Concrete tile from the subdivision era is easier, though colors fade under Gulf sun and a factory-fresh piece can read as a patch on a twenty-year-old roof. Ridge and hip pieces on the 1920s houses are commonly mortar-set, which means the bed gets rebuilt, not just the tile replaced.

### The trap where replacement turns into retrofit

There is a threshold worth knowing before a crew starts pulling tile. Existing Building code section 706.7 covers an existing structure with a sawn lumber, wood plank or wood structural panel deck. When the roof covering there is removed and replaced, roof-decking attachment and secondary water barrier requirements come with it. There is an exception for structures already permitted subject to the Florida Building Code. That is the same distinction Tampa's roofing guidance draws when it points homes permitted before March 1, 2002 to the hurricane mitigation retrofit requirements. The state ties roof-to-wall work to the same moment: section 553.844 requires those connections in the windborne debris region on buildings with an insured or just value of $300,000 or more, and caps the obligation so that evaluation and installation stay within roughly 15 percent of the cost of reroofing. Replacing scattered pieces does not trigger any of this. Removing and replacing the covering over an area does, and the difference should be stated in the scope before anyone quotes it.

### What not to do while you wait

Do not leave bare felt uncovered through a rainy week, and do not climb up to cover it. Exposed underlayment fails from the top down, the tiles bordering a gap are the loosest ones on the roof, and a foot on the course beside a gap usually adds to the count. Do not glue a salvaged tile down with construction adhesive or bed it in mortar simply because that is what is in the garage. Do keep the fallen pieces, including broken ones, since a fragment identifies a profile faster than a photograph and can supply a color reference.

### What the visit looks like

Count and location first, then attachment method, since the roof will already tell you whether the tile was nailed, foam-adhered or mortar-set, and the replacement has to match the system. Bordering courses get lifted and inspected, felt under the gaps gets read and patched where sun has reached it, sourcing gets settled before the crew books a day, and the new pieces get fastened to specification with the perimeter bands treated as perimeter. Where the exposed and relaid area is large enough to require a permit, the dry-in inspection before tile goes back down sets the calendar. Expect a note about tile that breaks during handling, because pieces lifted after twenty years of sun are brittle.

### What it costs

Pieces and labor at the low end, with the range widening for height, slope, mortar rebuild and sourcing difficulty. Availability drives it more than quantity: fifty in-production concrete tiles can cost less to set than a dozen matched clay barrels chased through salvage. Ask whether the price includes felt patching under the gaps, whether ridge or hip mortar is being rebuilt or just repointed, and how many spare tiles will be left on site, because the next windy afternoon is easier when the matching pieces are already in the garage.

## svc_clay_vs_concrete_tile_lede

Clay and concrete tile are held to different material standards, weigh different amounts, and behave differently after thirty years on a Tampa roof. Which one you have changes how a repair is sourced, how the tile is fastened, and what your structure is carrying.

## svc_clay_vs_concrete_tile_body

<h2>Two materials, two code standards</h2>
<p>The Florida Building Code does not treat these as interchangeable products. Clay roof tile must comply with ASTM C1167; concrete roof tile must comply with ASTM C1492. Both are then installed under the same chapter rules for slope, underlayment and attachment, which is where people get the impression they are the same thing. They are not, and the difference shows up first in sourcing. Whichever you have, the failure map is the same one described across <a href="../services/">our tile repair services</a>: the covering is the shield and the membrane underneath is the waterproofing.</p>
<h2>Weight, and why your framing cares</h2>
<p>Installed weight is the number that most often surprises homeowners. Published installed weights run 600 to 1,100 pounds per hundred square feet for concrete tile and 600 to 1,500 pounds for clay, against 195 pounds for three-tab asphalt shingles and 305 for architectural shingles. So a tile roof can be putting five to seven times the dead load of a shingle roof onto the same rafters. That is fine on a house framed for it and a real problem on a house that was not, which is the reason a change of covering type is an engineering question rather than a shopping question.</p>
<h2>Weight also changes the fastening schedule</h2>
<p>Attachment tables in the code are written partly around installed weight. In the general tile table, at the lower design wind speeds a slope of 7:12 or less may take a single fastener per tile where the installed weight exceeds 7.5 pounds per square foot and the tile is not more than sixteen inches wide. For interlocking tile with projecting anchor lugs installed over battens, fasteners are not required in some low-slope, low-wind cases, except that tile with an installed weight under 9 pounds per square foot always needs at least one fastener per tile. Above an allowable-stress design wind speed of 110 miles per hour, none of those shortcuts apply and the fastening system has to resist the calculated wind forces. A lighter tile is not a lower-maintenance tile; it is a tile with a stricter fastening rule.</p>
<h2>How each material ages in this climate</h2>
<p>Clay is fired and its color runs through the body, so a 1920s barrel tile that has never been painted still reads close to its original tone. It is also the more brittle of the two under foot traffic, and pre-war profiles are long out of production. Around here that generally means the housing stock built long before the City of Tampa's March 1, 2002 hurricane mitigation retrofit cutoff, where a matching piece has to be found rather than ordered. Concrete tile is the subdivision material, tougher underfoot, and usually surface-colored, so twenty years of Gulf sun leaves a factory-fresh replacement looking like a patch. Neither material is the reason those roofs leak. Florida guidance puts tile life at 50 to 100 years and the membrane beneath it at 20 to 25.</p>
<h2>The sourcing problem is the repair problem</h2>
<p>On a broken-tile call the material decides the whole approach. A discontinued clay barrel usually means salvage stock, or reclaiming pieces from a rear slope and putting the new tile where nobody looks. A current concrete profile can often be ordered, and then the question becomes color match rather than availability. Mortar-set hip and ridge trim, common on the older clay roofs, means the bed gets rebuilt rather than the tile swapped. Ask any estimate to state the profile, the exposure and whether the replacement pieces are new, salvaged or relocated from elsewhere on your own roof.</p>
<h2>What to do with this before you spend anything</h2>
<p>Identify what you have before you get quotes, because the two materials produce very different scopes at the same square footage. Clay is thinner in section, rings when tapped and shows a consistent color on a broken edge. Concrete is heavier in the hand, has a coarse aggregate face, and shows gray body under a colored surface where a corner has snapped. Keep any fallen fragments, since a piece in hand identifies a profile faster than a photograph. Then have the membrane assessed rather than the tile, because that is the layer with the shorter clock. If pieces are simply missing, <a href="../roof-tile-replacement/">roof tile replacement</a> is the narrower job; if the underlying felt is what failed, <a href="../tile-re-roof/">tile re-roof</a> is the conversation.</p>

## svc_emergency_roof_tarping_lede

There is one fact every Tampa tile homeowner should know before a storm: the federal Operation Blue Roof program cannot install its covering on a clay tile roof. Tile owners have to arrange their own emergency covering.

## svc_emergency_roof_tarping_body

<h2>The federal program excludes your roof</h2>
<p>Operation Blue Roof is run by the U.S. Army Corps of Engineers for FEMA and installs fiber-reinforced sheeting on damaged roofs at no cost to the homeowner. Its eligibility rules are specific, and one of them matters enormously here: contractors cannot cover metal roofs, clay, or slate tile roofs, and homes with asbestos siding are not eligible. The other rules are that the home must be a primary residence or a permanently occupied rental, the structure must be structurally sound, and roofs with more than 50 percent structural damage to the framing do not qualify. Homeowners sign a Right of Entry form to allow the work. If you own a clay tile roof in Tampa, plan on the assumption that this program is not your fallback. The repairs that follow are indexed on <a href="../services/">our services page</a>.</p>
<h2>Why an exposed tile roof is urgent rather than untidy</h2>
<p>A displaced section of tile is a rectangle of underlayment left in the open, and that membrane is the actual waterproofing layer. The Insurance Institute for Business and Home Safety states that asphalt-saturated felt is not waterproof and is known to fail after long ultraviolet exposure. Florida guidance puts membrane replacement under tile on a 20 to 25 year cycle even fully covered, with older felt under dark tile reported failing in as little as 10 to 15 years because deck temperatures past 150 degrees drive the asphalt out of the mat. An opening in August in a city that takes its rain in intense summer downpours gets tested within days. Covering buys the membrane time; it does not repair it.</p>
<h2>Covering a tile roof is a different job from covering shingles</h2>
<p>On shingles a tarp is nailed down through the covering and the holes are dealt with at replacement. On tile that approach is wrong twice. Fasteners driven through tile crack it, and the pieces broken are frequently a discontinued profile that cannot be replaced. Sheeting stretched over a curved barrel profile bridges the hollows instead of bearing on them, so wind gets underneath and the covering becomes a sail that pulls tile with it when it goes. Tile also cannot be walked anywhere: traffic belongs near the supported head lap, not mid-pan. So covering a tile roof means working from the edges and from stable paths, spreading load across battens or the deck where tile has already gone, weighting and securing at the perimeter rather than pinning the middle, and accepting that some tile must be removed deliberately in order to secure the covering without breaking more.</p>
<h2>The wind numbers that decide whether a covering holds</h2>
<p>Coverings fail at their edges, and Florida's retrofit guidance shows why. A 100 mile per hour wind drives water roughly three and a half inches up through any crack or opening, about six inches at 130 and about eight at 150, and attic pressure sits slightly negative during a storm, pulling inward. Tile attachment itself is calculated against design pressures that run 32.7 pounds per square foot at 120 miles per hour and 51.1 at 150 for a Category II building at 15 feet in Exposure C. A temporary covering is not engineered to any of those numbers, which is the honest reason it is temporary. An edge lapped upslope, or a loose corner, is the failure point in every case.</p>
<h2>Documentation while the roof is open</h2>
<p>The window when a roof is open is the only chance to record what actually failed. Photograph each opening before covering, note which band it sits in, whether the eave, the rake, the hip or the open field, keep displaced fragments so a discontinued profile can be identified later, and record the condition of the exposed felt. Roof age under Florida statute is calculated from the last date on which 100 percent of the surface area was built or replaced, so patch history does not reset the clock and a well-documented event is worth more than an invoice. Citizens Property Insurance, whose roof rules were updated for Senate Bill 2-D effective May 1, 2023 for new business and September 1, 2023 for renewals, states that it may inspect at any time to verify that a roof continues to satisfy underwriting requirements. None of that promises an outcome on your policy, and nobody should sell it that way.</p>
<h2>What the visit looks like and what it costs</h2>
<p>The sequence is: get the openings covered, then diagnose. Access and safety are assessed first, since a tile roof in wet weather is the least forgiving surface in residential construction and a wet, steep hip is sometimes a from-below job until it dries. Coverings are secured at the perimeter, laps run downslope, and the interior is checked for where water traveled so ceiling damage is found before it spreads. Price follows the covered area, roof pitch and height, weather conditions, and how much tile has to be removed to secure the edges. Ask what the covering is expected to survive and how long it is rated to stay, because a covering left through a whole summer is a second problem. Once it is dry, <a href="../storm-damage-tile-repair/">storm damage tile repair</a> is the permanent scope, and <a href="../roof-leak-repair/">roof leak repair</a> is the right page if the interior stain has not yet been traced.</p>

## svc_hip_and_gable_tile_repair_lede

Hips and gable rakes are the two edges where a tile roof is most heavily loaded and most heavily fastened. They also carry the most cut tile, and cut tile is the piece that gets left unattached.

## svc_hip_and_gable_tile_repair_body

<h2>The 36 inch rule that governs both</h2>
<p>The code defines perimeter fastening areas as three tile courses, and in no case less than 36 inches from either side of a hip or ridge and from the edges of eaves and gable rakes. That single sentence explains most of what happens at these two features. Inside those bands the fastening standard is higher because the uplift is higher, so damage there is expected under strong wind and damage outside them points at workmanship or foot traffic instead. When you are trying to work out whether your roof failed or was failed, the band boundary is the first measurement to take. The rest of the repairs are indexed on <a href="../services/">our services page</a>.</p>
<h2>A hip is a line of cut tile over a raised member</h2>
<p>Every course running into a hip has to be cut on an angle, and cutting removes the nail holes the tile was made with. The tile manual is specific about the consequence. All cut field tiles adjacent to a hip or ridge must be attached to the cap sheet or the adjacent tile with approved adhesive, mortar, or mechanical fasteners with adhesive. At the hip, the two cut tiles on each side are attached to the substrate, and field tile is cut to leave at least half an inch of clearance from the hip member. The high wind manual adds that on hip cuts above a 7:12 slope, mechanical fastening may be required. Underneath sits the member itself: pressure treated wood fastened eighteen inches on center with four number eight screws per strap or H-bracket of at least 26 gauge, or metal set in plastic cement and fastened six inches on center each side of its flange with ring shank nails. A wavy hip line is a member problem, not a tile problem.</p>
<h2>A gable rake is a wind edge with nothing beyond it</h2>
<p>At a gable the roof simply ends. There is no adjoining slope to share pressure with, so the rake tile takes the full separation of airflow at the edge. Florida Building Commission soffit research found that the worst negative pressure on a house occurs when wind runs parallel to the ridgeline, concentrated at the windward sidewall, which is precisely the plane a gable rake and its overhang occupy. That research also found loads changing in under a second, with a Category 5 case showing a 70 pounds per square foot swing inside one second, and identified the supporting channels rather than the panels as the likely weak point in soffit assemblies. The rake trim, the rake tile and the soffit return under the overhang are one connected detail, and they usually fail together.</p>
<h2>What goes wrong specifically in Tampa</h2>
<p>Three patterns recur. Mortar-set hip trim on the older clay roofs, the ones built well before the City of Tampa's March 1, 2002 retrofit cutoff, has often been repointed rather than rebedded, so the caps are resting on a shell of mortar with a hollow behind it. Concrete tile on subdivision hip roofs frequently has cut pieces sitting loose because the original crew glued only the visible ones. And corrosion works at the fasteners in exactly the band that needs them: within roughly ten miles of a Florida coast, 57.8 percent of surveyed roofing professionals reported seeing fastener corrosion usually or always, against 17.3 percent inland, in the Florida Building Commission's study of 385 respondents.</p>
<h2>Why a loose edge becomes an interior leak</h2>
<p>A cut tile that can rock does two things. It works the mortar or adhesive beneath it until the bond is gone, and it lets driven water past the head lap onto the membrane. The Florida Hurricane Retrofit Guide notes that a 100 mile per hour wind pushes water roughly three and a half inches up through any opening on a roof, about six inches at 130 and about eight at 150. A rocking rake tile is that opening. The membrane below then takes water it was never meant to see uncovered, and the stain appears indoors a course or two downslope, well away from the actual entry.</p>
<h2>What the visit looks like and what drives the cost</h2>
<p>Each hip and rake is walked and sounded for hollow beds and movement, then measured against the perimeter band so the scope has a boundary. Trim comes off in order, beds are cleared to sound substrate, the member and its straps or flange fastening are inspected, cut tiles are re-attached as specified rather than laid back, and the membrane beneath any lifted course is read and patched where sun or water has reached it. Cost follows the linear footage of hip and rake, roof pitch and height, whether members are being rebuilt, and whether matching trim is still in production. Two adjacent jobs: <a href="../ridge-cap-repair/">ridge cap repair</a>, which shares the bedded-trim craft, and <a href="../storm-damage-tile-repair/">storm damage tile repair</a> when the whole slope moved in one event.</p>

## svc_ridge_cap_repair_lede

Ridge and hip caps are the highest, most wind-loaded tile on the roof and the pieces most often set in mortar that has since cracked. A loose cap is rarely just a loose cap, because the same bed that holds it also blocks weather at the joint.

## svc_ridge_cap_repair_body

<h2>The line where wind pulls hardest</h2>
<p>Uplift concentrates at the ridge, the hips and the rakes, and the code responds by defining those bands as perimeter fastening areas. Section 1507.3.6 sets them at three tile courses, and in no case less than 36 inches from either side of a hip or ridge and from the edges of eaves and gable rakes. Anything inside that band is attached to a higher standard than the open field, which is why a cap that has worked loose is a structural finding rather than a cosmetic one. If cracked field pieces are the reason you started looking up there, the rest of <a href="../services/">our repair services</a> cover the field; this page is about the trim.</p>
<h2>Mortar is an attachment system, not a filler</h2>
<p>The FRSA and Tile Roofing Institute manual treats hip and ridge tile as a bedded assembly. Caps are set in a continuous bed of pre-bagged mortar approved under the Florida Building Code, and that bed serves two jobs at once: structural support and weather blocking. The cavity under the trim tile is filled, not skimmed at the edges. Starter tiles are secured at both ends with mechanical fasteners, mortar or adhesive. When a homeowner points at a gap under a ridge cap and asks whether it can be pointed up with a trowel, the honest answer is that the bed either supports the tile or it does not, and repointing a failed bed reproduces the failure on a schedule.</p>
<h2>What holds the cap up underneath</h2>
<p>Most Tampa ridges are built over a raised member that gives the caps a plane to sit on, and the manual specifies how that member is fixed. A metal member is set in plastic cement or a compatible sealant and fastened six inches on center on each side of its flange with ring shank roofing nails at least an inch and a quarter long. A wood member is pressure treated, fastened eighteen inches on center with four number eight screws per metal strap or H-bracket, two fasteners on each side, using straps or brackets of at least 26 gauge. Height adjustments come from stacked nailer boards limited to two inches per board. When a ridge line has gone wavy, the member and its straps are usually the reason, and nothing you do to the tile above will fix it.</p>
<h2>Cut tiles are the part people skip</h2>
<p>Where field tile meets a hip or ridge it has to be cut, and cut pieces lose their nail holes. The manual requires all cut field tiles adjacent to a hip or ridge to be attached to the cap sheet or the adjacent tile using approved adhesive, mortar, or mechanical fasteners with adhesive. It goes further at the hip: the two cut tiles on each side and the top course of ridge tiles are attached to the substrate, and where the ridge course itself was cut, the full course below it is attached too. Field tiles are cut to leave at least half an inch of clearance from the member. The high wind manual also sets a minimum two inch head lap on hip and ridge tile and notes that on hip cuts above a 7:12 slope mechanical fastening may be required. Those small rules exist to stop cut pieces from moving in the zones that see the worst loads.</p>
<h2>Salt air, and why coastal ridges age differently</h2>
<p>The Florida Building Commission surveyed 385 roofing professionals in May 2014 and found that 79.5 percent had seen corrosion on the exposed portion of metal fasteners. Location mattered more than anything else in the data: within roughly ten miles of a Florida coast, 57.8 percent said corrosion was usually or always present, against 17.3 percent inland. A ridge line within sight of the bay is running its straps, screws and cap nails in a measurably harsher environment than an identical ridge sitting a few miles further inland, and the ridge is the highest and wettest point on the roof. Compatible metals are not a preference in that setting.</p>
<h2>What the visit looks like and what it costs</h2>
<p>The ridge gets walked and sounded for hollow beds, loose caps and open joints, then read for the reason: a failed member, corroded fasteners, mortar that was never fully bedded, or storm movement. Caps come off in order, the old bed is cleared to sound substrate, the member and its attachment are inspected and repaired, and caps are reset in a full bed with cut tiles attached as the manual requires. Cost follows linear footage of ridge and hip, height and access, whether the member has to be rebuilt, and whether matching trim pieces are still made. A short section of ridge is a modest visit; a house where every hip has gone soft is a different scope, and it should be measured against the twelve-month repair limit before anyone starts. Related work: <a href="../hip-and-gable-tile-repair/">hip and gable tile repair</a> for the slopes that meet at those lines, and <a href="../roof-valley-repair/">roof valley repair</a> for the opposite condition, where water collects instead of leaving.</p>

## svc_roof_cleaning_moss_and_algae_lede

The black streaks on Tampa tile are not dirt and not mold. They are a cyanobacterium colony, and how they get removed matters more than that they get removed, because the wrong method breaks tile and drives water under it.

## svc_roof_cleaning_moss_and_algae_body

<h2>What is actually growing up there</h2>
<p>The organism behind dark roof staining is Gloeocapsa magma, a cyanobacterium rather than an alga. It is airborne, it feeds on moisture and on calcium carbonate and limestone material in roofing products, and it gained notoriety across the Southeastern United States in the 1990s. As a colony matures it develops a dark, hard, ultraviolet-protective outer coating, and that coating is what you see. Gravity then pulls the colony downslope, which is why the staining reads as vertical smears rather than patches. Growth holds moisture against the surface it occupies, which is the mechanism behind the premature aging attributed to it. The rest of <a href="../services/">our tile services</a> deal with water getting in; this page is about not creating that problem while cleaning.</p>
<h2>Why pressure washing a tile roof is a repair job waiting to happen</h2>
<p>A pressure washer solves the appearance and creates three new problems. First, tile is designed to be walked only in specific places, near the supported head lap and never mid-pan, and a person dragging a hose across an open field breaks pieces. Second, high pressure aimed uphill drives water under the head laps and directly onto the underlayment, which is the layer doing the actual waterproofing and the layer with the shortest remaining life. Third, on concrete tile it strips surface color and on older clay it works at the mortar in hip and ridge beds. The accepted approach for this organism is chemical rather than mechanical: sodium hypochlorite or copper sulfate solutions, applied at low pressure, with non-pressure application preferred specifically to avoid costly damage.</p>
<h2>What Tampa's conditions do to the timeline</h2>
<p>Two local factors set how fast staining returns. Shade is the first: a slope under live oak canopy stays damp between rains, and a damp surface with organic debris on it is a food source rather than a roof. Rain concentration is the second, since this city takes its water in intense summer downpours that wet the roof thoroughly and then leave it to bake. North-facing and shaded slopes stain first and worst. Moss, which is a plant rather than a bacterium, appears where debris has built enough depth to hold soil, typically in valleys, at ridge junctions and behind chimneys, and where moss is growing there is standing debris to clear regardless of appearance.</p>
<h2>Cleaning is also an inspection opportunity, and a risk</h2>
<p>Anyone on a tile roof with a wand is standing on the most fragile roofing material in common use here. Older clay barrel tile is brittle enough that a cleaning visit routinely produces broken pieces, and on a profile that has been out of production for decades each break is a sourcing problem rather than a line item. That is worth knowing in both directions: insist that any cleaning scope state who replaces tile broken during the work, and take advantage of the access to have the ridge beds, valleys and penetrations looked at while someone is up there. A cleaning visit that produces a written note on trim mortar and valley debris is worth more than one that only produces a clean roof.</p>
<h2>Where the insurance angle is real and where it is not</h2>
<p>Roof condition is recorded by carriers, and staining and debris are visible in an inspection photograph. Citizens Property Insurance revised its roof rules to comply with Senate Bill 2-D, effective May 1, 2023 for new business and September 1, 2023 for renewals, and among those changes the condition rule referring to excessively patched areas was rewritten to remove its 25 percent reference. Citizens also states that it may conduct inspections at any time to verify that a roof continues to satisfy underwriting requirements. What follows from that is narrow and worth stating carefully: a documented, clean, debris-free roof photographs differently from a stained one, and no cleaning should ever be sold as producing a specific decision from a specific carrier.</p>
<h2>What the visit looks like and how it prices</h2>
<p>Debris comes off first by hand, out of valleys, off ridge lines and out of gutters, because chemistry cannot reach a surface under a leaf mat. Solution is applied at low pressure and given dwell time rather than being scrubbed. Landscaping and gutters are protected and rinse water is managed. Foot traffic is planned along supported paths, and broken pieces are logged and replaced. Ask whether the quote is for a chemical soft wash or for pressure, and decline pressure on tile. ## svc_roof_deck_and_sheathing_repair_lede

The deck is the structural surface everything else is fastened to. When it goes soft under a tile roof, no amount of tile or membrane work holds, and Florida's retrofit rules attach to the deck the moment the covering comes off.

## svc_roof_deck_and_sheathing_repair_body

<h2>How a tile roof rots its own deck</h2>
<p>Water that gets past the covering does not usually pour in. It wets a small area repeatedly, and repeated wetting is what destroys sheathing. The mechanism starts above: tile is a shield and the underlayment is the waterproofing, and that membrane runs on a 20 to 25 year replacement cycle, with older asphalt-saturated felt under dark tile reported failing in as little as 10 to 15 years as deck temperatures past 150 degrees drive the asphalt out of the mat. Once the membrane goes brittle at a lap, water reaches the panel below and stays there under the tile, out of the sun and out of the wind. The panel delaminates or the plank softens years before anything shows on a ceiling. Everything else on <a href="../services/">our services list</a> sits above this layer.</p>
<h2>Reading a deck instead of guessing at it</h2>
<p>Three sources of evidence are worth more than any moisture meter reading taken through a finished ceiling. Underfoot feel, since a soft panel deflects between rafters. The attic underside, where staining, rust rings around fastener points and darkened sheathing map where water has traveled, usually downslope of the actual entry. And the fasteners themselves, because tile fasteners pulling free by hand mean the wood holding them has lost its grip. That last check is why an inspection that never lifted a tile has not inspected the deck.</p>
<h2>The retrofit that arrives with the strip</h2>
<p>Once the covering is off, deck attachment requirements apply. Sawn lumber or plank decks up to twelve inches wide are considered sufficiently connected where at least two 8d nails penetrate each framing member the board crosses, with 8d defined as at least 0.113 inch in diameter and at least two and a quarter inches long. Panel decks are graded by table: existing staples or 6d nails require supplemental fasteners at no more than six inches on center, while 8d nails already spaced at six inches on center or closer need no addition. Supplemental fasteners are specified as ring shank nails with 16 to 20 rings per inch, a shank of at least 0.113 inch, a head of at least 0.280 inch, and ring shank extending at least an inch and a half from the tip; RSRS-01, RSRS-03 and RSRS-04 nails are named. Where an existing self-adhered membrane is in place and would be damaged, the code provides an exception rather than requiring it be torn up.</p>
<h2>Panel thickness and what can go back</h2>
<p>Replacement sheathing thickness is not a matter of preference. The residential wood structural panel table sets minimums by rafter spacing and load, and it is the reason a crew cannot substitute thinner panels to save money on a tile roof carrying 600 to 1,100 pounds per hundred square feet for concrete tile or 600 to 1,500 for clay, against 195 pounds for three-tab shingles. That load is also the reason a deck under tile fails differently: a softened panel under a heavy covering deflects visibly and shows up as a wavy plane in the tile field, which is often the first thing a homeowner notices from across the street.</p>
<h2>Where permits and inspections land</h2>
<p>Deck work is structural, and that changes the paperwork. Hillsborough County's possible exemption under Florida House Bill 803 covers non-structural roof repair or replacement, siding and soffit for single-family residences under $7,500, and expressly does not apply where the work includes structural components or where the property is in a flood hazard area, and the scope may not be split into multiple projects to stay under the number. So found rot generally means a permitted job. The county's re-roof process runs a Progress Check inspection, which may be taken virtually with photographs and confirms attachments were strengthened and corrected as required, with a final following that check or an uploaded Roof Mitigation Verification Statement. Inside the city, roof work in excess of 500 square feet requires a permit and tile roofs need a dry-in inspection before tile is set, and homeowners doing their own work can call a sheathing inspection after mitigation and before the covering goes on. Florida roofing is a licensed trade, and structural deck work is exactly the category where a permitted, licensed contractor is what the rules contemplate.</p>
<h2>What the work involves and what drives the price</h2>
<p>Tile and membrane come off over the affected area, panels or planks are cut back to sound framing, rafter tails and framing tops are checked where rot ran into them, replacement sheathing is installed to the required thickness and fastened to the retrofit schedule, the whole deck's existing attachment is assessed for supplemental fastening, and membrane and tile go back with a breakage allowance. Cost follows the area of deck replaced, roof pitch, height and access, how much framing is involved, whether supplemental fastening extends beyond the repair, and how much tile survives handling. Get the found-rot rate written into the estimate before the job starts, because the extent is genuinely unknown until the covering is off. Related pages: <a href="../tile-re-roof/">tile re-roof</a>, which is where deck retrofit usually happens, and <a href="../roof-ventilation/">roof ventilation</a>, because a deck that stayed damp often had no drying path.</p>

## svc_roof_flashing_repair_lede

Flashing is the metal that carries water across every place the tile field stops: a wall, a chimney, a vent, a valley, a rake. On Tampa tile roofs it fails long before the tile does, and it fails quietly because it lives under the tile.

## svc_roof_flashing_repair_body

<h2>What flashing actually does on a tile roof</h2>
<p>Every interruption in the field is a place where the shedding surface ends and water has to be handed off to something else. Metal does that handoff. The Florida Building Code sets a floor on the material: where flashing and counterflashing at the juncture of roof and vertical surfaces are metal, they must be corrosion resistant and not less than 0.019 inch, which is No. 26 galvanized sheet gage. That is a minimum, not a target, and it says nothing about how the metal is laid, which is where most failures actually start. The broader map of these repairs sits on <a href="../services/">the services page</a>.</p>
<h2>The failure is almost always the layering, not the metal</h2>
<p>Flashing works because water meets each piece in the correct order from the top down. Turn one lap the wrong way and the metal becomes a funnel pointed at the deck. The recurring Tampa examples are a wall flashing whose vertical leg was tucked outside the building paper instead of behind it, a chimney with counterflashing surface-sealed to stucco rather than let into a joint, and a pipe collar sealed with mastic after its rubber split. All three test fine in ordinary rain and leak in a squall, because sealant is a consumable and lapping is not.</p>
<h2>Wind changes the direction water travels</h2>
<p>The Florida Hurricane Retrofit Guide puts numbers on this. Above 60 miles per hour, rain is driven against a house with real penetrating force, and the pressure differential from a 100 mile per hour wind will push water roughly three and a half inches up through any crack or opening on the roof, a gable wall or a soffit. At 130 miles per hour that becomes about six inches, and at 150 about eight. It also notes that attic and interior pressures are usually slightly negative, so the building is pulling water inward at the same time the wind is pushing it. A flashing detail with three inches of vertical leg is not a defect on a calm day and is a hole in a named storm.</p>
<h2>Fasteners at penetrations corrode first</h2>
<p>The Florida Building Commission's fastener corrosion study found that 23.8 percent of surveyed roofing professionals usually or always saw corrosion on penetration fasteners, against 16 percent for tile fasteners in the open field. That ordering is the useful part: the metal around a vent, a stack or a wall is fixed with more fasteners, in wetter conditions, often with mixed metals, and it gives up first. Within about ten miles of a Florida coast, 57.8 percent of the same respondents reported corrosion usually or always present. On a house that predates March 1, 2002, the date the City of Tampa uses to trigger its hurricane mitigation retrofit requirements, decades of successive repairs usually mean several generations of metal are stacked in the same wall detail, quietly reacting with each other.</p>
<h2>The layer underneath decides the scope</h2>
<p>Flashing is set into the underlayment, so repairing it means opening the membrane. If the felt around a failed pan flashing has been wet through two rainy seasons, replacing the metal alone buys a few dry weeks. That is the point at which a flashing call becomes an underlayment call, and it is better discovered with a measuring tape than with a second leak in October. Where the exposed and relaid area grows, the city's 500 square foot permit threshold and the dry-in inspection before tile goes back down enter the calendar; where the work stays genuinely non-structural and under $7,500 on a single-family house, Hillsborough County's House Bill 803 exemption may apply, though an exemption application still goes through the county portal with documentation of the scope and value.</p>
<h2>What the visit looks like and how it is priced</h2>
<p>Tile is lifted at and above the suspect detail, the metal is exposed, and the laps are traced in the direction water travels rather than the direction they were installed. Corroded or undersized metal is replaced to at least the code gage, the vertical leg is set behind the water-shedding layer, the membrane is tied in with compatible self-adhering material, and the tile is reset with a breakage allowance. Price follows linear feet of detail, the number of penetrations, whether stucco or masonry has to be cut for a reglet, and how much membrane has to be renewed. Ask for the felt condition around the flashing in writing even when the news is good. Two neighbors of this repair: <a href="../roof-valley-repair/">roof valley repair</a>, where the same metal carries far more water, and <a href="../skylight-leak-repair/">skylight leak repair</a>, the penetration that most often gets sealed instead of flashed.</p>

## svc_roof_underlayment_replacement_lede

The membrane between your tile and your plywood is the part of the roof that actually keeps water out, and it is the part with the shortest life. Replacing it is the single largest repair a Tampa tile roof normally needs, and it is usually the correct answer once leaks start repeating on the same slope.

## svc_roof_underlayment_replacement_body

<h2>Why this is the repair that decides the roof</h2>
<p>Tile is a sunshade and a rain deflector. It is not a sealed surface, and driven water moves between the pieces routinely. What catches that water and carries it back down to the eave is the membrane laid on the deck. Florida roofing guidance places tile service life at 50 to 100 years while putting the waterproof layer beneath it on a 20 to 25 year replacement cycle, which is why a roof can look sound from the street and still be finished as a water barrier. The other pages under <a href="../services/">our Tampa tile services</a> all sit downstream of that one fact: a cracked piece, a slipped ridge or a rusted valley is a symptom, and the membrane is the patient.</p>
<h2>What heat does to the layer nobody sees</h2>
<p>Under dark tile the deck surface can run well past 150 degrees on a summer afternoon. Older asphalt-saturated felt does not tolerate that, because the heat drives the asphalt compounds out of the mat until it is brittle enough to crumble when a hand touches it. Florida contractor guidance reports failure of that older felt under dark tile in as little as 10 to 15 years. The Insurance Institute for Business and Home Safety is blunter about the material itself: asphalt-saturated felt is not waterproof, and it is known to fail when installed poorly, when made to a low specification, or after long ultraviolet exposure. That last item is why an uncovered slope during a repair is a schedule problem rather than an inconvenience.</p>
<h2>Weight class is not a detail</h2>
<p>Florida Building Code section 1507.3.3 accepts ASTM D226 Type II felt, or ASTM D2626 or D6380 Class M mineral-surfaced roll roofing, under tile. The state's own roofing fact sheet for the 2020 code tightened this further: where felt is used it must be 30 pound or equivalent, meaning D226 Type II or D4869 Type III or IV, and ply count, lap width and fastener spacing were all strengthened. The numbers behind those labels matter. D226 Type II carries a minimum of 26 pounds per hundred square feet; the Type I product often called 15 pound carries 11.5. In 2014 wind-driven-rain testing at the Insurance Institute one sheet sold as a 30 pound felt did not meet either heavy specification, failed at the ridge under high wind, and left the sheathing exposed. Ask which specification is going on your deck and get the answer in writing.</p>
<h2>The slope of your roof changes the assembly</h2>
<p>Tile is permitted from two and a half units of rise in twelve. Between that and four in twelve the code requires a double underlayment application, so the shallow hip roofs common on 1990s and 2000s stucco houses here are carrying two layers of the same aging material rather than one. For clay and concrete tile specifically, the state fact sheet points underlayment compliance at the FRSA and Tile Roofing Institute high wind manual, sixth edition, which was rewritten to line up with ASCE 7-16 wind loads. A crew quoting felt by the roll without naming that manual is quoting a shingle job.</p>
<h2>Paperwork, inspections, and the open deck</h2>
<p>Underlayment replacement is not spot work, so the permit questions arrive immediately. Hillsborough County's re-roof process runs a Progress Check inspection, which may be taken virtually with photographs, where the inspector confirms roof attachments were strengthened and corrected as the code requires, and a final only after that check or after a Roof Mitigation Verification Statement is uploaded through the county portal. The application itself carries an attestation under Florida Building Code B110, signed under penalty of perjury, that the existing decking, underlayment and covering will go in per code. Inside the city, roof work in excess of 500 square feet needs a permit and a tile roof needs a dry-in inspection before any tile is set. Practically that means the deck stays open until someone with authority looks at the new membrane, so the job needs a dry stretch of weather and a booked inspector, not just an available crew.</p>
<h2>What the work involves and where the money goes</h2>
<p>Tile is inventoried, lifted and stacked in courses so it can be reset in the same order. Old felt comes off, the deck gets read for rot and fastener condition, new membrane is laid with laps running downslope, penetrations and valleys are tied in, and the tile goes back with a breakage allowance because twenty-year-old pieces snap during handling. Cost tracks square footage, roof complexity and how much of the original tile survives the round trip, not the number of leaks you had. A scope worth signing names the underlayment product and specification, the number of plies, the measured area, the expected breakage percentage, and who is sourcing replacement tile if the profile is out of production. If only one slope has failed, say so on paper, because a documented section boundary is what keeps a partial job partial. When the pieces coming off are already breaking at the nail holes, read <a href="../tile-re-roof/">tile re-roof</a> next; if the deck itself reads soft, <a href="../roof-deck-and-sheathing-repair/">roof deck and sheathing repair</a> is the prior step.</p>

## svc_roof_valley_repair_lede

A valley is the only part of a tile roof where two slopes hand their water to a single line of metal. It carries more volume than anything else up there, which is why it is where rust, debris and short laps turn into interior leaks.

## svc_roof_valley_repair_body

<h2>The code is unusually specific here</h2>
<p>Most tile details are handed to a manufacturer's instructions. Valleys are not. The Florida Building Code requires valley flashing of corrosion-resistant metal not less than 0.019 inch, which is No. 26 galvanized sheet gage, extending not less than eleven inches from the centerline in each direction, with a splash diverter rib at least one inch high formed as part of the flashing at the flow line, and end laps of at least four inches between sections. On slopes of three units in twelve and over it also requires a 36-inch-wide underlayment down the valley in addition to everything else on the roof: either one layer of Type I underlayment running the full length, or a self-adhering polymer-modified bitumen sheet labeled to ASTM D1970. Those numbers give you a checklist. Compare them against what is actually on your roof, then work back through <a href="../services/">the rest of our tile repairs</a>.</p>
<h2>What that splash diverter rib is for</h2>
<p>The raised rib down the center of a valley pan is not decoration and it is not a stiffener. In heavy rain, water arriving from one slope crosses the valley under momentum and tries to run up under the tile on the opposite side. The rib interrupts that crossing. Tampa gets most of its rain in short, intense summer downpours, and a valley without a functioning rib, or with one flattened by a previous repair or by foot traffic, will push water sideways into the tile ends on the far slope. The leak then shows up on the wrong side of the house from the actual fault, which is why valley leaks get misdiagnosed so often.</p>
<h2>Debris is the mechanism, not the nuisance</h2>
<p>Valleys collect everything the roof catches: oak leaves, needles, sand, broken tile fragments, wind-blown roofing granules. That load holds moisture against the metal continuously instead of letting it dry between rains, and it dams water above the pan so it stands at the tile ends. Standing water plus a mixed-metal fastener is how a valley pan rusts through from the top while looking intact from the ground. The Florida Building Commission's survey of 385 roofing professionals found corrosion of exposed metal fasteners reported by 79.5 percent of them, with 57.8 percent seeing it usually or always within about ten miles of a coast, against 17.3 percent inland. A shaded, debris-packed valley on a bayside street is the worst combination of those conditions available.</p>
<h2>Cut tile at the valley edge</h2>
<p>Field tile has to be cut along both sides of a valley, and cut pieces lose the nail holes that fastened them. The tile manual requires cut pieces to be attached with approved adhesive, mortar, or mechanical fasteners with adhesive, rather than left resting on the pan. Two failures follow from ignoring that. Loose cut tile slides down into the valley and blocks it, and the sliding pieces gouge the flashing coating that is protecting the metal. When a valley repair comes back with a note that half the cut tiles were unattached, that is not a complaint about the previous crew, it is the reason the valley filled.</p>
<h2>What the visit looks like</h2>
<p>The valley is cleared first, because nothing can be judged through a debris load. Then the pan is read for pitting, perforation and flattened rib, the end laps are checked for direction and length, and the tile ends on both slopes are lifted to see whether the extra 36-inch valley underlayment is present and intact. Repair means removing cut tile in order, replacing the pan in full runs rather than patching a hole, tying the valley underlayment into the surrounding membrane with laps running downslope, and resetting cut tile attached as specified. A pan is replaced end to end for a reason: a lap spliced mid-valley in the wrong direction is the exact defect being fixed.</p>
<h2>Cost and paperwork</h2>
<p>Price follows the linear footage of valley, roof height and pitch, how much tile must come off on each side, whether the underlayment beneath is sound, and whether matching cut tile survives. A single short valley on a low hip roof is a modest job; a long, steep valley between two large slopes with failed felt beneath it is section work. Measure the exposed area, because inside the city a roof repair in excess of 500 square feet needs a permit and a tile roof needs its dry-in inspection before tile is reset. Next door to this repair: <a href="../roof-flashing-repair/">roof flashing repair</a> for the wall and penetration details built from the same metal, and <a href="../roof-leak-repair/">roof leak repair</a> if the stain inside has not been traced yet.</p>

## svc_roof_ventilation_lede

Attic ventilation on a tile roof is a code-quantified requirement, not a preference. It is also the part of the roof most likely to let water in during a storm, because a vent is an opening by design.

## svc_roof_ventilation_body

<h2>The numbers the code actually sets</h2>
<p>Florida Building Code section 1203.2 requires ventilation for enclosed attics and enclosed rafter spaces where ceilings are applied directly to the underside of roof framing. The base ratio is one square foot of net free ventilating area for every 150 square feet of the space being ventilated. That drops to one in 300 in two cases: where a Class I or II vapor retarder is installed on the warm-in-winter side of the ceiling, or where at least 40 percent and not more than 50 percent of the required area is provided by ventilators located in the upper portion of the space, positioned at least three feet above the eave or cornice vents, with the remainder provided by eave or cornice vents. Openings are protected against rain and snow entry, are covered with corrosion-resistant mesh with openings between 1/16 and 1/4 inch, and where insulation is present a minimum one inch airspace must remain between it and the roof sheathing at the vent location. Those figures let you check a roof rather than argue about it. The rest of <a href="../services/">our tile repairs</a> share the same approach.</p>
<h2>Why the ratio matters under tile specifically</h2>
<p>Tile sits above a ventilated cavity and gets hot. Under dark tile the deck surface can run past 150 degrees on a summer afternoon, and heat is what drives asphalt compounds out of older felt until it goes brittle, with failures reported in as little as 10 to 15 years. An underventilated attic keeps that deck hotter for longer every day of a Tampa summer. So ventilation is not only a comfort and moisture issue, it is a direct input into how long the waterproofing layer beneath your tile lasts. The same is true of moisture: a humid attic with no exhaust path holds water vapor against the underside of the sheathing continuously.</p>
<h2>Vents are the roof's designed openings, and storms use them</h2>
<p>The Florida Hurricane Retrofit Guide is direct about this. Roof vents are not watertight, and if wind is blowing up a roof slope during a hurricane, water is likely to be blown up and into a roof vent. It notes that ridge vents can be dislodged by high winds, that many turbine vents were seen missing after the 2004 hurricanes, and that a number of gable vents were also lost. It quantifies the push as well: a 100 mile per hour wind will drive water roughly three and a half inches up through any crack or opening on the roof, a gable wall or a soffit, about six inches at 130 and about eight at 150, while attic pressures typically sit slightly negative and pull inward. That combination is how an attic gets wet under an intact tile field.</p>
<h2>The trade-off, stated honestly</h2>
<p>You cannot solve storm water intrusion by closing vents, because then the deck cooks and the attic sweats. What you can do is choose vent types and locations deliberately, protect openings as the code requires, and keep the eave-to-ridge path clear so the system moves air with the smallest total opening that satisfies the ratio. The retrofit guide's own emphasis is on limiting water entry at openings rather than eliminating openings. That is the frame worth holding onto when a contractor proposes either extreme.</p>
<h2>Corrosion at the ridge, in numbers</h2>
<p>Ridge vents are the single worst component in Florida's fastener corrosion data. In the Florida Building Commission's survey of 385 roofing professionals, 41.8 percent reported seeing corrosion usually or always on ridge vent fasteners, against 23.8 percent for penetration fasteners and 16 percent for tile fasteners. Overall, 79.5 percent had observed corrosion on the exposed portion of metal fasteners. Location dominated: within roughly ten miles of a coast, 57.8 percent saw it usually or always, against 17.3 percent inland. A ridge vent is at the top of the roof, in the wettest wind, with the most fasteners, often in mixed metals. It should be inspected on that basis and not assumed sound because it is new.</p>
<h2>What the work looks like and how it prices</h2>
<p>Assessment starts with a measurement: attic floor area, the net free area of the intake and exhaust actually present, and where each sits relative to the ridge. Then the physical checks, which are usually where the fault is: soffit vents blocked by insulation or by paint, no baffle maintaining the one inch airspace at the eave, exhaust and intake fighting each other, mesh corroded away, and ridge vent fasteners rusted. Repair means restoring intake at the eave, correcting the upper-to-lower split so it falls in the 40 to 50 percent range if the one in 300 ratio is being used, and reinstalling exhaust with attention to fastener metal. Cost follows attic area, roof pitch and height, how much soffit has to be opened, and whether ridge tile has to be lifted to reach a ridge vent. Related work: <a href="../soffit-and-fascia-repair/">soffit and fascia repair</a>, which is where intake lives, and <a href="../roof-deck-and-sheathing-repair/">roof deck and sheathing repair</a> when the underside of the sheathing has already darkened.</p>

## svc_secondary_water_barrier_installation_lede

A secondary water barrier is the layer that keeps a house dry when the covering above it is gone. Florida requires one at roof replacement, and for tile roofs the requirement is answered in a specific way most homeowners have never been told.

## svc_secondary_water_barrier_installation_body

<h2>What the term means in the code</h2>
<p>The Florida Building Code defines a secondary water barrier as an underlayment installed either between the primary roof covering and the roof sheathing, or directly onto the roof sheathing, that provides protection to the sheathing in the event the primary roof covering is blown off. It is deliberately a system requirement rather than a product. That definition is worth quoting to a contractor, because it makes clear what the layer is for: the moment the tile is gone, this is what is standing between wind-driven rain and your plywood. The repairs that sit above it are on <a href="../services/">our services page</a>.</p>
<h2>The two accepted methods</h2>
<p>Where a secondary water barrier is required, the code accepts either of two approaches. The first is a self-adhering polymer-modified bitumen tape at least four inches wide, applied directly to the sheathing to seal every horizontal and vertical joint, then covered with an approved underlayment installed for the primary roof covering. The second is two layers of an approved underlayment installed as required for the primary covering, or one layer installed in a continuous coating of approved asphalt roofing cement, or an approved self-adhering polymer-modified bitumen sheet applied directly to the sheathing. Statute reinforces the flexibility: the state may not limit a secondary water barrier requirement to one particular type of method or material. So a quote that presents one method as the only legal option is overstating the code.</p>
<h2>The tile-specific answer, and why it is not a loophole</h2>
<p>Here is the part specific to your roof. The Existing Building Code provides that clay and concrete tile roof systems installed as required by the Florida Building Code are deemed to comply with the secondary water barrier provision. That reflects how a tile assembly is actually built: on slopes between two and a half and four units in twelve a double underlayment application is required, and where felt is used the state requires 30 pound or equivalent material, meaning ASTM D226 Type II or ASTM D4869 Type III or IV, under section 1507.3.3 which accepts D226 Type II felt or Class M mineral-surfaced roll roofing. The practical consequence is important and easy to misread: deemed-to-comply is not permission to install less. It means your underlayment specification is doing the barrier's job, so the specification on your scope of work is the barrier. If the paper says felt without a standard, nothing has been promised.</p>
<h2>When the requirement is actually triggered</h2>
<p>Statute requires a roof replacement to incorporate a secondary water barrier and to strengthen or correct roof-decking attachments and fasteners. That is a replacement trigger, not a repair trigger, and Florida's Existing Building Code sets the boundary by limiting repair, replacement and recovery inside a twelve-month period to a quarter of a roof area or roof section, with an exception where the existing system or section was built, repaired or replaced in compliance with the 2007 code or a later edition, in which case only the portion being worked must meet current requirements. Local governments cannot amend that exception by ordinance. So the date of the last permitted roof work at your address, and the measured area of your current job, together decide whether this layer is on the table.</p>
<h2>The Tampa paperwork that goes with it</h2>
<p>Inside the city, every single-family residence built before March 1, 2002 is directed to the hurricane mitigation retrofit requirements, roof work in excess of 500 square feet requires a permit, and a tile roof requires a dry-in inspection before tile is installed. Homeowners performing their own work may satisfy the mitigation requirement by calling for a sheathing inspection after mitigation and before the covering goes on, or by having a licensed professional complete the Roof Mitigation Verification Affidavit. In the county, the permit application carries an attestation under code section B110 signed under penalty of perjury, the re-roof process includes a Progress Check inspection that may be conducted virtually with photographs, and a final follows either that check or an uploaded Roof Mitigation Verification Statement. This is the layer inspectors are looking at during dry-in, which is why the schedule has to allow for it.</p>
<h2>What to require on paper</h2>
<p>Three things. First, the method, named: taped joints plus approved underlayment, or a full-deck application, with the product and its standard written out. Second, the underlayment specification by ASTM designation and weight, plus the number of plies and whether your slope requires the double application. Third, the measured area, since that number is what a later partial repair gets judged against. Verify who is calling inspections and when, since the deck cannot be covered before dry-in is approved. And be careful with insurance expectations: Florida carriers do impose roof-age conditions, and none of this work should be sold as producing a specific decision from a specific carrier. For the fuller replacement path see <a href="../tile-re-roof/">tile re-roof</a>, and for the one-slope version see <a href="../roof-underlayment-replacement/">roof underlayment replacement</a>.</p>

## svc_skylight_leak_repair_lede

A skylight on a tile roof is a hole through the waterproofing layer with a window sitting in it. When it leaks, the cause is almost always the flashing and the membrane around the curb rather than the glass.

## svc_skylight_leak_repair_body

<h2>Sealant is not a flashing method</h2>
<p>The single most common Tampa skylight repair we see undone later is a bead of mastic run around a curb. It works for a season. The reason it fails is structural rather than chemical: glass, aluminum, wood curb and tile all move at different rates under a roof deck that can run past 150 degrees under dark tile on a summer afternoon, and a rigid sealant line across four dissimilar materials is being asked to absorb all of that movement. Engineered flashing kits exist for tile roofs specifically and are built to give three layers of water protection without field-fabricated metal or reliance on sealants. If a previous repair was a tube of caulk, the skylight has not actually been flashed. The wider set of these repairs is on <a href="../services/">our services page</a>.</p>
<h2>Deck-mount and curb-mount leak differently</h2>
<p>Deck-mount units fasten directly to the roof deck and rely on peel-and-stick underlayment plus an engineered flashing kit, so their failures are almost always at the membrane tie-in or at a lap turned the wrong way. Curb-mount units sit on a built-up curb, so their failures cluster at the curb-to-deck joint, at the counterflashing that laps down the curb, and at the head, where water arriving from upslope has to be diverted around the unit. Knowing which type you have tells you where to open the roof, which is worth more than any amount of hose testing from below.</p>
<h2>The pressure numbers explain the mystery leaks</h2>
<p>The Florida Hurricane Retrofit Guide reports that wind pressure at 100 miles per hour drives water about three and a half inches up through any crack or opening on a roof, rising to roughly six inches at 130 and eight inches at 150. A skylight head flashing with two inches of upstand is therefore compliant with nothing in particular and open in a squall. That is also why a skylight can pass a garden hose test in the morning and leak that afternoon in a thunderstorm with wind behind it: the hose only reproduces gravity.</p>
<h2>Penetration fasteners are the ones that corrode</h2>
<p>The Florida Building Commission's survey of roofing professionals found penetration fasteners reported as usually or always corroded by 23.8 percent of respondents, higher than the 16 percent reported for tile fasteners in the open field. Skylights concentrate every risk factor in that finding: many fasteners, mixed metals between frame, flashing and deck, and a detail that stays damp longer than the surrounding tile. Within roughly ten miles of a Florida coast, 57.8 percent of the same respondents saw corrosion usually or always, against 17.3 percent inland.</p>
<h2>If the unit is being replaced, the code has requirements</h2>
<p>Replacing a skylight rather than reflashing it brings the glazing and energy rules with it. Florida's residential energy provisions require a solar heat gain coefficient of .30 or lower for skylights, and the requirement applies to replacement fenestration. Glazing has its own rule: laminated glass with a minimum 0.015 inch polyvinyl butyral interlayer is permitted for panes of sixteen square feet or less whose highest point is not more than twelve feet above a walking surface, with a thicker 0.30 inch interlayer required for larger or higher installations, and a safety screen requirement where any part of the unit sits more than twelve feet above the floor. Because Tampa sits in the windborne debris region, whether the replacement has to be impact rated is a question to settle before ordering, not after.</p>
<h2>What the visit looks like and how it prices</h2>
<p>Diagnosis starts inside, noting whether water appears at the head, the sill or a corner of the shaft, and whether it runs only in wind-driven rain. On the roof, tile comes off above and beside the unit, the flashing is exposed and read lap by lap, and the underlayment around the curb is checked for the darkened, brittle condition that means it has been wet for seasons. Repair means replacing failed flashing components with an engineered kit or properly formed metal, tying self-adhering membrane into sound underlayment with laps running downslope, and resetting tile with a breakage allowance. Cost follows unit size, mount type, roof pitch and height, how much membrane must be renewed, and whether the glass unit itself is being replaced. Where the opened area grows past 500 square feet inside the city, the permit and the dry-in inspection before tile is reset join the schedule. Related: <a href="../roof-flashing-repair/">roof flashing repair</a> for the same craft at walls and vents, and <a href="../roof-underlayment-replacement/">roof underlayment replacement</a> when the membrane around the curb turns out to be the real finding.</p>

## svc_soffit_and_fascia_repair_lede

Soffit and fascia are not roofing trim in a hurricane. They are the horizontal opening into your attic, and Florida Building Commission research is direct about the consequence: losing the soffit makes a house badly exposed to wind-driven rain intrusion.

## svc_soffit_and_fascia_repair_body

<h2>Why this is a water problem before it is an appearance problem</h2>
<p>Air enters an attic through soffit vents by design. Water enters the same way when it is pushed. The Florida Hurricane Retrofit Guide quantifies the push: above 60 miles per hour, rain is driven at the outside of a house with real penetrating force, and the pressure produced by a 100 mile per hour wind will drive water roughly three and a half inches up through any crack or opening on the roof, a gable wall, or through the soffit. At 130 miles per hour that becomes about six inches and at 150 about eight. The guide also points out that attic pressure is usually slightly negative during a storm, so the house is pulling at the same water the wind is pushing. This is why an attic can flood with a completely intact tile field above it, and why soffit work belongs beside <a href="../services/">the roof repairs on our services page</a>.</p>
<h2>What the research found about how soffits fail</h2>
<p>Florida Building Commission soffit research tested panels and vented assemblies rather than guessing. The worst positive pressure occurs when wind runs perpendicular to the ridgeline, concentrated at the middle of the building; the worst negative pressure occurs when wind runs parallel to the ridgeline, at the windward sidewall. Loads change suddenly and peaks last less than a second, with a Category 5 case showing a 70 pounds per square foot change inside one second. In static testing, failure was observed at plus 40 pounds per square foot with 580 cubic feet per minute of flow per square foot. The most useful finding for a repair is about where failure starts: undeformed soffit panels are usually found on the ground after an event, and the supports, the channels, appear to be the weak point. In other words the panel did not break, it was released.</p>
<h2>Fascia is what the gutter and the drip edge hang on</h2>
<p>Fascia carries the gutter, backs the drip edge, and closes the tail of the deck. Once it softens, the gutter pulls, the drip edge loses its nailing surface, and water tracks behind the eave rather than off it. Rotted fascia on a tile roof is usually a downstream symptom: the eave course of tile sits closest to the fascia, sheds the greatest volume of any course, and is where displaced or slipped tile exposes underlayment first. Repairing fascia without checking the eave tile, the underlayment termination and the drip edge lap just puts fresh wood into the same wet detail.</p>
<h2>Where this sits in the permit picture</h2>
<p>Hillsborough County's list of possible exemptions under Florida House Bill 803 names siding, soffit, and non-structural roof repair or replacement, for single-family residences where the value of the work is under $7,500. The conditions attached to it matter more than the headline. The owner or the owner's contractor still applies for the exemption through the county portal, a copy of the contract or other documentation of scope and value is submitted with it, and the scope may not be split into more than one project to get under the number. The exemption does not apply where the work reaches $7,500 or more, where the property is partly or entirely in a flood hazard area, or where the work includes structural, electrical, plumbing, mechanical or gas components. Rotted framing at an eave tail is structural, and that is a common finding once soffit comes down.</p>
<h2>What the visit looks like</h2>
<p>Panels come down at the affected run so the framing can be seen, because the diagnosis lives in the lookouts, the sub-fascia and the rafter tails rather than in the panel. Support channels and their fastening are checked against the failure mode the research describes. Rotted fascia and sub-fascia are cut back to sound wood, the deck edge and underlayment termination are read for water tracking, drip edge lap direction is corrected, and panels or vented soffit are reinstalled with fastening at the supports rather than only at the panel field. Net free vent area is preserved, since blocking soffit vents to stop water creates a ventilation problem addressed on <a href="../roof-ventilation/">roof ventilation</a>.</p>
<h2>Cost and sequencing</h2>
<p>Price follows linear footage, eave height, whether the material is aluminum, vinyl or wood, and how much framing is being replaced behind it. The framing is the variable that moves a quote, and it cannot be measured from the driveway. Sequence matters too: soffit and fascia work at the eave should be scheduled with any eave-course tile or drip edge work rather than before it, so the same detail is not opened twice. If tile at the eave and rake has moved, <a href="../storm-damage-tile-repair/">storm damage tile repair</a> covers the field side of the same event.</p>

## svc_storm_damage_tile_repair_lede

Storm damage on a tile roof follows the wind, not the rain. Tile lets go along the rake, the hip and the ridge where uplift concentrates, and every displaced piece leaves the waterproofing layer beneath it exposed to whatever falls next week.

## svc_storm_damage_tile_repair_body

<h2>The load the tile was engineered against</h2>
<p>Tile attachment in Florida is not a rule of thumb; it is a calculation. The high wind manual for concrete and clay tile states that tile shall be attached to resist the aerodynamic moment determined from the design pressures for the building. Its tables show how quickly that requirement climbs. Required design pressure for a Category II building at a 15 foot mean roof height in Exposure C runs 32.7 pounds per square foot at 120 miles per hour, 38.4 at 130, 44.5 at 140 and 51.1 at 150. The matching aerodynamic uplift moment for field tile at pitches of 6:12 and less goes 16.2, 19.0, 22.0 and 25.3 across the same wind speeds. A ten mile per hour step in design wind speed is roughly a fifteen percent step in what every tile has to hold. That is the reason a storm that passed one street over left a different roof intact. The broader repair map is on <a href="../services/">our services page</a>.</p>
<h2>Read the damage pattern before you count tiles</h2>
<p>Tile that leaves from the first courses above the eave, from a gable rake, or from either side of a hip or ridge is behaving exactly as the code expects, which is why those bands are defined as perimeter fastening areas: three tile courses, and never less than 36 inches from either side of hips and ridges and from eave and gable rake edges. Damage confined to those bands says the roof met wind in a normal way. Tile missing from the middle of an open field says something else, usually prior foot traffic, an earlier repair set without proper attachment, or cut pieces that were never fixed down. The pattern is diagnostic and it should appear in the written scope.</p>
<h2>The exposed membrane is the emergency, not the missing tile</h2>
<p>A gap in the field is a rectangle of underlayment now taking direct sun and driven rain. That layer was never designed for exposure. Florida guidance puts membrane replacement under tile on a 20 to 25 year cycle even when it is fully covered, and older asphalt-saturated felt under dark tile has been reported failing in as little as 10 to 15 years. The Insurance Institute's material review notes plainly that asphalt-saturated felt is not waterproof and is known to fail after extended ultraviolet exposure. Tampa concentrates its rain in the summer months, so an August gap gets tested weekly while a homeowner waits for a scope. Covering the opening comes before repairing it.</p>
<h2>Scope the slope, not the tile you can see from the yard</h2>
<p>The recurring mistake after a named storm is a scope written from a driveway photograph. Wind that lifted one tile loosened the fasteners of its neighbors, cracked the mortar bed under nearby trim, and moved the metal at the closest rake. A defensible post-storm scope covers the whole affected slope: the field around each loss, the ridge and hip beds, the rake and eave courses, the valley pans, and every penetration on that plane. It also measures. Florida's Existing Building Code limits repair, replacement and recovery to a quarter of a roof area or roof section inside any twelve-month period before the whole section has to be brought current, and the exception for sections built or replaced under the 2007 code or later turns on the date of the last permitted roof work at your address.</p>
<h2>What your insurance file records about a patched roof</h2>
<p>Carriers write roof condition down, and the records outlive the repair. Florida statute calculates a roof's age from the last date on which 100 percent of the surface area was built or replaced, so a run of storm patches does not restart that clock. Citizens Property Insurance, which rewrote its roof rules to comply with Senate Bill 2-D effective May 1, 2023 for new business and September 1, 2023 for renewals, bases roof age on the oldest part of the roof where the whole roof was not replaced at once, and states that it may inspect at any time to confirm a roof still satisfies underwriting requirements. None of that guarantees any particular outcome on your policy, and no repair should be sold as if it does. What it does mean is that a storm repair documented with a measured area, the failed layer named and the tile profile recorded is a more useful piece of paper than an invoice that says roof repair.</p>
<h2>What the visit looks like and how it is priced</h2>
<p>Losses are counted and mapped by band. Displaced pieces are recovered, since fragments identify a discontinued profile faster than a photograph. Openings are covered so the membrane stops taking sun. Tile is then lifted around each loss to read the felt, fasteners are checked in the perimeter areas, trim beds are sounded, and the scope is written against the measured area. Price follows the number and location of losses, roof height and pitch, whether the profile is still made, how much membrane needs patching, and whether trim mortar has to be rebuilt. Ask specifically what happens to the price if the crew finds wet decking, because that is the branch point on most Tampa storm calls. If the roof is open right now, <a href="../emergency-roof-tarping/">emergency roof tarping</a> is the immediate step; if you want the whole roof documented before the next season, <a href="../tile-roof-inspection/">tile roof inspection</a> is the one to book.</p>

## svc_tile_re_roof_lede

A tile re-roof strips the covering to the deck, renews the waterproofing layer, and puts tile back. It is not a bigger repair; it is a different job, with a code path, an inspection sequence and a decision about how much of your existing tile survives.

## svc_tile_re_roof_body

<h2>When a re-roof is the honest recommendation</h2>
<p>The trigger is rarely the tile. It is a slope that has produced three leaks in two rainy seasons, felt that tears rather than bends when it is lifted, and repair areas that have started to overlap. At that point patching stops being cheaper, because every visit means lifting, stacking and resetting tile you are going to lift again. The rest of <a href="../services/">our Tampa tile work</a> is repair; this page is the replacement path, and the deciding evidence is the condition of the membrane, not the appearance of the roof.</p>
<h2>The twelve-month limit, and the exception that changes everything</h2>
<p>Florida's Existing Building Code caps how much of a roof area or roof section can be repaired, replaced or recovered inside any twelve-month window at a quarter of it. Cross that line and the whole system or section has to be brought to current code. The exception is the part worth knowing before you sign anything: where the existing system or section was built, repaired or replaced in compliance with the 2007 code or any later edition, only the portion actually being worked has to meet today's requirements, and the statute bars a local government from amending that exception by ordinance. So the date of the last permitted roof job on your address is a genuine fork. Hillsborough County states the same rule in its own re-roof guidance, framed around roofs installed before the 2007 code.</p>
<h2>Deck attachment comes with the strip</h2>
<p>Once the covering is off a wood deck, the retrofit requirements arrive. The code treats sawn lumber or plank up to twelve inches wide as sufficiently connected when at least two 8d nails reach each framing member it crosses, with 8d defined as at least 0.113 inch in diameter and at least two and a quarter inches long. Panel decks are graded by a table: existing staples or 6d nails require supplemental fasteners no more than six inches on center, while 8d nails already at six inches on center or tighter need nothing added. Supplemental fasteners are specified tightly, as ring shank nails with 16 to 20 rings per inch, a shank at least 0.113 inch, a head of at least 0.280 inch, and ring shank extending at least an inch and a half from the tip. This is the work the county's Progress Check inspection exists to verify.</p>
<h2>The secondary water barrier question, answered specifically for tile</h2>
<p>Statute requires a roof replacement to incorporate a secondary water barrier and to strengthen or correct roof-decking attachments and fasteners, and it forbids the state from limiting the barrier to one method or material. Tile owners get a specific answer in the Existing Building Code: clay and concrete tile roof systems installed as the Florida Building Code requires are deemed to comply with the secondary water barrier provision. That is not a loophole to skip the layer, it is a statement that a correctly built tile assembly already carries one, which is why the underlayment specification on your scope is doing double duty. Separately, in the windborne debris region a building with an insured value or just valuation of $300,000 or more must incorporate roof-to-wall connection techniques at replacement, with the obligation capped so evaluation and installation stay near 15 percent of the cost of reroofing.</p>
<h2>The inspection sequence sets your calendar</h2>
<p>Inside the city, roof work in excess of 500 square feet requires a permit, a dry-in inspection is required on tile before tile is installed, and every single-family residence built before March 1, 2002 is directed to the hurricane mitigation retrofit requirements. Homeowners doing their own work may satisfy that by calling a sheathing inspection after mitigation and before the covering goes on, or by having a licensed professional complete the Roof Mitigation Verification Affidavit. In the county, the permit application itself carries an attestation under code section B110, signed under penalty of perjury, and the final roof inspection follows either the Progress Check or an uploaded Roof Mitigation Verification Statement. None of this is optional and all of it takes calendar days, which is why a re-roof is scheduled around weather windows.</p>
<h2>Saving your tile, and what drives the number</h2>
<p>Concrete field tile from the subdivision era is often worth salvaging; clay barrel from the pre-war districts is brittle, frequently out of production, and breaks at a higher rate coming off. Expect a stated breakage allowance and expect the crew to inventory and stack rather than sweep. Cost is driven by squares, roof complexity, height and access, the underlayment specification, deck repairs found after the strip, retrofit work triggered by the build date, and how much new tile has to be bought. Insist that the estimate separate the tile from the membrane and name the measured area, because that measurement is what a future partial repair will be judged against. Read <a href="../roof-underlayment-replacement/">roof underlayment replacement</a> for the one-slope version of this work, and <a href="../secondary-water-barrier-installation/">secondary water barrier installation</a> for the layer the statute is aimed at.</p>

## svc_tile_roof_inspection_lede

An inspection on a Tampa tile roof has one job that matters more than the rest: establishing the condition of the underlayment, because that is the layer with the shortest remaining life and the one nobody can see from the ground.

## svc_tile_roof_inspection_body

<h2>What is being inspected, and it is not the tile</h2>
<p>A walk that reports cracked and slipped tile has described the shield, not the waterproofing. Florida guidance puts tile life at 50 to 100 years and the membrane beneath it at 20 to 25, and reports that older asphalt-saturated felt under dark tile can fail in as little as 10 to 15 years because heat drives the asphalt compounds out of the mat until it is brittle. Under dark tile the deck surface can pass 150 degrees on a summer afternoon. So a useful inspection lifts tile in chosen places, at the suspected leak, at the highest suspect course, and at a sound control spot, so the felt can be compared against itself. Everything else on <a href="../services/">our services list</a> starts from that comparison.</p>
<h2>The checklist worth insisting on</h2>
<p>Ask for findings against specific code items rather than adjectives. Valley pans should be corrosion-resistant metal of at least 0.019 inch, extending at least eleven inches from the centerline each way, with a one inch splash diverter rib and four inch end laps. Tile fasteners should be corrosion resistant, at least 11 gage with a five sixteenths inch head, penetrating the deck at least three quarters of an inch or through it. Perimeter fastening areas should be treated as three courses and at least 36 inches from either side of hips and ridges and from eave and rake edges. Hip and ridge trim should sit in a continuous, full bed rather than a pointed edge. Underlayment should be identified by specification, since where felt is used the state requires 30 pound or equivalent, meaning ASTM D226 Type II or ASTM D4869 Type III or IV.</p>
<h2>The findings that change what a repair costs</h2>
<p>Four things in an inspection report move a scope more than anything else. Whether the felt is pliable or crumbling. Whether the tile profile is still in production, which decides whether replacements are ordered or hunted through salvage. Whether the deck is sound, because a soft deck converts a covering job into a structural one. And the measured area of everything that needs work, because Florida's Existing Building Code limits repair, replacement and recovery to a quarter of a roof area or roof section within any twelve-month window before the whole section must come up to current code, with an exception where the section's last permitted work complied with the 2007 code or a later edition.</p>
<h2>Who is allowed to sign what</h2>
<p>Florida statute lists who counts as an authorized inspector for roof-age purposes: a licensed home inspector, a certified building code inspector, a general, building or residential contractor, a roofing contractor, a licensed professional engineer, a licensed architect, or another person the insurer recognizes as qualified. That list exists because the statute gives an inspection real weight. An insurer may not refuse to issue or renew a homeowner's policy on a house with a roof under 15 years old solely because of roof age. At 15 years or more, the insurer must allow an inspection at the homeowner's expense before requiring replacement as a condition of coverage, and may not refuse solely on roof age where that inspection shows five or more years of useful life remaining. Those provisions apply to policies issued or renewed on or after July 1, 2022. Nothing here promises any particular decision from any particular carrier, and any inspection sold that way should be treated with suspicion.</p>
<h2>How the paperwork gets used afterward</h2>
<p>Roof age is calculated from the last date on which 100 percent of the surface area was built or replaced, or from the initial date of a partial replacement where later partials added up to the whole. Spot repairs do not reset it. Citizens Property Insurance, which updated its roof rules to comply with Senate Bill 2-D effective May 1, 2023 for new business and September 1, 2023 for renewals, sets its documentation threshold at 50 years for tile, slate, concrete or metal roofs and 25 years for shingle, takes remaining useful life from a 4-Point Inspection Form or a Roof Inspection Form with a maximum of five years allowed, bases age on the oldest part where the roof was not replaced at once, and requires a finalized roof permit or a paid-in-full contract, work order or receipt to record a replacement. The form outlasts the visit, so it is worth having filled out carefully.</p>
<h2>What the visit looks like and what it costs</h2>
<p>Expect an exterior walk of the field, hips, ridges, rakes, valleys and every penetration; a lift at selected courses to read felt, battens and fasteners; an attic look where one is accessible, reading sheathing and framing for staining and travel direction; photographs of each finding; and a measured sketch with areas. Cost follows roof size, pitch, height, access and whether tile is being lifted, which it should be. An inspection that never lifted a tile did not inspect the roof.
