## symptom_1_title

You are moving across Raleigh, but the two homes do not match

## symptom_1

A local move sounds simple until the origin and destination ask for different kinds of work. A one-story house in Five Points may give the truck a driveway and a short path to the door. A delivery to a newer apartment near Glenwood South can mean a loading area, a freight elevator reservation, a service entrance, and a strict time slot. The distance between addresses is only one part of the job. The number of rooms, stairs, doors, long carries, parking restrictions, and furniture pieces decide how much crew time and equipment belong on the plan.

Start by separating what must happen before the truck arrives from what can happen on moving day. Beds that need disassembly, wall-mounted items, fragile lamps, loose shelves, and boxes that are still open slow down loading. Decide where each large piece will go at the new home before it is wrapped. That prevents a heavy dresser from being carried upstairs, brought back down, and carried somewhere else after the room layout changes.

On site, the crew should walk both access paths before loading begins. They check doorway clearance, protect floors and corners where needed, wrap furniture, and load the pieces needed first at the destination in a sensible order. The price should be settled from the confirmed scope before work starts. If the walk-through exposes a real difference from the inventory — an unmentioned third-floor walk-up or a storage unit packed to the ceiling — the change should be explained before the work continues. A careful local move is not about racing across town. It is about making each carry once, protecting the home, and arriving with a plan for where everything lands.

## symptom_2_title

Your move leaves North Carolina, and the timeline is less forgiving

## symptom_2

A long-distance move has more handoffs to control than a move from one Raleigh neighborhood to another. The practical questions start with the dates: when the home must be empty, when the destination is available, and whether the delivery window leaves room for a delay. Then comes the inventory. A general room count is not enough for a move that will be loaded, secured, and carried a long way. Large furniture, unusually fragile pieces, boxes going to storage, and anything that needs special handling should be identified before the load plan is set.

Packing matters more here because the boxes and furniture will spend longer in transit. Use sturdy, closed cartons; keep dense items such as books in smaller boxes; cushion breakables; and label every carton with both its contents and destination room. Keep medication, documents, keys, chargers, and the items needed for the first night with you rather than inside the truck. Take photos of electronics before cables are disconnected and empty drawers that could shift in transit.

The crew should explain the written scope, pickup preparation, delivery plan, and any access limitation at either address before the work begins. Do not treat state rules as a substitute for planning: North Carolina’s Utilities Commission regulates household-goods moves within the state, while a move crossing a state line has a different operating framework. In either case, the useful standard is the same: the mover should be identifiable, the paperwork should match the work, and the plan should not depend on a vague promise that details will be sorted out later. A long move goes better when the pickup is ready, the inventory is honest, and the delivery address is confirmed before the truck is loaded.

## symptom_3_title

Your apartment move is controlled by the building, not just your lease date

## symptom_3

Apartment moves in Raleigh often succeed or fail at the entrance. Before choosing a start time, ask the property for its moving rules in writing. Find out which door the crew must use, whether there is a freight elevator, how long it can be reserved, where the truck may stand, what certificate or building paperwork the manager needs, and whether mats or wall protection are required. A move into a mid-rise in North Hills is not the same as a third-floor walk-up off Hillsborough Street. The same sofa can take ten minutes in one building and an hour in another when the elevator is busy or the carry route is long.

Parking needs its own answer. Raleigh’s Right-of-Way Services says moving-van parking passes can be obtained directly from Raleigh Parking for timed or paid spaces. That does not mean every ordinary move needs a right-of-way permit, but it does mean the curb plan should be settled before moving day. Do not assume a truck can block a drive aisle, a sidewalk, or a loading area simply because the building is close to the street. Confirm the space, the time, and the building contact.

Pack the apartment around the move, not around the room you are sitting in. Label boxes by the new room, tape hardware to the furniture it belongs to, and set aside a small bag with keys, lease papers, chargers, cleaning supplies, and basic tools. The crew can wrap, carry, and place the household goods; they cannot make an unscheduled elevator appear. Give them the accurate access details early, and the job can be staffed and scheduled to fit the building instead of becoming a rushed carry through a lobby full of residents.

## symptom_4_title

You need packing help because the move date is close

## symptom_4

Packing is not just filling boxes. It is the part of a move that keeps glass, electronics, artwork, and furniture hardware from becoming a pile of loose parts. When the move date is close, start with the rooms and pieces that require the most care: kitchens, bookcases, framed art, lamps, electronics, and closets. Work one room at a time. Pack dense items in small cartons, leave no empty space around breakables, and label the top and two sides of every box with the destination room. A box marked only “miscellaneous” is hard to place and even harder to find after the truck is unloaded.

A packing crew can bring the materials, wrap fragile goods, protect furniture, and build a load-ready inventory before moving day. That is useful when work, travel, a closing date, or an apartment handoff has compressed the calendar. It is also useful for the rooms people underestimate. Kitchens have sharp, heavy, breakable items. Closets hide bags and loose belongings that are awkward to load. Home offices hold cables and records that should not be mixed into random cartons.

Set aside anything that travels with you: identification, financial and medical documents, medication, heirlooms, jewelry, house keys, and the first-night essentials. Remove batteries where appropriate, empty and defrost appliances on the schedule the mover gives you, and do not pack hazardous, perishable, or prohibited items into ordinary household boxes. Before the crew leaves, read the labels, verify which items are going to storage or another address, and point out the pieces that need extra attention. Good packing makes moving day quieter and faster because the crew is lifting sealed, labeled cartons instead of trying to stabilize a home that is still half-open.

## pricing_lede

North Carolina is one of the few states that publishes a legal ceiling on what an intrastate household move may cost by the hour. This page puts that tariff next to what Raleigh and Wake County companies actually post, so both numbers are visible at once. Nothing here is a quote.

## pricing_body

### A move inside this state has a legal price ceiling

A household move that starts and ends in North Carolina is regulated transportation, not an unregulated service. Carriers hold a certificate from the North Carolina Utilities Commission Maximum Rate Tariff No. 2 regime, and the tariff itself sets maximum charges rather than suggested ones. The current edition was issued December 30, 2025 and took effect January 1, 2026.

For a move of fifty miles or less the tariff allows hourly billing, and Rule 53 sets the ceilings: $188.40 an hour for a vehicle and two crew, $243.30 with three, $297.90 with four and $352.95 with five, each additional crew member $54.80 and each additional vehicle $52.50. Outside 8 a.m. to 5 p.m. Monday through Friday the same crews cap at $235.15, $310.25, $385.15 and $460.10. Compare those against the table above and the useful conclusion appears immediately: posted Triangle rates for two movers run roughly seventy to a hundred percent of the legal maximum, so the ceiling is real but the market is competing below it.

### How the clock is defined, which matters more than the rate

The tariff does not leave billable time to interpretation. Time runs from the crew's arrival at the origin address through completion at the destination, rounded to the quarter hour, and travel between the two addresses is chargeable at up to one hour per fifty miles. That is a materially different structure from California's doubled driving time or Florida's shop-to-shop practice, and it is why a Raleigh crew moving a household from Cary to North Raleigh cannot inflate a bill with mileage games.

Beyond the hours, the tariff prices the things people forget. Carrying a piano of thirty-eight inches or taller caps at $111.90, a slate pool table at $128.25 and a laden freezer at $93.30. Carrying goods up or down the first flight of stairs inside a building caps at $33.55, each additional flight $16.85. An elevator caps at $3.10 per hundred pounds and stairs at $2.20 per hundred pounds. Full value protection is a dollar per hundred dollars of declared value on a single truck, with a minimum valuation of five dollars a pound.

### Why weight-based quoting shows up here

Some Raleigh carriers decline to post an hourly figure at all, because the tariff also permits distance-and-weight pricing, and for longer intrastate hauls that is the structure they use. One local nonprofit carrier states outright that North Carolina pricing is based on weight and distance rather than an hourly rate. Neither method is a trick. What matters is knowing which one is being applied before load day, because the same household can be quoted two very different ways.

### Labor, and where the money actually goes

Freight and material movers in the Raleigh-Cary metro had a median wage of $18.50 an hour in May 2025 across 17,060 workers, per the BLS Occupational Employment and Wage Statistics, May 2025 release. Two crew at that median is $37 of a $190 hourly charge. The remainder is the truck, insurance the Commission requires, fuel, equipment, dispatch and unbilled travel. The tariff ceiling exists because that spread is where overcharging used to hide.

### Reading an estimate here

Ask for the tariff basis in writing: hourly under Section II or weight and distance. Confirm crew size, the regular-versus-overtime rate that applies to the scheduled day, the billing minimum, the travel fee and whether it is per truck, the per-mile charge outside Wake County, and the valuation election. A Commission certificate number should be on the paperwork. Downtown jobs need the curb settled in advance, since Raleigh Right-of-Way Services directs moving-van parking passes through Raleigh Parking and a paid space needs the approved pass displayed.

### The trap

The one to watch is the overtime boundary. A job scheduled to start at 3 p.m. on a Friday, or a Saturday move, is billed at the higher tariff band for every hour, and a crew that arrives late pushes ordinary hours into that band. The second is the three-hour minimum most companies apply, which makes a genuinely small move cost more per box than a large one. The third is August. NC State housing runs fall move-in on assigned appointment slots, and the whole market tightens around it.

### What a posted total leaves out

Published job figures assume a boxed household with ground-floor or elevator access. They exclude packing labor and materials, mattress and television cartons, floor protection, disassembly, appliance servicing, storage between closings, second stops, out-of-county mileage, and the specialty items the tariff prices separately.
