## pricing_lede

North Carolina is one of the few states that publishes a legal ceiling on what an intrastate household move may cost by the hour. This page puts that tariff next to what Raleigh and Wake County companies actually post, so both numbers are visible at once. Nothing here is a quote.

## pricing_body

### A move inside this state has a legal price ceiling

A household move that starts and ends in North Carolina is regulated transportation. Carriers hold a certificate from the North Carolina Utilities Commission and bill under its Maximum Rate Tariff No. 2, which sets maximum charges rather than suggested ones. The current edition was issued December 30, 2025 and took effect January 1, 2026.

For a move of fifty miles or less the tariff allows hourly billing, and Rule 53 sets the ceilings: $188.40 an hour for a vehicle and two crew, $243.30 with three, $297.90 with four and $352.95 with five, each additional crew member $54.80 and each additional vehicle $52.50. Outside 8 a.m. to 5 p.m. Monday through Friday the same crews cap at $235.15, $310.25, $385.15 and $460.10. Set those beside the table above and the conclusion appears immediately: posted Triangle rates for two movers run roughly seventy to a hundred percent of the legal maximum, so the ceiling is real but the market competes below it.

### How the clock is defined, which matters more than the rate

Billable time is defined rather than left to interpretation. It runs from the crew's arrival at the origin address through completion at the destination, rounded to the quarter hour, and travel between the two addresses is chargeable at up to one hour per fifty miles. A crew bringing a household from Cary to North Raleigh therefore cannot inflate a bill with mileage games.

Beyond the hours, the tariff prices the things people forget. Carrying a piano of thirty-eight inches or taller caps at $111.90, a slate pool table at $128.25 and a laden freezer at $93.30. Carrying goods up or down the first flight of stairs inside a building caps at $33.55, each additional flight $16.85. An elevator caps at $3.10 per hundred pounds and stairs at $2.20 per hundred pounds. Full value protection is a dollar per hundred dollars of declared value on a single truck, with a minimum valuation of five dollars a pound.

### Why weight-based quoting shows up here

Some carriers here decline to post an hourly figure at all, because the tariff also permits distance-and-weight pricing, and that is the structure required above fifty miles. One local nonprofit carrier states outright that its pricing rests on weight and distance rather than an hourly rate. Neither method is a trick, but the same household can be quoted two very different ways, so establish which one applies before load day.

### Labor, and where the money actually goes

Freight and material movers in the Raleigh-Cary metro had a median wage of $18.50 an hour in May 2025 across 17,060 workers, per the BLS Occupational Employment and Wage Statistics release. Two crew at that median is $37 of a $190 hourly charge. The rest is the truck, the insurance the Commission requires, fuel, equipment, dispatch and unbilled travel.

### Reading an estimate here

Ask for the tariff basis in writing: hourly under Section II or weight and distance. Confirm crew size, the regular-versus-overtime rate that applies to the scheduled day, and the billing minimum. Then pin down the travel fee and whether it is charged per truck, the per-mile charge outside Wake County, and the valuation election. A Commission certificate number should be on the paperwork. Downtown jobs need the curb settled in advance, since Raleigh Right-of-Way Services directs moving-van parking passes through Raleigh Parking and a paid space needs the approved pass displayed.

### The trap

Watch the overtime boundary first. A Saturday move, or a job starting at 3 p.m. on a Friday, is billed in the higher tariff band for every hour, and a late crew pushes ordinary hours into it. Second is the three-hour minimum most companies apply, which makes a genuinely small move cost more per box than a large one. Third is August, when NC State housing runs fall move-in on assigned appointment slots and the whole market tightens.

### What a posted total leaves out

Published job figures assume a boxed household with ground-floor or elevator access. They exclude packing labor and materials, mattress and television cartons, floor protection, disassembly, appliance servicing, storage between closings, second stops, out-of-county mileage, and the specialty items the tariff prices separately.

## svc_local_moving_lede

Almost every move inside Wake County falls under a state rate ceiling most residents have never heard of. At fifty miles or less the clock decides the bill rather than the mileage, so how that clock is defined matters more than the advertised rate.

## svc_local_moving_body

### The state sets a ceiling and the mover needs a certificate

A household move that begins and ends in North Carolina is regulated transportation. Under General Statute 62-262(a), no person may transport household goods in intrastate commerce without a certificate obtained from the Utilities Commission, and the Commission runs a criminal history record check on applicants and current holders under 62-273.1. Rates come from Maximum Rate Tariff No. 2, whose current edition was issued December 30, 2025 and took effect January 1, 2026. Rule 2 makes those figures maximums, not suggestions, so a carrier may quote below them but never above. Claiming to hold a certificate without one is a Class 3 misdemeanor carrying a fine up to five hundred dollars for a first offense, plus a civil penalty the Commission may set as high as five thousand dollars.

### Fifty miles is the line between two pricing systems

Rule 17 restricts hourly billing to a ratemaking distance of fifty miles or less, which covers essentially any move between Raleigh addresses. Rule 53 then caps the hourly charge: $188.40 for a vehicle and two crew during regular hours, $243.30 with three and $297.90 with four, with $54.80 for each additional crew member. Regular hours mean 8 a.m. to 5 p.m. on weekdays, excluding legal holidays, and everything outside that window shifts to the overtime band, where two crew cap at $235.15. Time runs from the moment vehicle and crew reach the origin until all services finish at the destination, and Rule 18 rounds to the quarter hour. The tariff's own illustration is useful: six hours and ten minutes bills as 6.25 hours, while eight hours and fifty minutes bills as nine.

### The one rule worth memorizing before you sign

The ordinary written estimate is governed by Rule 13, which says the final billed charges shall not exceed 120 percent of the estimated amount. Going past that ceiling requires a change order or addendum signed by the shipper before the move starts, or at the point the shipper asks for extra service. Two exceptions matter. The protection does not apply when the estimate is requested fewer than five business days before the move, or when the shipment weighs under a thousand pounds. Booking a week ahead is therefore a consumer protection decision, not just a scheduling one. Binding not-to-exceed estimates were retired from the tariff effective January 1, 2026, so the practical choice now is a nonbinding estimate with the percentage cap or a binding guaranteed price.

### What your belongings are worth on paper

Coverage defaults to sixty cents per pound for the damaged article under Rule 7, and the tariff illustrates that with a fifty-pound, sixty-five-inch television whose recoverable value is thirty dollars no matter what the receipt said. Full value protection is priced separately at one dollar per hundred dollars of declared value on a single truck, with a minimum valuation of five dollars a pound. One clause rewards attention: if the carrier fails to obtain your signature in the valuation section, the shipment is deemed released to coverage of up to fifteen thousand dollars at no extra charge. Articles or matched sets worth five thousand dollars or more sit outside full value protection unless you disclose them in writing.

### Older Raleigh blocks change the crew plan

The access facts, not the map distance, drive the hours. A frame house in Oakwood or Mordecai may have no driveway, a deep front walk and a narrow street shared with parked cars. Boylan Heights adds grade. Five Points and Hayes Barton bring mature canopy and tight turns. A mid-rise off North Hills replaces all of that with a loading area and a reserved elevator. Occupying the street, the sidewalk or the sidewalk shoulder requires a right-of-way permit from the city, while a pass for a moving van in a timed or paid space comes directly from Raleigh Parking and is picked up at 310 W. Martin Street. Whichever applies, the pass has to stay visible the whole time the space is held.

### Heat and storm days belong in the schedule

National Weather Service normals for Raleigh-Durham put the average daily high at ninety degrees or above from July 2 through July 27, topping out near 90.4 degrees in mid-July. That argues for an early start, shaded staging and real water breaks rather than a heroic afternoon. September brings the other problem. When Tropical Storm Ophelia crossed on September 22 and 23, 2023, a gauge four and a half miles northwest of the city recorded 3.66 inches, and the Lake Wheeler Road field lab measured 3.53 inches. Floor protection and a plan to pause a carry are worth agreeing on in advance.

### Paperwork at the door

Rule 28 requires the bill of lading to carry the carrier's own name rather than an agent's, its Commission certificate number, a preferred delivery date or window and a Commission telephone number for questions. If the document is electronic, the screen has to measure at least seven inches diagonally. Statute 62-271 lets a carrier hold the goods until tariff charges are paid, and Rule 11 adds a one percent monthly finance charge on balances past thirty days. A card processing fee may be passed along only up to what the processor actually charges the carrier, and it has to appear on the bill of lading.

## svc_long_distance_moving_lede

Cross the fifty-mile mark and North Carolina stops billing by the hour. Cross the state line and the tariff stops applying altogether, replaced by federal rules with a different overage limit, a survey requirement and its own claim clocks.

## svc_long_distance_moving_body

### Two rulebooks, and the distance decides which

A long haul that stays inside the state leaves hourly billing behind. Rule 17 sends any move over fifty miles to the weight-and-distance schedule, quoted in dollars per hundred pounds, which is why some carriers will not publish an hourly figure at all. Where no adequate scale is available, the Commission allows constructive weight of seven pounds per cubic foot, so the cube of the load becomes the billing basis. A move that ends in Virginia, Tennessee or anywhere else outside North Carolina is interstate, and the Utilities Commission has no jurisdiction over it. Federal Motor Carrier Safety Administration regulations in 49 CFR Parts 370 through 375 take over instead, and the difference is not cosmetic.

### The federal number is 110 percent, not 120

Section 375.405 requires a nonbinding estimate to state plainly that the shipper will not be required to pay more than 110 percent of it at delivery. Section 375.407 then obliges the carrier to hand over the shipment once that amount, plus separately itemized charges for services requested after the paperwork was issued, has been paid. Charges for impracticable operations collectible at delivery are capped at fifteen percent of the other charges due, and anything else waits at least thirty days before it can be billed. Section 375.103 puts a name on the abuse this prevents: deliberately holding a load hostage after the shipper offers the binding amount or 110 percent of a nonbinding one violates the reasonable dispatch requirement. Note the ten-point gap between the federal ceiling and the state's 120 percent figure, because a household that moves out of state loses one protection and gains another.

### The survey and the booklet are requirements

Under 375.401 the carrier has to conduct a physical survey of the household goods, either on site or by live or recorded video, and base its written estimate on that survey. A shipper may waive the survey, but the waiver has to be in writing. Section 375.213 requires the mover to furnish two federal publications before the move: the tips brochure titled Ready to Move? and the contents of the rights and responsibilities booklet, either as copies or as working website links. When printed, that booklet's cover has to carry the required sentence in twelve-point or larger bold type. Section 375.207 rounds it out by requiring every advertisement to show the carrier's assigned name and its federal transportation number.

### Check the number before you sign anything

For an interstate mover, the federal household goods search tool lets you look up a company by name or list every registered mover in a state, which is where the operating authority and complaint history live. For a move that stays in North Carolina, the Commission publishes a list of carriers holding a certificate of exemption and refreshes it monthly. That refresh interval is the catch: a certificate cancelled or newly issued since the last update will not appear, so a phone call to confirm current standing beats a screenshot. The Public Staff transportation rates division, reachable at 919-880-1837, handles unresolved claims and reports of movers operating without a certificate.

### Claims run on two different clocks

Federal rules give a carrier thirty days from receipt of a written claim to acknowledge it in writing under 370.5, then 120 days to pay, decline or make a firm written compromise offer under 370.9. If it still cannot resolve the matter, written status updates are owed every sixty days after that. The state tariff is tighter at the front end and shorter overall. Rule 52 requires acknowledgment with claim procedures no later than fifteen days after a written claim arrives, and final resolution within sixty. The filing window is generous either way: nine months from the point a reasonable time for delivery has passed. Should a claim be denied in writing, suit must follow within two years and one day. Rule 51 also bars a carrier from collecting freight charges on a shipment totally lost or destroyed in transit.

### Valuation matters more over distance

Released value under federal rules is sixty cents per pound per article, and the rights booklet illustrates the arithmetic with a ten-pound stereo component worth a thousand dollars that recovers six dollars. Anything worth more than a hundred dollars a pound counts as extraordinary value and has to be listed, or liability can be limited to that hundred-dollar-per-pound figure. North Carolina adds a wrinkle few states recognize: ready-to-assemble particle-board furniture is capped at sixty cents a pound or fifty dollars per article, whichever is greater, regardless of the coverage chosen. Storage in transit stretches the exposure further, running up to 180 days in thirty-day increments, with full value protection billed at fifteen percent of its rate per period.

### Why so many Wake County households are doing this

The volume is not imagined. Census Bureau estimates put Wake County at 1,257,235 residents on July 1, 2025, an increase of 27,760 in one year and the fifth largest numeric gain of any county in the country. The Raleigh-Cary metro reached 1,595,720, up 2.4 percent and tenth nationally by rate. State demographers counted 22,574 net migrants into Wake in 2023 and 2024, about 9,870 of them arriving from elsewhere in the United States. Peak weeks fill quickly, and a mover with a truck already committed cannot invent capacity in July.

## svc_apartment_moving_lede

An apartment move answers to the building first. Elevator windows, the door a crew is allowed to use, the distance from truck to unit and downtown meter hours can all outrank the date printed on your lease.

## svc_apartment_moving_body

### The building sets the hour, the tariff sets the charge

Vertical access is priced, not absorbed. On weight-and-distance moves the tariff allows $3.10 per hundred pounds for elevator use at each end, and $2.20 per hundred pounds for each flight of stairs, inside or outside, at each end. On an hourly job the same obstacles appear as clock time instead. The tariff defines a flight as at least eight steps inside a building and treats an elevator as one flight. It caps the inside flight carry at $33.55 for the first flight and $16.85 for each one after, with an outside first flight at $32.15 and sixty cents per step beyond the counted range. None of this is negotiable in the abstract, which is exactly why the elevator reservation belongs in the plan before a start time is chosen.

### Long carries have a legal definition

Distance from the truck to the door is measured, not estimated. The tariff treats the first seventy-five feet between vehicle and residence entrance as included, then charges an extra carry for each additional fifty feet or fraction of it at $2.20 per hundred pounds. In practice that turns a garden-style complex where the truck stops at a shared bay into a materially different job from a unit twenty feet off a service door. Walk the actual route and count it before booking. A crew that knows about a two-hundred-foot push across a courtyard brings dollies and a second body for it.

### Downtown curb hours are not what most people assume

Payment at Raleigh meters is required from 8 a.m. to 6 p.m. Monday through Friday in downtown districts including Glenwood South, and from 8 a.m. to 8 p.m. in the Hillsborough district near the university. Weekends are free. Enforcement, though, runs twenty-four hours every day, so a bagged or restricted space stays restricted at 7 a.m. on a Sunday. A meter or time-zone violation costs twenty dollars, doubling with a late fee if it sits unpaid for twenty-one days. Parking passes for moving vans come from Raleigh Parking at 310 W. Martin Street, Suite 109, and construction-style permits are priced by the number of days requested times the daily rate of each space held.

### Permit blocks and the older streets

Several neighborhoods near downtown and the university run residential permit programs, where a permit costs thirty dollars per cycle, guest passes cover a single twenty-four-hour period and restrictions commonly apply from 8 a.m. to 5 p.m. on weekdays. Restrictions differ block by block, so the answer for one street tells you nothing about the next. Occupying a lane, a sidewalk or the sidewalk shoulder is a separate matter that requires a right-of-way permit through the city's development portal, along with a traffic control plan when a lane or the full street is involved. Most apartment moves need none of that. The ones that do are usually the ones nobody checked.

### A very small shipment loses the estimate cap

The state's 120 percent ceiling on billed charges above an estimate carries two exceptions that catch studio and one-bedroom moves in particular. It does not apply when the shipment weighs less than a thousand pounds, and it does not apply when the estimate is requested fewer than five business days before the move. A lightly furnished apartment can easily sit under that weight threshold. If yours might, ask for a binding guaranteed price instead of relying on a percentage cap that will not cover you, and book far enough ahead that the five-day exception never comes into play.

### Campus move-in runs on assigned slots

University housing does not open its doors to whoever arrives first. NC State's fall 2026 residence hall move-in runs August 13 through 15 with residents selecting check-in appointments, which fixes both the arrival window and how long a vehicle can sit at the curb. That single week also tightens truck availability across the market, since off-campus leases in the same corridor turn over alongside it. Confirm whether the destination is university housing or a private building, then hand the crew the appointment time rather than a vague morning.

### Flat-pack furniture and what a claim can return

Apartments hold a lot of ready-to-assemble furniture, and North Carolina prices that reality honestly. Liability on particle-board and knock-down pieces is capped at sixty cents a pound or fifty dollars per article, whichever is greater, whatever coverage was elected. The general default is the same sixty cents per pound the tariff applies to any released shipment. Full value protection changes the arithmetic, and a carrier that never obtained a signature in the valuation section leaves the shipment deemed released to coverage of up to fifteen thousand dollars at no charge. Photograph assembled pieces before disassembly, and keep hardware bagged and taped to the frame it came from.

## svc_packing_services_lede

Packing is priced apart from the move itself, container by container, and the state tariff publishes a ceiling for each carton and each hour of packing labor. Who packs a box also changes what a claim looks like months later.

## svc_packing_services_body

### Packing carries its own published price list

Item 1 of the tariff sets maximum charges for packing and unpacking, and each figure covers the labor, the container and the materials together. A dish pack for kitchen glassware caps at $74.05. A three-cubic-foot carton caps at $22.25, a four-and-a-half at $26.65 and a six at $26.80. A wardrobe carton runs to $33.10, a king or queen mattress carton to $47.95, and a corrugated container for mirrors, glass or marble to $47.60. Custom crating is priced by volume at $26.95 per cubic foot with a minimum of $107.85 per crate. Because these are ceilings rather than list prices, an itemized packing estimate can be checked line by line against the published schedule.

### Buying the boxes and packing them yourself

Item 3 covers containers supplied without labor, and the gap between the two schedules is the real decision. The same three-cubic-foot carton is $7.05 empty, the four-and-a-half is $8.30 and the six is $9.45. A dish pack is $17.35, a wardrobe $22.50 and a king or queen mattress carton $26.40. Flat-screen television boxes are $66.50 under forty inches and $82.50 at forty inches or more. Unprinted newsprint is $1.90 a pound and two-inch plastic tape $7.20 a roll. Splitting the work is legitimate and common: hand over the kitchen, the artwork and the electronics, and box your own closets and linens.

### Packing on move day bills differently than packing ahead

Timing changes the rate structure. When packing happens incidentally during an hourly move, the tariff bills it at the hourly crew rate plus container charges from Item 3 rather than at the full packing rates. Packing performed on a separate earlier day falls under Item 1, or under Item 2 when it happens before 8 a.m., after 5 p.m., or at any hour on a weekend or holiday. On weight-and-distance moves there is a straight labor line as well: $41.85 per crew member per hour during regular hours and $51.40 outside them. Ask which of those applies to your scheduled day, because a Saturday pack and a Tuesday pack are not the same transaction.

### Who packed the carton matters when something breaks

A carton packed by the carrier and a carton packed by the household sit differently in a claim file. Owner-packed boxes invite an argument about whether the contents were adequately protected, so the more fragile the item, the stronger the case for professional packing and a written notation of it on the inventory. Either way the filing window is nine months from the point a reasonable time for delivery has passed. On an intrastate move the carrier owes an acknowledgment with claim procedures within fifteen days and a resolution within sixty. Photograph the inside of any box you pack yourself.

### Three separate caps on what a claim can return

Recovery is bounded three different ways, and none of them tracks what you paid at retail. The unpriced default is sixty cents per pound for the damaged article, which the tariff illustrates with a fifty-pound television valued at thirty dollars. Anything worth more than a hundred dollars a pound is an article of extraordinary value and has to be listed in writing, or liability can be held to that per-pound figure. Ready-to-assemble particle-board furniture is capped at sixty cents a pound or fifty dollars per article, whichever is greater. Full value protection changes the calculation but excludes single articles or matched sets worth five thousand dollars or more unless they are disclosed.

### What does not belong in an ordinary carton

Some categories never go on a household truck: fuels, propane, aerosols, ammunition, corrosives, paint thinner and anything else flammable or explosive. Perishable food, open liquids and live plants create their own problems on a summer haul. Appliances need servicing rather than packing, and a laden home freezer is handled as a specialty item at a capped charge of $93.30. Keep identification, medication, financial and medical records, keys, jewelry and small heirlooms with you in your own vehicle. Set aside a labeled first-night carton with bedding, tools, chargers and cleaning supplies so nobody has to open six boxes at midnight.

### Sequencing a pack in a Raleigh summer

Order the work backward from the load. Storage rooms, books, artwork and out-of-season clothing come first, then the rooms you can live without, with the kitchen and bathrooms last. Reserve the coolest hours for the heaviest carries, since normal daily highs at Raleigh-Durham sit at or above ninety degrees for most of July. Label the top and two sides of every carton with its destination room, and mark fragile loads on the side that will face out on the truck. A crew arriving to sealed, labeled cartons loads faster and touches your belongings less.
