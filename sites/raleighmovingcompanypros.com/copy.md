## pricing_lede

North Carolina is one of the few states that publishes a legal ceiling on what an intrastate household move may cost by the hour. This page puts that tariff next to what Raleigh and Wake County companies actually post, so both numbers are visible at once. Nothing here is a quote.

## pricing_body

### A move inside this state has a legal price ceiling

A household move that starts and ends in North Carolina is regulated transportation. Carriers hold a certificate from the North Carolina Utilities Commission and bill under its Maximum Rate Tariff No. 2, which sets maximum charges rather than suggested ones. The current edition was issued December 30, 2025 and took effect January 1, 2026.

For a move of fifty miles or less the tariff allows hourly billing, and Rule 53 sets the ceilings: $188.40 an hour for a vehicle and two crew, $243.30 with three, $297.90 with four and $352.95 with five, each additional crew member $54.80 and each additional vehicle $52.50. Outside 8 a.m. to 5 p.m. Monday through Friday the same crews cap at $235.15, $310.25, $385.15 and $460.10. Set those beside the table above and the conclusion appears immediately: posted Triangle rates for two movers run roughly seventy to a hundred percent of the legal maximum, so the ceiling is real but the market competes below it.

### How the clock is defined, which matters more than the rate

Billable time is defined rather than left to interpretation. It runs from the crew's arrival at the origin address through completion at the destination, rounded to the quarter hour, and travel between the two addresses is chargeable at up to one hour per fifty miles. A crew bringing a household from Cary to North Raleigh therefore cannot inflate a bill with mileage games.

Beyond the hours, the tariff prices the things people forget. Carrying a piano of thirty-eight inches or taller caps at $111.90, a slate pool table at $128.25 and a laden freezer at $93.30. Carrying goods up or down the first flight of stairs inside a building caps at $33.55, each additional flight $16.85. An elevator caps at $3.10 per hundred pounds and stairs at $2.20 per hundred pounds. Full value protection is a dollar per hundred dollars of declared value on a single truck, with a minimum valuation of five dollars a pound.

### Why weight-based quoting shows up here

Some carriers here decline to post an hourly figure at all, because the tariff also permits distance-and-weight pricing, and that is the structure required above fifty miles. One local nonprofit carrier states outright that its pricing rests on weight and distance rather than an hourly rate. Neither method is a trick, but the same household can be quoted two very different ways, so establish which one applies before load day.

### Labor, and where the money actually goes

Freight and material movers in the Raleigh-Cary metro had a median wage of $18.50 an hour in May 2025 across 17,060 workers, per the BLS Occupational Employment and Wage Statistics release. Two crew at that median is $37 of a $190 hourly charge. The rest is the truck, the insurance the Commission requires, fuel, equipment, dispatch and unbilled travel.

### Reading an estimate here

Ask for the tariff basis in writing: hourly under Section II or weight and distance. Confirm crew size, the regular-versus-overtime rate that applies to the scheduled day, and the billing minimum. Then pin down the travel fee and whether it is charged per truck, the per-mile charge outside Wake County, and the valuation election. A Commission certificate number should be on the paperwork. Downtown jobs need the curb settled in advance, since Raleigh Right-of-Way Services directs moving-van parking passes through Raleigh Parking and a paid space needs the approved pass displayed.

### The trap

Watch the overtime boundary first. A Saturday move, or a job starting at 3 p.m. on a Friday, is billed in the higher tariff band for every hour, and a late crew pushes ordinary hours into it. Second is the three-hour minimum most companies apply, which makes a genuinely small move cost more per box than a large one. Third is August, when NC State housing runs fall move-in on assigned appointment slots and the whole market tightens.

### What a posted total leaves out

Published job figures assume a boxed household with ground-floor or elevator access. They exclude packing labor and materials, mattress and television cartons, floor protection, disassembly, appliance servicing, storage between closings, second stops, out-of-county mileage, and the specialty items the tariff prices separately.

## svc_local_moving_lede

Almost every move inside Wake County falls under a state rate ceiling most residents have never heard of. At fifty miles or less the clock decides the bill rather than the mileage, so how that clock is defined matters more than the advertised rate.

## svc_local_moving_body

<h2>The state sets a ceiling and the mover needs a certificate</h2>
<p>A household move that begins and ends in North Carolina is regulated transportation. Under General Statute 62-262(a), no person may transport household goods in intrastate commerce without a certificate obtained from the Utilities Commission, and the Commission runs a criminal history record check on applicants and current holders under 62-273.1. Rates come from Maximum Rate Tariff No. 2, whose current edition was issued December 30, 2025 and took effect January 1, 2026. Rule 2 makes those figures maximums, not suggestions, so a carrier may quote below them but never above. Claiming to hold a certificate without one is a Class 3 misdemeanor carrying a fine up to five hundred dollars for a first offense, plus a civil penalty the Commission may set as high as five thousand dollars.</p>
<h2>Fifty miles is the line between two pricing systems</h2>
<p>Rule 17 restricts hourly billing to a ratemaking distance of fifty miles or less, which covers essentially any move between Raleigh addresses. Rule 53 then caps the hourly charge: $188.40 for a vehicle and two crew during regular hours, $243.30 with three and $297.90 with four, with $54.80 for each additional crew member. Regular hours mean 8 a.m. to 5 p.m. on weekdays, excluding legal holidays, and everything outside that window shifts to the overtime band, where two crew cap at $235.15. Time runs from the moment vehicle and crew reach the origin until all services finish at the destination, and Rule 18 rounds to the quarter hour. The tariff's own illustration is useful: six hours and ten minutes bills as 6.25 hours, while eight hours and fifty minutes bills as nine.</p>
<h2>The one rule worth memorizing before you sign</h2>
<p>The ordinary written estimate is governed by Rule 13, which says the final billed charges shall not exceed 120 percent of the estimated amount. Going past that ceiling requires a change order or addendum signed by the shipper before the move starts, or at the point the shipper asks for extra service. Two exceptions matter. The protection does not apply when the estimate is requested fewer than five business days before the move, or when the shipment weighs under a thousand pounds. Booking a week ahead is therefore a consumer protection decision, not just a scheduling one. Binding not-to-exceed estimates were retired from the tariff effective January 1, 2026, so the practical choice now is a nonbinding estimate with the percentage cap or a binding guaranteed price.</p>
<h2>What your belongings are worth on paper</h2>
<p>Coverage defaults to sixty cents per pound for the damaged article under Rule 7, and the tariff illustrates that with a fifty-pound, sixty-five-inch television whose recoverable value is thirty dollars no matter what the receipt said. Full value protection is priced separately at one dollar per hundred dollars of declared value on a single truck, with a minimum valuation of five dollars a pound. One clause rewards attention: if the carrier fails to obtain your signature in the valuation section, the shipment is deemed released to coverage of up to fifteen thousand dollars at no extra charge. Articles or matched sets worth five thousand dollars or more sit outside full value protection unless you disclose them in writing.</p>
<h2>Older Raleigh blocks change the crew plan</h2>
<p>The access facts, not the map distance, drive the hours. A frame house in Oakwood or Mordecai may have no driveway, a deep front walk and a narrow street shared with parked cars. Boylan Heights adds grade. Five Points and Hayes Barton bring mature canopy and tight turns. A mid-rise off North Hills replaces all of that with a loading area and a reserved elevator. Occupying the street, the sidewalk or the sidewalk shoulder requires a right-of-way permit from the city, while a pass for a moving van in a timed or paid space comes directly from Raleigh Parking and is picked up at 310 W. Martin Street. Whichever applies, the pass has to stay visible the whole time the space is held.</p>
<h2>Heat and storm days belong in the schedule</h2>
<p>National Weather Service normals for Raleigh-Durham put the average daily high at ninety degrees or above from July 2 through July 27, topping out near 90.4 degrees in mid-July. That argues for an early start, shaded staging and real water breaks rather than a heroic afternoon. September brings the other problem. When Tropical Storm Ophelia crossed on September 22 and 23, 2023, a gauge four and a half miles northwest of the city recorded 3.66 inches, and the Lake Wheeler Road field lab measured 3.53 inches. Floor protection and a plan to pause a carry are worth agreeing on in advance.</p>
<h2>Paperwork at the door</h2>
<p>Rule 28 requires the bill of lading to carry the carrier's own name rather than an agent's, its Commission certificate number, a preferred delivery date or window and a Commission telephone number for questions. If the document is electronic, the screen has to measure at least seven inches diagonally. Statute 62-271 lets a carrier hold the goods until tariff charges are paid, and Rule 11 adds a one percent monthly finance charge on balances past thirty days. A card processing fee may be passed along only up to what the processor actually charges the carrier, and it has to appear on the bill of lading.</p>

## svc_long_distance_moving_lede

Cross the fifty-mile mark and North Carolina stops billing by the hour. Cross the state line and the tariff stops applying altogether, replaced by federal rules with a different overage limit, a survey requirement and its own claim clocks.

## svc_long_distance_moving_body

<h2>Two rulebooks, and the distance decides which</h2>
<p>A long haul that stays inside the state leaves hourly billing behind. Rule 17 sends any move over fifty miles to the weight-and-distance schedule, quoted in dollars per hundred pounds, which is why some carriers will not publish an hourly figure at all. Where no adequate scale is available, the Commission allows constructive weight of seven pounds per cubic foot, so the cube of the load becomes the billing basis. A move that ends in Virginia, Tennessee or anywhere else outside North Carolina is interstate, and the Utilities Commission has no jurisdiction over it. Federal Motor Carrier Safety Administration regulations in 49 CFR Parts 370 through 375 take over instead, and the difference is not cosmetic.</p>
<h2>The federal number is 110 percent, not 120</h2>
<p>Section 375.405 requires a nonbinding estimate to state plainly that the shipper will not be required to pay more than 110 percent of it at delivery. Section 375.407 then obliges the carrier to hand over the shipment once that amount, plus separately itemized charges for services requested after the paperwork was issued, has been paid. Charges for impracticable operations collectible at delivery are capped at fifteen percent of the other charges due, and anything else waits at least thirty days before it can be billed. Section 375.103 puts a name on the abuse this prevents: deliberately holding a load hostage after the shipper offers the binding amount or 110 percent of a nonbinding one violates the reasonable dispatch requirement. Note the ten-point gap between the federal ceiling and the state's 120 percent figure, because a household that moves out of state loses one protection and gains another.</p>
<h2>The survey and the booklet are requirements</h2>
<p>Under 375.401 the carrier has to conduct a physical survey of the household goods, either on site or by live or recorded video, and base its written estimate on that survey. A shipper may waive the survey, but the waiver has to be in writing. Section 375.213 requires the mover to furnish two federal publications before the move: the tips brochure titled Ready to Move? and the contents of the rights and responsibilities booklet, either as copies or as working website links. When printed, that booklet's cover has to carry the required sentence in twelve-point or larger bold type. Section 375.207 rounds it out by requiring every advertisement to show the carrier's assigned name and its federal transportation number.</p>
<h2>Check the number before you sign anything</h2>
<p>For an interstate mover, the federal household goods search tool lets you look up a company by name or list every registered mover in a state, which is where the operating authority and complaint history live. For a move that stays in North Carolina, the Commission publishes a list of carriers holding a certificate of exemption and refreshes it monthly. That refresh interval is the catch: a certificate cancelled or newly issued since the last update will not appear, so a phone call to confirm current standing beats a screenshot. The Public Staff transportation rates division, reachable at 919-880-1837, handles unresolved claims and reports of movers operating without a certificate.</p>
<h2>Claims run on two different clocks</h2>
<p>Federal rules give a carrier thirty days from receipt of a written claim to acknowledge it in writing under 370.5, then 120 days to pay, decline or make a firm written compromise offer under 370.9. If it still cannot resolve the matter, written status updates are owed every sixty days after that. The state tariff is tighter at the front end and shorter overall. Rule 52 requires acknowledgment with claim procedures no later than fifteen days after a written claim arrives, and final resolution within sixty. The filing window is generous either way: nine months from the point a reasonable time for delivery has passed. Should a claim be denied in writing, suit must follow within two years and one day. Rule 51 also bars a carrier from collecting freight charges on a shipment totally lost or destroyed in transit.</p>
<h2>Valuation matters more over distance</h2>
<p>Released value under federal rules is sixty cents per pound per article, and the rights booklet illustrates the arithmetic with a ten-pound stereo component worth a thousand dollars that recovers six dollars. Anything worth more than a hundred dollars a pound counts as extraordinary value and has to be listed, or liability can be limited to that hundred-dollar-per-pound figure. North Carolina adds a wrinkle few states recognize: ready-to-assemble particle-board furniture is capped at sixty cents a pound or fifty dollars per article, whichever is greater, regardless of the coverage chosen. Storage in transit stretches the exposure further, running up to 180 days in thirty-day increments, with full value protection billed at fifteen percent of its rate per period.</p>
<h2>Why so many Wake County households are doing this</h2>
<p>The volume is not imagined. Census Bureau estimates put Wake County at 1,257,235 residents on July 1, 2025, an increase of 27,760 in one year and the fifth largest numeric gain of any county in the country. The Raleigh-Cary metro reached 1,595,720, up 2.4 percent and tenth nationally by rate. State demographers counted 22,574 net migrants into Wake in 2023 and 2024, about 9,870 of them arriving from elsewhere in the United States. Peak weeks fill quickly, and a mover with a truck already committed cannot invent capacity in July.</p>

## svc_apartment_moving_lede

An apartment move answers to the building first. Elevator windows, the door a crew is allowed to use, the distance from truck to unit and downtown meter hours can all outrank the date printed on your lease.

## svc_apartment_moving_body

<h2>The building sets the hour, the tariff sets the charge</h2>
<p>Vertical access is priced, not absorbed. On weight-and-distance moves the tariff allows $3.10 per hundred pounds for elevator use at each end, and $2.20 per hundred pounds for each flight of stairs, inside or outside, at each end. On an hourly job the same obstacles appear as clock time instead. The tariff defines a flight as at least eight steps inside a building and treats an elevator as one flight. It caps the inside flight carry at $33.55 for the first flight and $16.85 for each one after, with an outside first flight at $32.15 and sixty cents per step beyond the counted range. None of this is negotiable in the abstract, which is exactly why the elevator reservation belongs in the plan before a start time is chosen.</p>
<h2>Long carries have a legal definition</h2>
<p>Distance from the truck to the door is measured, not estimated. The tariff treats the first seventy-five feet between vehicle and residence entrance as included, then charges an extra carry for each additional fifty feet or fraction of it at $2.20 per hundred pounds. In practice that turns a garden-style complex where the truck stops at a shared bay into a materially different job from a unit twenty feet off a service door. Walk the actual route and count it before booking. A crew that knows about a two-hundred-foot push across a courtyard brings dollies and a second body for it.</p>
<h2>Downtown curb hours are not what most people assume</h2>
<p>Payment at Raleigh meters is required from 8 a.m. to 6 p.m. Monday through Friday in downtown districts including Glenwood South, and from 8 a.m. to 8 p.m. in the Hillsborough district near the university. Weekends are free. Enforcement, though, runs twenty-four hours every day, so a bagged or restricted space stays restricted at 7 a.m. on a Sunday. A meter or time-zone violation costs twenty dollars, doubling with a late fee if it sits unpaid for twenty-one days. Parking passes for moving vans come from Raleigh Parking at 310 W. Martin Street, Suite 109, and construction-style permits are priced by the number of days requested times the daily rate of each space held.</p>
<h2>Permit blocks and the older streets</h2>
<p>Several neighborhoods near downtown and the university run residential permit programs, where a permit costs thirty dollars per cycle, guest passes cover a single twenty-four-hour period and restrictions commonly apply from 8 a.m. to 5 p.m. on weekdays. Restrictions differ block by block, so the answer for one street tells you nothing about the next. Occupying a lane, a sidewalk or the sidewalk shoulder is a separate matter that requires a right-of-way permit through the city's development portal, along with a traffic control plan when a lane or the full street is involved. Most apartment moves need none of that. The ones that do are usually the ones nobody checked.</p>
<h2>A very small shipment loses the estimate cap</h2>
<p>The state's 120 percent ceiling on billed charges above an estimate carries two exceptions that catch studio and one-bedroom moves in particular. It does not apply when the shipment weighs less than a thousand pounds, and it does not apply when the estimate is requested fewer than five business days before the move. A lightly furnished apartment can easily sit under that weight threshold. If yours might, ask for a binding guaranteed price instead of relying on a percentage cap that will not cover you, and book far enough ahead that the five-day exception never comes into play.</p>
<h2>Campus move-in runs on assigned slots</h2>
<p>University housing does not open its doors to whoever arrives first. NC State's fall 2026 residence hall move-in runs August 13 through 15 with residents selecting check-in appointments, which fixes both the arrival window and how long a vehicle can sit at the curb. That single week also tightens truck availability across the market, since off-campus leases in the same corridor turn over alongside it. Confirm whether the destination is university housing or a private building, then hand the crew the appointment time rather than a vague morning.</p>
<h2>Flat-pack furniture and what a claim can return</h2>
<p>Apartments hold a lot of ready-to-assemble furniture, and North Carolina prices that reality honestly. Liability on particle-board and knock-down pieces is capped at sixty cents a pound or fifty dollars per article, whichever is greater, whatever coverage was elected. The general default is the same sixty cents per pound the tariff applies to any released shipment. Full value protection changes the arithmetic, and a carrier that never obtained a signature in the valuation section leaves the shipment deemed released to coverage of up to fifteen thousand dollars at no charge. Photograph assembled pieces before disassembly, and keep hardware bagged and taped to the frame it came from.</p>

## svc_packing_services_lede

Packing is priced apart from the move itself, container by container, and the state tariff publishes a ceiling for each carton and each hour of packing labor. Who packs a box also changes what a claim looks like months later.

## svc_packing_services_body

<h2>Packing carries its own published price list</h2>
<p>Item 1 of the tariff sets maximum charges for packing and unpacking, and each figure covers the labor, the container and the materials together. A dish pack for kitchen glassware caps at $74.05. A three-cubic-foot carton caps at $22.25, a four-and-a-half at $26.65 and a six at $26.80. A wardrobe carton runs to $33.10, a king or queen mattress carton to $47.95, and a corrugated container for mirrors, glass or marble to $47.60. Custom crating is priced by volume at $26.95 per cubic foot with a minimum of $107.85 per crate. Because these are ceilings rather than list prices, an itemized packing estimate can be checked line by line against the published schedule.</p>
<h2>Buying the boxes and packing them yourself</h2>
<p>Item 3 covers containers supplied without labor, and the gap between the two schedules is the real decision. The same three-cubic-foot carton is $7.05 empty, the four-and-a-half is $8.30 and the six is $9.45. A dish pack is $17.35, a wardrobe $22.50 and a king or queen mattress carton $26.40. Flat-screen television boxes are $66.50 under forty inches and $82.50 at forty inches or more. Unprinted newsprint is $1.90 a pound and two-inch plastic tape $7.20 a roll. Splitting the work is legitimate and common: hand over the kitchen, the artwork and the electronics, and box your own closets and linens.</p>
<h2>Packing on move day bills differently than packing ahead</h2>
<p>Timing changes the rate structure. When packing happens incidentally during an hourly move, the tariff bills it at the hourly crew rate plus container charges from Item 3 rather than at the full packing rates. Packing performed on a separate earlier day falls under Item 1, or under Item 2 when it happens before 8 a.m., after 5 p.m., or at any hour on a weekend or holiday. On weight-and-distance moves there is a straight labor line as well: $41.85 per crew member per hour during regular hours and $51.40 outside them. Ask which of those applies to your scheduled day, because a Saturday pack and a Tuesday pack are not the same transaction.</p>
<h2>Who packed the carton matters when something breaks</h2>
<p>A carton packed by the carrier and a carton packed by the household sit differently in a claim file. Owner-packed boxes invite an argument about whether the contents were adequately protected, so the more fragile the item, the stronger the case for professional packing and a written notation of it on the inventory. Either way the filing window is nine months from the point a reasonable time for delivery has passed. On an intrastate move the carrier owes an acknowledgment with claim procedures within fifteen days and a resolution within sixty. Photograph the inside of any box you pack yourself.</p>
<h2>Three separate caps on what a claim can return</h2>
<p>Recovery is bounded three different ways, and none of them tracks what you paid at retail. The unpriced default is sixty cents per pound for the damaged article, which the tariff illustrates with a fifty-pound television valued at thirty dollars. Anything worth more than a hundred dollars a pound is an article of extraordinary value and has to be listed in writing, or liability can be held to that per-pound figure. Ready-to-assemble particle-board furniture is capped at sixty cents a pound or fifty dollars per article, whichever is greater. Full value protection changes the calculation but excludes single articles or matched sets worth five thousand dollars or more unless they are disclosed.</p>
<h2>What does not belong in an ordinary carton</h2>
<p>Some categories never go on a household truck: fuels, propane, aerosols, ammunition, corrosives, paint thinner and anything else flammable or explosive. Perishable food, open liquids and live plants create their own problems on a summer haul. Appliances need servicing rather than packing, and a laden home freezer is handled as a specialty item at a capped charge of $93.30. Keep identification, medication, financial and medical records, keys, jewelry and small heirlooms with you in your own vehicle. Set aside a labeled first-night carton with bedding, tools, chargers and cleaning supplies so nobody has to open six boxes at midnight.</p>
<h2>Sequencing a pack in a Raleigh summer</h2>
<p>Order the work backward from the load. Storage rooms, books, artwork and out-of-season clothing come first, then the rooms you can live without, with the kitchen and bathrooms last. Reserve the coolest hours for the heaviest carries, since normal daily highs at Raleigh-Durham sit at or above ninety degrees for most of July. Label the top and two sides of every carton with its destination room, and mark fragile loads on the side that will face out on the truck. A crew arriving to sealed, labeled cartons loads faster and touches your belongings less.</p>

## svc_appliance_moving_lede

North Carolina's tariff does not include disconnecting or servicing a washer, a dryer or an icemaker. Those are priced separately, and if nobody prepares the appliance before load day, the truck waits.

## svc_appliance_moving_body

<h2>Servicing is a shipper responsibility with three options</h2>
<p>Rule 35(A) is explicit: except where the tariff says otherwise, the services it covers do not include servicing, meaning using a device to secure a washer drum, or disconnecting washers, dryers or icemakers. It is the shipper's responsibility to have those articles serviced and disconnected before loading. The rule then gives three routes. You can take responsibility yourself and hold the carrier harmless. You can have the carrier do it at the published Item 5 rates, provided the carrier has qualified personnel. Or you can have the carrier contract a third party under Rule 44, whose charge is listed on the bill of lading as a Third Party Charge in addition to everything else. Choosing among those three is a decision, and it belongs in the estimate rather than in a conversation on the driveway.</p>
<h2>What Item 5 actually costs</h2>
<p>The maximums are modest and worth knowing so an itemized bill can be checked. Carrier servicing of appliances at origin caps at $32.80 for the first article and $19.80 for each additional article. Unservicing at destination caps at $19.80 for the first and $15.20 for each after that. Rule 44 names the third-party services most often needed here: disconnecting and reconnecting washers, dryers and icemakers, and separately, disassembling and reassembling waterbeds, hot tubs, swing sets and playhouses. A refrigerator with a plumbed water line to an icemaker is the single most common appliance that stalls a load, because the shutoff, the saddle valve and the line all need attention before the unit rolls.</p>
<h2>Waiting time is a billable hour</h2>
<p>Item 9 prices waiting time not caused by the carrier at $94.50 per hour per vehicle and driver, with additional crew billed at Item 4 labor rates. It also fixes the working assumption: unless otherwise agreed, loading and unloading are performed between 8 a.m. and 5 p.m., and free waiting time exists only inside those hours, with one free hour on shipments traveling more than 100 but fewer than 200 miles and three free hours at 200 miles or more. The item does not apply on Sundays or legal holidays. A washer still full of water, a dryer still on a hard gas connection, or a refrigerator still cold and full at 8 a.m. is what turns a scheduled start into billable standing time.</p>
<h2>Frozen food has a hard rule attached</h2>
<p>Rule 40 says carriers will not accept frozen foods or other articles requiring refrigeration, with one narrow exception. Frozen food may be transported if it is inside a freezer that is operating at normal deep-freeze temperature at the time of loading, the shipment travels no more than 150 miles and delivery happens within 24 hours of loading, and no storage of the shipment is required. Item 10 then prices the handling charge for a laden home freezer containing frozen food at $93.30, once per shipment, in addition to the flight carry charges. Any move that involves storage in transit fails the exception outright, so the freezer travels empty and the contents get eaten, given away or discarded before the day.</p>
<h2>Twenty-four hours before the truck, not two</h2>
<p>Refrigerators and freezers need to be emptied, defrosted and dried, with the doors propped so nothing sours in a closed cabinet. Washers need the drum secured and the supply hoses drained. Dryers need the vent disconnected and the duct cleaned, and a gas dryer needs the fuel line handled by someone qualified to do it, not by a moving crew. Dishwashers hold standing water in the sump that will find the truck floor. Once at destination, an upright refrigerator that was transported upright can be plugged in after it has stood a few hours; one that traveled on its side needs longer for the compressor oil to drain back before power goes on. Manufacturer instructions for the specific unit govern, and the manual is worth reading rather than guessing.</p>
<h2>Raleigh's summer heat is part of the plan</h2>
<p>Appliance work is heavy, slow and often done in a garage or a laundry closet with no airflow. National Weather Service normals for Raleigh-Durham put the daily high at or above 90 degrees from July 2 through July 27 and still at 89.7 degrees on August 1, sliding to 86.4 by August 31. Combine that with the record July dew points NC State's climate office recorded across 33 of its stations in 2025 and an afternoon appliance carry becomes a heat-illness question. Schedule the heaviest units first, in the morning, and get the refrigerator inside and standing before the day peaks rather than leaving it on a ramp.</p>
<h2>Mid-rise laundry and the loading dock window</h2>
<p>In a downtown or Glenwood South building the constraint moves from the appliance to the building. Published move-in guidance for Skyhouse Raleigh, a 23-floor downtown tower of 352 units, describes a freight elevator reserved by calling the concierge desk at least 48 hours ahead, Saturday slots filling one to two weeks out, move-in hours from 8 a.m. to 5 p.m. Monday through Saturday, a certificate of insurance sent to the building on request, and the truck standing at a loading dock on Hillsborough Street. A stacked washer-dryer set, a French-door refrigerator and a reserved elevator slot have to fit inside that same window, which is a reason to confirm the appliance is disconnected and drained the night before rather than the morning of.</p>

## svc_commercial_moving_lede

A commercial relocation in North Carolina has no state rate ceiling and no state complaint route, because commercial office and equipment moves sit outside the Utilities Commission's jurisdiction. The contract is the whole protection.

## svc_commercial_moving_body

<h2>The jurisdiction line, stated by the regulator</h2>
<p>The Utilities Commission's consumer publication on moving in North Carolina notes that interstate and international shipments, government and military moves, commercial office and equipment moves, and furniture and retail deliveries are not under the Commission's jurisdiction. Household goods moving inside the state is the regulated category, carrying a certificate requirement under Commission Rule R2-37, a maximum rate tariff, and a required consumer booklet. None of that applies to a warehouse relocation, a restaurant buildout, a medical office move or an equipment transfer. So the questions that a tariff answers for a household have to be answered by the agreement instead: what the rate is, what counts as billable time, what the liability per pound or per article is, and what insurance is carried.</p>
<h2>What to require in writing before a commercial move</h2>
<p>Six items, every time. A rate structure with the billable-time definition, travel, and any minimum spelled out. Crew size and whether the same crew works both ends of a multi-day move. Cargo liability stated as a number, plus certificates of insurance naming your landlord and, where required, the destination landlord. A written asset inventory method, with tags or serials recorded at origin for anything with a serial number. The after-hours and weekend premium, since most commercial work happens outside business hours. And a schedule that names which systems must be operational at which hour, because a commercial move is judged by downtime rather than by boxes moved.</p>
<h2>Occupying the street is a permitted activity</h2>
<p>For anything larger than a van at the curb, Raleigh's right-of-way rules apply. The city requires a permit for any person who wishes to install an object in, or occupy, the right-of-way, and it names four occupancy types: sidewalk occupancy with pedestrian detours, occupying a lane, a temporary full street closure, and use of parking spaces for dumpsters or storage. The city notes that when a lane is occupied a flagger is often present to assist with the flow of traffic, and applications route through the Permit and Development Portal. It also states that requests for parking passes for construction vehicles, moving vans or other vehicles are obtained from Raleigh Parking directly. A downtown move that needs both a lane and two metered spaces is dealing with two different city processes on two different timelines.</p>
<h2>Oversized equipment adds three reviewers</h2>
<p>Machinery, tanks, transformers and shop equipment can trip the oversized-transport threshold. Raleigh notes a permit may be required if, per NCDOT, total width exceeds eight feet six inches, overall height is greater than thirteen feet six inches, a single vehicle is over 40 feet or a combination over 60 feet, or the weight exceeds the legal weight under North Carolina law. The application must be reviewed by the Raleigh Police Department, Raleigh Parking if parking spaces are affected, and the Traffic Signal Supervisor before the move, and it requires a route map plus an approved NCDOT oversize or overweight permit. The city adds that its Traffic personnel may have to adjust the height of traffic signals and travel with the transport during the move. Build weeks into the schedule for that, not days.</p>
<h2>Parking enforcement runs around the clock</h2>
<p>Commercial moves happen at night, and the assumption that enforcement sleeps is wrong. Raleigh states that parking regulations are enforced 24 hours, every day. Downtown areas including Glenwood South require meter payment from 8 a.m. to 6 p.m. Monday through Friday and the Hillsborough District near NC State from 8 a.m. to 8 p.m. Monday through Friday, with no payment required on weekends, but the enforcement sentence stands independent of the payment windows. Raleigh Parking field agents cover downtown, the Hillsborough Street corridor and residential permit areas. A meter or time-zone citation is twenty dollars, with a twenty dollar late fee past 21 days. Across four trucks over three nights, unmanaged, that is a line item.</p>
<h2>Sequence by system, not by floor</h2>
<p>The plan that minimizes downtime is ordered by dependency. Freeze purchasing and filing at a fixed date. Label everything by destination room on the new floor plan, since the crew has never seen the origin layout and does not need to. Move network and server equipment first, in a dedicated trip with its own inventory, and confirm the destination is live before anything else arrives. Then bring departments across in the order they must come back online, workstation by workstation, keeping each user's monitor, dock and peripherals as one labeled unit. Common areas, storage and archives go last, since nothing depends on them. Anything with a serial number gets photographed at its origin desk.</p>
<h2>Raleigh's growth is why commercial space keeps turning over</h2>
<p>The churn is documented. Wake County issued 15,248 building permits in 2024, held 528,746 housing units as of July 1, 2024, and was estimated at 1,257,235 residents on July 1, 2025, a one-year numeric gain of 27,760 that ranked fifth among all U.S. counties. The Raleigh-Cary metro reached 1,595,720 on the same date, a 2.4 percent gain that ranked tenth nationally by percentage. Businesses following that population growth are relocating, expanding and consolidating constantly, which is exactly why a downtown building's freight elevator and loading dock are contested. Published guidance for one 23-floor downtown tower of 352 units describes reserving the freight elevator by calling the concierge desk at least 48 hours ahead, with move hours of 8 a.m. to 5 p.m. Monday through Saturday and a certificate of insurance required. Reserve earlier than that guidance suggests, and confirm in writing.</p>

## svc_furniture_moving_lede

Sofas, sectionals, armoires and bed frames are what the tariff actually calls set-up pieces, and taking them apart and putting them back together is already inside the transportation rate. What sits outside that rate is the part most households get wrong.

## svc_furniture_moving_body

<h2>Disassembly and reassembly are already paid for</h2>
<p>Rule 39 of North Carolina's Maximum Rate Tariff No. 2 spells out what a transportation rate buys. It covers taking apart set-up pieces of furniture where they stand in the owner's home, carrying them to the vehicle, stowing them inside the truck body, unloading at destination, carrying them to the room you designate, and setting back up whatever was taken apart at origin. That is one continuous obligation, not a menu. So a bed frame broken down in a Mordecai bedroom and rebuilt in a Wake Forest Road townhouse is inside the rate, and a crew that treats reassembly as an upsell is charging for something the tariff already includes. The exception is a warehouse stop: when a shipment is picked up at or delivered to a warehouse, the rate covers only loading or unloading at the door or platform.</p>
<h2>The 400-pound line, and why it exists</h2>
<p>Rule 35(B) draws a hard boundary. The services covered by the tariff do not include handling, loading or unloading any single article weighing 400 pounds or more. Above that weight, the extra handling is the shipper's to provide, or the carrier's to provide as a separately priced service under Item 6 when it has the personnel and equipment on hand. Item 6 prices any bulky article over 400 pounds and not otherwise named at $151.40, covering both ends of the trip plus the handling and blocking of the piece. Bracing and blocking material that is not part of the truck's regular equipment also falls to the shipper. Two pieces of ordinary furniture cross that line more often than people expect: a full slate-bed armoire and a solid-wood entertainment wall with stone shelving.</p>
<h2>Flat-pack and engineered wood recover almost nothing</h2>
<p>Rule 8 limits liability on ready-to-assemble furniture built from press board, particle board and engineered wood, and North Carolina is unusual in naming that category at all. The cap is sixty cents a pound or fifty dollars per article, whichever is greater, and it holds whichever valuation option you selected. A ninety-pound engineered-wood dresser that arrives with a blown-out corner is therefore a fifty-dollar event on paper. Solid-wood and metal pieces sit under the ordinary valuation rules instead. This changes what is worth moving. A four-year-old flat-pack wardrobe that has already been assembled once is frequently better replaced than trucked, and the honest version of that math belongs in the walkthrough rather than in a claim form six weeks later.</p>
<h2>Screens, mirrors and glass tops are separate line items</h2>
<p>A television over 40 inches is a bulky article under Item 6 at $179.90 for loading and unloading, and home gym equipment such as electronic stairsteppers, tanning beds and Bowflex frames is priced at $107.15 the same way. Glass table tops, mirror plate and marble need a corrugated container or a built crate rather than a blanket, and when the carrier does not have qualified personnel to build a special crate, Rule 44 lets it engage a third party whose charge appears on the bill of lading as a Third Party Charge, on top of everything else. None of that is padding. It is the difference between a stone top that arrives whole and one that arrives in two pieces with a sixty-cent-per-pound settlement attached.</p>
<h2>Raleigh's older frame houses set the real limit</h2>
<p>Inside the Oakwood historic district, developed primarily during the fifty-year stretch from 1880 to 1930 and designated in 1975, larger two-story homes are flanked by one-story cottages. That means original stair runs, narrow turns at the landing, and door openings sized for the furniture of the period. A modern eight-foot sectional does not negotiate any of it in one piece, which is exactly why Rule 39 puts disassembly inside the rate. Boylan Heights, platted beginning in 1907 and made a district in 1984, adds the street problem: typical lot frontages run 30 to 50 feet with front-yard setbacks of 15 to 30 feet, and the main street curves through six blocks in an S. A 26-foot truck parked legally on a 30-foot frontage blocks the neighbor's drive, so the staging plan gets decided before the ramp comes down, not after.</p>
<h2>Humidity, adhesive and stretch wrap</h2>
<p>Wrapping choices matter more here than in a dry climate. NC State's climate office recorded record-high average July dew points at 33 of its stations in July 2025, and Raleigh-Durham's daily normal high sits at or above 90 degrees from July 2 through July 27. Stretch wrap applied directly over a finished surface in that air traps moisture against the finish, and veneer glue lines on mid-century case pieces soften. Pads first, then wrap over the pads. Leather and upholstery go under breathable covers rather than plastic. Anything that has been sitting in an unconditioned garage or attic gets time to equalize instead of going straight into a sealed truck body.</p>
<h2>What the walkthrough should produce</h2>
<p>A furniture-only job should end the survey with four written answers: which pieces come apart and who reassembles them, which single articles are over 400 pounds, which items need a crate or a container instead of pads, and where the truck legally stands at both ends. Photograph every assembled piece before a screw comes out, bag the hardware and tape the bag to the frame it belongs to. Then check the inventory sheet as it is written, because condition noted at origin is what a claim gets measured against later.</p>

## svc_gun_safe_moving_lede

A full-height gun safe weighs more than everything else in the room combined, and North Carolina's tariff removes any single article of 400 pounds or more from the services an ordinary move includes. That one sentence reshapes the whole job.

## svc_gun_safe_moving_body

<h2>What a safe actually weighs</h2>
<p>Published manufacturer specifications make the scale concrete. Liberty Safe's American Series lists a 27-long-gun model at 60.5 by 30 by 24 inches and 733 pounds, a 40-gun model at 66.5 by 36 by 27.5 inches and 954 pounds, and a 50-gun model at 72.5 by 42 by 27.5 inches and 1,133 pounds, all before a single firearm, ammunition can or document box goes inside. Those are empty weights on a footprint smaller than a refrigerator, which is what makes the load so unforgiving: the mass is high, the center of gravity sits above the midpoint, and the steel body offers nothing to grip. Add the outlet kit, the plug and the handle and the depth grows by several inches, which matters at a door jamb measured to the quarter inch.</p>
<h2>The tariff hands the weight problem back to you</h2>
<p>Rule 35(B) states that the services covered by the tariff do not include the handling, loading or unloading of any single article weighing 400 pounds or more. Every full-size safe is on the wrong side of that. The extra handling must be provided by the shipper, or by the carrier when it has additional personnel and equipment available, at the charges in Item 6. Item 6 prices any bulky article over 400 pounds not otherwise named at $151.40, and that figure covers loading, unloading, and the handling and blocking of the article. The rule adds one more obligation that gets skipped: where bracing or blocking requires material that is not part of the vehicle's regular equipment, that material and any extra labor must be furnished by the shipper.</p>
<h2>Empty the safe, and document what came out</h2>
<p>A safe should travel empty, and not only for weight. Rule 9 defines articles of extraordinary value as those worth more than $100 per pound, and it requires items of extraordinary value to be listed, with the shipper preparing and retaining an inventory of items valued in excess of $100 per pound. Collectible firearms, coins, precious stones and documents clear that threshold easily. Unlisted, they sit under a per-pound cap that will not resemble their value. So the contents ride in your own vehicle with their own written inventory, the safe rides as a bulky article, and the two are handled as separate problems. Remove the shelving, tape the door shut, and never move a safe with the door unlocked.</p>
<h2>Raleigh's older housing stock is the access problem</h2>
<p>The Oakwood historic district, developed primarily between 1880 and 1930 and Raleigh's only intact 19th century neighborhood, is built on original framing with original stairs. A thousand-pound point load on a hundred-year-old tread or a pier-and-beam floor system is a structural question, not a moving question, and it gets answered before the safe leaves the wall. Rule 36 is the tariff's version of the same caution: a carrier is not obligated to perform pickup or delivery where the condition of roads, streets, driveways, alleys or their approaches makes it impracticable to operate vehicles, or where loading and unloading facilities are inadequate. A safe going into a basement reached only by exterior steps, or up a drive too soft to take a loaded truck, is the case that rule was written for.</p>
<h2>Attics, crawl spaces and unlit basements</h2>
<p>Rule 41 puts removal from and placement into certain locations squarely on the shipper. The list is specific: any location not accessible by a permanent stairway, which explicitly excludes disappearing staircases and ladders of any type, not adequately lighted, without a flat continuous floor, or that does not allow a person to stand erect. If the carrier agrees to work in such a space anyway, Item 4 labor charges apply at $41.85 per crew member per hour during regular weekday hours and $51.40 outside 8 a.m. to 5 p.m. or at any hour on a weekend or holiday. Raleigh houses of the Oakwood and Mordecai era frequently store a safe in exactly that kind of low basement, so the answer to who is responsible for getting it out should be settled in writing.</p>
<h2>The truck has to stand somewhere legal</h2>
<p>A 1,100-pound safe cannot be walked a hundred feet. In Boylan Heights, where typical lot frontages run 30 to 50 feet and the main street curves through six blocks, and in downtown blocks where any occupation of a lane, a sidewalk or a sidewalk shoulder needs a right-of-way permit from the city, the truck position is part of the rigging plan. Occupying a lane often requires a flagger to keep traffic moving, and passes for a moving van in a legal parking space come from Raleigh Parking directly rather than from the right-of-way office. Where the shortest path from door to truck is measured in single digits of feet, the safe job becomes ordinary. Where it is not, the plan needs a ramp route and a surface that will carry the load.</p>
<h2>Anchoring, floors and the destination side</h2>
<p>Half the failures happen at delivery, not at origin. A safe that was bolted down at origin leaves anchor holes, and the destination floor needs a decision before the truck arrives: slab, framed floor over a crawl space, or a second story. Point loading concentrated under four small feet is different from the same weight spread across a room. Protect the finish floor along the entire path, not just at the resting spot, because a steel edge dragged eighteen inches across engineered plank is the damage nobody photographs in time. Set the safe, level it, then bolt it once you are certain the door swing clears.</p>

## svc_hoisting_and_difficult_access_lede

When the stairwell will not take the piece, the remaining routes are a window, a balcony or a crane. North Carolina's tariff treats hoisting as a conditional service, not a guaranteed one.

## svc_hoisting_and_difficult_access_body

<h2>Hoisting is performed only under stated conditions</h2>
<p>Rule 38 covers hoisting, and it is careful about what the carrier is obligated to do. Hoisting service is performed only where the carrier has the necessary equipment and experienced personnel available, and only weather conditions permitting. Where the carrier cannot perform it, the rule says it will attempt to arrange qualified hoisting service at the shipper's expense, and it states that the carrier is not responsible for damage where the service is not performed with its own personnel. If qualified service cannot be arranged, the carrier may deposit that portion of the shipment in the nearest available warehouse subject to a lien for all lawful charges, and the shipment is then considered delivered. Read that sequence twice before assuming a hoist will simply happen on the day. It is a service with three possible outcomes, and one of them is that the piece ends up in a warehouse.</p>
<h2>Weather is written into the rule for a reason</h2>
<p>The phrase weather conditions permitting is doing real work in a market like this one. The National Weather Service office in Raleigh documented 4,150 severe weather reports and 284 tornadoes in its county warning area between 1950 and 2005, an average of five tornadoes a year in the area, and found that severe report frequency peaks in the late afternoon with the highest counts between 5 p.m. and 7 p.m. local time and most activity between 3 p.m. and 8 p.m. The Storm Prediction Center's climatology for the Raleigh radar area puts the primary severe season from April 13 through August 29, with an average of 65 severe report days per year. A suspended load in gusting wind is not a scheduling inconvenience, it is the reason a rigging window belongs in the morning from spring through late summer.</p>
<h2>The equipment brings its own permit questions</h2>
<p>A crane or a boom truck on a Raleigh street is an oversized-vehicle problem before it is a moving problem. The city notes that a permit may be required to move oversized equipment through Raleigh's jurisdiction if, per NCDOT criteria, total width exceeds eight feet six inches, overall height is greater than thirteen feet six inches, a single vehicle is over 40 feet or a combination over 60 feet, or weight exceeds the legal limit under North Carolina law. The application must be reviewed by the Raleigh Police Department, Raleigh Parking if parking spaces are affected, and the Traffic Signal Supervisor before the move, and it requires a route map and an approved NCDOT oversize or overweight permit. The city also states that City of Raleigh Traffic personnel may have to adjust the height of traffic signals and travel with the transport during the move. That is a lead time measured in weeks, not days.</p>
<h2>Occupying the street is a separate permit</h2>
<p>Even without oversized equipment, a hoist occupies space that belongs to the public. Raleigh requires a permit to install an object in, or occupy, the right-of-way, and it defines four occupancy types: sidewalk occupancy with pedestrian detours, occupying a lane, a temporary full street closure, and use of parking spaces for dumpsters or storage. The city notes that when a lane is occupied, a flagger is often present to assist with the flow of traffic, and that applications route through the Permit and Development Portal. Separately, requests for parking passes for moving vans are obtained from Raleigh Parking directly. A balcony hoist over a sidewalk therefore needs a pedestrian detour plan, not just a truck space.</p>
<h2>An additional vehicle is billed by the hour</h2>
<p>Difficult access usually means a second vehicle: a shuttle that can reach a street a full-size truck cannot, or a lift. Item 8 prices auxiliary service at $52.50 per hour for each additional vehicle used, plus Item 4 labor charges at $41.85 per crew member per hour in regular hours and $51.40 outside them. Rule 35(B) adds that where bracing or blocking requires material that is not part of the vehicle's regular equipment, the shipper furnishes the material and any extra labor, and that the tariff's services do not include handling any single article of 400 pounds or more. Between them, those figures explain most of the cost of a job that looks simple from the curb.</p>
<h2>Raleigh geometry that forces the question</h2>
<p>Boylan Heights is the district where this comes up most. Development began in 1907, the area became a historic district in 1984, typical lot frontages run 30 to 50 feet with an average lot depth of 130 feet and front-yard setbacks of 15 to 30 feet, and the main street runs southward in an S curve for six blocks. A 26-foot straight truck cannot always find a legal position within reach of the door, and the houses of that era have stair runs and landings that will not turn a modern sofa. Oakwood, developed primarily between 1880 and 1930 with two-story homes flanked by one-story cottages, presents the same problem inside the house rather than at the curb.</p>
<h2>What a survey should produce before anyone rigs</h2>
<p>Five written answers. The exact dimensions of the piece with packaging on, and the exact dimensions of the opening it is going through. The anchor and load path, meaning what the rigging attaches to and what carries the weight. Who performs the hoist, since the tariff excuses the carrier from responsibility for damage where the service is not performed with its own personnel. Which permits are required and who is filing them. And the weather abort criterion, agreed in advance, so the decision to stop is a threshold rather than an argument on the day.</p>

## svc_hot_tub_moving_lede

A hot tub is a single article of several thousand pounds with an eight-foot footprint and a plumbing and electrical connection at both ends. North Carolina's tariff names it specifically, twice, and both mentions cost money.

## svc_hot_tub_moving_body

<h2>What the tariff charges, and what it hands to a third party</h2>
<p>Item 6 of Maximum Rate Tariff No. 2 prices hot tubs, spas, whirlpools and jacuzzis over 100 cubic feet at $186.50 each, and states that bulky article charges cover both loading and unloading, including the handling and blocking of the article. Separately, Rule 44 lets the carrier engage a third party for services it has no qualified personnel to perform, and it names the disassembly and reassembly of waterbeds, hot tubs, swing sets and playhouses on that list, along with disconnecting and reconnecting washers, dryers and icemakers. Third party charges appear on the bill of lading in addition to all other charges. So a realistic hot tub quote has three parts: the bulky article charge, the third party service, and the transportation itself.</p>
<h2>The weights are larger than almost anyone expects</h2>
<p>Published manufacturer guidance puts the numbers in perspective. Hydropool's planning material gives dry weights of roughly 2,400 pounds for a three-person model, about 3,000 pounds for a five-person model and about 4,100 pounds for an eight-person model, and states that a 450 gallon six-to-eight seater can easily reach over 5,500 pounds once filled with water and bathers. It also notes that most hot tubs are on either side of 80 by 80 inches and around 37 inches high, with heights ranging from about 30 to 40 inches, and that you need at least 40 inches of width and eight feet of height to get most hot tubs in place. That 40-inch clearance figure is the one that decides most jobs, because a standard garden gate and many side yards are narrower.</p>
<h2>Four hundred pounds, and who supplies the bracing</h2>
<p>Rule 35(B) removes from the tariff's ordinary services the handling, loading or unloading of any single article weighing 400 pounds or more. Every hot tub is past that by a factor of several. Above that weight, extra handling is furnished by the shipper or by the carrier at bulky article charges where it has the additional personnel and equipment available, and any bracing or blocking material that is not part of the vehicle's regular equipment must be furnished by the shipper. In practice a hot tub move is planned as a rigging operation, and the tub travels on edge on a purpose-built cart rather than flat.</p>
<h2>Draining and winterizing is the step that cannot be skipped</h2>
<p>Water is the weight and the damage. The tub gets drained fully, and then the residual water gets removed from the pump, the heater, the plumbing lines and the jets, because a few gallons trapped in a pump housing is enough to crack a wet end in a freeze or to soak a truck floor in transit. Power gets disconnected at the panel by someone qualified to do it. Filters, covers, steps, cover lifters and headrests come off and travel separately, labeled. Photograph the equipment bay, wiring and plumbing before anything is disconnected, because reassembly is a reversal of what you documented.</p>
<h2>Getting it out of the yard is the real job</h2>
<p>Access decides the price. Measure the narrowest point on the path with the gate open, since manufacturer guidance calls for at least 40 inches of width and eight feet of height. Note fences that must come down and go back up, decks that will need boards removed, air conditioning units, irrigation heads and soft ground that a loaded cart will sink into. A tub that has to come over a deck rail or between two houses is a rigging plan, not a carry. Rule 36 is the tariff's version of the same warning: a carrier is not obligated to perform pickup or delivery where it is impracticable to operate vehicles because of the condition of roads, streets, driveways, alleys or their approaches, or where loading facilities are inadequate.</p>
<h2>Street permits in Raleigh, twice over</h2>
<p>Two separate city processes can apply. Raleigh requires a permit to install an object in, or occupy, the right-of-way, with four occupancy types defined: sidewalk occupancy with pedestrian detours, occupying a lane, a temporary full street closure, and use of parking spaces for dumpsters or storage. The city notes a flagger is often present when a lane is occupied, and applications route through the Permit and Development Portal. Separately, if the transport itself is oversized, a permit may be required where per NCDOT the total width exceeds eight feet six inches, overall height is greater than thirteen feet six inches, a single vehicle is over 40 feet or a combination over 60 feet, or the weight exceeds North Carolina's legal weight. That application is reviewed by the Raleigh Police Department, Raleigh Parking where spaces are affected, and the Traffic Signal Supervisor, and requires a route map and an approved NCDOT permit. An 80-inch tub on a trailer is close enough to the width threshold that the measurement is worth taking rather than assuming.</p>
<h2>The destination pad has to exist first</h2>
<p>A hot tub does not go on grass or on a deck that was not designed for it. Filled weight in the several-thousand-pound range concentrated on a small footprint needs a slab, a reinforced deck or an engineered base, plus an electrical circuit run and inspected by a licensed electrician and, in many cases, an inspection under local requirements. Confirm the pad is cured and the circuit is ready before the delivery date, because a tub that arrives with nowhere to sit becomes a storage problem. Rule 37 describes where that leads: where delivery cannot be completed from the transporting vehicle, the carrier may warehouse the shipment at the shipper's expense, with accrued charges due and later delivery treated as a new shipment.</p>

## svc_labor_only_moving_lede

A crew without a truck is the cheapest way to move a household and the easiest way to lose the protection a regulated move carries. Both halves of that sentence matter.

## svc_labor_only_moving_body

<h2>What labor-only actually is under state law</h2>
<p>North Carolina regulates the transportation of household goods within the state. A mover needs a certificate from the Utilities Commission under Commission Rule R2-37 and must bill under Maximum Rate Tariff No. 2, and it must provide potential customers the booklet titled Moving in North Carolina, Your Rights and Responsibilities. A labor-only job, where you rent and drive the vehicle and the crew loads it, is not transportation for hire, so the tariff's rate ceilings and its valuation framework do not govern the transaction. The rate is contractual. The liability is contractual. This can be a perfectly good trade for the price difference, but the protection has to be read rather than assumed, and the one question worth asking directly is what the provider's coverage says about damage to the goods, to the rented truck and to the building.</p>
<h2>What an hour of moving labor is worth here</h2>
<p>The tariff's labor item is the best published reference in this state. Item 4 sets labor charges at $41.85 per crew member per hour during regular hours, meaning 8 a.m. to 5 p.m. Monday through Friday excluding legal holidays, and $51.40 per crew member per hour outside those hours or at any hour on a Saturday, Sunday or legal holiday. Those are maximums for a regulated carrier's labor, and they are the natural benchmark for what a labor-only crew hour should cost. The same item is what applies when a carrier does work the tariff otherwise excludes, which is a useful signal about which parts of a move are considered labor rather than transportation.</p>
<h2>The rule that decides who empties the attic</h2>
<p>Rule 41 puts a specific category of work on the shipper. Removal from or placement into attics, basements and other locations is the shipper's responsibility where the location is not accessible by a permanent stairway, and disappearing staircases and ladders of any type are explicitly excluded; where it is not adequately lighted; where there is no flat continuous floor; or where a person cannot stand erect. If a carrier agrees to work there anyway, Item 4 labor charges apply. On a labor-only job nobody is bound by that rule, which cuts both ways: a crew may agree to a low crawl space, and there is no published framework covering what happens if someone is hurt or something is broken doing it. Decide it explicitly and in writing.</p>
<h2>Rented trucks load differently than moving vans</h2>
<p>A rental box has a smooth floor, limited tie points and no logistics track in the common sizes, so the method changes. Build complete floor-to-ceiling tiers from the front wall back, finishing each tier before starting the next, because a half-height tier collapses forward. Heaviest and most rectangular items go on the floor against the front wall. Mattresses and box springs stand upright along the side walls. Table tops, headboards and mirrors travel flat against a wall, never loose. Voids get filled with soft goods so nothing can shift, and each tier gets strapped at the truck's own rails. A properly tiered rental truck holds noticeably more than a stacked one, which matters when the alternative is a second trip.</p>
<h2>Shaw University's window is the kind of constraint that decides a date</h2>
<p>Campus move-in schedules are the sharpest example of a fixed labor window in Raleigh. Shaw University's academic calendar sets new and transfer students reporting to campus on Friday, August 7, and move-in and clearance for returning students on August 10 through 12 from 10 a.m. to 3 p.m., with the calendar stating no weekend move-ins. That is a five-hour daily window on three weekdays. NC State University Housing scheduled fall 2026 move-in for August 13 through 15, with residents selecting a check-in appointment slot in the MyPack Portal. A labor-only crew has to be booked to a window that narrow, and there is no flexibility to push into the evening.</p>
<h2>Curb, elevator and certificate</h2>
<p>Three logistical items stop self-moves more often than the loading does. First, the parking: requests for parking passes for moving vans go to Raleigh Parking directly, downtown and Glenwood South meters run 8 a.m. to 6 p.m. Monday through Friday, the Hillsborough District runs 8 a.m. to 8 p.m., and enforcement operates 24 hours every day. Second, the elevator: published guidance for one 23-floor downtown building of 352 units asks for a freight elevator reservation by phone at least 48 hours ahead and notes Saturday slots filling one to two weeks out. Third, the certificate of insurance that same guidance says the building requires, which a rental truck contract does not provide. Confirm all three before the crew is booked.</p>
<h2>Getting your money's worth from the hours</h2>
<p>Labor-only is billed by the hour, so preparation is the discount. Have every carton sealed, labeled by destination room and stacked near the door before the crew arrives. Disassemble furniture in advance, or agree that the crew is doing it and bag the hardware to each frame. Lay floor runners and door protection. Park the truck as close as legally possible with the ramp down and the pass displayed. Have water available and start early, since Raleigh-Durham's normal daily high sits at or above 90 degrees from July 2 through July 27 and still reaches 89.7 on August 1. Two prepared hours at each end regularly removes an hour from the bill.</p>

## svc_last_minute_moving_lede

Booking a move fewer than five business days out costs you a specific consumer protection written into North Carolina's tariff. Knowing which one is gone changes how you handle the paperwork.

## svc_last_minute_moving_body

<h2>The 120 percent cap does not apply on short notice</h2>
<p>Rule 13 of Maximum Rate Tariff No. 2 is the clause that protects a household from an estimate that grows. It provides that final billed charges shall not exceed 120 percent of the estimated amount, and that going above that ceiling requires a change order or addendum signed by the shipper before the move begins or at the point additional service is requested. Then it names two exceptions. The protection does not apply when the estimate is requested fewer than five business days before the move, and it does not apply to a shipment weighing under 1,000 pounds. A Tuesday call for a Friday move falls squarely in the first exception. That does not mean the price is uncapped in practice, since Rule 2 still makes every tariff figure a maximum rather than a suggestion. It means the specific percentage guardrail is not available, so the substitute is a detailed written scope with crew size, rate band and minimum stated before load day.</p>
<h2>Overtime rates are the default on a short-notice date</h2>
<p>Short-notice moves land on whatever slot exists, and the leftover slots are early, late and weekend. Rule 53 caps hourly charges by band. In regular hours, meaning 8 a.m. to 5 p.m. Monday through Friday excluding legal holidays, a vehicle and two crew cap at $188.40 an hour, three at $243.30 and four at $297.90, with $54.80 for each additional crew member. Outside that window the same crews cap at $235.15, $310.25 and $385.15. Item 4 labor follows the same pattern at $41.85 and $51.40 per crew member per hour. A Saturday move is not billed at overtime for part of the day; the whole day sits in the higher band. If the date is negotiable by even one weekday, that is where the money is.</p>
<h2>Waiting time bills at ninety-four dollars and fifty cents an hour</h2>
<p>Rushed moves produce unready households, and Item 9 prices the consequence. Waiting time not the fault of the carrier is charged at $94.50 per hour per vehicle and driver, with additional crew billed at Item 4 rates. The item also sets the working assumption that loading and unloading occur between 8 a.m. and 5 p.m. unless otherwise agreed, and free waiting time exists only inside those hours: one free hour when the shipment travels more than 100 but fewer than 200 miles, three free hours at 200 miles or more. It does not apply on Sundays or legal holidays. A keys-not-ready delay, a building elevator not reserved, or a household still packing at 8 a.m. converts directly into that hourly figure.</p>
<h2>Raleigh's medical calendar creates real short-notice demand</h2>
<p>Some of Raleigh's short-notice moving demand is structural rather than careless. UNC Health Rex states that its pharmacy residency program start date is July 1, with orientation occupying the first month. WakeMed's internal medicine residency accepts five residents each year for a three-year program with 15 residents at full complement. WakeMed's new graduate nurse residency hires clinical nurse residents into a cohort twice a year, with spring recruitment typically September through November of the prior year and summer recruitment typically January through March, and clinical orientation running two days on designated Tuesdays and Wednesdays from 8:30 a.m. to 4:30 p.m. Match results, license transfers and start dates land close together, which is why the last week of June and the first week of July are tight in this city every year.</p>
<h2>And August is four campuses in one week</h2>
<p>The other compression is academic. NC State University Housing scheduled fall 2026 move-in for August 13 through 15, with residents choosing a check-in appointment time slot in the MyPack Portal. William Peace University welcomed new students on Thursday, August 13, 2026. Meredith College set Move-In Day for Saturday, August 15, 2026. Shaw University's academic calendar puts returning-student move-in and clearance on August 10 through 12 from 10 a.m. to 3 p.m., stating no weekend move-ins, with new and transfer students reporting August 7. A short-notice request inside that window competes with several thousand scheduled arrivals for the same crews and the same curb.</p>
<h2>What to do with two days' notice</h2>
<p>Work in this order. Confirm in writing the rate band that applies to the date, the crew size, the billing minimum and how travel time is charged. Get the tariff basis named, hourly or weight and distance, since hourly requires a ratemaking distance of fifty miles or less. Have the parking settled before the truck rolls, because a pass for a moving van comes from Raleigh Parking directly and Raleigh enforces parking regulations 24 hours every day. Reserve any building elevator immediately, since published guidance for one downtown Raleigh high-rise asks for at least 48 hours' notice to the concierge desk and notes Saturday slots filling one to two weeks out. Then reduce the load. Every carton you take yourself is an hour you do not buy in the higher band.</p>
<h2>The paperwork still has to be right</h2>
<p>Speed is not a reason to accept a thin bill of lading. Rule 28 requires the document to carry the carrier's own name rather than an agent's, its Commission certificate number, a preferred delivery date or window and a Commission telephone number for questions, and if the document is electronic the screen must measure at least seven inches diagonally. Read the inventory sheet as it is written and correct it at the origin. Sign the valuation section deliberately, because released value at sixty cents a pound is the default and it is not insurance. Those three minutes are the ones people skip on a rushed morning and regret at a claim.</p>

## svc_loading_and_unloading_lede

When the truck is rented and the driving is yours, what remains is the part that hurts: getting a household through a door, up a stairwell and stacked so it survives the highway. That work has its own rules.

## svc_loading_and_unloading_body

<h2>Loading help is labor, not regulated transportation</h2>
<p>North Carolina regulates intrastate household goods transportation, requiring a Commission certificate and billing under Maximum Rate Tariff No. 2. A crew that loads a truck you rented and drove is providing labor rather than transportation for hire, so the tariff's hourly ceilings and its valuation rules are not the governing document. That has two consequences. The rate is set by agreement instead of by a published maximum, and the protection for your belongings comes from whatever the labor provider's own contract and insurance say. Ask for both in writing. Ask specifically what the provider's liability is for damage to the goods, to the rented truck, and to the building, since a rented vehicle's own coverage is usually silent about cargo loaded by a third party.</p>
<h2>Where the tariff still tells you what work is being done</h2>
<p>Even outside its jurisdiction the tariff is a good description of the job. Rule 39 lists what a full-service move includes: taking apart set-up furniture where it stands, carrying it to the vehicle, stowing it in the truck body, unloading, carrying to the designated place and reassembling. A loading-only job is that list minus the driving, and it is worth agreeing which of those steps your crew is doing. Rule 45(B) is directly relevant when a self-storage unit is involved: where a carrier enters a warehouse, including self-storage or mini-storage, to remove or place items in space rented by the shipper, Item 4 labor charges apply, at $41.85 per crew member per hour in regular weekday hours and $51.40 outside them. That is a fair reference point for what an hour of moving labor is worth in this state.</p>
<h2>Loading a rented truck is a load-bearing problem</h2>
<p>A rental truck is a box with a smooth floor and no ratchet tracks in most sizes, which changes the method. Build in tiers, front wall to back door, filling the full height of each tier before starting the next, because a tier that is only chest high will fall into the space in front of it. Heaviest and squarest goes against the front wall and on the floor: appliances, filing cabinets, dressers with drawers removed. Mattresses and box springs stand vertically along the side walls, protected. Long items such as table tops, headboards and mirrors go flat against a wall, never loose in the middle. Fill every void with soft goods, then use the truck's own tie rails at each tier. A load that cannot shift does not need luck on I-40.</p>
<h2>Stairs and elevators, counted the way the tariff counts them</h2>
<p>The tariff's flight-carry definitions are a useful way to describe the work before anyone quotes it. Inside a building, the first flight means at least eight steps, additional flights are complete floors above or below, and an elevator counts as one flight. Outside, the first flight is 8 but not more than 20 steps, with each step over twenty counted separately. So the honest way to describe a job is not third floor walkup but two full interior flights plus six exterior steps, and a crew told the second version brings the right equipment and the right number of people.</p>
<h2>August in Raleigh is four campuses at once</h2>
<p>If the plan is a rented truck and hired hands in August, the calendar is against you. NC State University Housing scheduled its fall 2026 move-in for August 13 through 15, with residents selecting a check-in appointment time slot in the MyPack Portal. William Peace University welcomed new students on Thursday, August 13, 2026. Meredith College set Move-In Day and New Student Orientation for Saturday, August 15, 2026. Shaw University's calendar puts new and transfer students on campus August 7 and returning-student move-in and clearance on August 10 through 12, from 10 a.m. to 3 p.m., with the calendar stating no weekend move-ins. Four institutions inside the city are absorbing labor and curb space in a single week, so a mid-August loading crew needs to be booked well before August.</p>
<h2>Mid-rise buildings will not improvise for you</h2>
<p>Downtown and Glenwood South towers control access on their own schedule regardless of who is driving the truck. Published move-in guidance for Skyhouse Raleigh, described as a 23-floor downtown building with 352 units, lists a freight elevator that must be reserved by calling the concierge desk at least 48 hours in advance, Saturday slots filling one to two weeks out, move hours of 8 a.m. to 5 p.m. Monday through Saturday, a certificate of insurance required, and truck parking at a loading dock on Hillsborough Street. A certificate of insurance is the item that stops a self-move cold, because the building wants one from whoever is doing the labor, and a rental truck contract is not one. Ask the labor provider for a certificate naming the building before the date, not on the day.</p>
<h2>What to have ready when the crew arrives</h2>
<p>Every carton sealed and labeled with its destination room. Furniture already disassembled, or a clear agreement that the crew is doing it and the hardware is being bagged and taped to each frame. Floor runners and door jamb protection in place. The truck parked as close to the door as the law allows, with a pass in the window if the space is metered or timed, since passes for moving vans come from Raleigh Parking directly and enforcement runs 24 hours every day. Water and a shaded break spot, because Raleigh-Durham's normal high sits at or above 90 degrees for most of July. Two hours of preparation regularly saves an hour of billed labor at each end.</p>

## svc_office_moving_lede

An office relocation is not governed by the same rulebook as a household move in North Carolina, and the difference determines who can bill you, how, and with what protection attached.

## svc_office_moving_body

<h2>Office moves sit outside the Commission's jurisdiction</h2>
<p>The North Carolina Utilities Commission's own consumer booklet draws the boundary plainly. It notes that interstate and international shipments, government and military moves, commercial office and equipment moves, and furniture and retail deliveries are not under the Commission's jurisdiction. Household goods moving inside the state is the regulated category, and it comes with a certificate requirement, a maximum rate tariff, and a published complaint route. An office move has none of that scaffolding. The rate is whatever the contract says, the liability is whatever the contract says, and there is no state rate ceiling to check the number against. That is not a reason to avoid a commercial mover. It is a reason to read the agreement, because the agreement is the entire protection.</p>
<h2>What to put in the contract instead of a tariff</h2>
<p>Since no published tariff governs, six items belong in writing before anyone touches a desk. First, the rate structure and what counts as billable time, including travel and any minimum. Second, crew size and whether the same crew works both ends. Third, liability per pound or per article, and what the mover carries for cargo and general liability. Fourth, a written inventory method for IT assets, with asset tags recorded at origin. Fifth, the after-hours and weekend premium, because most office work happens outside business hours. Sixth, who is responsible for disconnecting and reconnecting workstations, monitors and network gear, and whether that person is an employee of the mover or a subcontractor.</p>
<h2>Cartons, totes and the reuse math</h2>
<p>The household tariff still supplies useful reference pricing even where it does not apply. Its packing item lists a 2.5 cubic foot office tote box at $11.75, a size chosen because it holds a hanging file and still gets carried one-handed up a flight. Corrugated cartons in the same schedule run $19.45 at 1.5 cubic feet, $31.00 at 3 cubic feet and $37.45 at 4.5 and 6 cubic feet. For an office, plastic totes usually win over corrugated for a different reason than price: they stack squarely on a four-wheel dolly, they survive a rainy load, and they come back out of the building instead of into a dumpster. Flat-screen monitors over 40 inches diagonally are priced at $125.50 to pack and unpack in the household schedule, which is a fair benchmark for what conference-room display handling should cost.</p>
<h2>The curb is the hardest part of a downtown Raleigh office move</h2>
<p>Raleigh requires a permit to install an object in or occupy the right-of-way, and the city defines four occupancy types: sidewalk occupancy with pedestrian detours, occupying a lane, a temporary full street closure, and use of parking spaces for dumpsters or storage. Which one applies is a function of where the truck and the ramp actually sit. The city notes that when a lane is occupied, a flagger is often present to help traffic flow, and applications route through the Permit and Development Portal. Separately, the city says requests for parking passes for construction vehicles, moving vans or other vehicles are obtained from Raleigh Parking directly. Two different offices, two different processes, and an office move downtown frequently needs both.</p>
<h2>Meter windows and the enforcement clock</h2>
<p>All downtown areas including Glenwood South require meter payment from 8 a.m. to 6 p.m. Monday through Friday, and the Hillsborough District near NC State runs 8 a.m. to 8 p.m. Monday through Friday, with no payment required on Saturday or Sunday. The sentence that catches office moves is separate from those hours: parking regulations are enforced 24 hours, every day. A truck left in a loading zone at 9 p.m. on a Friday is still enforceable, and Raleigh Parking field agents work the downtown area and the Hillsborough Street corridor as well as residential permit zones. A meter or time-zone violation runs twenty dollars plus a twenty dollar late fee if it is not paid inside 21 days. That is small money per ticket and large money across a fleet of trucks on a two-night move.</p>
<h2>Sequence the move around the network, not the furniture</h2>
<p>The order that works is boring and it works every time. Cut a freeze date after which nothing new is bought or filed. Label by destination room, not by origin room, because the crew is delivering to a floor plan they have never seen. Move the network and server equipment first, in its own trip, with its own inventory, so the destination is live before anyone needs it. Then workstations by department in the order those departments come back online. Then common areas and storage last. Anything with a hard-drive serial number gets photographed with its tag before it leaves the desk it sits on.</p>
<h2>Wake County's build rate is why office space keeps moving</h2>
<p>The churn is real and it is documented. Wake County issued 15,248 building permits in 2024 and held 528,746 housing units as of July 1, 2024, and Census Vintage 2025 estimates put the county at 1,257,235 residents on July 1, 2025, a numeric gain of 27,760 in a single year that ranked fifth nationally. Growth at that rate keeps commercial tenants moving between buildings, and it also means the elevator, the dock and the freight window at a popular downtown address are contested resources. Reserve them further out than feels necessary, and confirm the reservation in writing the week before rather than the day before.</p>

## svc_piano_moving_lede

A piano is priced as its own event in North Carolina, separately from the hours the rest of the move takes, and the height of the instrument is what decides which of two handling charges applies.

## svc_piano_moving_body

<h2>Two handling charges, one measurement</h2>
<p>Item 10 of Maximum Rate Tariff No. 2 sets a flat handling charge for pianos and organs, and the dividing line is 38 inches of height. Pipe organs, every grand, and any other piano standing 38 inches or taller carry a $111.90 maximum handling charge. Organs and pianos under 38 inches carry $81.65. The charge applies once per shipment for each instrument, and it is in addition to the flight carry charges, not instead of them. Measure the case before anyone quotes: a tall upright and a studio piano can look alike in a living room and land on opposite sides of that line. Portable organs and pianos that one person can conveniently hand carry are exempt from both the handling charge and the flight carry.</p>
<h2>Stairs are counted, not estimated</h2>
<p>Inside a building the first flight is defined as at least eight steps, additional flights are counted as complete floors above or below it, and an elevator counts as one flight. The maximum inside carry is $33.55 for the first flight and $16.85 for each one after. Outside the building the first flight is 8 but not more than 20 steps at $32.15, and each step past twenty adds sixty cents. Fewer than eight exterior steps is not a flight at all. These charges apply each time the service is rendered, so a piano that comes down four exterior steps at origin and goes up a full interior flight at destination is billed for both events. Count the steps on both ends yourself before the day.</p>
<h2>Above 400 pounds the rules change again</h2>
<p>Most uprights and every grand clear 400 pounds, and Rule 35(B) removes articles at that weight from the ordinary services the tariff covers. Extra handling above that threshold is either furnished by the shipper or performed by the carrier at Item 6 charges when it has the people and the equipment available. The same rule puts bracing and blocking material on the shipper when what is needed is not part of the truck's regular equipment. In practice that is why a piano job is planned as a separate operation with a board, a skid, straps and four hands, rather than as one more item on a load list.</p>
<h2>Boylan Heights and the curb you cannot use</h2>
<p>Piano work in Raleigh's older inner neighborhoods is usually a street problem before it is a stair problem. Boylan Heights runs in an S curve for six blocks with typical frontages of 30 to 50 feet and front setbacks of 15 to 30 feet, so there is rarely a legal place to put a 26-foot truck directly in front of the door, and the carry from wherever the truck fits is longer than the plan assumed. Downtown adds a payment window: all downtown areas including Glenwood South require meter payment from 8 a.m. to 6 p.m. Monday through Friday, the Hillsborough District near NC State runs 8 a.m. to 8 p.m., and enforcement operates 24 hours every day regardless. A parking pass for a moving van comes from Raleigh Parking directly, and the approved pass has to be displayed the entire time the space is held.</p>
<h2>Afternoon storms are the scheduling constraint</h2>
<p>An instrument sitting on a driveway or a sidewalk in the wrong hour is the avoidable failure. The National Weather Service office in Raleigh documented 4,150 severe weather reports across its county warning area between 1950 and 2005, with severe report frequency concentrated in the late afternoon and the highest counts between 5 p.m. and 7 p.m. local time. The Storm Prediction Center's climatology for the Raleigh radar area puts the primary severe season from April 13 through August 29 and averages 65 report days a year. That argues for a morning slot from May through August, with the instrument wrapped and inside before the daily convection fires rather than staged outside while a crew eats lunch.</p>
<h2>Impracticable is a defined condition, not an opinion</h2>
<p>Rule 36 says the carrier is not obligated to perform pickup or delivery where it is impracticable to operate vehicles, and it names the reasons: the condition of roads, streets, driveways, alleys or the approaches to them, and inadequate loading or unloading facilities. Rule 37 covers the other end, when delivery cannot physically be completed from the transporting vehicle because the building, its structure, or the nature of the article makes it impossible. In that case the carrier may place the shipment in the nearest available warehouse at the shipper's expense, accrued charges become due at that point, and later delivery counts as a new shipment. A gravel drive on a soft May week in Five Points, or a stairwell that will not take a grand on its side, is worth identifying at the survey rather than discovering with the instrument on a dolly.</p>
<h2>What tuning has to do with the move</h2>
<p>Moving a piano knocks it out of tune because the soundboard and the strings respond to a change in humidity and temperature, not because of a bump in the truck. Give the instrument two to four weeks in its new room before a tuner comes, so the wood has settled at the new indoor conditions. Keep it off an exterior wall, away from a supply register, and out of direct afternoon sun. If the piano is going into storage rather than a room, ask which storage the shipment is entering, because the tariff's climate-controlled storage is specifically defined as equipment able to hold 60 to 85 degrees and humidity no greater than 90 percent at all times.</p>

## svc_pool_table_moving_lede

A slate pool table is not moved. It is disassembled, transported as separate heavy components, and then reassembled and releveled. North Carolina's tariff prices the handling and the labor as two different things.

## svc_pool_table_moving_body

<h2>The published handling charge, and what it excludes</h2>
<p>Item 10 of Maximum Rate Tariff No. 2 sets a handling charge of $128.25 for a pool table with a slate top that is at least 42 inches wide, at least 84 inches long, and weighs more than 400 pounds. Then it says something crucial: that charge does not include disassembly or reassembly of the pool table, and where the carrier performs that work, Item 4 labor charges apply. Item 4 is $41.85 per crew member per hour during regular hours, 8 a.m. to 5 p.m. Monday through Friday excluding legal holidays, and $51.40 per crew member per hour outside those hours or at any hour on a weekend or legal holiday. So a slate table quote has at least two components, and a quote that shows only one of them is incomplete. The handling charge applies once per shipment for the item, in addition to any flight carry charges.</p>
<h2>Four hundred pounds is why it comes apart</h2>
<p>Rule 35(B) states that the services covered by the tariff do not include handling, loading or unloading any single article weighing 400 pounds or more, that above that weight the extra handling is furnished by the shipper or by the carrier at bulky article charges when it has the additional personnel and equipment, and that bracing or blocking material not part of the vehicle's regular equipment must be furnished by the shipper. A three-piece slate bed on a seven, eight or nine foot table is well past that threshold as an assembled unit, which is exactly why the correct method is to reduce it to components that a crew can carry. Individual slates on a standard table commonly run in the low hundreds of pounds each, and each one travels flat and separately supported rather than on edge.</p>
<h2>The order of operations, and where tables get ruined</h2>
<p>Disassembly follows a fixed sequence and skipping a step is what breaks a table. Rails and pockets come off first, with the bolts counted, bagged and labeled by position. The cloth comes off the slate next, which for a stapled cloth means removing staples rather than pulling. Then the slate itself is unbolted and unwaxed at the seams and lifted flat, one piece at a time, onto padded support. The frame and legs come apart last. Reassembly reverses it, and it ends with the part that decides whether the table plays: leveling the frame, shimming under the legs and at the slate seams, and checking across the bed in several directions before the cloth goes back on. Every seam gets refilled and sanded flush. A table assembled without releveling looks finished and rolls wrong.</p>
<h2>Flight carries are counted, not estimated</h2>
<p>Basement and bonus-room installations are the norm, so the stair charges matter. Inside a building the maximum flight carry is $33.55 for the first flight and $16.85 for each additional flight, where the first flight means at least eight steps, additional flights are complete floors above or below it, and an elevator counts as one flight. Outside, the first flight is 8 but not more than 20 steps at $32.15, with each step over twenty adding sixty cents. Those charges apply each time the service is rendered, so a table that comes up from a basement at origin and goes down to a basement at destination is two carry events. Count the steps at both ends before anyone quotes.</p>
<h2>Raleigh's older houses limit the route more than the weight</h2>
<p>The Oakwood historic district was developed primarily during the fifty-year period from 1880 to 1930, is described as Raleigh's only intact 19th century neighborhood, and mixes larger two-story homes with one-story cottages. Original stair runs, tight landings and period door widths are the practical constraint, and a single slate that measures roughly a third of the table's length still needs a straight path and two or three people around it. Boylan Heights, developed beginning in 1907 and designated a district in 1984, has typical frontages of 30 to 50 feet, an average lot depth of 130 feet, front setbacks of 15 to 30 feet, and a street that curves through six blocks, which means the truck often stands farther from the door than the plan assumed and the slate carry gets longer.</p>
<h2>Schedule the work in the morning</h2>
<p>A slate on a driveway in an afternoon storm is the avoidable failure. The National Weather Service office in Raleigh documented 4,150 severe weather reports and 284 tornadoes across its county warning area between 1950 and 2005, and found severe report frequency peaking in the late afternoon with the highest concentration between 5 p.m. and 7 p.m. local time. The Storm Prediction Center's climatology for the Raleigh radar area gives a primary severe season of April 13 through August 29 and an average of 65 severe report days a year. Slate and cloth both dislike sudden rain, and a table is in pieces for hours, so the whole operation belongs in a morning slot from spring through the end of August.</p>
<h2>What to confirm before booking</h2>
<p>Table size and slate configuration, one piece or three. Whether the crew disassembles, transports, reassembles and relevels, and whether that labor is billed separately from the handling charge. Whether new cloth is being installed or the existing cloth reused, since a stapled cloth sometimes survives removal and a glued one usually does not. The stair and carry count at both addresses. And the destination floor, because a leveled table on a framed floor over a crawl space needs the leveling checked again a few weeks after the floor settles under the load.</p>

## svc_senior_moving_lede

Downsizing a house of forty years is a different job than moving a two-bedroom apartment, and the parts that go wrong are almost always the basement, the attic, and the things worth more than a hundred dollars a pound.

## svc_senior_moving_body

<h2>Attics and basements are the shipper's responsibility by rule</h2>
<p>Rule 41 of North Carolina's Maximum Rate Tariff No. 2 says the removal of articles from, or placement into, attics, basements and other locations is the shipper's responsibility where the location meets any of four conditions: it is not accessible by a permanent stairway, and disappearing staircases and ladders of any type are explicitly excluded; it is not adequately lighted; it has no flat continuous floor; or it does not permit a person to stand erect. That describes a great many Raleigh basements and nearly every pull-down attic. If the carrier agrees to work in such a space anyway, Item 4 labor charges apply, at $41.85 per crew member per hour during regular weekday hours and $51.40 outside 8 a.m. to 5 p.m. or at any hour on a weekend or legal holiday. Whoever empties the attic should be decided, in writing, weeks ahead.</p>
<h2>Extraordinary value has a definition and a form</h2>
<p>Rule 9 defines articles of extraordinary value as items worth more than $100 per pound, and requires that such articles be listed, with the shipper preparing and retaining an inventory of items valued in excess of $100 per pound. That threshold catches exactly the categories a long-held household accumulates: jewelry, coins, stamps, currency, precious stones, small antiques, and papers. A four-ounce ring worth two thousand dollars is far past $100 per pound. Unlisted, it recovers almost nothing. The practical answer for most downsizing moves is that this category does not ride on the truck at all. It goes in a car, hand-carried, with its own written list and photographs, and the moving inventory notes that it was withheld.</p>
<h2>Sort by destination room, then by decision</h2>
<p>Downsizing fails when it becomes a single overwhelming judgment. It works when it becomes many small ones made in a fixed order. Start with the floor plan of the new place and measure the walls, because what fits is a fact rather than an argument. Assign every large piece to a room or to a category: family, donate, sell, discard. Then work one room at a time, front to back, and finish it before opening another. Papers get their own pass, because financial and medical records need to travel with the person rather than in a carton. Photographs and albums get their own pass too, since they are the items most often regretted and least often replaceable.</p>
<h2>Wake County is aging, and the housing stock is turning over</h2>
<p>Thirteen point four percent of Wake County residents are 65 or older, per Census QuickFacts, against a county of 1,257,235 people as of July 1, 2025 and 528,746 housing units as of a year earlier. Eighty-four point four percent of residents lived in the same house one year ago, which means the sixteen percent who moved make up a very large absolute number in a county that grew by 27,760 in twelve months. Practically, that means the retirement communities, the independent-living buildings and the family homes in Five Points and Hayes Barton are all trading hands in the same season, and the moving capacity for a mid-August or end-of-month date is genuinely scarce. Booking early is not upselling. It is the only way to get a weekday date, which is also the cheaper rate band.</p>
<h2>Reassembly is included, and that matters more here</h2>
<p>Rule 39 puts disassembling set-up furniture at the origin and setting it back up at the destination inside the transportation rate, along with the carries at both ends. For a downsizing move this is the single most valuable clause in the tariff, because the difference between a completed move and a chaotic one is whether the bed is assembled and made that night. Say out loud which three pieces must be usable by evening: the bed, one chair, and the table. Those come off the truck last at origin and go in first at destination, and they get set up before anything else is unwrapped.</p>
<h2>Access at the destination building</h2>
<p>Independent-living and continuing-care buildings run their own move-in rules, and they are stricter than an apartment complex. Expect a reserved elevator window, a required certificate of insurance naming the community, a designated dock or entrance, and a hard stop in the late afternoon. Published guidance for one downtown Raleigh high-rise, a 23-floor building of 352 units, describes calling the concierge desk at least 48 hours ahead for a freight elevator reservation, Saturday slots filling one to two weeks out, and move hours of 8 a.m. to 5 p.m. Monday through Saturday. Whatever the building, get its rules in writing before the date is set, because the building's window, not the crew's availability, is the real constraint.</p>
<h2>The week before, and the day itself</h2>
<p>Pack a single clearly marked carton that rides in the car: medications, chargers, glasses, a change of clothes, the coffee maker, and the paperwork. Confirm the inventory sheet is read and corrected at origin rather than signed unread. Plan for the person moving to be somewhere comfortable during the load rather than supervising a driveway in July heat, when Raleigh-Durham's normal daily high sits at or above 90 degrees from July 2 through July 27. Then set the bed, the chair and the lamp first, and leave the rest for tomorrow.</p>

## svc_single_item_moving_lede

One heavy piece is not a small move. It is a short move with all the same access problems and a different set of tariff charges, and shipments under a thousand pounds lose one protection outright.

## svc_single_item_moving_body

<h2>Under a thousand pounds, the estimate cap does not apply</h2>
<p>Rule 13 caps final billed charges at 120 percent of the estimated amount, with any excess requiring a signed change order. It then excludes two situations, and one of them is a shipment weighing less than 1,000 pounds. A sectional sofa, a treadmill, a china cabinet or a single armoire almost always sits under that weight, so the percentage guardrail is not available for a one-item job. What replaces it is specificity. Get the charge structure in writing: hourly with a stated minimum, or a flat item charge, plus any stair and access charges named separately. The tariff still limits the maximums under Rule 2, so the figures below are ceilings you can check a quote against.</p>
<h2>Which single items have their own published charge</h2>
<p>The tariff's bulky article item names a surprising number of household pieces and prices loading and unloading together, including the handling and blocking of the article. A television over 40 inches is $179.90. Home gym equipment, listed as electronic stairsteppers, tanning beds, Nordic tracks and Bowflexes, is $107.15. A satellite disc four feet or larger is $174.80. A motorcycle, go cart or riding mower under 25 horsepower is $107.15. A playhouse, tool shed or utility shed transported set up and over 100 cubic feet is $202.85. Anything else over 400 pounds that is not specifically named is $151.40. A separate item prices pianos and organs by height and a slate-top pool table at least 42 inches wide, 84 inches long and over 400 pounds at $128.25. If your piece is on that list, the ceiling is published.</p>
<h2>Four hundred pounds is the line that changes the job</h2>
<p>Rule 35(B) removes from the tariff's ordinary services the handling, loading or unloading of any single article weighing 400 pounds or more. Above that weight the extra handling is furnished by the shipper, or by the carrier at the bulky article charges when it has the additional personnel and equipment available, and any bracing or blocking material that is not part of the vehicle's regular equipment must be furnished by the shipper. This is why a genuinely heavy single item is quoted as a small crew with equipment rather than as two people and a dolly. Weigh the assumption before the day: a solid-slate armoire, a full-size gun safe, a commercial treadmill and a laden chest freezer all clear that line more often than people expect.</p>
<h2>Stairs are billed by flight, and the definitions are exact</h2>
<p>Inside a building, the maximum flight carry is $33.55 for the first flight and $16.85 for each additional one, where the first flight means at least eight steps, additional flights are complete floors above or below, and an elevator counts as one flight. Outside a building the first flight is 8 but not more than 20 steps at $32.15, and each step past twenty adds sixty cents. Fewer than eight exterior steps is not a flight. Count both ends before quoting, because a single item that comes down one interior flight at origin and up six exterior steps at destination is two separate carry events.</p>
<h2>The truck still has to park legally for one item</h2>
<p>A single-item move gets treated casually and that is where the ticket comes from. All downtown areas including Glenwood South require meter payment from 8 a.m. to 6 p.m. Monday through Friday, the Hillsborough District near NC State requires payment from 8 a.m. to 8 p.m. Monday through Friday, and parking regulations are enforced 24 hours every day regardless of the payment window. A meter or time-zone violation is twenty dollars, with a twenty dollar late fee if it goes unpaid past 21 days, and Raleigh Parking field agents work downtown, the Hillsborough Street corridor and residential permit areas. Requests for parking passes for moving vans go to Raleigh Parking directly. Ten minutes of curb planning is cheaper than the fine and much cheaper than a long carry.</p>
<h2>Oakwood doors, Boylan Heights curbs</h2>
<p>Access in Raleigh's inner neighborhoods is the whole difficulty on a one-item job. The Oakwood historic district was developed primarily during the fifty-year period from 1880 to 1930 and is described as Raleigh's only intact 19th century neighborhood, with larger two-story homes flanked by one-story cottages. Original stairs, original landings and original door openings were not sized for a modern sectional or a 75-inch television carton. Boylan Heights, laid out beginning in 1907, has typical lot frontages of 30 to 50 feet, an average lot depth of 130 feet, front setbacks of 15 to 30 feet, and a main street that runs in an S curve for six blocks. Measure the narrowest point on the path, not the widest, and measure the piece with its feet and its packaging on.</p>
<h2>Practical preparation for one piece</h2>
<p>Measure the item in all three dimensions, then measure every door, turn, stair width and landing on the route at both addresses. Photograph the piece from four sides before it is wrapped, since a one-item claim rests entirely on origin condition. Remove doors, drawers, glass, legs and feet, and bag hardware taped to the frame. Pad first and wrap over the pads rather than wrapping directly onto a finished surface, which matters in a market where 33 stations recorded record-high average July dew points in 2025. Then decide, honestly, whether the piece fits. Knowing it does not is worth more than discovering it on the landing.</p>

## svc_storage_and_moving_lede

When the closing dates do not line up, the shipment goes into storage in transit, and North Carolina's tariff puts hard limits on how long that can last and what it costs per hundred pounds.

## svc_storage_and_moving_body

<h2>One hundred eighty days, once</h2>
<p>Rule 48 governs storage in transit on an intrastate move, and it is precise. A shipment may be placed in storage in transit only once, and for not more than 180 days from the date of unloading into the warehouse. At the end of that period the shipment loses its intrastate transportation character and converts to permanent storage, with the warehouse becoming the destination. The Commission's consumer booklet adds that storage beyond 180 days, and permanent storage rates generally, are not under its jurisdiction. So the day the shipment enters the warehouse starts a clock with a legal end, and a household waiting on a construction delay should know that date.</p>
<h2>Sixty days is the payment checkpoint</h2>
<p>The same rule requires that after 60 days in storage in transit, all accumulated charges of the carrier must be paid. There is also a protection in the shipper's favor worth knowing. Storage in transit is billed for each 30 days or fraction of 30 days, and if the shipper gives notice for final delivery at least 15 days before the end of a 30-day period and the carrier fails to provide the transportation within that period, storage charges do not apply beyond it. Partial withdrawals are handled separately: where a withdrawal requires unstacking or restacking of the lot, the labor is billed under Item 4.</p>
<h2>What the storage itself costs per hundred pounds</h2>
<p>Item 13 sets the storage in transit maximums on a 1,000-pound minimum weight, charged for each 30 days or fraction. Ordinary storage caps at $6.40 per 100 pounds, climate-controlled storage at $8.50, and warehouse handling, the labor of getting the lot in and out of the storage area, at $10.85. Then Item 14 prices the transportation to and from the warehouse per 100 pounds by weight bracket: $30.10 at 1,000 to 1,999 pounds, $23.50 at 2,000 to 3,999, $19.60 at 4,000 to 7,999, and $16.00 at 8,000 pounds and over. A 6,000-pound household in ordinary storage for a month is therefore a calculable number, and the arithmetic is worth doing before the decision.</p>
<h2>Climate control has a legal definition here</h2>
<p>Most storage advertising uses the phrase loosely. The tariff does not. Its note defines climate-controlled storage as meaning that adequate heating and air conditioning equipment is in place to maintain a temperature range of 60 to 85 degrees and a humidity reading of no greater than 90 percent at all times. That is a specification you can ask a warehouse to confirm. Note what it does not promise: 90 percent relative humidity is not dry. Instruments, artwork, leather and unsealed wood want a tighter band than the tariff's floor, so ask what the facility actually holds rather than what category it is billed under.</p>
<h2>Raleigh's summer air is the reason to care</h2>
<p>The humidity question is not theoretical in Wake County. NC State's climate office reported that in July 2025, 33 of its stations recorded record-high average dew points for the month of July, one station logged 34 hours with thunderstorms occurring, the most in any month since July 2004, and the statewide average temperature reached 80.4 degrees with a 102-degree reading on July 27. National Weather Service normals put the Raleigh-Durham daily high at or above 90 degrees from July 2 through July 27. Furniture and cartons that sit through a Raleigh July in an unconditioned metal building are exposed to conditions that soften glue lines, corrode metal and grow mold on upholstery. If the storage window overlaps June through September, the climate-controlled line item stops being optional.</p>
<h2>Keeping the load on the truck is a different price</h2>
<p>Sometimes the gap is two days, not two months, and Item 12 covers that case. Storage in vehicle is charged at $257.75 per shipper-requested 24-hour period per vehicle, for periods beyond the scheduled loading and unloading days, and the total cannot exceed 10 calendar days or the equivalent of one month's warehouse storage charge, whichever comes first. For a short closing gap that can beat a double handling into and out of a warehouse, because the goods are never touched again between origin and destination. For anything past a few days the arithmetic reverses quickly.</p>
<h2>Who is liable once the goods are in the building</h2>
<p>Rule 49 says that where goods are consigned to a warehouse, the warehouse is the destination and the carrier's liability ceases when the shipment is unloaded there. Rule 45(B) adds that when the carrier enters a warehouse, including self-storage or mini-storage, to remove or place items in space rented by the shipper, Item 4 labor charges apply. Those two rules together explain a common surprise: a shipment delivered into a self-storage unit you rented is a completed delivery, valuation coverage ends at that point, and anything that happens afterward is between you and the facility. If your own unit is the destination, buy the facility's coverage or your own, and take photographs of every piece as it goes in.</p>
<h2>Practical sequencing for a Raleigh closing gap</h2>
<p>Decide before load day which lot is going to storage and which is going straight into the new house, and pack them as two separate shipments with two separate inventories. Storage-bound goods want sturdier cartons, nothing perishable, and a written index by carton number. Keep the storage lot as small as the calendar honestly requires, because everything in Item 13 is priced per hundred pounds. Then confirm in writing the date the 180-day window opens and the date the 60-day payment checkpoint falls.</p>

## svc_unpacking_services_lede

Unpacking has a legal definition in North Carolina, and it is narrower than most households assume. Knowing what regular unpacking includes is the difference between a finished kitchen and a room full of empty cartons.

## svc_unpacking_services_body

<h2>What regular unpacking means, exactly</h2>
<p>The packing item in Maximum Rate Tariff No. 2 defines it in one sentence. Regular unpacking services consist of the removal of carrier-packed items from carrier-furnished materials, basic placement of items in a suitable location, and disposal of unwanted packing materials, at the time of delivery. Three things follow. It applies to cartons the carrier packed with the carrier's own materials, not boxes you packed yourself. Basic placement means on a counter or a shelf, not organized into a system. And it happens at delivery, not next week. The item then adds that specialized unpacking services may be requested by the shipper at an agreed-upon cost, which is where cabinet-by-cabinet organizing, closet setup and shelf lining actually live.</p>
<h2>Overtime unpacking is a separate rate schedule</h2>
<p>Item 1 Note 5 states that when packing and unpacking services are performed before 8 a.m. or after 5 p.m. on weekdays, or at any hour on a Saturday, Sunday or legal holiday, Item 2 rates apply. Item 2 prices the overtime work by container: a drum or dish-pack at $76.75, a box not over 5 cubic feet at $35.40, a box over 5 but not over 8 cubic feet at $62.45, a 1.5 cubic foot carton at $19.45, a 3 cubic foot carton at $31.00, and 4.5 and 6 cubic foot cartons at $37.45 each, with crates at $34.65 per cubic foot. Dish-packs are the line that moves a bill, because a kitchen produces a lot of them.</p>
<h2>Screens and clocks are priced by name</h2>
<p>Two categories have their own figures worth checking against a quote. Packing and unpacking a flat-screen television is $109.65 for a set under 40 inches diagonally and $125.50 at 40 inches or more, and a separate bulky article charge of $179.90 applies to loading and unloading a set over 40 inches. A grandfather clock carton is $99.20, and Rule 44 separately allows the carrier to engage a third party to prepare a grandfather clock, with the charge shown on the bill of lading as a third party charge in addition to everything else. Both items reward asking whether the number quoted is the packing figure, the handling figure, or both.</p>
<h2>Do the kitchen first, then the beds, then everything else</h2>
<p>The order that produces a livable house on night one is not the order the truck unloads in. Unpack to function. The kitchen comes first because it is the room that makes the house work: one shelf of dishes, the coffee setup, a pan, the utensils. Then beds, made. Then bathrooms. Then a single work surface. Everything else can wait, because putting an item away before you know where it lives means moving it twice. Keep one carton per room open at a time and flatten each empty box as it is finished.</p>
<h2>Raleigh humidity is hard on cardboard and on people</h2>
<p>Two local realities shape the unpacking day. NC State's climate office reported that 33 of its stations recorded record-high average dew points for the month of July in 2025, with a statewide July average of 80.4 degrees and one station logging 34 hours with thunderstorms occurring, the most in any month since July 2004. National Weather Service normals put the Raleigh-Durham daily high at or above 90 degrees from July 2 through July 27 and at 89.7 degrees on August 1. Cartons that sat in a truck all morning arrive damp at the seams, so they get unpacked and removed rather than stacked in a garage where they will soften and attract pests. Paper packing material absorbs that same moisture, which is a reason to get it out of the house the same day.</p>
<h2>The disposal half of the definition is doing real work</h2>
<p>Disposal of unwanted packing materials is written into the tariff's own definition of regular unpacking, and it is the part households value most after the fact. A three-bedroom move generates more cardboard than a city cart holds, so decide the route in advance: hauled away by the crew, broken down across collection cycles, or taken to a drop-off. Confirm which one is included in the price rather than assuming. The same conversation should cover wardrobe boxes, mattress cartons and picture crates, which are bulky, reusable and the items most often left behind in a garage for a year.</p>
<h2>August in Raleigh compresses the whole calendar</h2>
<p>Timing an unpack is also a scheduling question, because the crews who unpack are the crews who move. Meredith College set Move-In Day and New Student Orientation for Saturday, August 15, 2026, with orientation ending August 18 and classes beginning August 19. NC State University Housing scheduled fall 2026 move-in for August 13 through 15 with appointment time slots selected in the MyPack Portal, William Peace University welcomed new students on Thursday, August 13, 2026, and Shaw University's calendar puts returning-student move-in on August 10 through 12 from 10 a.m. to 3 p.m. with no weekend move-ins. A mid-August unpacking crew is competing with four institutions' arrival weeks, and a weekend slot in that window also triggers the overtime rates.</p>
<h2>What to settle before the crew arrives</h2>
<p>Four answers. Whether the service is regular unpacking as the tariff defines it, or specialized unpacking at an agreed cost, and if the latter, what specifically is included. Whether the work falls inside 8 a.m. to 5 p.m. on a weekday, since a weekend or evening moves it to the overtime schedule. Who removes the packing material and where it goes. And the room order, decided by you in advance, so the crew works to a plan rather than opening whatever is nearest the door.</p>
