## pricing_lede

The Los Angeles Superior Court publishes what it charges for every step of a civil case, down to a court reporter's half day. California's fee rules publish no percentage at all. This page sets both sides of that out for a West Covina crash claim.

## pricing_body

### Los Angeles prices the process, not the lawyer

A civil case in Los Angeles County has a published price list, and it explains where money actually goes. The first paper in an unlimited civil case -- a complaint pleading more than $35,000 -- costs $435, and each other party pays $435 to appear. Limited civil filings cost $370 or $225 depending on the pleaded amount. Any paper requiring a hearing costs $60. A summary judgment or summary adjudication motion costs $500. Where a case is designated complex, the plaintiffs pay $1,000 and every defendant pays $1,000 more, to a per-case ceiling of $18,000.

Then come the line items that surprise people. Court reporting for a proceeding of one hour or less is $30, but a proceeding running over an hour but under four hours is $382, and one lasting four hours or more is $764. The advance jury fee is $150, nonrefundable; from the second day onward the court's deposit is $15 per juror per day plus $0.34 a mile one way. A single contested motion day with a reporter can cost more than opening the case did.

### What California actually limits about a fee

Nothing in the state's rules assigns a percentage to injury work. Rule 1.5(a) of the California Rules of Professional Conduct prohibits an unconscionable or illegal fee. Rule 1.5(b) then supplies the measuring stick. It weighs whether the lawyer engaged in fraud or overreaching in setting the fee, whether material facts were disclosed, the fee compared with the value of the services performed, and how sophisticated the client was relative to the lawyer. It also weighs the novelty and difficulty of the work, whether the case foreclosed other work, the amount involved and the result obtained, time pressure, and the length of the relationship. The last factors are the lawyer's experience and ability, whether the fee is fixed or contingent, the hours and labor involved, and whether the client gave informed consent.

Read that as a review mechanism rather than a rate schedule. The state does not pre-approve a percentage; it reserves the right to examine one afterwards.

### The malpractice statute, labeled as such

Business and Professions Code section 6146 is the only California provision that caps an injury contingency percentage, and it reaches only claims for injury or death charging a health care provider with professional negligence. In those, the ceiling is twenty-five percent of what is recovered when the matter closes by settlement and release before anyone files a complaint or demands arbitration, and thirty-three percent once it closes after that point, absent a motion granted on evidence of good cause. The percentage runs against the net recovery after costs, and the statute forbids treating a patient's own medical care charges or a firm's overhead as deductible.

A rear-end collision on Azusa Avenue or a lane-change crash on the 10 near Citrus is not a malpractice claim, so section 6146's percentages say nothing about it. Any page quoting those numbers for a car crash has mislabeled the statute.

### Section 6147 is the consumer protection that does apply

The contract does the work the statute declines to do. Section 6147(a) requires a contingency agreement in writing, signed by attorney and client alike, with a duplicate signed copy handed to the plaintiff at the outset. Three disclosures belong in the document: the rate settled on, the effect disbursements and costs have on both the fee and what the client keeps, and any compensation that could come due on related matters the contract does not cover. Unless section 6146 governs the claim, a fourth line is required, saying plainly that no statute fixes the fee and the rate remains open to negotiation. Section 6147(b) makes a defective contract voidable at the plaintiff's option, with the lawyer limited to a reasonable fee.

That negotiability line is the most useful sentence in the statute.

### Cost-shifting is narrower than most people expect

Code of Civil Procedure section 1033.5 lists what a prevailing party may recover as costs: filing, motion and jury fees, service of process, ordinary witness fees, court-ordered expert fees, statutory court reporter fees, and exhibit costs that helped the trier of fact. Taking and transcribing necessary depositions is recoverable too, along with travel to attend them. The section also lists what is not allowable without express authority: fees of experts the court did not order, investigation expenses in preparing the case, postage, telephone and photocopying outside exhibits, and transcripts the court did not order.

The biggest advances in a real file are usually on the wrong side of that line. A reconstruction or life-care expert the court never appointed is not recoverable from the defense, even in a case that wins. So the treatment of costs in the agreement matters as much as the percentage.

### The two sentences to look for in any agreement

First, whether costs are subtracted before or after the fee is calculated -- section 6147 requires the contract to address it, and the two orders produce different numbers on identical facts. Second, whether the percentage shifts at a milestone such as filing suit or setting trial, because the court fees above show why a filed case costs more to run than a pre-suit claim. Everything else on this page can be verified from the Los Angeles Superior Court's own fee schedule and from the statutes named beside each figure.

<h2>What the Hospital Lien Act permits, in figures</h2>
<p>Civil Code section 3045.1 gives a licensed hospital a lien on damages you recover, measured by its reasonable and necessary charges for treating the injury. Section 3045.3 makes that lien ineffective unless the hospital mails written notice by registered mail, return receipt requested, before any money is paid out. The notice has to name you, the accident date, the hospital, the amount claimed, and each party the hospital believes is liable. A copy goes to that party's known insurer as well. Section 3045.4 then supplies the ceiling: a payer who ignores the notice is liable for the lien, or for <a href="https://law.justia.com/codes/california/code-civ/division-3/part-4/title-14/chapter-4/section-3045-4/">so much as can be satisfied from 50 percent</a> of the money due under the judgment or settlement after prior liens are paid.</p>
<h2>The half that is supposed to stay with you</h2>
<p>Read that ceiling as a floor under your own recovery. A Court of Appeal decision in 2023 described the statute as securing part of the recovery for the hospital bill while making sure the patient keeps enough to cover other losses. The same case is a warning about how these get closed out. An insurer mailed a check naming both the patient and the hospital, nobody cashed it, and the court held that <a href="https://caselaw.findlaw.com/court/ca-court-of-appeal/115099230.html">delivering such a check is not payment</a> of the lien. Section 3045.5 lets the hospital sue the payer within one year after money goes to the injured person. Section 3045.6 carves out claims against a common carrier under Public Utilities Commission or Interstate Commerce Commission jurisdiction.</p>
<h2>The first week, in order</h2>
<p>Photograph both vehicles and the roadway before repairs begin. Identify the agency that responded, since a collision on the freeway and one on a city street are usually written by different departments. Ask the emergency department for an itemized statement rather than a balance, because the itemization is what any charge argument later runs on. Request footage from the nearest business or apartment property while it still exists. Keep every envelope a hospital or health plan sends, including the ones that look like ordinary billing. Those envelopes are the lien record.</p>
<h2>The mistake that costs money in this kind of case</h2>
<p>Treating the hospital's charge as the value of the care. California measures past medical damages as the lesser of the amount paid or incurred and the reasonable value of the services, and the sum a provider bills is <a href="https://law.justia.com/cases/california/court-of-appeal/2014/b247493.html">not itself relevant to that figure</a>. So a five-figure chargemaster number can sit next to a much smaller settled amount, and the difference is negotiating room a claimant only has if the itemization was collected. The second mistake is signing a property-damage release without reading whether it reaches the injury claim too.</p>

<p>The opening pass is a document list: crash date and location, report number, photographs, the itemized hospital statement, any lien notice, and the declarations pages. ## svc_uninsured_driver_claim_lawyer_lede

## svc_uninsured_driver_claim_lawyer_body

<h2>The rule that decides what your claim contains</h2>
<p>Civil Code section 3333.4 reaches any action arising out of the operation or use of a motor vehicle. In such an action it bars recovery of noneconomic losses, meaning pain, suffering, inconvenience, physical impairment, disfigurement and other nonpecuniary damages, when one of three things is true. The injured person was convicted of driving under the influence in that crash. Or the injured person owned a vehicle involved and it was not insured as the financial responsibility laws require. Or the injured person was operating a vehicle and cannot establish financial responsibility. Subdivision (b) also stops an insurer from indemnifying those noneconomic losses, directly or indirectly, under liability or uninsured motorist coverage.</p>
<h2>The exceptions, stated precisely</h2>
<p>Two limits keep the section from swallowing everything. Subdivision (c) restores noneconomic damages to an uninsured owner who was hurt by a motorist convicted of drunk driving in the same collision. And the California Supreme Court held in 1999 that the measure reaches motor vehicle claims, not every claim a crash produces, so a <a href="https://law.justia.com/cases/california/supreme-court/4th/21/109.html">products claim against a manufacturer falls outside it</a>. The court described the section as enacted by the voters at the 1996 General Election as part of Proposition 213. Economic damages survive in every version: medical charges, wage loss, and the cost of care still needed.</p>
<h2>Why the bills then become the entire argument</h2>
<p>Strip out pain and suffering and what remains is a pile of medical numbers that other parties already have claims against. That is where an uninsured-driver file gets decided. Civil Code section 3040 caps what a Knox-Keene health plan or a disability insurer may take from the recovery. The lien cannot exceed what the plan actually paid providers, plus its reasonable costs of perfecting the lien. If you have an attorney it also cannot exceed one-third of the money due you, and if you do not it cannot exceed one-half. Two more subdivisions matter: where a judge, jury or arbitrator finds you partly at fault, the lien drops by the same comparative percentage, and the lien takes a <a href="https://law.justia.com/codes/california/code-civ/division-3/part-4/title-14/chapter-3-5/section-3040/">pro rata reduction for fees and costs</a> under the common fund doctrine. Hospital liens and Medi-Cal are expressly outside that section.</p>
<h2>If Medi-Cal paid for the treatment</h2>
<p>The state program runs on its own arithmetic and its own calendar. A member or representative must report an action or claim to the Department of Health Care Services, and the agency asks for that notice within 30 days of filing. Welfare and Institutions Code section 14124.72 reduces the director's lien by <a href="https://law.justia.com/codes/california/code-wic/division-9/part-3/chapter-7/article-3-5/section-14124-72/">25 percent as its share of attorney fees</a> where the beneficiary alone brought the claim, plus a proportional slice of litigation expenses. Section 14124.78 caps the recovery so the director never takes more than the beneficiary keeps after fees and costs. Section 14124.76 confines recovery to the portion of the settlement representing payment for medical care and points courts to the Ahlborn decision. The agency also waits 120 days from settlement or last treatment before ordering payment data, because providers have up to a year from the date of service to bill.</p>
<h2>Coverage on your own side of the ledger</h2>
<p>An absent or uninsured driver does not always mean an absent policy. Vehicle ownership, permissive use, an employer's vehicle, a rental agreement, and your own uninsured motorist coverage are separate questions answered from documents rather than from what was said at the curb. For scale, the floor set by Insurance Code section 11580.1b is <a href="https://www.dmv.ca.gov/portal/vehicle-registration/insurance-requirements/">$30,000 per injured person</a>, doubling to $60,000 when two or more are hurt, with property damage at $15,000. A single overnight hospital stay can exceed the first of those numbers, which is why the search for additional policies happens early rather than after a demand is sent.</p>
<h2>The mistake that costs the case</h2>
<p>Accepting a quick payment before anyone has checked whether section 3333.4 applies and what the liens are. Registration status on the crash date, the policy in force, and any lapse notice from the carrier are provable facts, and they are worth establishing before conceding the point. The other frequent error is silence toward a public program. Notice is required, and the limitations on the state's claim are tolled until it learns that every claim tied to the injury is resolved.</p>
<h2>After you make contact, and how the money works</h2>
<p>The file opens with paperwork rather than a narrative: registration, declarations pages, any lapse or reinstatement notice, the crash report, itemized medical statements, and each lien or reimbursement letter received. Deadlines run in the background, two years for an injury action, and only six months to present a claim if a public agency is involved. As for fees, no statute assigns a percentage to a vehicle case in this state. The professional conduct rule forbids an unconscionable or illegal fee, and section 6147 requires the rate, the treatment of costs, and the fact that the rate is negotiable to appear in a signed written contract.</p>

<h2>How you know an employer plan is in your file</h2>
<p>Photograph the doors, the trailer, and the placards, then look at your own insurance card. If your coverage came through a job rather than a policy you bought, the summary plan description will contain a reimbursement or subrogation paragraph, and that paragraph is the one to read before anything is negotiated. Ask the benefits office one question: is the plan self-funded, or is it insured by a carrier? The answer changes which rules apply to the money you recover.</p>
<h2>Why a self-funded plan is treated differently</h2>
<p>Federal benefits law preempts state law about employee benefit plans, and its saving clause hands insurance regulation back to the states. The bridge between the two is the deemer clause, and the Supreme Court read it in 1990 to mean a self-funded plan cannot be <a href="https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/498/52/">deemed an insurer for that purpose</a>. The case struck down the application of a Pennsylvania antisubrogation statute to a company's salaried health care plan. The practical consequence in this county is a split. A claimant with an insured plan can point to Civil Code section 3040 and its one-third ceiling. A claimant on a self-funded plan usually cannot, because that section does not make a lien enforceable where federal law preempts it.</p>
<h2>What the plan's own wording does</h2>
<p>In 2013 the Court took up a plan that had paid $66,866 for an employee's crash injuries against a recovery of $110,000, assembled from a $10,000 payment by the at-fault driver and $100,000 from the employee's own insurer. The fee agreement was 40 percent, so $44,000 went to counsel. The Court held that in an action to enforce an equitable lien by agreement the plan's terms govern, and neither the double-recovery rule nor the common fund rule can override them. Then came the sentence worth remembering. Because the plan said nothing about the cost of recovery, <a href="https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/569/88/">the common fund doctrine filled that gap</a> as the default rule for attorney fees. Silence helps the claimant; explicit drafting does not.</p>
<h2>If Medicare paid any of it</h2>
<p>A federal formula, not a negotiation, sets the reduction. Under 42 CFR 411.37, where Medicare paid less than the settlement, the ratio of procurement costs to the total settlement is applied to the Medicare payment, and that share of costs is <a href="https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/42/411.37">subtracted from what Medicare recovers</a>. Where the payments equal or exceed the settlement, recovery is the settlement minus total procurement costs. The calendar is unforgiving. The recovery contractor issues a conditional payment letter within 65 days of the rights and responsibilities letter, and a conditional payment notice must be answered within 30 calendar days or a demand issues <a href="https://www.cms.gov/medicare/coordination-benefits-recovery/beneficiary-services/recovery-process">without any reduction for fees or costs</a>. Disputes take 45 days, interest runs from the demand, and referral to the Treasury follows 150 days after it.</p>
<h2>The corridor these files come from</h2>
<p>Freight and commuter traffic collide here because the routes overlap. Just east of the city, the interchange where State Route 57 meets State Route 60 carries more than 356,000 vehicles a day, roughly 100 million a year, including an estimated 26,000 goods-carrying trucks. The $444.12 million <a href="https://www.sgvcog.org/5760project">confluence project began construction in July 2023</a> and is scheduled to finish in July 2028, adding lanes, rebuilding the Grand Avenue bridge, and separating movements that currently weave. West of that, the I-10 bridge-preservation closures shift heavy vehicles onto surface streets on weekends. Both conditions belong in a crash description, along with direction, lane, and time.</p>
<h2>If a public agency is anywhere in the picture</h2>
<p>A signal, a striping decision, a road defect, or an agency vehicle changes the deadline dramatically. Government Code section 911.2 gives an injury claim <a href="https://law.justia.com/codes/california/code-gov/title-1/division-3-6/part-3/chapter-2/article-1/section-911-2/">six months from accrual</a> to reach the entity, and twelve months for other categories. Claims presented to the Department of General Services carry a $25 filing fee, or an affidavit requesting a waiver. Six months, not two years, is the number that quietly ends these cases.</p>
<h2>After you make contact, and how costs and fees work</h2>
<p>Early work is documentary: a preservation letter to the carrier, the collision report, vehicle inspection before repair or release, the summary plan description, and every reimbursement letter. Those are costs, and costs are not fees. On fees, section 6147 requires the injury contract to be written and signed by both sides, to explain how disbursements affect both the fee and what you keep, and to say that no law fixes the rate. The 25 and 33 percent figures people repeat come from section 6146, which applies to claims against health care providers rather than to a truck case. Court charges stand apart, including $500 for a summary judgment motion and a $150 nonrefundable advance jury deposit.</p>

## svc_injury_claim_negotiation_lede

An offer is a gross number. What matters is the net, and the net depends on who else has a claim to the same dollars: a hospital, a health plan, a public program, or a medical financing company that bought your bill.

## svc_injury_claim_negotiation_body

<h2>The number nobody prints on the offer letter</h2>
<p>Gross settlement minus fees, minus case costs, minus every lien and reimbursement claim, equals what reaches your bank. Two files with identical gross figures can end thousands apart because of what sat in the middle. So the useful first question about an offer is not whether it feels fair. It is who is standing in line behind it, and what each of them is legally allowed to take.</p>
<h2>Who is standing in line</h2>
<p>The list is usually shorter than people fear and never empty. A treating hospital may hold a statutory lien reaching up to half of the money due after prior liens. A health plan governed by state insurance law is capped by Civil Code section 3040 at what it actually paid, and at one-third of your recovery when you are represented. Medi-Cal, Medicare, and self-funded employer plans each run on their own rules. Medical payments coverage on your own auto policy has a contractual reimbursement right rather than a claim against the other driver. And a provider who treated on a lien may have sold that account to a financing company at a discount.</p>
<h2>The apportionment rules that shrink each claim</h2>
<p>California has long made a passive beneficiary of a recovery contribute to the cost of creating it. The Supreme Court applied that idea to a workers' compensation carrier in 1975, directing courts to fix a reasonable fee first and <a href="https://law.justia.com/cases/california/supreme-court/3d/15/162.html">then apportion it between the beneficiaries</a> of the recovery. In 2009 the Court worked the same logic through a small automobile file. The insurer had paid $1,000 in medical payments benefits, the case settled for $6,000, and fees and costs came to about $2,106.50. Because the med-pay recovery was one-sixth of the settlement, $400 of those fees were charged against it, and accepting $600 <a href="https://law.justia.com/cases/california/supreme-court/2009/s154790/">discharged the insurer's obligation</a>. Order matters too: an attorney lien for fees and costs takes <a href="https://caselaw.findlaw.com/court/ca-court-of-appeal/1343433.html">priority over a medical lien</a> regardless of which came first.</p>
<h2>Billed is not paid, and the gap is negotiable</h2>
<p>Past medical damages are limited to the lesser of the amount paid or incurred and the reasonable value of the services, so a chargemaster figure is not the measure of the loss. Where a provider sold its account to a factor, an appellate court allowed discovery into that transaction, noting that what a factor pays reflects the expense and risk of collection rather than the worth of the treatment. Practical effect: the itemized statement, the plan's remittance advice, and the assignment paperwork are all leverage, and they are easier to obtain before a release is signed than after.</p>
<h2>Fault percentages travel down the whole stack</h2>
<p>This state assigns responsibility in direct proportion to each party's negligence, the rule adopted when the older all-or-nothing bar was discarded in 1975. A finding that you were 20 percent responsible cuts the award, and under section 3040 a special finding of comparative fault cuts a covered health plan lien by the same percentage. That is why a defense theory about following distance or a lane change is worth answering with photographs and measurements rather than an estimate spoken aloud into a recorded statement.</p>
<h2>If talks fail, where a case from this city goes</h2>
<p>Los Angeles County is not one courthouse. The <a href="https://www.lacourt.ca.gov/courthouse/info/address/cit">West Covina Courthouse at 1427 West Covina Parkway</a> sits in the East Judicial District and handles civil, criminal, small claims, and traffic matters, with the courthouse open from 8:00 a.m. to 4:30 p.m. and the clerk's office from 8:30. Pomona civil cases were moved to this courthouse in 2012. The Court also cautions that the district covering an address does not by itself decide where a case is assigned, so case type and local rules get checked before a complaint is filed.</p>
<h2>After you make contact, and how a fee is set</h2>
<p>Assembly comes before argument: records and itemized statements requested, limits confirmed from declarations pages, every lien identified and priced against its own ceiling, then a demand built from documents. Nothing here is settled from a recorded phone call. On fees, no California statute sets a percentage for a crash claim. Rule 1.5 measures a fee against an unconscionability standard. Business and Professions Code section 6147 requires a signed written contract, a duplicate copy in your hand, disclosure of how costs affect both the fee and your share, and a line stating that the rate is negotiable. Court charges are published separately, among them $435 for a first paper in an unlimited civil case and $60 for a motion requiring a hearing.</p>

## svc_back_and_spine_injury_claims_lede

Spine claims are decided on future numbers rather than past ones. The bills already paid are the smallest part of the file, and the projections are the part insurers contest hardest.

## svc_back_and_spine_injury_claims_body

<h2>The two columns a spine case lives in</h2>
<p>Under Civil Code section 1431.2, one column holds objectively verifiable monetary losses. The statute's examples include medical costs, lost earnings, loss of use of property, repair or replacement costs, the price of substitute domestic services, and lost employment or business opportunity. The other column holds the subjective losses, which the statute enumerates as pain and suffering, inconvenience, mental suffering, emotional distress, lost society and companionship, lost consortium, reputational injury and humiliation. A herniated disc that ends a warehouse career loads both columns, and the statute makes only the noneconomic column several, meaning each defendant owes it strictly in proportion to that defendant's percentage of fault, while the economic column can be pursued in full from any defendant found partly responsible.</p>
<h2>Future care is a projection, and projections have rules</h2>
<p>Civil Code section 3333 authorizes compensation for all detriment proximately caused, including detriment not yet incurred. What limits a future medical figure is the evidence it rests on. A 2013 Court of Appeal decision held that an expert testifying about the reasonable value of future medical services <a href="https://law.justia.com/cases/california/court-of-appeal/2013/b236227.html">may not rely on the full amounts billed</a> for the plaintiff's past care, because billed sums that no one expected to collect are not relevant to value. That ruling shapes how a life care plan or surgical cost projection has to be built: on rates actually paid in the relevant market, not on chargemaster arithmetic scaled forward.</p>
<h2>The pre-existing condition argument, and what defeats it</h2>
<p>Degenerative disc changes appear on imaging in a large share of adults with no symptoms, so almost every spine file draws the argument that the findings predate the crash. The response is comparative rather than rhetorical: prior imaging if it exists, an employment history showing full duty before the collision, and a functional record afterward. California law does not reduce damages because a claimant was more vulnerable than average; it asks what detriment this collision proximately caused. Where prior treatment exists, the aggravation is the claim, and the records that establish a stable baseline become the most valuable documents in the file.</p>
<h2>Where serious injuries get treated in this area</h2>
<p>There is no designated trauma center in West Covina. The state list of designated and verified trauma centers shows the nearest facilities serving this part of the county include <a href="https://emsa.ca.gov/wp-content/uploads/sites/71/2025/12/California-Designated-ACS-Verified-Trauma-Centers-12.02.25.pdf">Pomona Valley Hospital Medical Center at adult Level II and Huntington Memorial Hospital at Level II</a>, with Level I centers at Los Angeles General Medical Center, Harbor-UCLA, Cedars-Sinai and Ronald Reagan UCLA. Within the city, Emanate Health Queen of the Valley Hospital is a general acute care facility of <a href="https://hcai.ca.gov/facility/citrus-valley-medical-center-qv-campus/">325 licensed beds with a basic emergency room service level</a> at 1115 South Sunset Avenue. For a spine injury that consequence is practical: an ambulance may transport out of the city, records will exist at more than one facility, and a complete chart requires requests to each.</p>
<h2>Severity, in the local record</h2>
<p>The City's roadway safety plan recorded 29 fatal and 75 severe-injury collisions over five years, together 2.1 percent of all collisions, and assigned an economic cost factor of <a href="https://records.westcovina.org/WebLink/DocView.aspx?id=68598&amp;dbid=0&amp;repo=WestCovina">$2.19 million to each fatal or severe-injury collision</a>. Broadside impacts, the city's leading collision type at 31 percent, load the spine differently than rear impacts because there is less crush distance in the side structure. Where a claim involves surgical intervention, those cost factors are worth reading alongside the statutory minimum liability limits, because the gap between them is what first-party coverage exists to bridge.</p>
<h2>Wage loss is a document problem</h2>
<p>Loss of earnings and loss of earning capacity are separate items, and the second is harder. Pay records, tax filings, employer statements about duty restrictions and, in physical trades, testimony about what the job actually requires are the raw material. A claimant who returns to work at reduced hours or in a lighter role is documenting a continuing loss whether or not anyone labels it that way. Self-employed claimants face the heaviest documentation burden, since the loss must be reconstructed from filings and contracts rather than from a paystub series.</p>
<h2>Deadlines run while treatment continues</h2>
<p>The two-year deadline for injury actions in Code of Civil Procedure section 335.1 does not pause because a claimant is still under care or awaiting a surgical decision. Where a public entity may share responsibility for a roadway condition, the presentation window is six months under Government Code section 911.2, far shorter than the court deadline. Filing before a prognosis is settled is a common necessity rather than a sign of haste, and it does not close out future damages.</p>
<h2>If a spine injury is the center of a file</h2>
<p>The useful first set is every treating facility name, the complete imaging record, the surgical recommendation if one exists, employment records covering the year before and after the crash, and the itemized statements of amounts paid. ## svc_concussion_and_head_injuries_lede

A concussion often appears in a collision report as a complaint of pain and in the emergency chart as a normal scan. Both entries are consistent with a real injury, and both get used to argue there was none.

## svc_concussion_and_head_injuries_body

<h2>The record that gets created at the scene</h2>
<p>Collision reports code injury severity in categories, and the milder category is where head injuries frequently land, because the visible evidence at a curbside is limited. The City's roadway safety plan assigned an economic cost factor of <a href="https://records.westcovina.org/WebLink/DocView.aspx?id=68598&amp;dbid=0&amp;repo=WestCovina">$80,900 to a collision recorded as complaint of pain</a> and $142,300 to one recorded as other visible injury, which shows how much weight the classification carries in the systems that price these events. A claimant whose symptoms developed over the following days is arguing against an initial coding decision made in minutes, and the way to argue against it is with a medical timeline rather than an objection to the officer's judgment.</p>
<h2>What normal imaging does and does not establish</h2>
<p>A computed tomography scan in an emergency department is performed to rule out bleeding and fracture. A normal result does not exclude a mild traumatic brain injury, because the diagnosis is clinical: mechanism of injury, alteration of consciousness or memory, and a symptom set including headache, light sensitivity, sleep disruption, irritability and cognitive slowing. The documents that carry a concussion claim are therefore the follow-up records: a primary care or neurology note describing symptom progression, any neuropsychological testing, and observations from people who see the claimant daily. Because the injury shows up in function rather than on film, the absence of a written record is indistinguishable from the absence of an injury.</p>
<h2>Where the treatment record will be scattered</h2>
<p>Within this city, Emanate Health Queen of the Valley Hospital operates as a general acute care hospital with <a href="https://hcai.ca.gov/facility/citrus-valley-medical-center-qv-campus/">a basic emergency room service level</a> at 1115 South Sunset Avenue. Higher-acuity head trauma is routed to designated trauma centers, and the state's designation list shows none in West Covina, with the nearest options including <a href="https://emsa.ca.gov/wp-content/uploads/sites/71/2025/12/California-Designated-ACS-Verified-Trauma-Centers-12.02.25.pdf">Pomona Valley Hospital Medical Center and Huntington Memorial at adult Level II</a> and several Level I centers in central Los Angeles. That routing means a serious head injury generates records at an ambulance provider, a receiving hospital, possibly a transferring hospital, and later outpatient specialists, each requiring its own authorization to release.</p>
<h2>The damages the statute contemplates</h2>
<p>Civil Code section 1431.2 lists among noneconomic damages pain, suffering, inconvenience, mental suffering, emotional distress and loss of enjoyment of life, and among economic damages loss of earnings and loss of employment opportunities. A head injury tends to load both unusually: the economic loss may arrive as reduced work capacity in a cognitively demanding job rather than as missed shifts, and the noneconomic loss is described by family members rather than measured. Full billed amounts cannot be used as an argumentative construct to help a jury set noneconomic damages, a Court of Appeal held in 2013, <a href="https://law.justia.com/cases/california/court-of-appeal/2013/b236227.html">and are inadmissible for that purpose</a>, so the qualitative record does the work instead.</p>
<h2>Delayed onset against a fixed deadline</h2>
<p>Symptoms that surface a week after the crash do not extend the two-year deadline in Code of Civil Procedure section 335.1, which runs from the date of injury for an action based on the wrongful act or neglect of another. Section 352 tolls the period while a claimant is a minor or lacks legal capacity to make decisions, with an express exception for claims against public entities. Insurance regulations require an insurer to give written notice of a limitation period it may rely on <a href="https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/california/10-CCR-2695.7">at least 60 days before expiration</a>, reduced to 30 days for a first-party uninsured motorist matter, but not where the claimant is represented by counsel. Nobody should treat that regulatory notice as a reminder service.</p>
<h2>Practical documentation that tends to matter</h2>
<p>A dated symptom log kept by the claimant, not reconstructed later. Written statements from a supervisor or coworker about performance changes. Records of accommodations, missed meetings, or dropped responsibilities. Screen-time and driving restrictions imposed by a provider. Where a claimant had prior headaches or a prior concussion, the comparison of before and after is more persuasive than a claim of no prior history, and easier to defend when the prior record surfaces in discovery, as it generally does.</p>

<p>The first documents worth gathering are the collision report and its injury coding, ambulance and emergency records, every follow-up note, and any employer documentation of changed duties. ## svc_dealing_with_insurance_adjusters_lede

An adjuster is not a neutral party, and also not an adversary without rules. California regulations put day counts on nearly every step, and those day counts are the most useful thing an unrepresented claimant has.

## svc_dealing_with_insurance_adjusters_body

<h2>The regulated timeline, in days</h2>
<p>California claims regulations require an insurer to acknowledge receipt of notice of claim <a href="https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/california/10-CCR-2695.5">within 15 calendar days</a> and to respond within 15 calendar days to communications from a claimant that reasonably suggest a response is expected. Within 40 calendar days of receiving a proof of claim, the insurer must <a href="https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/california/10-CCR-2695.7">accept or deny the claim in whole or in part</a>. If more time is needed, it must provide written notice of the need and then update the claimant every 30 calendar days. A denial must be in writing and state all bases relied upon, and must disclose the right to have the matter reviewed by the Department of Insurance. Upon acceptance, payment must be tendered immediately and in no event more than 30 calendar days later.</p>
<h2>What the regulations forbid outright</h2>
<p>The same regulations require a thorough, fair and objective investigation, and prohibit persisting in seeking information not reasonably required for or material to resolving the claim dispute. They prohibit delaying or denying a first-party claim on the basis that someone else should pay. They prohibit settlement offers that are unreasonably low, and list the factors the Insurance Commissioner considers, including whether the insurer weighed the claimant's evidence, the advice of its own adjuster on damages, the probable liability of its insured, and the likely jury verdict. Insurance Code section 790.03 separately lists unfair claims settlement practices, including failing to attempt in good faith to effectuate prompt, fair and equitable settlement where liability has become reasonably clear, <a href="https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=INS&amp;sectionNum=790.03.">directly advising a claimant not to obtain the services of an attorney</a>, and misleading a claimant as to the applicable statute of limitations.</p>
<h2>The recorded statement</h2>
<p>A liability adjuster for the other driver's insurer has no legal entitlement to a recorded statement from an injured claimant, and the request usually arrives within days, before treatment has been evaluated. Two categories of answer create most of the damage: speculation about speed and distance, and premature characterizations of injury such as feeling fine or being a little sore. Both convert into percentage points and valuation arguments. A first-party policy is different, because cooperation clauses and the discovery duties in Insurance Code section 11580.2 subdivision (o) impose obligations on the insured. Knowing which type of insurer is calling determines what the obligation is.</p>
<h2>Why the first offer is structured the way it is</h2>
<p>An early offer is usually calculated on the property damage and the emergency visit, before any treatment record exists, and it is paired with a release that closes the injury claim. The regulations require insurers to provide a reasonable written explanation of the basis for a compromise offer, which means an itemized breakdown can be requested. Where the offer rests on a valuation report or a medical review, the underlying document can be requested as well. An offer nobody can explain in writing is difficult to defend as reasonable under the factors listed above.</p>
<h2>The leverage that exists after suit is filed</h2>
<p>Code of Civil Procedure section 998 allows either party to serve a written offer to compromise, with cost consequences if it is not accepted and the result at trial is not better for the rejecting party. Civil Code section 3291 adds a specific consequence in personal injury cases: where a plaintiff makes a section 998 offer that the defendant does not accept and the plaintiff then obtains a more favorable judgment, the judgment <a href="https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=CIV&amp;sectionNum=3291.">bears interest at the legal rate of 10 percent per annum</a> from the date of that offer until satisfaction. The section does not apply to public entities. Those provisions are why the economics of a claim change once litigation begins.</p>
<h2>Written correspondence is the whole strategy</h2>
<p>Every regulatory day count above starts from a communication, and an undated phone call does not create a record. A short letter or email confirming what was requested, what was provided and what date it was sent converts a conversation into evidence. Where an insurer misses a deadline, the record of the missed deadline is only as good as the record of the request. Where an insurer requests the same information twice in different formats, the regulations describing repeated submissions of substantially the same information become relevant.</p>
<h2>Local timing worth knowing</h2>
<p>Los Angeles Superior Court's personal injury procedures set trial approximately 18 months after filing and require service within 60 days of the complaint, and since October 10, 2022 new personal injury cases are filed in the judicial district where the incident arose. Adjusters evaluating a West Covina claim are working against that schedule too.</p>
<h2>If negotiations have stalled</h2>
<p>The useful record is the claim number, the dates of every communication, the written offer and its stated basis, and the complete medical and property documentation already submitted. Regulatory day counts and the two-year injury deadline in Code of Civil Procedure section 335.1 run simultaneously. This page describes California regulations and statutes in general terms and is not legal advice about any negotiation. Specific questions can be raised with the attorney who advertises on this site.</p>

## svc_fault_and_comparative_negligence_lede

Fault in California is not a switch with two positions. It is a set of percentages, and the percentages are assigned by different people at different times: an officer at the curb, an adjuster in a cubicle, and, much later, a jury.

## svc_fault_and_comparative_negligence_body

<h2>Percentages, not verdicts of character</h2>
<p>The rule comes from a 1975 California Supreme Court decision that threw out the old all-or-nothing approach, under which a claimant who contributed anything to a crash recovered nothing. The court replaced it with <a href="https://law.justia.com/cases/california/supreme-court/3d/13/804.html">liability in direct proportion to fault</a>, so a person found twenty percent responsible still collects eighty percent of proven losses. There is no cutoff. A claimant assessed at seventy percent still has a claim worth thirty percent of the damages that can be proven, which is why insurers argue over ten-point increments rather than over blame in the abstract.</p>
<h2>The split that decides who pays what</h2>
<p>Civil Code section 1431.2 divides damages into two buckets and treats them differently. Economic losses -- medical costs, lost earnings, property repair, replacement services -- remain jointly owed, so any defendant found even partly at fault can be pursued for the whole economic figure. Noneconomic losses, the statute's list of pain, suffering, inconvenience, mental suffering, emotional distress, loss of enjoyment, disfigurement and physical impairment, are several only: each defendant owes them strictly in proportion to that defendant's own percentage. In a three-vehicle chain on Azusa Avenue, the practical consequence is that fighting over five percentage points changes the noneconomic figure and leaves the medical figure untouched.</p>
<h2>Where the first percentage gets written</h2>
<p>In this city, the initial account usually comes from the West Covina Police Department, whose Traffic Unit <a href="https://www.westcovina.gov/301/Traffic">finalizes the determination of fault</a> and issues citations after a collision investigation. Freeway and unincorporated-area collisions go to the California Highway Patrol's Baldwin Park Area office instead. Neither determination binds a civil case. An officer works from statements, physical evidence and applicable Vehicle Code sections in the hour after the crash; a comparative-fault analysis later works from downloaded event data, signal timing, business camera footage and sometimes a reconstruction. The two often agree. When they do not, the report is a document to be explained rather than a result to be accepted.</p>
<h2>What the local collision record suggests about apportionment</h2>
<p>The City's roadway safety plan, adopted in 2022 on five years of collision data, counted <a href="https://records.westcovina.org/WebLink/DocView.aspx?id=68598&amp;dbid=0&amp;repo=WestCovina">5,044 collisions</a> and put automobile right-of-way violations third among primary causes at 17.5 percent, behind speed and turning movements, with signal and sign violations at 14.0 percent. Roughly 88 percent of collisions happened at or near intersections. Those two facts together explain most disputed-fault files here: right-of-way and signal cases are exactly the fact patterns where two drivers give incompatible accounts and where a percentage, rather than a winner, is the honest outcome. The same document flagged aggressive driving in 40.2 percent of collisions, a category that includes the speed-plus-lane-change combination that produces shared fault.</p>
<h2>The evidence that moves a percentage</h2>
<p>Numbers move on documents, not adjectives. Signal phasing and timing records for the intersection, obtained from the agency that operates the signal. Event data recorder downloads showing pre-impact speed, braking and throttle. Roadway photographs taken before the debris is swept. Statements from independent witnesses recorded within days, while sequence is still recoverable. Vehicle Code sections that apply to the specific movement, such as the basic speed law in section 22350, which requires a speed reasonable for conditions regardless of the posted number, or section 22107, which requires a signal before turning or moving laterally when other traffic may be affected. Each item narrows the range of defensible percentages.</p>
<h2>Two mistakes that make apportionment worse</h2>
<p>The first is arguing the crash instead of the damages. A claimant who spends every conversation on whose light was green, while medical records go uncollected and a wage loss stays undocumented, may win the fault argument and still hold a small claim, because the percentage multiplies a number that was never proven. The second is treating a recorded statement as a chance to be fair. Volunteered concessions about speed, distraction or "not really looking" convert into percentage points and are difficult to walk back later. Accuracy is required. Estimation under pressure is not.</p>
<h2>If this is the argument in a specific file</h2>
<p>The material worth gathering first is the collision report number, the responding agency, photographs of both vehicles and the roadway, any citation issued, and the names of witnesses who were not in either car. Both the two-year deadline for injury actions in Code of Civil Procedure section 335.1 and the shorter windows insurers apply to their own investigations run while that collection happens. This page describes California rules in general terms; it is not legal advice and it does not evaluate anyone's crash. Questions about how fault might be apportioned in one specific collision belong with the attorney who advertises on this site, reached at the number above.</p>

## svc_freeway_and_merge_collisions_lede

A crash on Interstate 10 through West Covina is investigated by a different agency than a crash on Garvey Avenue, sits in a different evidentiary universe, and often involves lane movements the Vehicle Code describes in a single sentence.

## svc_freeway_and_merge_collisions_body

<h2>The lane-change rule that decides most merge cases</h2>
<p>Vehicle Code section 21658 requires a vehicle on a roadway divided into clearly marked lanes to be driven as nearly as practical entirely within a single lane, and prohibits moving out of that lane until the driver has ascertained the movement can be made with reasonable safety. Section 22107 adds the signal requirement: no turn or lateral movement until it can be made with reasonable safety, and then only after an appropriate signal when other traffic may be affected. Section 21804 covers vehicles entering the highway from a driveway or private property, requiring a yield to traffic close enough to constitute an immediate hazard. Section 22348 governs speed limits in designated lanes. In merge collisions the dispute is almost never about the existence of these duties; it is about which vehicle was established in the lane at the moment of contact.</p>
<h2>Who writes the report matters</h2>
<p>Collisions on the freeway and in unincorporated county territory fall to the California Highway Patrol rather than to city police. The relevant office here is the Baldwin Park Area command at <a href="https://www.chp.ca.gov/find-an-office/southern-division/525-baldwin-park/">14039 Francisquito Avenue in Baldwin Park</a>, reachable at (626) 517-8500, open weekdays from eight to five, staffed by 102 uniformed and 10 professional employees, and responsible for a service area that includes Interstate 10 and the State Route 57 and 60 interchange, which the office describes as one of the busiest in California. Requesting a freeway collision report from the city police department is a common early misstep and costs weeks.</p>
<h2>Construction changes the merge geometry</h2>
<p>Caltrans has been running the San Gabriel Valley Bridge Preservation Project through this corridor, and its closure notices describe eastbound Interstate 10 between Pacific Avenue/West Covina Parkway and Sunset Avenue reduced to two lanes for <a href="https://dot.ca.gov/caltrans-near-me/district-7/district-7-news/d7-55hr-extended-lane-closure-eb-i10-westcovina-bridge-preservation">55 hours at a time, from 10 p.m. Friday to 5 a.m. Monday</a>, with eastbound on-ramps and off-ramps at Pacific Avenue closed for the duration and the high-occupancy lane left open. Work is scheduled to continue through fall 2026. Separately, Metro and Caltrans are studying an <a href="https://www.metroexpresslanes.net/10-expresslanes-extension-project/">ExpressLanes extension</a> running east from Interstate 605 to the Los Angeles and San Bernardino county line, with West Covina inside the study area and alternatives that include converting the existing carpool lane. For a collision inside a work zone, the closure notice for that weekend, the traffic control plan and the signage in place are part of the record.</p>
<h2>The reporting duty nobody remembers at the scene</h2>
<p>Vehicle Code section 20008 requires the driver of a vehicle involved in a collision resulting in injury or death to file a written report with the Highway Patrol, or with the police department of the city where the collision occurred, within 24 hours. That obligation exists independently of whether officers responded. Section 16000 separately requires a report to the Department of Motor Vehicles within ten days when property damage exceeds one thousand dollars or anyone is injured or killed, and section 16070 authorizes the department to suspend the driving privilege of a driver who was without financial responsibility at the time, thirty days after mailing a notice of intent.</p>
<h2>Freeway evidence behaves differently</h2>
<p>Vehicles are towed to storage yards rather than left at the scene, so inspection requires locating the yard before storage fees force a disposal. Skid and yaw marks on concrete are documented by the responding officer or not at all. There is no nearby business camera; the available imagery is agency traffic monitoring, which is generally not retained for long. Commercial vehicles in the mix carry their own records, and passenger-vehicle event data recorders hold seconds of pre-impact information that vanishes with the car. Speed disputes on a freeway are resolved by downloads and physical evidence, because no witness estimate of a seventy-mile-per-hour closing speed survives cross-examination.</p>
<h2>Where the case would be filed</h2>
<p>Personal injury cases in Los Angeles County are no longer centralized. Under a general order effective in 2024, since October 10, 2022 newly filed personal injury cases must be <a href="https://lascpubstorage.blob.core.windows.net/cpw/LIBOPSCivil-119-GeneralOrderReAssignmentandTransferofPersonalInjuryCases(EffectiveMay17,2024).pdf">filed in the judicial district where the incident arose</a>, and the court has been moving from the former personal injury hub to an independent calendar model, closing the two hub departments during 2024. For a crash on the freeway through this city, that means the East District rather than the downtown courthouse, and the court's filing court locator is the tool that confirms it.</p>

<p>The first items are the report number and the Highway Patrol area office, the tow yard name and storage location, photographs taken before the vehicle moved again, the direction of travel and nearest postmile or exit, and whether the collision occurred inside an active closure. The two-year injury deadline in Code of Civil Procedure section 335.1 applies here as anywhere, and a claim implicating a public entity carries a much shorter presentation window. ## svc_hit_and_run_and_uninsured_drivers_lede

When the other driver leaves, two separate machines start running: a criminal investigation with its own priorities, and an insurance claim with a 24-hour reporting requirement that most people miss.

## svc_hit_and_run_and_uninsured_drivers_body

<h2>What the driver who left has done, in statutory terms</h2>
<p>Vehicle Code section 20001 requires the driver of a vehicle involved in a collision resulting in injury or death to stop and fulfill the duties of sections 20003 and 20004. A violation is punishable as a felony or misdemeanor, with a <a href="https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=VEH&amp;sectionNum=20001.">fine of one thousand to ten thousand dollars</a> and, where the collision resulted in death or permanent serious injury, imprisonment terms the statute sets at two, three or four years in state prison. Section 20002 governs property-damage-only collisions, requiring the driver to stop and either locate the owner or leave a written notice with name, address and a statement of the circumstances, and to notify police in the case of an unattended vehicle. These are duties on the departing driver, not conditions on the injured person's claim.</p>
<h2>The local unit that takes these cases</h2>
<p>The West Covina Police Department investigates all reported collisions within the city, and its Traffic Unit specifically handles <a href="https://www.westcovina.gov/301/Traffic">all hit-and-run and all fatal collisions</a>, finalizes the determination of fault, and issues citations. The department notes that fatal collision scenes are preserved and streets may be closed for several hours. Partial plates, paint transfer, broken trim pieces left at the scene, and directional debris fields are the material that supports identification, and they are documented at the scene or lost. On the freeway the equivalent investigation belongs to the Highway Patrol.</p>
<h2>The uninsured motorist rule with a clock on it</h2>
<p>Insurance Code section 11580.2 requires uninsured motorist coverage to be offered with every California liability policy, and subdivision (b) sets conditions for claims involving an unidentified driver. There must have been physical contact between the unknown vehicle and the insured or the insured's vehicle. The insured must have <a href="https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=INS&amp;sectionNum=11580.2.">reported the accident within 24 hours</a> to the police department of the city where it occurred, or to the sheriff or Highway Patrol in unincorporated territory. And the insured must file a sworn statement with the insurer within 30 days, stating the facts and that the insured has a cause of action against an unknown person. Miss the contact requirement and a phantom-vehicle claim generally fails; miss the reporting windows and the insurer will say so in writing.</p>
<h2>Deadlines that are shorter than the court deadline</h2>
<p>Subdivision (i) of the same statute requires that within two years of the accident date the insured either bring suit against the uninsured motorist, reach a settlement agreement with the insurer, or formally demand arbitration by certified mail. Arbitration must then be concluded within five years, absent tolling. Subdivision (c) allows the insurer to exclude coverage where the insured settled with anyone legally liable without the insurer's consent, which is why signing a release from another party first can quietly close the uninsured motorist route. Subdivision (k) requires the insurer to notify the insured of an approaching limitation date 30 days in advance unless the insured is represented by counsel, and failure to do so tolls the period by 30 days.</p>
<h2>What happens to the driver who had no insurance</h2>
<p>Vehicle Code section 16025 requires drivers involved in a collision to exchange identifying information including evidence of financial responsibility, an infraction carrying a fine of up to two hundred fifty dollars. Where a report shows a driver was not in compliance, section 16070 directs the Department of Motor Vehicles to suspend that driver's privilege thirty days after mailing a notice of intent. None of that produces money for an injured person. Minimum liability limits also explain why so many claims exceed available coverage: section 16056 required 15,000, 30,000 and 5,000 for older policies, and raises the figures to <a href="https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=VEH&amp;sectionNum=16056.">thirty thousand, sixty thousand and fifteen thousand</a> for policies issued or renewed on or after January 1, 2025.</p>
<h2>The first 48 hours in a hit-and-run</h2>
<p>Report to the agency with jurisdiction the same day, in person if possible, and obtain the report number. Photograph paint transfer, tire marks and every fragment left behind before the street is cleared. Canvass for cameras immediately, because arterial retail footage is short-lived. Notify the insurer in writing, referencing the uninsured motorist coverage, and calendar the 30-day sworn statement. Seek medical evaluation the same day so the injury record begins before the identity question is resolved, which can take months or never resolve at all.</p>
<h2>If the other driver is gone or uncovered</h2>
<p>The documents that matter are the collision report number and the investigating agency, the insurer claim number, the declarations page showing uninsured motorist limits, and the written report and sworn statement dates. Statutory windows in section 11580.2 run faster than the two-year injury deadline in Code of Civil Procedure section 335.1, and both run while an investigation continues. A specific hit-and-run or uninsured-driver question can be raised with the attorney who advertises on this site.</p>

## svc_intersection_and_left_turn_crashes_lede

Broadside collisions are the most common serious crash type in this city, and the left turn is where most of them begin. The Vehicle Code gives the turning driver a duty that does not end when the light turns green.

## svc_intersection_and_left_turn_crashes_body

<h2>The yield duty, in the statute's own terms</h2>
<p>Vehicle Code section 21801 puts the burden on the driver intending a left turn or a U-turn: oncoming traffic near enough to be a hazard has the right of way, and the yield continues until the movement can be completed with reasonable safety. Subdivision (b) then flips the burden: once the turning driver has yielded and begun the turn, approaching drivers must yield to the turning vehicle. Section 21800 covers uncontrolled intersections, giving priority to the vehicle on the right when two arrive at approximately the same time, and section 21453 requires a stop at a steady circular red and prohibits entering the intersection until a green appears. Section 22450 sets the stop-line rule at posted stop signs.</p>
<h2>Why a permissive green is the whole dispute</h2>
<p>A left turn made on a solid green ball is legal and also unprotected. The turning driver is responsible for judging the approaching vehicle's distance and speed, so the entire case becomes a question of what could reasonably be judged from the turn pocket. That is why signal phasing records matter more than statements. If the intersection ran a protected left arrow at that time of day, the analysis changes completely; if it ran permissive, the argument moves to sight lines, approach speed and whether the opposing driver entered on a stale yellow. The physical evidence for that argument is the impact geometry: the point of contact on each vehicle and the final rest positions.</p>
<h2>The city is rebuilding these intersections for a reason</h2>
<p>West Covina obtained a Highway Safety Improvement Program award of <a href="https://www.westcovina.gov/238/Highway-Safety-Improvements-Program-HSIP">$3,184,700 in state funds with no local match</a> for signal improvements at ten intersections, with protected left-turn phasing identified as the major improvement at Sunset Avenue and Merced Avenue, Cameron Avenue and Orange Avenue, Vincent Avenue and Puente Avenue, Lark Ellen Avenue and Badillo Avenue, Hollenbeck Avenue and Cameron Avenue, and Sentous Avenue and La Puente Road. For a collision that happened before that work was completed at a given location, the phasing in place on the crash date is the relevant fact, and the grant record is a useful indicator of which junctions were operating on permissive phasing.</p>
<h2>What the local data shows about broadsides</h2>
<p>The City's roadway safety plan recorded broadside as the leading collision type at <a href="https://records.westcovina.org/WebLink/DocView.aspx?id=68598&amp;dbid=0&amp;repo=WestCovina">31 percent</a>, with improper turning second among primary causes at 19.5 percent, and reported that 31.3 percent of fatal and severe-injury collisions in the city involved intersections against a statewide figure of 23.8 percent. Individual locations skew far higher than the citywide average: Valinda Avenue at Cameron Avenue produced 48 collisions of which 48 percent were broadside and six were head-on, Lark Ellen Avenue at Badillo Street produced 41 collisions with broadsides at 66 percent, and Citrus Street at Cortez Street produced 26 collisions with broadsides at 62 percent. A claim arising at one of those junctions sits inside a documented pattern.</p>
<h2>The occupant-side problem</h2>
<p>Broadside impacts load the side structure, where there is far less crush distance than in a front or rear collision, and they frequently involve occupants other than the drivers. That produces two separate consequences. Injuries tend to be more severe at the same closing speeds, and multiple claimants tend to compete for a single liability limit. Where one policy has to cover several injured people, the sequence in which claims are documented and presented becomes a practical issue, and Civil Code section 1431.2's rule that noneconomic damages are owed severally in proportion to fault becomes the arithmetic that shapes each person's share.</p>
<h2>Evidence with an expiration date</h2>
<p>Signal timing data is retained by the operating agency for a limited period, and the phase in effect on a particular date must be requested rather than assumed. Business and residential camera footage near a signalized arterial is typically overwritten within days to a few weeks. The vehicles themselves hold pre-impact speed and braking data that disappears when they are crushed. Independent witnesses at intersections are usually other drivers stopped on the opposing approach, and their contact information exists only in the collision report or nowhere.</p>
<h2>If a specific intersection collision is the question</h2>
<p>Useful first documents are the report number and responding agency, the exact intersection and approach direction, the time of day, photographs showing damage location on both vehicles, and any citation issued to either driver. Requests to the signal-operating agency and to nearby businesses are time-sensitive for the reasons above, while the two-year injury deadline in Code of Civil Procedure section 335.1 continues to run. The material here is general information about California traffic and damages rules, not legal advice about any collision. Specific questions can be taken to the attorney who advertises on this site.</p>

## svc_medical_payments_and_liens_lede

Two different things get called medical coverage after a crash. One is a small first-party benefit that pays regardless of fault. The other is a claim against your eventual settlement, and it is capped by statute.

## svc_medical_payments_and_liens_body

<h2>What medical payments coverage actually is</h2>
<p>The California Department of Insurance describes medical payments coverage as an optional first-party benefit that pays medical expenses for the policyholder and passengers <a href="https://www.insurance.ca.gov/01-consumers/105-type/95-guides/01-auto/auto101.cfm">no matter who is at fault</a>, with a minimum available limit of one thousand dollars per injured person and higher limits available on request. That is a small number relative to an emergency department visit, which is why the coverage functions as a bridge for early treatment rather than as a solution. The same consumer guide notes that uninsured motorist coverage must be offered and declined in writing, that uninsured motorist property damage carries a limit of three thousand five hundred dollars and pays only if the uninsured driver is identified, and states minimum liability limits of thirty thousand, sixty thousand and fifteen thousand.</p>
<h2>Why using it is usually still worth it</h2>
<p>Medical payments benefits pay promptly and without a fault determination, which keeps treatment continuous in the weeks when a liability claim is going nowhere. The cost is that the insurer paying those benefits typically asserts a right to reimbursement from any later recovery. That right is not unlimited, and the arithmetic of it belongs in the file from the beginning rather than at the end, because a claimant who does not know the reimbursement figure cannot evaluate a settlement offer.</p>
<h2>The statutory ceiling on health care liens</h2>
<p>Civil Code section 3040 caps what a health care service plan or insurer may recover by lien or reimbursement from a personal injury recovery. Two ceilings appear in the section, keyed to representation: with an attorney engaged, the recovery is limited to <a href="https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=CIV&amp;sectionNum=3040.">a third of what the settlement or judgment yields</a>, and with no attorney engaged the fraction becomes a half. The section also limits the lien to the costs of perfecting it plus the amount actually paid for care, or eighty percent of usual and customary charges where the provider was capitated. It requires the lien to be reduced in proportion to the enrollee's comparative fault, and to bear a pro rata share of attorney fees and costs where a common fund was created. Subdivision language further provides that the existence of such a lien is not admissible in evidence.</p>
<h2>What section 3040 does not cover</h2>
<p>The same section excludes several categories from its reach, including workers compensation, Medi-Cal claims governed by the Welfare and Institutions Code, and hospital liens under the Hospital Lien Act. Federal programs follow their own rules. That patchwork is the reason a claimant can receive several reimbursement demands calculated on entirely different theories, each asserting a different ceiling, and why a settlement figure that looked adequate on the day it was accepted can shrink afterward. Identifying every payer that touched the treatment, before signing anything, is the only reliable protection.</p>
<h2>The order of operations that protects a recovery</h2>
<p>Determine which policies paid what, in writing. Request an itemized ledger of amounts actually paid rather than billed, since amounts paid are the statutory measure and also the measure California applies to past medical damages generally. Obtain each asserted lien or reimbursement demand in writing with the legal basis identified. Confirm the comparative fault reduction and any common fund reduction is applied. Only then evaluate whether a gross offer produces an acceptable net figure. Reversing that order is how a household ends up paying providers out of a settlement it already spent.</p>
<h2>The insurer's clocks apply here too</h2>
<p>Claims regulations require an insurer to acknowledge notice of a claim <a href="https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/california/10-CCR-2695.5">within 15 calendar days</a> and to respond to claimant communications within the same period, and to accept or deny a claim within 40 calendar days of receiving proof of claim, with written status notices every 30 days after that when more time is needed. Where medical payments benefits are being processed slowly, those deadlines are the reference point for a written follow-up, and they apply to first-party benefits as much as to liability claims.</p>
<h2>If reimbursement math is the open question</h2>
<p>The documents that resolve it are the declarations page showing medical payments limits, the health plan's written reimbursement demand, the itemized provider ledgers, and any correspondence stating a lien amount. Deadlines continue to run throughout: the two-year injury deadline in Code of Civil Procedure section 335.1 does not wait for lien negotiations to conclude. This page is general information about California statutes and regulations rather than legal advice, and it does not evaluate any claim or any lien. Specific reimbursement questions can be taken to the attorney who advertises on this site.</p>

## svc_pedestrian_and_cyclist_collisions_lede

Pedestrians and cyclists account for a small share of collisions in this city and a large share of the deaths. The Vehicle Code assigns duties to both sides of that encounter, and juries hear about both.

## svc_pedestrian_and_cyclist_collisions_body

<h2>The crosswalk duty and its limits</h2>
<p>Vehicle Code section 21950 requires a driver to yield the right of way to a pedestrian crossing within any marked crosswalk or within an unmarked crosswalk at an intersection, and directs drivers to exercise due care and reduce speed or take other action necessary for safety. The same section states that its provisions do not relieve a pedestrian from using due care, and prohibits a pedestrian from suddenly leaving a curb or place of safety and walking or running into the path of a vehicle so close as to constitute an immediate hazard. Section 21954 covers crossing outside a crosswalk, requiring the pedestrian to yield to vehicles close enough to constitute an immediate hazard while also stating that its provisions do not relieve drivers of the duty to exercise due care. In a disputed file the two duties coexist and the outcome is a percentage.</p>
<h2>Bicycles are vehicles for most purposes</h2>
<p>Section 21200 gives a person riding a bicycle on a roadway all the rights and duties applicable to the driver of a vehicle. Section 21202 requires a rider traveling slower than the traffic flow to ride as close as practicable to the right curb or edge, with express exceptions for passing, preparing for a left turn, avoiding hazards including parked vehicles and debris, and approaching a right turn, and for substandard-width lanes where a bicycle and a vehicle cannot safely travel side by side. That exception list is the core of most contested bicycle claims, because a rider positioned away from the curb was frequently exercising a statutory exception rather than violating the rule.</p>
<h2>The three-foot passing rule</h2>
<p>Section 21760, the Three Feet for Safety Act, requires a driver overtaking a bicycle traveling in the same direction to pass at a safe distance that does not interfere with the safe operation of the bicycle, and specifies a distance of at least three feet between any part of the vehicle and any part of the bicycle or its operator. Where that distance cannot be maintained given traffic and roadway conditions, the driver must slow to a reasonable and prudent speed and pass only when it will not endanger the rider. Sideswipe and overtaking collisions on arterials are the fact pattern this section addresses.</p>
<h2>The local numbers</h2>
<p>The City's roadway safety plan, covering 2017 through 2021, recorded <a href="https://records.westcovina.org/WebLink/DocView.aspx?id=68598&amp;dbid=0&amp;repo=WestCovina">154 pedestrian-involved collisions, of which 14 were fatal and 10 severe</a>, and 98 bicycle-involved collisions including one fatal and five severe, for 252 vulnerable road user collisions in total. Against 5,044 collisions citywide, that is a small proportion of events and a large proportion of the 29 fatalities. The plan also reported that roughly 88 percent of collisions occurred at or near intersections and that 34 percent occurred during darkness, dusk or dawn, which describes the lighting condition in which most serious pedestrian collisions here take place.</p>
<h2>What the city is changing at signals</h2>
<p>West Covina's Highway Safety Improvement Program award of <a href="https://www.westcovina.gov/238/Highway-Safety-Improvements-Program-HSIP">$3,184,700 in state funds</a> covers signal improvements at ten intersections, with the countermeasures identified including protected left-turn phasing and pedestrian-related upgrades, at locations including Sunset Avenue and Merced Avenue, Vincent Avenue and Puente Avenue, Lark Ellen Avenue and Badillo Avenue, and Azusa Avenue and Fairgrove Avenue. For a collision at one of those junctions, the configuration in place on the crash date is a documentable fact and differs from the configuration a person sees there today.</p>
<h2>Evidence in a vulnerable user case</h2>
<p>Injuries are typically severe enough that the claimant has no usable memory of the impact, so the record is built from physical evidence and third parties. Point of impact on the vehicle, throw distance, clothing marks, bicycle frame and wheel damage, helmet damage, and the position of debris all speak to speed and geometry. Lighting conditions and the state of any streetlight are documentable through the operating agency. Camera coverage near retail frontage is the most common source of imagery and the most quickly overwritten.</p>
<h2>If a pedestrian or bicycle collision is the question</h2>
<p>The first items are the report number and investigating agency, photographs of the vehicle and the bicycle before either is repaired or discarded, the complete medical record beginning with the ambulance run sheet, and the exact location including approach direction and lighting. Where a roadway or signal condition may be at issue, the six-month presentation window for public entity claims in Government Code section 911.2 controls and is much shorter than the two-year injury deadline in Code of Civil Procedure section 335.1. Specific questions belong with the attorney who advertises on this site.</p>

## svc_police_report_and_evidence_lede

The collision report is the spine of an insurance file, and in this city it is obtainable from a counter on West Garvey Avenue. The rest of the evidence has a shorter life than the report does.

## svc_police_report_and_evidence_body

<h2>Where the report lives and what it costs</h2>
<p>The West Covina Police Department Records Bureau is at <a href="https://www.wcpd.org/records/">1444 West Garvey Avenue, reachable at (626) 939-8500</a>, open weekdays from 7:30 in the morning to 5:30 in the afternoon. A traffic collision report is provided at no charge at the counter, with an additional ten dollar fee for an online copy. The department directs law offices and insurance companies to make requests by mail with a self-addressed stamped envelope. Records subpoenas require a minimum fifteen dollar check, an appearance subpoena for a department employee carries a two hundred seventy-five dollar fee, and civil subpoenas need five days. The traffic page notes that reports for collisions occurring after <a href="https://www.westcovina.gov/301/Traffic">September 1, 2016 are available online</a>, with earlier reports obtained in person.</p>
<h2>Which agency wrote it</h2>
<p>The city police department investigates all reported collisions within West Covina, and its Traffic Unit handles all hit-and-run and all fatal collisions, finalizes the determination of fault, and issues citations. Collisions on Interstate 10 and in unincorporated county areas belong to the California Highway Patrol, and the local command is the <a href="https://www.chp.ca.gov/find-an-office/southern-division/525-baldwin-park/">Baldwin Park Area office at 14039 Francisquito Avenue</a>, (626) 517-8500. Requesting from the wrong agency is the most common reason a claimant waits weeks for a document that was available in days.</p>
<h2>The reporting duties that fall on drivers</h2>
<p>Vehicle Code section 20008 requires a driver involved in a collision resulting in injury or death to file a written report with the Highway Patrol or with the city police department within 24 hours, whether or not officers attended. Section 16000 requires a report to the Department of Motor Vehicles within ten days when property damage exceeds one thousand dollars or anyone is injured or killed. Section 16025 requires drivers at the scene to exchange names, addresses, vehicle information and evidence of financial responsibility, and makes failure an infraction punishable by a fine of up to two hundred fifty dollars.</p>
<h2>Who can obtain the report</h2>
<p>Vehicle Code section 20012 makes these reports confidential and provides that their contents are to be disclosed only to the persons involved, their representatives, their insurers and other specified parties with a proper interest. That is why a records clerk asks for identification and a statement of interest, and why a third party cannot simply purchase someone else's collision report. It is also why obtaining a report about a collision involving a family member sometimes requires documentation of the relationship or an authorization.</p>
<h2>What the report is, and what it is not</h2>
<p>A report contains the responding officer's account: parties, vehicles, statements, measurements, a diagram, an opinion as to the primary collision factor and any Vehicle Code section cited. It is an investigative document, not an adjudication. A fault opinion in a report is persuasive to adjusters and is not binding in a civil case, and a report can contain errors of position, direction, or spelling that propagate through an entire claim if not corrected early. Requesting a supplemental or corrected report is possible and is easier within weeks than within months.</p>
<h2>Evidence with a shorter life than the report</h2>
<p>Private camera footage along commercial arterials is typically overwritten within days to a few weeks, so a written preservation request delivered early is the only reliable step. Vehicle event data recorders hold seconds of pre-impact speed, throttle and braking information that disappears when the vehicle is crushed, which happens quickly once a total loss is settled and storage fees accrue. Roadway evidence -- skid marks, gouges, debris fields -- survives until the next rain or street sweeping. Independent witnesses exist in the report or nowhere. Signal phasing records for a specific date and time are held by the operating agency and must be requested rather than reconstructed.</p>
<h2>The first-week evidence list</h2>
<p>Photograph both vehicles from all four corners, including the underside of bumper covers, plus the roadway, sight lines, signal heads and any obstruction. Note the exact intersection or freeway direction and postmile. Collect the report number, the officer's name and the agency. Identify the tow yard and the storage rate. Write down the names of businesses within camera range. Keep the itemized medical statements. Preserve damaged property such as a child seat or a phone mount, which corroborate occupant positions.</p>
<h2>If a document is the current obstacle</h2>
<p>The report number and responding agency determine every subsequent request, and the storage location determines how long vehicle evidence remains available. Claims regulations require insurers to respond to claimant communications within 15 calendar days, which is a reason to make requests in writing. Deadlines run in parallel, including the two-year injury deadline in Code of Civil Procedure section 335.1 and the six-month presentation window for public entity claims under Government Code section 911.2. This page is general information about California records and reporting rules, not legal advice. Document-specific questions can be raised with the attorney who advertises on this site.</p>

## svc_rear_end_collisions_lede

The rear driver is usually at fault, and "usually" is doing a great deal of work in that sentence. Chain collisions, sudden lane intrusions and disabled vehicles all produce rear impacts where the percentages land somewhere else.

## svc_rear_end_collisions_body

<h2>The statute the argument actually runs on</h2>
<p>Vehicle Code section 21703 states the following distance rule in general language: a driver may not follow another vehicle more closely than is reasonable and prudent, with regard to the speed of that vehicle and the traffic on and condition of the roadway. There is no car-length table and no fixed second count in the statute. It pairs with section 22350, the basic speed law, which prohibits a speed greater than is reasonable or prudent for weather, visibility, traffic and roadway conditions, and which can be violated at or below the posted limit. Together they explain why rear-impact liability is presumed in practice but not automatic in law.</p>
<h2>Chain reactions and the middle vehicle</h2>
<p>Multi-vehicle stacks are the common exception on this side of the San Gabriel Valley. A middle vehicle may have been pushed into the car ahead, in which case its rear crush pattern and the absence of pre-impact braking in its own event data matter more than any driver's recollection. Every driver in a stack tends to describe a single impact, and the number of impacts is precisely what determines who owes what. Vehicle damage photographed before repairs, bumper reinforcement deformation, and the sequence recorded by any vehicle with a functioning data recorder decide the sequence. Verbal accounts, collected an hour after the event by people who were struck from behind, rarely do.</p>
<h2>Where the stopping happens here</h2>
<p>The City's roadway safety plan, built on 2017 through 2021 data, ranked rear-end impacts second among collision types at <a href="https://records.westcovina.org/WebLink/DocView.aspx?id=68598&amp;dbid=0&amp;repo=WestCovina">26 percent of all collisions</a>, behind broadsides. Its intersection tables show the pattern concentrating on the long commercial arterials rather than in residential grids: the Azusa Avenue and Amar Road intersection alone accounted for 122 collisions, of which 33 percent were rear-end and 37 percent sideswipe, with 39 occurring in darkness. That mix -- rear-end plus sideswipe, after dark, at a wide signalized junction between two arterials -- describes the queue-and-lane-change behavior that follows freeway backups spilling onto surface streets.</p>
<h2>When the registered owner is a separate question</h2>
<p>Vehicle Code section 17150 makes a vehicle owner liable for injury or death resulting from negligent operation by any person driving with the owner's permission. Section 17151 then caps that particular exposure at <a href="https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=VEH&amp;sectionNum=17151.">fifteen thousand dollars for one person</a>, thirty thousand for more than one person in one accident, and five thousand for property damage, and bars punitive damages against an owner for the driver's conduct. The cap matters when a borrowed or family vehicle is involved and the driver carries little or no coverage of their own: the owner's statutory liability is real, limited, and separate from whatever the owner's insurance policy may also cover.</p>
<h2>Proving the injury, not just the impact</h2>
<p>Rear impacts generate low-visibility injuries and low property damage estimates in the same file, and adjusters read the second as evidence against the first. California measures past medical damages by what was paid or incurred rather than what was billed. The California Supreme Court held in 2011 that the collateral source rule <a href="https://law.justia.com/cases/california/supreme-court/2011/s179115/">does not allow recovery of amounts</a> a provider billed but never expected to collect, so an itemized statement showing paid amounts is more useful than a chargemaster total. A Court of Appeal decision two years later extended the point to future care and to noneconomic argument, holding that full billed amounts are <a href="https://law.justia.com/cases/california/court-of-appeal/2013/b236227.html">inadmissible for those purposes</a> as well. Continuous treatment records, not a large bill, carry this kind of claim.</p>
<h2>The gap that costs the most</h2>
<p>Delay between the crash and the first medical visit. In a low-speed rear impact the defense argument is almost always that symptoms which appear a week later came from something else, and the only reliable answer is a contemporaneous record. The second gap is photographic. Bumper covers hide structural damage, and a repair estimate listing reinforcement bar replacement or trunk floor work reads very differently from a photograph of an intact plastic panel. Once the vehicle is repaired or sold to salvage, that comparison is gone.</p>
<h2>What a first contact usually covers</h2>
<p>The document set is short: collision report number and responding agency, photographs of both vehicles including the underside of the bumper cover, the repair estimate rather than the check amount, the itemized medical statement, and any wage documentation. The two-year injury deadline in Code of Civil Procedure section 335.1 runs from the collision date, and the three-year deadline for property damage under section 338 runs separately. ## svc_rideshare_crash_claims_lede

A crash in an Uber or Lyft vehicle is governed by a statute most drivers have never read. Which of three coverage layers applies depends entirely on what the app was doing at the moment of impact.

## svc_rideshare_crash_claims_body

<h2>The three phases the statute creates</h2>
<p>Public Utilities Code section 5433 sets insurance requirements for transportation network companies by activity period. From the moment a participating driver accepts a ride request until the ride is complete and the passenger exits, the company must maintain <a href="https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=PUC&amp;sectionNum=5433.">primary commercial coverage of one million dollars</a> for death, personal injury and property damage. During that same period the company must also carry uninsured and underinsured motorist coverage of sixty thousand dollars per person and three hundred thousand dollars per incident for passengers in the vehicle. When the app is on but no ride has been accepted, the statute requires far smaller primary limits of fifty thousand per person, one hundred thousand per accident and thirty thousand for property damage. With the app off, the driver's personal auto policy stands alone.</p>
<h2>Why the phase question is the whole case</h2>
<p>The difference between the app-on-unmatched tier and the ride-accepted tier is a factor of ten or more in available coverage, and the only reliable record of which applied is held by the company. Trip receipts, in-app timestamps, and the driver's activity log establish when a request was accepted and when the ride was reported complete. A passenger has an obvious record; a third party struck by a rideshare vehicle usually does not, and must ask for it. Screenshots taken by a passenger in the hours after a crash, including the trip detail page and driver name, are worth more than any note written later.</p>
<h2>Who these companies are, legally</h2>
<p>Public Utilities Code section 5431 defines a transportation network company as an organization that provides prearranged transportation for compensation using an online-enabled application or platform to connect passengers with drivers using their personal vehicles, and defines a personal vehicle as one seating eight or fewer including the driver, not a taxicab or charter-party carrier. The California Public Utilities Commission licenses these companies and sets their <a href="https://www.cpuc.ca.gov/regulatory-services/licensing/transportation-licensing-and-analysis-branch/transportation-network-companies">insurance, reporting and driver record check requirements</a>. The regulatory frame matters because it explains why the driver's own insurer may decline a claim outright: personal auto policies commonly exclude carrying passengers for compensation, which is precisely the gap the statutory tiers were written to fill.</p>
<h2>The local pattern behind these trips</h2>
<p>Rideshare volume concentrates where nightlife, retail frontage and freeway ramps overlap, which in this city means the arterials feeding Interstate 10 and the retail corridors along Azusa Avenue and Glendora Avenue. The City's roadway safety plan reported that <a href="https://records.westcovina.org/WebLink/DocView.aspx?id=68598&amp;dbid=0&amp;repo=WestCovina">34 percent of collisions occurred during darkness, dusk or dawn</a> and that 219 collisions in the five-year study period involved a driver under the influence. Late-night pickups and drop-offs happen in that same lighting and traffic environment, frequently involving a passenger loading at a curb lane rather than in a driveway.</p>
<h2>Passengers are rarely at fault, which changes the fight</h2>
<p>A back-seat passenger is almost never assigned a percentage of fault, so the dispute moves from liability to two other places: which coverage layer opens, and how the injuries are valued. That makes documentation of the trip and documentation of treatment the two decisive files. It also means multiple passengers in one vehicle may present claims against the same limit, and the sequence and completeness of each presentation affects what remains. Where the rideshare driver was not at fault and the striking driver was uninsured, the statutory uninsured motorist layer for passengers becomes the operative coverage rather than the million-dollar liability layer.</p>
<h2>What to preserve in the first days</h2>
<p>The trip identifier and receipt, screenshots of the in-app trip detail, the driver's name and vehicle, the collision report number and responding agency, and photographs of the vehicle interior and exterior. Ambulance and emergency department records establish the injury timeline. Where the collision happened on the freeway, the report comes from the Highway Patrol's Baldwin Park Area office; on city streets, from the West Covina Police Department. Reporting duties fall on the drivers involved, including the written injury report to police required within 24 hours by Vehicle Code section 20008, but a passenger benefits from knowing whether that report exists.</p>

<p>The two-year deadline for injury actions in Code of Civil Procedure section 335.1 governs, and any claim reaching an uninsured motorist layer carries its own contractual and statutory deadlines that run sooner than the court deadline. The practical first step is assembling the trip record before it becomes inconvenient to obtain. ## svc_statute_of_limitations_california_lede

Most people have heard the two-year figure. It holds for an ordinary injury claim against another driver, and it is wrong for at least four situations that come up regularly on these streets.

## svc_statute_of_limitations_california_body

<h2>The general rule, and what it applies to</h2>
<p>The governing provision is Code of Civil Procedure section 335.1, and its two-year period reaches <a href="https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=CCP&amp;sectionNum=335.1.">injury to, or the death of, an individual</a> brought about by another party's wrongful act or neglect. That is the lawsuit deadline in an ordinary collision between private drivers, measured from the injury date. Filing a claim with an insurer does not satisfy it. Negotiating with an adjuster does not extend it. The only thing that stops the clock in an ordinary case is a complaint filed with the court within the period.</p>
<h2>Property damage runs on a different clock</h2>
<p>Section 338 sets three years for an action for taking, detaining or injuring goods or chattels, which is the provision that governs vehicle damage. A claimant can therefore be out of time on the injury claim while still within time on the property claim, or the reverse if a release was signed early. Small dollar disputes have their own inexpensive forums: section 116.221 permits a small claims action by a natural person for up to twelve thousand five hundred dollars, and section 85 defines a limited civil case as one where the amount in controversy does not exceed thirty-five thousand dollars.</p>
<h2>Public entities compress everything to six months</h2>
<p>Where a claim involves a city, county, transit agency or the state -- a roadway design issue, a signal malfunction, a government vehicle -- the Government Claims Act applies first. Under Government Code section 911.2, a claim covering death, bodily injury or damage to personal property has to reach the entity <a href="https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=GOV&amp;sectionNum=911.2.">within six months of accrual</a>, while other categories of claim get twelve months. Section 945.4 bars suit until the claim has been acted upon or deemed rejected. Section 945.6 then allows six months to file suit after written notice of rejection is personally delivered or mailed, or two years from accrual if that notice was never given in the required form. Section 946.6 provides a petition for relief where a claim was not presented in time, on stated grounds. In this corridor the possibility is concrete: the City operates most signals and Caltrans operates Interstate 10.</p>
<h2>Minors and incapacity</h2>
<p>Section 352 tolls a limitation period while a person entitled to bring an action is under the age of majority or lacks the legal capacity to make decisions, so a child injured as a passenger generally has until two years after turning eighteen for the injury claim. Subdivision (b) carves out an important exception: that tolling does not apply to an action against a public entity or public employee governed by the claim presentation requirements. A minor's claim against a government defendant therefore runs on the six-month presentation clock like anyone else's.</p>
<h2>Uninsured motorist deadlines are contractual and shorter in effect</h2>
<p>Insurance Code section 11580.2 subdivision (i) requires that within two years of the accident date the insured bring suit against the uninsured motorist, agree in writing with the insurer on the amount due, or formally demand arbitration by certified mail. Because a claim against one's own insurer is a contract matter, the operative deadline is that statutory and policy language rather than section 335.1, and formal demand by certified mail is not the same act as filing suit. Subdivision (k) requires the insurer to warn an unrepresented insured of an approaching deadline 30 days in advance, with failure tolling the period by 30 days.</p>
<h2>Filing is not the last deadline</h2>
<p>Code of Civil Procedure section 583.210 requires the summons and complaint to be served on a defendant within three years after the action is commenced. Los Angeles Superior Court's personal injury standing order is tighter in practice, directing service within 60 days of filing the complaint and setting trial approximately <a href="https://lascpubstorage.blob.core.windows.net/cpw/LIBOPSCivil-109-StandingOrderForProceduresInThePersonalInjuryHubCourts.pdf">18 months from the filing date</a>, with a final status conference roughly eight court days before trial. Since October 10, 2022 a new personal injury case must be filed in the judicial district where the incident arose, so a West Covina collision belongs in the East District rather than downtown.</p>
<h2>The regulatory notice that is not a safety net</h2>
<p>Claims regulations require an insurer to give written notice of a limitation period it may rely on to deny a claim, at least 60 days before expiration, or 30 days in a first-party uninsured motorist matter, and expressly exclude claimants represented by counsel from that requirement. Insurance Code section 790.03 also lists misleading a claimant as to the applicable statute of limitations among unfair claims settlement practices. Neither provision creates an extension, and neither is a reason to rely on an adjuster's calendar.</p>
<h2>If a deadline is the immediate concern</h2>
<p>The facts that determine which clock applies are the collision date, the identity of every potentially responsible party including any public agency, whether the injured person was a minor, and whether an uninsured or underinsured motorist claim is involved. Dates cannot be recovered once they pass, and section 946.6 relief is narrow. This page states general California deadlines and is not legal advice, and it does not calculate any specific deadline. A specific timing question should be raised promptly with the attorney who advertises on this site.</p>

## svc_totaled_vehicle_and_property_damage_lede

Total loss valuation is one of the few parts of a car accident claim California regulates in detail. The rules name what a comparable vehicle is, how far the insurer may look for one, and what must be added to the number.

## svc_totaled_vehicle_and_property_damage_body

<h2>What the regulation requires the offer to contain</h2>
<p>California claims regulations state that where a total loss is settled on the basis of actual cash value or replacement with a comparable automobile, the insurer must pay the <a href="https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/california/10-CCR-2695.8">actual cost of a comparable automobile including all applicable taxes, license fees and other transfer fees</a>, less any deductible, with license fees prorated. A comparable automobile is defined by attributes: the same make and model, the same or newer model year, similar body style, similar options and mileage, and availability for retail purchase by the general public in the local market area. The regulation also requires that the comparable vehicles used be identified with sufficient particularity, including vehicle identification, stock or license plate number, and that they have been available within 90 calendar days of the settlement offer.</p>
<h2>Deductions have to be shown, not asserted</h2>
<p>The same regulation requires that any adjustments to the value of a comparable vehicle be discernible, measurable, itemized and specified as to dollar amount, and that they be appropriate in dollar amount. It prohibits a deduction for condition unless the vehicle's condition was below average for its model year. Practically, a claimant who receives a valuation report showing unexplained downward adjustments for mileage or condition is entitled to see the arithmetic. The document to request is the full valuation report with each comparable identified, rather than the summary letter stating a single figure.</p>
<h2>Repairs, shops and parts</h2>
<p>Where a vehicle is repairable, the regulation prohibits an insurer from requiring repairs at a specific shop, and from suggesting a different shop after the claimant has chosen one, except as permitted by statute. It also prohibits requiring a claimant to travel an unreasonable distance or wait an unreasonable time for an inspection, estimate or repair. If a partial loss is settled on the insurer's written estimate, the insurer must supply a copy, and where the claimant obtains a higher written estimate the insurer must pay the difference, name at least one shop that will do the work for its figure, or reasonably adjust the claimant's shop estimate and identify each specific adjustment. Non-original-equipment crash parts may be required only if they are at least equal to original parts in kind, quality, safety, fit and performance, with the insurer paying for necessary modifications and warranting the parts.</p>
<h2>Title and salvage paperwork</h2>
<p>Vehicle Code section 11515 governs what happens to the title once a vehicle is settled as a total loss, requiring the insurer or owner to make a report to the Department of Motor Vehicles and to forward the certificate of ownership or a salvage certificate application. Owner retention of a salvage vehicle is possible and changes the title status, which in turn affects future resale and insurability. Where an owner keeps the vehicle, the salvage value is deducted from the settlement, and that deduction should be stated as a figure rather than folded into a net offer.</p>
<h2>Different deadlines than the injury claim</h2>
<p>Property damage is not governed by the two-year injury deadline. Code of Civil Procedure section 338 sets three years for an action for taking, detaining or <a href="https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=CCP&amp;sectionNum=338.">injuring goods or chattels</a>, which covers vehicle damage. Small property claims also have inexpensive forums: section 116.221 allows a small claims action by a natural person for up to twelve thousand five hundred dollars, and section 85 defines a limited civil case as one where the amount in controversy does not exceed thirty-five thousand dollars. For a disputed total loss valuation of a few thousand dollars, the forum question is often more useful than the argument.</p>
<h2>The local complication of storage and towing</h2>
<p>Vehicles from freeway collisions are towed to storage yards where daily fees accrue, and a delay in identifying the yard can consume much of the vehicle's value before an inspection happens. Where a collision occurred on Interstate 10 through the city, the responding agency is the Highway Patrol's <a href="https://www.chp.ca.gov/find-an-office/southern-division/525-baldwin-park/">Baldwin Park Area office</a>, and the report identifies the tow operator. Photographing the vehicle before it is released for salvage also preserves evidence for the injury claim, since damage patterns are the physical record of the impact and disappear when the car does.</p>
<h2>If a valuation is the dispute</h2>
<p>The useful documents are the insurer's full valuation report with comparables identified, the repair estimate or total loss declaration, registration and loan payoff information, photographs from before the collision if any exist, and the tow and storage invoices. Claim-handling regulations run on calendar days and correspondence in writing is what makes them enforceable. ## svc_underinsured_motorist_claims_lede

Underinsured motorist coverage is not extra money stacked on top of the other driver's policy. California builds it as a difference, and the statute that defines the difference also sets the order of operations.

## svc_underinsured_motorist_claims_body

<h2>The offset rule that surprises people</h2>
<p>Insurance Code section 11580.2 subdivision (p) governs underinsured motorist claims. An underinsured motor vehicle is one whose liability limits are less than the insured's own uninsured motorist limits. The statute conditions the claim on exhaustion: the insured must first exhaust the bodily injury liability limits of the underinsured driver's policy, by settlement or judgment. The insured's own coverage is then <a href="https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=INS&amp;sectionNum=11580.2.">reduced by the amounts paid</a> or payable under that liability policy. A claimant carrying one hundred thousand dollars in coverage who collects thirty thousand from the at-fault driver has seventy thousand of remaining capacity, not one hundred thirty thousand. The statute also entitles the insurer to a credit and requires the insured to serve it with a copy of any complaint filed against the underinsured driver.</p>
<h2>No stacking, and a limit on what must be offered</h2>
<p>Subdivision (q) prohibits stacking: the limit of uninsured motorist coverage available is the highest single limit under any one policy, not the sum of limits across vehicles or policies. Subdivision (m) obligates insurers to offer these limits only up to thirty thousand dollars per person and sixty thousand per accident, so a higher figure exists only if it was purchased. Reading the declarations page for both the per-person figure and the presence of a written waiver is therefore the first practical step in any file where damages exceed the at-fault driver's limits.</p>
<h2>Why this coverage gets used so often here</h2>
<p>Statutory minimum liability limits are low relative to hospital costs. Vehicle Code section 16056 sets minimums of thirty thousand, sixty thousand and fifteen thousand for policies issued or renewed on or after January 1, 2025, with the older 15/30/5 figures still relevant to earlier policies. The City's roadway safety plan assigned an economic cost factor of <a href="https://records.westcovina.org/WebLink/DocView.aspx?id=68598&amp;dbid=0&amp;repo=WestCovina">$2.19 million to each fatal or severe-injury collision</a> and $142,300 to a collision producing other visible injury, and counted 29 fatal and 75 severe-injury collisions in the city over five years. Those cost factors sit far above minimum limits, which is the entire reason a first-party layer exists.</p>
<h2>The consent trap</h2>
<p>Subdivision (c) permits an insurer to exclude coverage where the insured or a representative settled with a person or organization legally liable without the insurer's written consent. In practical terms, accepting the at-fault driver's policy limits and signing a general release before notifying the underinsured motorist carrier can extinguish the second claim. The sequence that preserves both is notice to the insurer, a request for written consent to settle for available liability limits, and only then a release. This is a documentation problem rather than a legal subtlety, and it is where otherwise sound claims fail.</p>
<h2>How the dispute gets resolved</h2>
<p>Because the underinsured claim runs against the claimant's own insurer, the vehicle is contractual and often arbitral. Subdivision (f) provides for determination by a single neutral arbitrator when the parties do not agree on liability or amount, absent a written agreement to a different number of arbitrators. Subdivision (o) sets out discovery duties on both sides with short response windows, including production of medical records and submission to examinations within stated day counts. Subdivision (i) fixes the two-year outer deadline: file suit against the uninsured motorist, conclude a settlement, or formally demand arbitration by certified mail within two years of the accident, and conclude the arbitration within five.</p>
<h2>Valuing damages against your own carrier</h2>
<p>The measure of damages does not change because the payer is the claimant's own insurer. Civil Code section 3333 states the general tort measure as compensation for all detriment proximately caused. Past medical damages remain limited by what was paid or incurred rather than billed, following the California Supreme Court's 2011 holding that the collateral source rule <a href="https://law.justia.com/cases/california/supreme-court/2011/s179115/">does not extend recovery</a> to amounts a provider never expected to collect. What does change is tone: the same insurer holding the claimant's premium record is now evaluating the claimant's injury, and every recorded statement enters that evaluation.</p>
<h2>If damages look larger than the other policy</h2>
<p>The documents that matter first are both declarations pages, the at-fault driver's policy limits in writing, the written consent correspondence, and the full medical and wage record. The two-year clock in section 11580.2 subdivision (i) is the operative one and runs from the accident date, independently of the two-year injury deadline in Code of Civil Procedure section 335.1. ## svc_whiplash_and_soft_tissue_injuries_lede

Soft tissue claims are not doubted because the injuries are minor. They are doubted because there is no image that proves them, which turns the case into a records case.

## svc_whiplash_and_soft_tissue_injuries_body

<h2>What the law lets a claimant recover</h2>
<p>Civil Code section 3333 sets the measure of damages for a tort not arising from contract as the amount that will compensate for all detriment proximately caused, whether or not it could have been anticipated. Nothing in that language distinguishes an injury visible on imaging from one diagnosed clinically. Civil Code section 1431.2 separates the recovery into economic damages, which the statute defines as objectively verifiable monetary losses including medical expenses and loss of earnings, and noneconomic damages, which include pain, suffering, inconvenience, mental suffering and loss of enjoyment of life. In a soft tissue file the economic column is often modest, so the noneconomic column carries the claim, and noneconomic damages are owed severally in proportion to each defendant's fault.</p>
<h2>The billing rule that quietly caps the medical figure</h2>
<p>California does not value past medical care at the amount a provider printed on a statement. The Supreme Court held in 2011 that a plaintiff may recover no more than the <a href="https://law.justia.com/cases/california/supreme-court/2011/s179115/">lesser of the amount paid or incurred</a> and the reasonable value of the services, and that the collateral source rule does not permit recovery of a billed sum the provider agreed in advance to accept less than. A Court of Appeal decision in 2013 went further, holding that evidence of full billed amounts is inadmissible for past medical damages, cannot be relied upon by an expert projecting <a href="https://law.justia.com/cases/california/court-of-appeal/2013/b236227.html">future medical expenses</a>, and is inadmissible as an argumentative device for noneconomic damages. For a claimant whose treatment was chiropractic and physical therapy over several months, this makes the itemized ledger of amounts actually paid the operative document.</p>
<h2>Why the property damage photograph is used against the injury</h2>
<p>Adjusters treat low repair cost as evidence of low injury, and rear or oblique impacts frequently produce exactly that combination. The counter is not argument but sequence: same-day or next-day evaluation, a consistent complaint recorded at each visit, a documented functional limitation such as missed shifts or restricted duty, and a discharge note. The City's own collision cost analysis illustrates that the system already prices these files separately, assigning a cost factor of <a href="https://records.westcovina.org/WebLink/DocView.aspx?id=68598&amp;dbid=0&amp;repo=WestCovina">$80,900 to a collision recorded as complaint of pain</a> against $142,300 for other visible injury. The category a report assigns at the scene tends to follow a claim for a long time.</p>
<h2>Gaps in treatment are the most expensive thing in the file</h2>
<p>A three-week interruption in care becomes the central exhibit in the defense evaluation, regardless of the reason for it. Work schedules, childcare, transportation and insurance authorization all cause real gaps, and none of them explain themselves inside a medical chart. When an interruption is unavoidable, a contemporaneous note in the record explaining why is worth more than a recollection offered a year later. The second common problem is a return-to-baseline note entered by a provider closing a file for administrative reasons rather than clinical ones.</p>
<h2>The local frame these claims sit inside</h2>
<p>The City's roadway safety plan recorded that 67.2 percent of the 5,044 collisions studied were property damage only, and ranked rear-end impacts at 26 percent of all collisions, with following too closely appearing as a primary cause in 1.8 percent of cases. That distribution says something practical: the most common collision in this city is a low-severity impact on a signalized arterial, so soft tissue claims here are not unusual events but the ordinary output of the local street network. Insurers evaluating a West Covina file are working from the same pattern.</p>
<h2>What the insurer's timelines require of them</h2>
<p>California claims regulations give an insurer 15 calendar days to acknowledge notice of a claim and to respond to communications from a claimant, and require acceptance or denial in whole or in part <a href="https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/california/10-CCR-2695.7">within 40 calendar days</a> of receiving a proof of claim, with written notice every 30 days thereafter if more time is needed. A denial must state all bases relied upon. Those deadlines are the claimant's leverage against silence, and they only work when correspondence is in writing and dated.</p>

<p>The productive first assembly is the collision report number, photographs of both vehicles, the complete treatment record including intake forms, the itemized statement of amounts paid rather than billed, and documentation of missed work or restricted duties. The two-year deadline for injury actions in Code of Civil Procedure section 335.1 applies.
