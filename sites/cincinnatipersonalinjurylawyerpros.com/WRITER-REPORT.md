## Angle used

This Ohio page is deliberately not a generic injury-site overview. Its anchor is Ohio’s two-year general bodily-injury/product-liability limitations provision, comparative fault’s “not greater than” threshold, the statutory treatment of noneconomic damages, and the practical Hamilton County Common Pleas civil-record path. The four Phase 1 problem cards apply that framework to premises, care-related, product and severe-injury fact patterns without claiming that any one fact pattern has a claim.

## Three sourced local facts

1. **Ohio’s general injury deadline.** Ohio Revised Code § 2305.10 generally requires bodily-injury and product-liability actions to be brought within two years after accrual; its text also contains special accrual/product provisions. This is why the site tells visitors to identify the date and claim type early rather than treating “two years” as universal. Sources: https://codes.ohio.gov/ohio-revised-code/section-2305.10 and https://codes.ohio.gov/ohio-revised-code/section-2305.113

2. **Fault and statutory damages analysis.** Ohio Revised Code § 2315.33 allows recovery when the claimant’s fault is not greater than the combined tortious conduct of others and directs a proportional reduction. Ohio Revised Code § 2315.18 separately defines economic and noneconomic loss and sets stated noneconomic-damages limits and exceptions for tort actions. Sources: https://codes.ohio.gov/ohio-revised-code/section-2315.33 and https://codes.ohio.gov/ohio-revised-code/section-2315.18

3. **Hamilton County civil-case record path.** The Hamilton County Clerk of Courts Civil Division says it maintains records for lawsuits including personal injury and that Common Pleas pleadings may be filed through e-filing. The site uses this fact only to explain the local record/filing path, not to promise the proper venue for any case. Sources: https://www.courtclerk.org/civil-division/ and https://hamiltoncountycourts.org/index.php/common-pleas/
