## symptom_1_title
A loud snap, then a door that feels far too heavy

## symptom_1
A torsion spring stores the lifting force that makes a heavy sectional door manageable. When it breaks, people often hear a sharp bang from the garage and then find that the opener will hum, strain, or move the door only a few inches. The break can look like a clean gap in the coil above the opening. With extension springs, the trouble may be along the horizontal track instead. In either case, the door has lost its counterbalance. It has not merely become inconvenient.

Do not keep pressing the wall button to see whether the motor can force it upward. An opener is designed to guide a balanced door, not lift the full dead weight by itself. Repeated attempts can damage the drive system or pull the door unevenly. Do not loosen set screws, wind bars, brackets, or cables. Spring work involves stored energy and is not a safe adjustment beside a parked car or a person holding the bottom section.

The repair visit starts with identifying the spring arrangement, the door weight and height, and whether the shaft, drums, bearings or cable ends were damaged when the spring failed. A correct replacement is fitted to the door rather than simply matching the old coil by appearance. The technician then balances the door by hand, checks the cable travel and roller path, and reconnects the opener only after the door moves smoothly. If a rental garage is involved, the tenant can document the bang and the stuck position; authorization for replacement normally comes from the owner or property manager. Keeping those roles clear avoids a repair stall while the garage remains unusable.

## symptom_2_title
The door starts down, then reverses or refuses to close

## symptom_2
A door that lowers partway and returns to the open position is often reacting to its safety system, but the cause is not automatically an electronic failure. Photo eyes mounted near the floor can be blocked by a bin, leaf, cobweb, snow residue or a misaligned bracket. A flashing opener light or repeated clicking can point in that direction. The practical first check is simple: remove anything across the opening and wipe the eye lenses gently. Do not defeat the safety system or tape a sensor out of the way just to get the door shut.

If the opening is clear and the behavior continues, the door may be meeting physical resistance. A roller can bind in a dirty or corroded track, a bent vertical section can pinch the roller stem, or a bottom seal can catch on a frozen or uneven threshold. The opener interprets excess resistance as an obstruction and reverses to protect people and property. Changing force settings without finding that resistance can turn a warning into a damaged door.

At the visit, the technician checks sensor alignment and wiring, then runs the door by hand with the opener disconnected when that can be done safely. That separates a travel problem from an opener problem. Track spacing, rollers, hinges, cable tension, bottom weather seal and limit settings are checked in sequence. The final test includes the photo eyes and the reversal response, not merely one successful close. In a student rental or duplex, do not leave the issue to the next roommate: notify the person responsible for maintenance while the door can still be secured, and make sure the repair window has an adult or authorized occupant available for access.

## symptom_3_title
A rusted bottom section, noisy track, or rollers starting to climb out

## symptom_3
The lowest foot of an Ann Arbor garage door works in the messiest part of the opening. Slush, grit and deicing residue come in on tires, settle along the threshold, and stay where the bottom bracket, hinges, track and cable hardware operate. Ann Arbor treats priority roads with anti-icing agents and salt, and also makes a sand-and-salt mix available to residents in winter. That does not mean every rusty door came from one source, but it does make a neglected wet lower edge worth taking seriously.

Early signs are orange staining at fasteners, white residue on metal, a roller that sounds rough, paint lifting near the lower seam, or a door that shudders at the first few inches of travel. Later, corrosion can seize a roller bearing, weaken a bottom bracket, narrow the track path, or let a cable travel poorly around the drum. A dented lower section adds a different problem: it can push a roller out of the track even when the opener itself is fine. Once a cable is loose or a roller is out, stop operating the door. A crooked door carries uneven weight and can damage more sections with every cycle.

A repair inspection distinguishes surface cleanup from compromised hardware. The technician examines the bottom brackets without removing tension-loaded components casually, measures whether the vertical track remains plumb, checks roller stems and hinges, and looks behind a dent for a bowed reinforcement strut. Depending on what is sound, the practical work may be hardware replacement, track correction, roller replacement, a lower-section repair, or a panel discussion. For historic-district properties, a like-for-like repair is a different decision from changing the visible door design. Keep photos of the existing door and do not order a replacement style before the property owner has checked the applicable review requirements.

## symptom_4_title
The opener runs, but the door stays on the floor

## symptom_4
An opener that makes its usual motor sound while the door does not move can be disconnected, stripped, or trying to operate a door with a larger mechanical problem. On many systems, a red emergency-release handle disconnects the trolley from the rail. That can happen intentionally during a power outage or accidentally when something catches the cord. If the door is fully closed and sits level, the release connection can sometimes be checked without lifting the door. If the door is open, crooked, or has a broken spring, leave the release alone; reconnecting or moving it can create a falling-door hazard.

Other causes lie inside the opener or at the arm that joins it to the door. A worn drive gear, broken chain or belt component, loose trolley, damaged door arm, or failed coupling can let the motor run without transferring motion. But a working opener can also be the victim rather than the source. A snapped spring, seized roller, corroded track, or off-track section can overload the operator until a gear fails. Replacing only the opener part without correcting the drag leaves the new component doing the same destructive work.

The technician checks whether the door is balanced and able to travel manually before deciding that the opener needs repair. The rail, trolley, arm, travel limits, drive parts and mounting are examined after the door hardware. When the fault is isolated, the price is presented for the required repair before it is performed. In an Ann Arbor rental, report the symptom in useful terms: “motor runs but door remains closed” is much better than “remote is broken.” Include whether the wall button behaves the same way and whether the door was manually released. That gives the owner or manager a clear maintenance record and helps dispatch bring the right likely components.

### Where the spread inside one row comes from

Three variables decide where any single job lands: which component failed, how much of the door has to be dismantled to reach it, and what hour of the week the truck arrives. A torsion spring on a straight shaft with sound bearing plates is a contained piece of work. The same spring on an Old West Side garage with a shortened header, a shaft that has worn its plates out of round, and winding cones frozen onto it takes considerably longer, and the invoice tracks the hour hand rather than the part number.

Corrosion is the local multiplier. The city treats 346 lane miles of priority streets down to bare pavement through the winter, and every vehicle that pulls in carries that brine over the threshold. Bottom brackets, cable ends, the lower two feet of vertical track and the fasteners buried in the bottom section absorb it first. Swapping a pair of lift cables on clean hardware is quick; swapping them where the bracket bolts shear off inside their holes turns into a bracket and track job too. A range that pretends otherwise gets rewritten in the driveway.

### Why this metro prices where it does

Federal wage data explains most of the labor side. Median pay in the general maintenance and repair occupation across the Ann Arbor metro is $23.64 an hour in the May 2025 Occupational Employment and Wage Statistics release. Once a truck, a stocked inventory of springs in several wire sizes, liability coverage, drive time from a shop out on the edge of the city and unproductive hours are loaded on top, the billed figure sits at a multiple of the wage. That is arithmetic, not markup, and it is why an hour saved on access shows up directly in the total.

The permit side is narrow but real. Rehanging a door, replacing springs, cables, rollers or an opener normally stays outside the permit process. Changing the size of the opening, altering the header or running a new circuit for an opener does not. The City of Ann Arbor building permit fee sheet, revised July 2025, prices a permit from the estimated project cost, with $100 covering the first tier and one rough plus one final inspection, and a nonrefundable $15 base application fee attached to every permit. Each additional inspection added to an open permit costs $35. Small money in isolation, but it sets a schedule, and schedules are what stretch a two-hour installation across two calendar days.

One more Ann Arbor step deserves a mention before anybody orders a door. Exterior work inside a local historic district needs an approved Certificate of Appropriateness before it starts, and that review is about how the door looks from the street. Owners in Old Fourth Ward, Water Hill and the streets around them should settle the district question before choosing a panel design, not after the deposit is placed.

### Reading a quote line by line

A quote worth acting on names the wire size, inside diameter and length of the spring going on the shaft, because those three numbers are what make a spring correct for a door. It states the door's weight or drum size rather than calling the door heavy, and separates the diagnostic visit from repair labor with a plain note on whether one credits against the other. It lists hardware individually: rollers by stem length and bearing count, hinges by number, cable by diameter. On a replacement it names the manufacturer, model, gauge of steel, insulation value, track configuration, whether the existing opener is being reused, and who hauls the old sections away.

### The upsell that runs in this trade

The pressure point is the leap from one broken spring to a full replacement door. A snapped spring produces a dramatic noise and a door that will not budge, which is the moment a homeowner is most likely to agree to whatever is proposed. Sometimes the door genuinely is finished, with rust perforating the bottom section and hinges tearing out of the stiles. Often it is a twenty-dollar bearing and a pair of springs on an otherwise straight door. Ask which specific parts are condemned and what condition each one is in, and ask whether the door still balances at mid-travel once new springs are on it.

The smaller version of the same trap is single-spring thinking. A two-car door running on one spring is not an economy; it is a shorter service life for both the opener and the remaining spring, and the second visit arrives sooner than anyone plans.

### What the figure people find online leaves out

National averages price parts and a flat labor allowance. They leave out the low-headroom track kit an older detached garage often needs, the haul-away of two hundred pounds of steel sections, the after-hours premium on a door frozen open in February, the second trip when a landlord signs off a day later, the extra inspection fee if a permitted job fails the first look, and the wait for historic review on a street where that applies. They also leave out matching: sourcing one section in the color and profile of a discontinued door can cost more than the section is worth, which is how a panel repair becomes a whole-door decision on the numbers alone.
