## symptom_1_title
The thermostat is set to heat, but the house is still cold

## symptom_1
Start with the simple observations, not the furnace cabinet. Make sure the thermostat is in heat mode, its set temperature is above the room temperature, and its display has power. Then look at the furnace itself. A unit that is completely quiet may have a power, thermostat, door-switch, or control problem. A unit that begins a cycle but does not produce heat is a different branch of the diagnosis.

A technician works through that sequence at the equipment: electrical supply and safety switches first, then the request for heat, then the furnace's own startup sequence. Modern furnaces do not simply turn the burners on when the thermostat asks. They check their safety conditions before allowing ignition. A fault in any of those checks can stop the cycle even though the thermostat looks normal from the hallway.

Do not keep flipping the service switch or cycling the breaker to make it try again. One reset after checking a tripped breaker is reasonable; repeated resets can erase the useful pattern of the failure and can leave the house cold again an hour later. Note what happens instead: silence, a fan, a clicking start attempt, a brief flame followed by shutdown, or a diagnostic light. That sequence gives the onsite inspection a faster starting point.

In Colorado Springs, a no-heat repair is not the same thing as a furnace replacement. A failed switch, igniter, sensor, thermostat connection, control component, or airflow issue may be repairable. The inspection separates the actual failed part from an age-based sales pitch. If the unit ultimately needs to be replaced, Pikes Peak Regional Building Department lists furnace work among projects requiring a permit in Colorado Springs, so that is a planned mechanical job, not something to rush through without the paperwork.

## symptom_2_title
The furnace starts, then shuts off before it heats the house

## symptom_2
A furnace that makes several short attempts and quits is telling you something specific: it is receiving a request for heat, but it is not completing one of the steps that lets the heating cycle continue. The room may feel especially frustrating because you hear the system wake up, then nothing changes at the registers. Watch from a safe distance and note the order: does an inducer fan start, does the igniter glow, do burners light briefly, or does the blower come on after the flame disappears? Do not put hands near burners, wiring, or the vent connection.

The useful repair is not to bypass a safety device. The technician checks the sequence against the furnace's wiring and fault indication, then tests the component or condition holding it up. The possible causes range from a dirty or failed flame-sensing path to an ignition fault, pressure-switch issue, blocked condensate drain on a condensing unit, control-board problem, or a venting condition. Those are not interchangeable parts, which is why a part guess over the phone is not a repair plan.

High winds are part of the local context worth mentioning. The National Weather Service documented a December 15, 2021 extreme-wind event with a 92 mph gust at Colorado Springs Airport. After a wind event, an exterior intake or exhaust termination deserves a visual check from the ground for obvious damage or blockage, especially if the furnace began failing afterward. Do not climb onto a roof or disconnect a vent to inspect it. Tell the dispatcher the timing; the technician can inspect the complete vent path and furnace safety response.

A repeated shutdown can leave a house without usable heat even though the blower still runs. Turn the system off if there is a gas odor, smoke, or a carbon-monoxide alarm. Otherwise, leave the controls alone, keep the filter area accessible, and arrange an inspection that identifies why the cycle is being stopped.

## symptom_3_title
The blower runs, but the vents are pushing cool air

## symptom_3
Cool air from supply registers does not always mean the blower motor is bad. The blower may be doing its job while the heating side has never lit, has shut down, or is waiting through a programmed delay. First, look at the thermostat setting. A fan switch set to “on” can circulate room-temperature air continuously even when there is no active heat cycle. Switching it to “auto” is a simple check, but it does not explain a furnace that was heating normally and suddenly is not.

Listen to the sequence after a heat request. If the blower starts immediately and stays on with cool air, the thermostat setting, control signal, or furnace safety response needs attention. If it starts later after a failed ignition attempt, the furnace may be protecting itself while clearing the heat exchanger area. The technician checks the complete cycle rather than treating the cool air as a stand-alone blower complaint.

Airflow is also part of the picture. A heavily loaded filter, closed return grille, collapsed duct, or blocked supply path can make rooms feel cold and can affect how the system operates. Replace only the filter size and type intended for the rack; forcing a thicker filter into a slot that cannot accommodate it creates a restriction rather than a fix. Do not run the furnace with the blower compartment panel removed, because the door switch and the cabinet enclosure are part of normal operation.

Colorado Springs housing has a median year of construction of 1986, according to the city's housing-needs fact sheet. That does not diagnose an individual furnace, but older duct layouts and later equipment replacements can make rooms behave differently. The technician should check the furnace at the unit and ask where the cool rooms are, rather than promising one part will make every register identical.

If there is smoke, a gas odor, or a carbon-monoxide alarm, leave the area and contact emergency services or the gas utility. Otherwise, stop repeated resets and have the furnace checked before an intermittent fault becomes a no-heat call.

## symptom_4_title
The furnace is making a new bang, scrape, rattle, or squeal

## symptom_4
A new furnace noise is worth locating before it becomes a bigger repair, but the sound alone is not a diagnosis. A light click at the start of a normal cycle is different from a hard bang, metal scraping, a high squeal, or a rattle that begins only when the blower is running. Stand back, note whether the noise starts before heat, during ignition, after the blower comes on, or at shutdown, and tell the technician which room or cabinet area it seems to come from. Do not remove the access panel to chase it.

A loose cabinet panel or duct connection can rattle. A blower problem can produce a scrape, vibration, or persistent hum. A delayed or rough ignition can create a more serious bang. The repair process begins by confirming that timing and inspecting the relevant assembly, including blower wheel, motor, mounts, burners, inducer, and duct connections as appropriate. Replacing a motor because there is a noise, without establishing where the noise originates, is an expensive way to miss a loose part.

Turn the furnace off and seek prompt help if the noise is paired with a gas odor, smoke, visible soot, repeated burner shutdown, or a carbon-monoxide alarm. Those conditions do not belong on a watch-and-wait list. A moderate rattle without those warnings may be suitable for a scheduled visit, but it should be addressed before a loose component damages another part or a vibration works a connection free.

Wind can make exterior terminations and loose flue components more noticeable, which is useful context after a Front Range event. The National Weather Service's December 2021 report recorded exceptional gusts across the region, including 92 mph at Colorado Springs Airport. If the sound began immediately after high wind, mention it when the visit is scheduled. The technician can include the accessible venting, exterior termination condition, and furnace operation in the inspection rather than focusing only on the sound heard inside.

The result should be a named cause, a repair recommendation, and a price before any component is replaced.

## pricing_body

### Why one furnace call has two prices

A furnace that shuts down in February produces one symptom and a dozen candidate causes. A dirty flame sensor and a cracked heat exchanger both end with a cold house, and they sit a factor of thirty apart on the invoice. That is why the bands here are wide at the top: the diagnosis decides which row you are in, and it happens after somebody puts a meter on the control board, not from the driveway.

Where inside a band a job lands comes down to three things. Whether the failed part is proprietary or a stocked universal. Whether nearby fittings crumble when touched, turning a one-part job into three. And whether the call lands on a quiet Tuesday or during the first hard freeze, when every truck in El Paso County is committed.

### Altitude, and the derate nobody mentions

This city sits above 6,000 feet, and gas equipment does not behave here the way its sea-level nameplate suggests. Input capacity has to be derated as elevation climbs, so a furnace sized off square footage alone tends to arrive oversized, short cycle all winter, and wear out its igniter, inducer and control board early. On a repeat failure the honest question is not only which part broke, but whether it broke because the equipment was mismatched to the elevation from the day it went in.

### What the building department charges

Replacing a furnace is permitted work, and the Pikes Peak Regional Building Department fee schedule states the numbers plainly: $50 for a furnace or boiler replacement, $75 when the vent goes with it, $40 for a water heater alone. Failed inspections escalate faster than the permit does, at $50, then $100, then $200, with the third re-inspection blocked for two working days. Heating and gas piping permits in one and two family dwellings go to Mechanical Contractor B and Gas Piping Contractor classes, so a changeout bid from an outfit that cannot pull a permit is not comparable to one that includes it.

### What an honest changeout estimate names

It states the manufacturer and model, the AFUE, the input rating at this elevation rather than at sea level, the stages, the blower type, the vent material and route, where the condensate goes and how it is kept from freezing, whether the gas line and circuit are adequate, the haul-away, the permit line and the inspection. The rebate belongs on the sheet as a separate line, not blended into the price: the utility pays $900 on a qualifying ENERGY STAR furnace.

### The replacement push, said plainly

A cracked heat exchanger is a genuine reason to stop repairing a furnace. The tell is being told about a crack that nobody shows you. A camera photograph, a combustion analyzer reading in parts per million, or a marked location takes two minutes to produce, and condemning an exchanger without one asks for four figures on faith. Igniters, flame sensors, pressure switches, inducers and boards are repairs, not death sentences, under fifteen years old. The other pattern worth naming is the tune-up that turns into an emergency: that visit is a $99 to $249 job here, and it should end with a written condition report.

### What a national calculator misses in this county

It prices equipment and a generic install. It does not carry the permit or a possible re-inspection, the chimney liner an orphaned water heater needs once a condensing furnace stops sharing the masonry flue, a sidewall vent penetration through brick or stucco, gas line resizing, altitude derating, or the second fault the first one caused. On a mid 1980s house, the median vintage here, it also skips the undersized return that has been cooking blower motors for years.
