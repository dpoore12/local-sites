## Federal refrigerant rules
- 700 GWP limit, residential and light commercial AC/heat pumps, manufacture/import compliance date January 1, 2025 — EPA Technology Transitions table: https://www.epa.gov/hfcs/technology-transitions-hfc-restrictions-sector
- Original rule: 88 FR 73098, October 24, 2023; December 26, 2023 interim final rule allowed pre-2025 inventory to be installed until January 1, 2026 — https://www.epa.gov/hfcs/regulatory-actions-technology-transitions
- **May 26, 2026 final rule (Reconsideration):** 91 FR 31284–31330, FR Doc 2026-10387, docket EPA-HQ-OAR-2025-0005, effective **July 27, 2026**. Retains the 700 GWP limit at 40 CFR 84.54(c)(1) but **removes the installation deadline** for systems whose specified components were made or imported before January 1, 2025. "Installing new R-410A systems using equipment domestically manufactured or imported after January 1, 2025, remains prohibited." Condensing unit remains a "specified component" (40 CFR 84.52); components may still be made and sold for service; after January 1, 2025 such components must carry a permanent label reading **"For servicing existing equipment only."** Replacing only the condensing unit is not installation of a new system; replacing the indoor coil afterward is, and that system may not exceed the limit. https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/full_text/text/2026/05/26/2026-10387.txt
- Servicing/repair of existing equipment is unaffected; owners may "maintain and repair their systems throughout the useful life of the equipment," including replacing a condensing unit or compressor with a similar R-410A component. Phasedown schedule: 90% of baseline 2020–2023, **60% 2024–2028, 30% 2029–2033**, 20% 2034–2035, 15% 2036 and after; 85% reduction by 2036. https://www.epa.gov/hfcs/frequent-questions-phasedown-hydrofluorocarbons
- Section 608: 40 CFR part 82 subpart F; certification required for technicians who maintain, service, repair or dispose of equipment that could release refrigerant; four types (I, II, III, Universal); credentials do not expire; attaching gauges to measure pressure makes a person a technician. https://www.epa.gov/section608/section-608-technician-certification-requirements
- Venting prohibition: "It prohibits individuals from intentionally venting ozone-depleting substances (ODS) refrigerants and their substitutes, such as hydrofluorocarbons (HFCs) while maintaining, servicing, repairing, or disposing of air conditioning or refrigeration equipment." https://www.epa.gov/section608
- Sales restriction: refrigerant may be sold only to Section 608/609 certified technicians or their employers; only 608 holders may buy refrigerant for stationary equipment. https://www.epa.gov/section608/refrigerant-sales-restriction
- Federal leak-repair/corrective-action rules apply to appliances with a **full charge of 50 or more pounds** of ozone-depleting refrigerant (comfort cooling trigger rate 10% over 12 months) — a house system is far below that threshold, so nothing federal forces the repair. https://www.epa.gov/section608/stationary-refrigeration-leak-repair-requirements
- A2L GWPs, SNAP acceptable with use conditions for residential/light commercial AC: R-454B 470, R-452B 700, R-454A 240, R-454C 150, R-457A 140, HFC-32 675. https://www.epa.gov/snap/substitutes-residential-and-light-commercial-air-conditioning-and-heat-pumps
- 2024 IRC M1411.2 requires HVAC appliances containing A2L refrigerants to be certified to UL 60335-2-40 and installed per manufacturer instructions; integral refrigerant detection system and minimum room size typically required above roughly a 2-pound charge (nonfixed factory-sealed) or 4 pounds (other types); A2L is heavier than air so sensors sit low, below the evaporator coil; charge limit question of 34.5 lb per independent system. https://www.ul.com/sites/default/files/2025-03/International_Residential_Code_IRC-2024.pdf

## Mesquite / Texas
- **Ordinance No. 5246**, passed May 18, 2026, effective **July 1, 2026**, repeals and replaces Chapter 5, Article VII and adopts the **2024 International Mechanical Code**. Local amendments include IMC 306.3 Appliances in Attics, 306.5.1 Sloped Roofs, 108.2 fees, 1104.2 Machinery Room, **1104.3.1.1 Group A2L high-probability systems (comply with ASHRAE 15 section 7.6)**, 1109.2.5 refrigerant pipe shafts, and new Section 202 definitions for "refrigerant detection system" and "refrigerant detector" referencing UL 60335-2-40 / UL 60335-2-89 and ASHRAE 15-2022. https://apps.cityofmesquite.com/city_secweb/ordinances/5246.pdf
- IMC 306.3 as amended by Mesquite: attic passageway not less than 30 in high, 22 in wide, not more than 20 ft long; continuous solid flooring not less than 24 in wide; level service space not less than 30 in by 30 in at the service side; clear access opening not less than 20 in by 30 in; attic access by permanent stair, pull-down stair with minimum 300 lb capacity, or a door from an upper floor. Same source.
- Adopted codes generally: "City of Mesquite is under the 2024 I.B.C. and I.F.C. Codes, 2023 National Electrical Code, and the 2024 I.C.C. Codes." https://www.cityofmesquite.com/355/Adopted-Codes
- Mechanical permits/inspections: "Mechanical installations of new or remodel projects shall be installed and permitted by a licensed mechanical contractor with the state of Texas." Inspections listed: mechanical duct rough-in and mechanical final. Homeowners living in the residence may apply for remodel permits for their own work. https://www.cityofmesquite.com/486/Inspection-Summary
- Auxiliary/secondary drain protection, IMC 307.2.3 (model text, verified via a jurisdiction reproducing the section verbatim): one of four methods — auxiliary pan with separate drain discharging to a conspicuous point; separate overflow drain line connected above the primary; auxiliary pan with a listed water-level detection device that shuts the equipment off; or a listed water-level device that shuts the equipment off. Pan depth not less than 1 1/2 inches and not less than 3 inches larger than the unit. https://codelibrary.amlegal.com/codes/newyorkcity/latest/NYCadmin/0-0-0-193068 (ICC section page: https://codes.iccsafe.org/s/IMC2024P1/chapter-3-general-regulations/IMC2024P1-Ch03-Sec307.2.3)
- TDLR: Class A license allows any size unit; Class B covers cooling systems 25 tons and under and heating 1.5 million BTU/hour and under; endorsements include Environmental Air Conditioning; license number format TACL/A/000000/C; application fee $115; licenses valid one year. https://www.tdlr.texas.gov/acr/contractor-apply.htm
- TDLR FAQ: "to offer to perform non-exempt air conditioning and refrigeration work in Texas requires that you hold a Texas Air Conditioning and Refrigeration Contractor license"; a person performing non-exempt maintenance must at minimum be a registered ACR technician, and all non-exempt work must be provided through a licensed contractor carrying the required liability insurance; a mini-split is not exempt (Occupations Code Sec. 1302.058; Rule 75.100). https://www.tdlr.texas.gov/acr/acrfaq.htm
- Texas cooling and tenants: "There is no state law that specifically gives tenants the right to be provided with climate control measures like air conditioning or heating." Property Code 92.052 requires a diligent effort to repair a condition materially affecting physical health or safety; 92.056 sets the notice steps; 92.0561(d)(3) covers repair-and-deduct for faulty cooling equipment, requiring among other things written notice from a local housing, building or health official and three days after the tenant's notice of intent. https://www.sll.texas.gov/faqs/tenants-rights-ac-heating/
- NWS Fort Worth DFW 100-degree data: 2024 = 23 days, high 107 on Aug 19; 2023 = 55; 2025 = 7; 2011 = 71; 1980 = 69; 2026 first 100-degree day July 7. https://www.weather.gov/fwd/d100data
