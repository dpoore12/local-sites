## hero_promise
Bellevue bathrooms range from compact rooms near the older Offutt and Capehart housing area to newer suites west of the freeway. A useful remodel starts with the actual room, not a showroom sketch. The aim is simple: a bathroom that works better every day, built in a sequence that does not leave the important decisions until the walls are open.

## what_happens_when_you_call
The first step is arranging a project visit. Bring the bathroom address, the change you are considering, and times when the room can be measured. At that visit, the remodeler can look at the tub or shower, vanity, toilet clearances, drain and supply locations, fan route, electrical needs, the path through the house, and any visible signs of prior water or repair work. That produces a real scope rather than an estimate based on a photo. You should be able to review what will be protected, removed, repaired, installed, and cleaned; which selections are included; and how any City permit or inspection steps fit into the construction order. Only then is there enough information for a written project price and a target finish date.

## what_they_will_ask
Expect questions that shape the job. Is the goal a complete rebuild, a shower change, a tub replacement, or an easier-entry layout? Are fixtures staying where they are, or will the drain, toilet, valve, outlets, lights, or walls move? Which bathroom must remain usable while construction is underway? Are there soft spots, cracked tile, a slow drain, a fan that does little, recurring staining, or a past water event in a lower level? It also helps to know the approximate age of the house and whether anyone has found older supply lines, drain piping, layered floor finishes, or earlier patchwork behind the room. Photos are helpful for planning, but dimensions and conditions are confirmed on site.

## qa_1_question
Do Bellevue bathroom remodels need permits and inspections?

## qa_1_answer
The answer depends on what changes. Bellevue’s Permits and Inspection Division says regulated construction, alteration, repair, improvement, or conversion needs a permit unless exempt, and it notes that separate plumbing, mechanical, and electrical permits are required unless an exemption applies. The same City guidance distinguishes a small repair from work that replaces or rearranges pipes or fixtures; replacement plumbing work is treated as new work that needs a permit and inspection. Moving a drain, altering a shower, changing walls, adding electrical work, or modifying ventilation therefore should be identified from the actual scope before construction. Purely cosmetic finishes are different from behind-the-wall changes. The sensible approach is to build the permit path and inspection checkpoints into the schedule before tile, wallboard, or flooring conceals the work.

## qa_2_question
Why does a Bellevue bathroom quote need an on-site visit?

## qa_2_answer
Because the most consequential parts of a bathroom are often hard to see in a photo. Bellevue includes long-established homes and base-adjacent housing as well as newer development, so fixture spacing, wall depth, access through the house, drain and supply locations, floor height, fan routing, and prior work vary widely. A site visit lets the remodeler measure the room, inspect accessible conditions, note what will have to be protected, and define selections against real dimensions. It is also the point to discuss a basement bathroom in light of Bellevue’s 2019 flood experience. That does not turn a remodel into a flood-restoration project; it makes it sensible to investigate signs of moisture before new finishes cover the room. The resulting proposal should say what is included, what requires a decision after demolition, and how a verified concealed issue will be handled.

## qa_3_question
How long will I be without the bathroom?

## qa_3_answer
The schedule follows the work, not an empty speed promise. A vanity and finish update is different from a project that removes a tub, rebuilds a shower, moves plumbing, changes outlets or lighting, repairs flooring, installs tile, waits for glass, and includes City permit or inspection steps. Material lead times and curing time matter too. During the site visit, the work can be arranged in a sensible order: protect the home, demolish, address approved conditions, complete rough plumbing and electrical, handle required inspections, prepare walls and floor, waterproof, install finishes and fixtures, allow required cure time, then test and clean. The written proposal should show the expected sequence and target finish date, as well as the conditions that could legitimately move it. That gives the household a way to plan for the loss of the room.

## pricing_body

### A thin market that publishes real projects

Actual project numbers are visible here instead of national averages. Firms working Bellevue, Papillion and La Vista post finished jobs with square footage attached: a 42 square foot Bellevue bathroom, an 86 square foot Papillion bathroom, a 120 square foot La Vista bathroom, a 6 by 10 Omaha bath and a 10 by 12 in Elkhorn all carry prices on remodelers' own sites.

National bath-liner franchises here publish promotions instead of prices, and one established local remodeler declines to post figures at all. Each row above rests on a handful of firms.

### What Bellevue charges, with the arithmetic shown

Bellevue still prices permits off the 1997 Uniform Administrative Code tables the city publishes. Building permit fees run $69.25 for the first $2,000 of job valuation plus $14.00 for each additional $1,000. Take a $20,000 bathroom: $69.25 plus eighteen increments at $14.00 is $321.25. A $34,000 project moves into the next band at $391.75 for the first $25,000 plus $10.10 per additional thousand, or $482.65.

Plumbing is permitted separately. Each fixture or trap is $9.80 on top of $23.50 permit issuance, so a toilet, lavatory and shower files at $52.90. Altering drainage or vent piping is $4.75 per fixture, and a plumbing reinspection is $49.50. Building inspections outside normal hours and additional plan review run $47.00 an hour. Total permit cost on that $20,000 bathroom is therefore about $374, and on the $34,000 Bellevue project about $536.

Those are small numbers next to the project. The penalty is not: the master fee schedule adopted by Resolution 2026-16 charges four times the regular permit fee when work starts before the permit issues. The Permits and Inspection Division treats replacing plumbing material or rearranging pipes and fixtures as new work needing a permit, so a bathroom is not exempt because nothing moved. Registration costs little: $75 a year for a city master plumber, $100 for a Class C residential contractor.

### Why Omaha metro labor costs what it does

Plumbers across the Omaha-Council Bluffs metro had a median wage of $30.48 an hour in May 2025 across 2,430 workers, with tile and stone setters at $29.34, per the BLS Occupational Employment and Wage Statistics, May 2025 release. With a modest number of qualified bath crews in the metro, scheduling is the real constraint: a good tile setter is booked weeks out.

### The Bellevue-specific things that move a bid

Housing vintage runs in distinct chapters here. Capehart-era base housing dates from around 1959 to 1961, and compact bathrooms of that era have shallow stud walls that will not take a modern valve body without furring out, which shrinks a room already tight on clearance. Cast-iron stacks and galvanized branch lines turn up regularly, and replacing one back to the main is a real line item.

Lower-level bathrooms deserve extra scrutiny. During the March 2019 Missouri River flood, water reached Offutt Air Force Base and affected roughly thirty buildings there. Not every Sarpy County basement took water, but moisture history is worth confirming before new finishes cover a lower level.

### Reading a bid properly

A usable bid spells out demolition and haul-away, which supply and drain runs are replaced or relocated, the waterproofing system by name, and tile square footage with an allowance for each type. It also gives vanity and top dimensions, fixtures by model number, glass thickness, fan capacity in cubic feet per minute, a permit line covering building and plumbing both, and money released against finished milestones. Where tile or fixtures ride on an allowance, the total will still move.

### The trap

Watch the wet-area-only sale. Replacing a tub or shower surround in a day is a real product with a place at the bottom of the table, and for a household that needs a dry, safe shower it can be right. It is not a full remodel, and its price does not predict one. Watch too for an accessibility upgrade priced like a luxury renovation: bars, a comfort-height toilet and a curbless entry sit below a gut job.

### What a posted figure does not cover

Every band here presumes the room keeps its footprint and that framing and subfloor are sound. Left out: both permits, relocating drain or supply lines, replacing a cast-iron stack, structural repair found at demolition, electrical work for a heated floor or new lighting, moisture remediation in a lower level, and the weeks a special-order vanity adds to a schedule already limited by crew availability.

## svc_bathroom_remodel_lede

Three quite different projects get written up as a bathroom remodel, and on paper they read alike. What separates them is how far past the tile the work travels, who is licensed to touch the pipe, and how the money is released.

## svc_bathroom_remodel_body

### Three jobs wear the same name

Work out which of three jobs a proposal describes before setting it beside another one. The narrowest replaces what you can see and leaves every drain, vent, and supply line where it sits. The middle version strips the room back to framing, renews substrate and waterproofing, and corrects whatever an earlier owner covered up. The widest moves fixtures, which means new drain and vent runs and a floor that has to be opened. Finish selections barely account for the gap. A surface job carries a fairly tight range because nothing behind the wall changes; a strip-out is wider by nature, since no honest number exists for a condition nobody has seen yet.

### Which Bellevue the house sits in

There is no single building era here. The City dates settlement to Joshua Pilcher's 1822 trading post, and a log cabin raised in the 1830s still stands at 805 Hancock Street in Olde Towne. Bellevue Public Schools records a lease signed on May 20, 1959 for a school inside the Offutt Family Housing Annex known as Capehart Housing, with the first of three elementary schools there dedicated in 1961, which dates the compact base-adjacent baths in that part of the city. Tregaron Golf Course opened to the public in the summer of 1997, so the subdivision wrapped around it is late-1990s work or newer. Fontenelle Hills sits on rolling ground and mixes ranch and multi-level houses on large lots, some backing onto Fontenelle Forest land. Twin Ridge, Golden Hills, and the streets near the Twin Creek district fall between those poles.

### What is under the bathroom floor

This is basement country, and that changes the whole plumbing conversation. NAHB's reading of the Census Survey of Construction found 62.3 percent of new single-family homes started in the West North Central region in 2024 had a full or partial basement, against 17 percent nationally. A drain in a joist bay can be reached from below, inspected before demolition, and rerouted without breaking concrete. The trade-off is the finished ceiling under the bathroom: opening it is part of the job, patching it is part of the job, and a scope that never mentions the room below has not been priced properly.

### Radon deserves a line in a Sarpy County scope

Nebraska DHHS reports that more than half of radon tests in the state come back above the action level of 4.0 picocuries per liter, and its measurement report to the Legislature puts the Sarpy County average at 5.3 across 5,649 tests between October 2019 and September 2024. Bathroom work in a finished lower level means cutting fresh openings through the floor slab for drainage, and any mitigation system already in place depends on that slab staying sealed. Penetrations get sealed as they are made, a suction pit and its piping stay untouched, and a retest afterward is cheap.

### Who is allowed to touch the plumbing

Nebraska handles this differently than most states. Under Neb. Rev. Stat. 18-1917, anyone installing or repairing sanitary plumbing must be a master plumber licensed by the city or village nearest the site, and the employees doing the work must hold journeyman licenses from that same city or village. Bellevue's code goes further: the plumbing permit is applied for by the master plumber and signed and paid for by that license holder, not by the homeowner. Separately, the Contractor Registration Act requires contractors and subcontractors to register with the Nebraska Department of Labor, and the Department says outright that registration does not ensure quality of work or protect against fraud. Check the registration, then check the plumbing license the City issued.

### The exemption that misleads people

Bellevue's Permits and Inspection Division states that no regulated structure may be altered, repaired, improved, or converted without a permit, and that plumbing, mechanical, and electrical permits are separate unless exempted. Painting, papering, and floor covering are listed as exempt cosmetic work, which is where the confusion starts. The plumbing exemption is narrow: clearing a stoppage or fixing a leak needs no permit, but once a defective trap, drainpipe, soil, waste, or vent pipe has to come out and be replaced with new material, the City treats that as new work requiring a permit and inspections. Bellevue builds to the 2021 International Residential Code and the 2018 energy code, and inspections happen before tile goes on, not after.

### Paying by milestone, and the lien statute behind it

The Nebraska Construction Lien Act treats a homeowner improving the house they live in as a protected party, and it defines residential real estate as four dwelling units or fewer. A subcontractor or supplier may send you a notice of the right to assert a lien, and the statute requires it to carry a warning that payments made after it arrives can expose you to double liability if you never contracted with the sender. Your exposure is the prime contract price less payments properly made in good faith, and a claimant has 120 days after last furnishing labor or materials to record. Practically: keep the notices, release money against finished stages, and collect signed waivers as you go.

## svc_shower_remodel_lede

A shower that never seems to dry out is reporting something specific. Water is getting behind the tile and staying there, and by the time a smell or a stain arrives, the layer that was supposed to stop it has been out of service a while.

## svc_shower_remodel_body

### Separating a surface complaint from an assembly failure

Two piles, and the sorting is not hard. Dull grout, hard water film, a scratched acrylic wall, and a door dragging on its track are surface complaints. An assembly failure looks different: grout opening repeatedly at one inside corner, a curb that has swollen out of square, or tile that flexes underfoot on the shower floor. The list also takes in baseboard gone spongy on the dry side of the wet wall, a smell back within a day of cleaning, and a mark on the basement ceiling directly under the drain. Drying time is the most useful single clue, because a wall that holds moisture for hours is holding it somewhere other than the glaze.

### What the wall is made of in a mid-century Bellevue bath

In the compact baths around the old Capehart Housing area, dated by the Bellevue Public Schools record of a 1959 lease and a first school dedicated in 1961, the original shower was usually tile set into a mortar bed over a sheet pan. Those beds serve for decades and then quit at the pan corners, after which the mortar simply stores water. Later remodels stacked their own eras on top: paper-faced board with nothing waterproof over it, an insert dropped onto the old bed, mastic used where mastic never belonged. Wall depth is the other constraint, since a shallow stud bay will not swallow a modern valve body or a recessed niche without furring out and losing room the bath does not have.

### A closed-up Nebraska house has one way out for moisture

Winter is when a weak shower shows itself. The National Weather Service office at Omaha/Valley logged 116 days with a low at or below 32 degrees during 2024, eight of them at or below zero, with 16.4 inches of snow against a 27.1 inch normal. For months at a stretch nobody opens a bathroom window, and Bellevue builds new work to the 2018 energy code, so the envelope is not leaking moisture out for you. Everything a shower produces has to leave through the fan or condense on the coldest surface in the room, which is usually the exterior wall behind the tile.

### The fan, the duct, and the cold attic above it

Capacity is set by code: bathroom local exhaust must deliver 50 cubic feet per minute intermittent or 20 continuous, measured at a quarter inch of water column. Capacity on the box is not the same as air actually moving, and the duct is where it goes wrong. A long flexible run, a sagging low spot, a missing damper, or a termination that simply ends above the insulation all cut real airflow. In this climate an uninsulated duct crossing a cold attic condenses inside itself and drips the water back down to the housing, which is how a newly rebuilt shower grows a stained ceiling in its second winter. Insulate the run, keep it short, slope it to the outside, and confirm it terminates through the roof or wall.

### Name the waterproofing on paper

Grout was never a waterproof layer, and a scope listing caulk, grout, and glass without naming a membrane is describing a cosmetic job on a failed assembly. Ask which system, whether it is a sheet membrane or a liquid applied one, how the corners and the drain flange are treated, and whether the pan gets a flood test before a single tile is set. Ask to see that test. Under the 2021 residential code Bellevue has adopted, a shower is an assembly of parts that have to work together, and the parts you will never see again are the ones worth putting in writing.

### Lower-level showers carry two extra questions

A basement shower ties into drainage below the floor slab, so both the connection and the opening cut for it belong in the scope. Radon is the second question: Nebraska DHHS puts the Sarpy County average at 5.3 picocuries per liter, measured over 5,649 tests between October 2019 and September 2024, so slab penetrations get sealed as they are made rather than left for later. Moisture history is worth asking about too. Water reached Offutt Air Force Base on March 15, 2019, and the Air Force reported roughly 30 base buildings affected two days afterward. That does not mean assuming damage in any particular lower level, only that evidence of it should be looked for before finishes hide the walls again.

### How the weeks run, and what widens the range

Plan on a staged week or two rather than a day. Protection and demolition, a real look at framing, drain, and vent, any corrective work you have approved, rough plumbing, inspection, backing and membrane, flood test, sloped bed and tile, grout, then glass, fixtures, and final sealing. Glass is a lead time rather than a task, since it gets templated on finished walls. What moves the number: whether the pan is rebuilt or replaced, whether the drain shifts, tile size and pattern, furring out a shallow wall, and framed versus frameless glass. A bid that cannot say which pan it intends to build has not looked behind the tile yet.

## svc_bathtub_replacement_lede

A tub swap looks like three days of work until the surround comes off the wall. What is waiting behind it, and what the removal route through the house looks like, decide whether that estimate survives contact with the room.

## svc_bathtub_replacement_body

### The evidence is usually one floor down

Most tub problems get diagnosed from the basement rather than the bathroom. Stand under the fixture and read the joist bay: rust weeping at the drain shoe, a white mineral trail on the trap arm, a stained ceiling panel, framing that has darkened, or a nail head bleeding through paint. Being able to see the connection from below is the one real advantage of building over a basement, and it beats guessing at the tub from above. Upstairs, press the floor of the tub with your weight on one foot. Movement in a steel or acrylic shell means the bed under it has failed or was never there.

### When refinishing is the honest answer

Not every tired tub needs to leave the house. A cast iron or steel tub with sound enamel and surface wear can be refinished, and a chip at the rim is a repair. Replacement earns its keep when the shell has a crack passing through it rather than across the glaze, or when the drain shoe or overflow gasket is feeding water into the framing. It also earns its keep when the surround has to come off for another reason anyway, or when the household would rather have something other than a tub. The question is never how the tub looks. It is where the water has been going.

### Getting it out of a Bellevue house

An enameled cast iron tub in a 1950s or 1960s bath is a heavy, awkward object in a room with one door. Either it comes apart in place, which means controlled breaking with the enamel covered and eye protection on everyone in the room, or it leaves whole. Going out whole makes the hall turn, the door casing, the stair run, and every finished surface between the bathroom and the driveway part of the plan. Fontenelle Hills is full of multi-level houses on rolling lots, and the base-adjacent housing near Offutt runs to narrow halls and compact rooms. Walking that route with a tape measure is not optional, and a crew that has not done it has not planned the day.

### The numbers taken before anything is ordered

Alcove length wall to wall at floor level and again at the rim, because tile and plaster walls are rarely parallel. Alcove depth. Rough-in from the finished wall to the drain center. Drain end, because a tub built for a drain on the wrong side cannot serve the alcove and goes back to the supplier. Apron height against the new unit, because a shallower tub changes the tile course above it. Valve type and spout height. Subfloor condition at the drain. Finished floor height on both sides of the threshold. Door and hall widths for the new unit boxed. Second visits on tub work almost always trace back to one of those numbers being taken from a product listing instead of the wall.

### What the surround has been hiding

Once the walls are open the job either confirms itself or changes. Common finds behind a Bellevue tub: an old trap arm run at the wrong pitch, or galvanized branch lines narrowed by scale. Others are backing that has been wet long enough to lose its grip, an earlier repair done with the wrong materials, or two layers of flooring meeting under the tub apron. Bellevue's rules matter at that moment. Clearing a stoppage or fixing a leak needs no permit, but the City treats a defective trap, drainpipe, waste, or vent pipe removed and replaced with new material as new work requiring a permit and inspection. The right response is a photograph, a written explanation, a price, and your approval before that correction starts.

### Doing this work in a Nebraska winter

Cold changes the sequence. The National Weather Service office at Omaha/Valley recorded 116 days with a low at or below 32 degrees in 2024 and eight at or below zero, with 27.1 inches as the normal seasonal snowfall. When a tub sits against an exterior wall and the wall gets opened, exposed supply lines and a house with the water off need attention before the crew leaves for the night during a cold snap. Deliveries and staging also take longer with snow on the drive, and the removal path picks up salt and slush that has to be protected against. None of that is dramatic, and all of it belongs in a January schedule.

### One bathroom, and what widens the band

Sequence matters more than finish selections when the tub being replaced is the only bathing fixture in the house. A swap in a sound alcove is a short interruption. If the framing behind the surround is wet, or the drain assembly has to be reworked below the floor, that window opens up, so ask how many nights the shower is out and what the fallback is. The band on this work stays narrow with a standard-size tub, a sound alcove, and a drain that stays put. It widens with a cast iron removal, wall and subfloor repair, a nonstandard opening, a tiled surround replacing a three-piece unit, or a drain that has to move.

## svc_walk_in_shower_installation_lede

Two very different builds get sold as a walk-in shower. One keeps a small dam and sits on the floor you already have. The other has no dam at all, which means the shower floor has to start lower than the room.

## svc_walk_in_shower_installation_body

### The dam is not the decision, the floor level is

A low threshold shower keeps a modest curb, often an inch or two, and can usually be built on the existing floor plane with a manufactured base. A level entry has nothing to step over, so the shower floor has to begin below the surrounding finished floor for the required fall to send water to the drain instead of into the room. That single difference drives the framing, the drain work, the permit path, and most of the price gap. Both are legitimate answers. Only one of them is a base swap, and plenty of households are better served by the version that keeps a small curb and spends the savings on the fixtures they use daily.

### What is under a Bellevue bathroom floor

Here the recess usually happens in a joist bay rather than in concrete. NAHB's analysis of the Census Survey of Construction found 62.3 percent of new single-family homes started in the West North Central region during 2024 had a full or partial basement, and older Bellevue stock follows the same habit. That is genuinely good news for a level entry: joists can often be headed off or a low-profile assembly set between them without breaking a slab. Joist direction and depth decide what is possible, and the finished ceiling below is part of the job, so the room under the bathroom gets opened, inspected, and put back.

### Sizes that actually work, borrowed from the accessibility standards

A house is not required to meet the ADA standards, but the dimensions in them are the most useful yardstick available. Section 608 sets a transfer type shower at 36 by 36 inches with a 36 inch entry and a 36 by 48 inch clear space measured from the control wall. A standard roll-in is at least 30 inches deep by 60 inches wide with a 60 inch entry, and an alternate roll-in is 36 by 60 with the entry at one end of the long side. Hold those numbers against the old tub alcove, which is commonly five feet long, and you learn quickly whether a wheelchair or a walker will turn in this room or whether depth has to be borrowed from a closet or hallway behind the wet wall.

### Blocking goes in while the wall is open

Grab bars are structural, and where they land is a framing decision made long before the tile layout. Solid backing across the wet wall, set at the heights the person actually reaches, costs very little while the studs are exposed. After tile goes on, bar placement is limited to wherever the studs happen to fall. The accessibility standards are again a useful guide: in a transfer type shower, bars run across the control wall and the back wall to a point 18 inches from the control wall. Those same standards allow a residence to skip the seat itself if the wall reinforcement for one is installed, which is exactly the right way to handle a bathroom that has to serve the household for another twenty years.

### Watch somebody use the room first

Before anything gets drawn, observe. Which hand goes out first, and to what. Whether the person sits to wash or stands. Whether balance fails on the step or on the turn. Whether help arrives from inside the spray or from the dry side of the opening. Whether the water can be brought up to temperature without standing under it, because waiting out a cold start is worse in a Nebraska February than anywhere warm. Those observations, rather than a product brochure, place the bench, the fixed head, the handheld, the controls, and the bars. A layout that skips this step produces a technically accessible shower nobody enjoys standing in.

### A lower-level conversion, and what comes up through the floor

Many of these projects happen in a finished basement, where a level entry is easier because the drain is already below the slab. Two things follow. First, Nebraska DHHS reports the Sarpy County radon average at 5.3 picocuries per liter across 5,649 tests between October 2019 and September 2024, and more than half of tests statewide come back above the 4.0 action level. That is why every new opening cut through the slab gets sealed as it is made and any existing mitigation piping stays intact. Second, moisture history is fair to ask about in a lower level, given that water reached Offutt Air Force Base on March 15, 2019 and roughly 30 buildings there were affected.

### Permits, licenses, and the order of the work

Moving a drain makes this regulated plumbing. Under Neb. Rev. Stat. 18-1917 the work belongs to a master plumber licensed by the city or village nearest the site, with licensed journeymen doing the installation, and Bellevue requires the plumbing permit to be applied for and signed by that master plumber rather than by the homeowner. Contractor registration with the Nebraska Department of Labor is a separate requirement, and the Department states plainly that it does not ensure quality of work. Expect this order: reach and layout decisions, permits, demolition, floor framing and drain work, blocking for bars and bench, rough plumbing, inspection, membrane and flood test, sloped base, tile, glass templated on finished walls, then fixtures and bars. Entry type, whether the drain moves, borrowed space, and glass set the range.

## svc_accessible_bathroom_remodel_lede

Accessible work is dimensional, and the dimensions come with tolerances. A grab bar mounted at 38 inches, a seat at 21 inches or a threshold at three quarters of an inch each defeat the purpose, so the numbers below are the specification rather than a guideline.

## svc_accessible_bathroom_remodel_body

<h2>Who this work is for in Bellevue</h2>
<p>The City reports 7,316 veterans living in Bellevue, per its <a href="https://www.bellevue.net/280/Economic-Community-Development">economic and community development page</a>, and Military OneSource counts about 26,000 military retirees inside a 75-mile radius of Offutt, per the <a href="https://installations.militaryonesource.mil/in-depth-overview/offutt-afb">installation overview</a>. A large share of the city's housing is also old enough to predate any thought of accessible layout; the 2023 plan Bellevue filed with the Legislature proposed a code inspection program for structures 60 or more years old, per the <a href="https://nebraskalegislature.gov/pdf/reports/committee/urban/Bellevue_2023_Affordable_Housing_Action_Plan.pdf">Affordable Housing Action Plan</a>. The result is a lot of 5 by 8 hall baths with a 24-inch door serving households where somebody now needs a seat and a bar.</p>
<h2>The compartment dimensions are absolute</h2>
<p>Federal accessibility guidance describes a transfer-type shower compartment as 36 inches by 36 inches, with those dimensions given as absolute rather than minimum, and an entry opening of at least 36 inches, per the <a href="https://www.access-board.gov/ada/guides/chapter-6-bathing-rooms/">Access Board's bathing rooms guide</a>. A roll-in compartment is a different geometry with different clearances. The reason 36 by 36 is absolute is that a transfer shower depends on the user reaching the opposite wall from the seat; make it bigger and the bars and controls move out of reach. Private homes are not subject to the same enforcement as public facilities, but the dimensions are the ones the research behind the guidance produced, and there is no advantage to guessing instead.</p>
<h2>Grab bars: height, clearance, and pull-out force</h2>
<p>Grab bars in bathing facilities are mounted with the gripping surface 33 to 36 inches above the finished floor, with 1-1/2 inches of clearance to the wall stated as an absolute, and they have to resist 250 pounds of force applied at any point in any direction, per the <a href="https://www.access-board.gov/ada/guides/chapter-6-bathing-rooms/">Access Board guide</a>. That last number is why blocking matters more than the bar. A bar screwed into tile over half-inch backer with plastic anchors will not hold 250 pounds no matter what the packaging says. Solid blocking between the studs across the whole bar zone, installed while the wall is open, is the only version of this that performs. Where the wall is already tiled, the alternative is a plywood backer field, which changes the tile plane and has to be planned before layout.</p>
<h2>Seat, controls, and water temperature</h2>
<p>A shower seat is set 17 to 19 inches above the floor and positioned within 3 inches of the compartment entry, the handheld spray unit has to be reachable from a point within 48 inches of the floor, controls take no more than 5 pounds of operating force, and delivered water temperature is limited to 120 degrees Fahrenheit, per the <a href="https://www.access-board.gov/ada/guides/chapter-6-bathing-rooms/">bathing rooms guide</a>. That temperature ceiling matches what the plumbing code already demands in Bellevue: UPC 408.3 requires a pressure balance, thermostatic, or combination valve to ASSE 1016 or ASME A112.18.1/CSA B125.1 with handle stops set to a 120 degree maximum mixed setting, and states that a water heater thermostat is not a suitable control for the purpose, per the <a href="https://forms.iapmo.org/email_marketing/codespotlight/2018/may17.htm">UPC 408.3 spotlight</a>.</p>
<h2>The threshold is where the structure argues back</h2>
<p>The accessibility guidance limits a shower compartment threshold to half an inch, beveled at 1:2 where it exceeds a quarter inch, and permits a 2-inch threshold in alterations only where providing a half-inch threshold would disturb the structural reinforcement of the floor slab, per the <a href="https://www.access-board.gov/ada/guides/chapter-6-bathing-rooms/">Access Board guide</a>. In a Bellevue house on a wood-framed floor the practical route to a low or level entry is recessing the pan into the joist bays, which means cutting and headering. On a slab-on-grade lower level it means either a pan built up above the slab with a ramped approach or breaking the slab, which is where that alteration exception earns its place. Either way the waterproofing changes: in a curbless shower IRC R307.2's 72-inch nonabsorbent finish is measured from the drain inlet rather than the floor, per <a href="https://www.chippy.tools/building-codes/us/wet-areas/waterproofing-requirements/">this reading of R307.2</a>.</p>
<h2>Getting into the room at all</h2>
<p>Fixture clearances still govern. P2705.1 sets 15 inches from a fixture center to any wall, partition or vanity, 30 inches between adjacent fixture centers, and 21 inches of clear space in front, per the <a href="http://media.iccsafe.org/code-development/group-a/IRC-P.pdf">ICC plumbing provisions</a>. None of that helps if the door is 24 inches wide and swings in. Widening the opening, changing the swing outward, or fitting a pocket door is often the single most useful change in an older hall bath, and it is framing work rather than plumbing work. For the floor surface and transition detail that follows, see <a href="../bathroom-flooring/">bathroom flooring</a>; the <a href="../walk-in-shower-installation/">walk-in shower page</a> covers the enclosure itself.</p>

## svc_basement_bathroom_addition_lede

A basement bathroom is not a bathroom that happens to be downstairs. The drain sits below the sewer, the slab has to be opened, and in Bellevue there are two documented local reasons to look hard before finishing anything below grade.

## svc_basement_bathroom_addition_body

<h2>Drainage below the sewer line</h2>
<p>The defining constraint is elevation. A basement fixture generally sits below the level of the building drain leaving the house, so waste cannot flow by gravity. Two solutions exist. Break the slab, trench to a sump, and set a sealed sewage ejector basin with a pump that lifts waste up to the gravity line. Or install an above-floor macerating system behind the fixture, which avoids the slab but puts a pump and a cutting assembly in the room. The ejector approach is more work and more durable; the macerating approach is faster and has consumable parts. Either way the drain layout, vent connection and pump discharge get decided before the framing goes up, because both routes change where the walls can be.</p>
<h2>Bellevue's flood record is a reason to inspect first</h2>
<p>The City's flood information page documents that on April 23, 2019 Bellevue sent certified mail letters to all residents whose dwellings were deemed unsafe or unfit for human occupancy because of damage and contaminates from flood waters, and its updates name the Paradise Lakes and Green Acres developments among the areas addressed, per the <a href="https://www.bellevue.net/information/flood-information">City of Bellevue flood information page</a>. At Offutt, flood water reached the installation on March 15, 2019 and the Air Force reported roughly 30 buildings affected within two days, per <a href="https://www.offutt.af.mil/News/Article/1787242/team-offutt-battling-flood-waters/">Offutt's own account of the response</a>. Most basements in the city were never touched. The point is narrower: before an impermeable finish goes over a below-grade floor or a stud wall goes against a foundation wall, take a moisture reading on the slab, look at the base of any existing framing, and confirm nothing was covered up after a prior water event.</p>
<h2>Radon: cutting the slab opens the path</h2>
<p>Sarpy County's average pre-mitigation radon level is 5.3 picocuries per liter, drawn from 5,649 measurements taken between October 1, 2019 and September 30, 2024, per the <a href="https://nebraskalegislature.gov/FloorDocs/108/PDF/Agencies/Health_and_Human_Services__Department_of/713_20241211-101151.pdf">Nebraska Department of Health and Human Services radon report</a>. The EPA action level is 4.0. Every penetration a bathroom makes in that slab — the closet bend, the shower drain, the ejector pit, the lavatory trap arm — is a potential entry route, and where a mitigation system already exists, its performance depends on the slab staying sealed. Seal each penetration as it is backfilled, keep the suction pit and its piping intact, and test after the work is finished. A post-work test costs a fraction of the finish it would otherwise call into question.</p>
<h2>Permits, and who files them</h2>
<p>Bellevue's permit division states that no structure regulated by the division within the city and its two-mile jurisdiction may be altered, repaired or converted without a permit first obtained from the building official, and that unless otherwise exempted separate plumbing, mechanical and electrical permits are required, per the <a href="https://www.bellevue.net/departments/permits-inspections/faqs?QuestionID=31&amp;AFMID=831">City's permit FAQ</a>. A new below-grade bathroom is new work by every measure. Under <a href="https://codelibrary.amlegal.com/codes/bellevuene/latest/bellevue_ne/0-0-0-7761">§ 27-58</a> the plumbing permit is applied for, signed and paid for by the master plumber, and <a href="https://nebraskalegislature.gov/laws/statutes.php?statute=18-1917">Nebraska statute 18-1917</a> requires that plumber to hold a license from the nearest city or village with journeymen licensed by the same. Plumbing fees are $23.50 for issuance plus $9.80 per fixture or trap, with $4.75 per fixture where drainage or vent piping is altered, per the <a href="https://www.bellevue.net/DocumentCenter/View/952/Plumbing-Fees-PDF">plumbing fee schedule</a>. Building permit valuation runs $69.25 for the first $2,000 plus $14.00 per additional $1,000 under the 1997 Uniform Administrative Code table Bellevue still uses, per the <a href="https://www.bellevue.net/DocumentCenter/View/951/Building-Permit-Fees-PDF">building permit fee schedule</a>.</p>
<h2>Insulating a below-grade room in Climate Zone 5</h2>
<p>Nebraska sits in Climate Zone 5, where the 2018 energy code Bellevue adopted alongside the IRC prescribes R-19 or R-15 continuous at a basement wall, R-49 at the ceiling and 3 air changes per hour at 50 pascals, per the <a href="https://insulationinstitute.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/N130-NE-Energy-Code-0425.pdf">state energy code summary</a>. Sarpy County's 99 percent heating design temperature is 2 degrees Fahrenheit, per the <a href="https://www.energystar.gov/ia/partners/bldrs_lenders_raters/downloads/County%20Level%20Design%20Temperature%20Reference%20Guide%20-%202015-06-24.pdf">county design temperature guide</a>, and Omaha reached 16 below zero on January 14, 2024, per the <a href="https://www.weather.gov/oax/2024climatesummary">National Weather Service annual summary</a>. The assembly that survives that is continuous rigid foam against the concrete with the framing held off the wall, not fiberglass batts stuffed against masonry where warm interior air can reach a cold surface. Any supply line run in a rim joist cavity or against a foundation wall is a freeze candidate and belongs inside the insulation, not behind it.</p>
<h2>Ventilation and heat for a room with no exterior window</h2>
<p>Below grade there is usually no glazing, so natural ventilation under R303.3 is unavailable and mechanical exhaust is the only route: 50 cfm intermittent or 20 cfm continuous under IRC M1505.4.4, per <a href="https://buildingcodetrainer.com/bathroom-ventilation/">this summary of the ventilation sections</a>. The duct has to reach the exterior, which below grade means a longer run and often a rim-joist penetration, so size the fan for the actual run and insulate the duct. Basements also run cold, which makes an electric floor a reasonable addition here; see <a href="../heated-bathroom-floor/">heated bathroom floor</a> for the circuit and protection requirements. The <a href="../services/">services list</a> shows how the pieces of a below-grade build fit together.</p>

## svc_bathroom_flooring_lede

Bathroom flooring is a moisture assembly that happens to be walked on. What decides whether it lasts is the subfloor stiffness, the transition at the door, and whether the layer under the finish can survive the day a supply line lets go.

## svc_bathroom_flooring_body

<h2>Stiffness is a number, not a feeling</h2>
<p>Tile does not tolerate movement. A floor that feels solid underfoot can still deflect enough between joists to open every grout line in a season, and the culprit is usually joist span or a single layer of thin subfloor rather than the tile. The correct order of operations is to check span and sheathing thickness, add a second layer or a stiffening membrane if the assembly is marginal, then set the finish. Sheet vinyl and luxury vinyl plank forgive far more deflection, which is one honest reason they end up in rentals and basements. It is also why a plank floor telegraphs every high spot in an old subfloor: the material bends but the seams do not hide a hump. The <a href="../services/">services list</a> keeps the floor work separate from the wet-area assembly for that reason.</p>
<h2>The toilet flange is where old floors are already rotten</h2>
<p>Almost every failed bathroom floor in an older Bellevue house fails within eighteen inches of the toilet. A wax seal that broke years ago wicks water into the subfloor edge around the flange, and the damage is invisible until the fixture is pulled. The repair is not a patch over the soft area; it is cutting back to sound sheathing, sistering or blocking as needed, and resetting the flange at the height the new floor build-up puts it. Get that height wrong and the toilet rocks, which restarts the same failure. The <a href="../toilet-installation/">toilet installation page</a> covers the flange height arithmetic in full.</p>
<h2>Build-up height and the door</h2>
<p>Every flooring decision changes the finished height, and in a bathroom that has three consequences at once. The door has to clear, the transition to the hall has to be safe rather than a trip edge, and the toilet flange has to sit at the right elevation relative to the new surface. A tile floor over cement board can add three quarters of an inch over a plank floor; an uncoupling membrane adds less; vinyl over the existing subfloor adds almost nothing. In houses where the bathroom door swings in and the room is tight — the standard condition in the compact base-adjacent baths dating from the Capehart-era additions the City records from 1959 onward, per <a href="https://ne50000580.schoolwires.net/domain/288">Bellevue Public Schools' account of the housing annex</a> — half an inch of build-up can mean cutting the door bottom or changing to an out-swing or a pocket door.</p>
<h2>Lower-level floors carry a moisture history</h2>
<p>Bellevue has a documented reason to look before covering a lower-level floor. During the 2019 flood the City sent certified letters on April 23, 2019 to residents whose dwellings were deemed unsafe or unfit for occupancy because of damage and contaminates from flood waters, naming the Paradise Lakes and Green Acres developments in its flood updates, per the <a href="https://www.bellevue.net/information/flood-information">City's flood information page</a>. At the base, water arrived on March 15 and the Air Force reported roughly 30 buildings affected two days later, per <a href="https://www.offutt.af.mil/News/Article/1787242/team-offutt-battling-flood-waters/">Offutt's own account</a>. That does not mean every lower level in the city has damage. It means a floor going into a below-grade bathroom deserves a moisture reading on the slab and a look at the base of the framing before an impermeable finish traps whatever is there.</p>
<h2>Radon and the floor you are about to cut</h2>
<p>Sarpy County's average pre-mitigation radon level is 5.3 picocuries per liter across 5,649 measurements taken between October 1, 2019 and September 30, 2024, per the <a href="https://nebraskalegislature.gov/FloorDocs/108/PDF/Agencies/Health_and_Human_Services__Department_of/713_20241211-101151.pdf">Nebraska DHHS radon measurement report</a>, against an EPA action level of 4.0. Any floor work in a basement bathroom that cuts the slab — a new drain, a relocated closet bend, a shower base — opens a path that a mitigation system depends on staying closed. Seal each penetration as it is made, leave a suction pit and its piping alone, and retest after the floor goes back. A retest costs almost nothing next to a finished floor.</p>
<h2>Choosing by exposure, not by showroom</h2>
<p>Porcelain tile with a rated slip coefficient handles a wet floor and a family bathroom better than most alternatives and is unaffected by standing water. Sheet vinyl in a single piece with heat-welded seams is genuinely waterproof and is the sane choice in a rental turning over quickly. Luxury vinyl plank is waterproof at the plank and not at the joints, so it does well beside a tub and poorly at a curbless shower. Engineered wood belongs in a powder room and nowhere near a shower. Whatever goes down, the perimeter needs a sealed joint rather than a painted-in gap, because in a Bellevue winter — Sarpy County's heating design temperature is 2 degrees Fahrenheit, per the <a href="https://www.energystar.gov/ia/partners/bldrs_lenders_raters/downloads/County%20Level%20Design%20Temperature%20Reference%20Guide%20-%202015-06-24.pdf">design temperature reference</a> — the coldest part of the room is the floor edge against an exterior wall, and that is where condensation collects. For the wall side of the same problem, see <a href="../bathroom-tile-installation/">bathroom tile installation</a>.</p>

## svc_bathroom_lighting_and_ventilation_lede

The fan and the fixtures are the two systems most often left alone in a remodel, and they are the two that decide whether the tile job survives. One removes moisture at a rate the code specifies. The other has to be listed for the place it hangs.

## svc_bathroom_lighting_and_ventilation_body

<h2>The exhaust number is 50 cfm, and it is not a suggestion</h2>
<p>IRC M1505.4.4 requires bathroom local exhaust ventilation of 50 cfm intermittent or 20 cfm continuous, while R303.3 permits natural ventilation as an alternative only where openable glazing of not less than 3 square feet is provided with at least half of that area openable, per <a href="https://buildingcodetrainer.com/bathroom-ventilation/">this account of the ventilation sections</a>. Bellevue builds residential work to the 2021 IRC, adopted as Chapters 2 through 24 and Chapter 44 at <a href="https://codelibrary.amlegal.com/codes/bellevuene/latest/bellevue_ne/0-0-0-2638">§ 8-17 of the city code</a>. Two practical notes. Rated cfm is measured at a specified static pressure, so a 50 cfm fan on a long flex duct with two elbows delivers less than 50. And in a climate where Sarpy County's 99 percent heating design temperature is 2 degrees Fahrenheit, per the <a href="https://www.energystar.gov/ia/partners/bldrs_lenders_raters/downloads/County%20Level%20Design%20Temperature%20Reference%20Guide%20-%202015-06-24.pdf">county design temperature guide</a>, the window is not a ventilation strategy for six months of the year.</p>
<h2>Duct routing is where most installations fail</h2>
<p>The failure pattern is consistent: rigid or smooth duct swapped for corrugated flex, a run longer than the fan is rated for, elbows stacked at the housing, and no insulation across an unconditioned attic. That last one matters most in Nebraska. Omaha's coldest reading in 2024 was 16 below zero on January 14, per the <a href="https://www.weather.gov/oax/2024climatesummary">National Weather Service annual climate summary</a>. Warm, humid air moving through cold uninsulated duct condenses inside the duct, and the water runs back down to the housing and stains the ceiling — a problem homeowners routinely diagnose as a roof leak. Insulated duct, a short run, a smooth interior, a backdraft damper, and a terminating hood with a real flap fix it. Terminate outside. Never into a soffit cavity or an attic.</p>
<h2>Sizing above the minimum</h2>
<p>The 50 cfm figure is a floor set for a modest room. A large primary bath with a separate shower compartment and a soaking tub moves more moisture than one fan at one point can capture, and the answer is either a higher-capacity unit or two pickup points — one over the shower, one over the tub. Run time matters as much as capacity, because moisture keeps leaving the surfaces after the shower stops. A timer or a humidity-sensing control that keeps the fan running after the room empties removes more water than a larger fan on a light switch, and it does so without depending on anyone remembering.</p>
<h2>Luminaires have a listing requirement inside the wet zone</h2>
<p>NEC 410.10(D) requires luminaires within the tub or shower zone to be listed for damp or wet locations, per <a href="https://wireref.com/gfci/bathroom/">this reference on bathroom electrical rules</a>. Bellevue has adopted the 2023 National Electrical Code with enforcement from January 1, 2025, per the <a href="https://www.bellevue.net/331/Permits-Inspections">City's permits and inspections page</a>. Ground-fault protection under 210.8(A)(1) applies to bathroom receptacles, and in the 2023 edition its scope covers receptacles rated 125 volts through 250 volts. A recessed can in a shower ceiling therefore needs a wet-location trim and gasket, not just a bulb that fits. A fan-light combination sitting over a shower needs the same listing for the whole assembly.</p>
<h2>Circuits, and what the City exempts</h2>
<p>NEC 210.11(C)(3) requires at least one 20-ampere branch circuit for bathroom receptacle outlets with no other outlets on it, though that circuit may serve more than one bathroom, and 210.52(D) requires at least one receptacle within 36 inches of the outside edge of each basin, per the <a href="https://www.naffainc.com/x/CB2/Elect/Bathrooms.htm">bathroom electrical reference</a>. Bellevue's exemption list is narrow: repair or replacement of a fixed approved appliance of the same type and rating in the same location needs no electrical permit, and neither does reinstallation of attachment plug receptacles, "but not the outlets," per the <a href="https://www.bellevue.net/departments/permits-inspections/faqs?QuestionID=31&amp;AFMID=831">City's permit FAQ</a>. Adding a fan circuit, a new box, or a heated floor circuit is outside that list.</p>
<h2>Layout that makes the room work</h2>
<p>One ceiling fixture puts every face in its own shadow. The arrangement that works is layered: vertical or flanking lights at the mirror at roughly eye height for even facial lighting, a wet-listed downlight in the shower, an ambient source for the room, and separate switching so the whole array is not on at 6 a.m. Keep the color temperature consistent across every lamp in the room, because mixed temperatures read as a defect in a small tiled space. Where the ceiling is being opened for the fan and the wiring anyway, that is the moment to insulate and air seal the ceiling plane to the R-49 the Nebraska energy code prescribes for Climate Zone 5, per the <a href="https://insulationinstitute.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/N130-NE-Energy-Code-0425.pdf">state energy code summary</a>. For the heated surface that shares those circuits, see <a href="../heated-bathroom-floor/">heated bathroom floor</a>.</p>

## svc_bathroom_plumbing_fixtures_lede

Fixtures are the visible end of a system that is mostly hidden. In Bellevue the hidden part decides the job: what the supply is made of, where the trap sits, whether the valve meets the scald standard, and who is legally allowed to file the permit.

## svc_bathroom_plumbing_fixtures_body

<h2>The valve standard is not optional</h2>
<p>Bellevue enforces the 2021 Uniform Plumbing Code, adopted at <a href="https://codelibrary.amlegal.com/codes/bellevuene/latest/bellevue_ne/0-0-0-7799">§ 27-85 of the city code</a> with local amendments at § 27-87. UPC 408.3 requires shower and tub-shower combination control valves to be of the pressure balance, thermostatic, or combination pressure balance and thermostatic type conforming to ASSE 1016 or ASME A112.18.1/CSA B125.1, with handle position stops adjusted per the manufacturer's instructions to deliver a maximum mixed water setting of 120 degrees Fahrenheit, and it states that water heater thermostats "shall not be considered a suitable control for meeting this provision," per the <a href="https://forms.iapmo.org/email_marketing/codespotlight/2018/may17.htm">UPC 408.3 code spotlight</a>. Two consequences follow. Turning the water heater down does not satisfy the code. And a trim kit sold to fit an old two-handle body does not convert that body into a compliant valve.</p>
<h2>Who can legally do the work</h2>
<p><a href="https://nebraskalegislature.gov/laws/statutes.php?statute=18-1917">Nebraska Revised Statute 18-1917</a> requires that any person who installs or repairs sanitary plumbing in the state be a duly qualified master plumber licensed by the city or village nearest the construction site, and that the employees performing the actual installation or repair also hold journeyman licenses from that same city or village. <a href="https://codelibrary.amlegal.com/codes/bellevuene/latest/bellevue_ne/0-0-0-7761">Bellevue city code § 27-58</a> adds that no licensed person may do plumbing work in the city or its two-mile zoning jurisdiction without a city-issued permit naming the authorized master plumber or firm, that permits under the article are applied for by the master plumber, and that once approved the permit is signed and paid for by that license holder. The fee side is published: $23.50 for permit issuance, $9.80 per fixture or trap, $4.75 per fixture where drainage or vent piping is altered, and $49.50 for a failed plumbing reinspection, per the <a href="https://www.bellevue.net/DocumentCenter/View/952/Plumbing-Fees-PDF">plumbing fee schedule</a>.</p>
<h2>Where the permit exemption ends</h2>
<p>The City draws the line clearly. No permit is required for clearing stoppages or repairing leaks in pipes, valves or fixtures where the repair does not involve replacing or rearranging valves, pipes or fixtures. But should any trap, drainpipe, soil, waste or vent pipe become defective and need removal and replacement with new material, that is considered new work and "a permit shall be procured and inspections made as provided," per the <a href="https://www.bellevue.net/departments/permits-inspections/faqs?QuestionID=31&amp;AFMID=831">City's permit FAQ</a>. In practice a fixture change crosses that line the moment the old trap or the old stub-outs will not take a new connection, which on a house of a certain age is most of the time.</p>
<h2>Supply piping decides how far the job goes</h2>
<p>Galvanized steel supply piping was common in houses built up to roughly 1950 and typically lasts 40 to 60 years, per <a href="https://inspectapedia.com/plumbing/Galvanized_Iron_Pipes.php">InspectApedia</a>, and CorrView reports galvanized supply failures inside that same 40 to 60 year window while extra heavy cast iron and ductile iron waste lines carry an expected life of 75 to 100 years, per its <a href="https://www.corrview.com/technical-bulletin-pd-02">piping bulletin</a>. Bellevue has a lot of housing in that band; the City's 2023 plan to the Legislature sought a code inspection program for every structure 60 or more years old and located the worst pockets near the downtown core and the oldest neighborhoods north and south of it, per the <a href="https://nebraskalegislature.gov/pdf/reports/committee/urban/Bellevue_2023_Affordable_Housing_Action_Plan.pdf">Affordable Housing Action Plan</a>. The tell is flow: open an old angle stop and if the stream is weak, the bore is narrowed by corrosion and a new faucet will disappoint on day one.</p>
<h2>Clearances are set by the fixture, not the room</h2>
<p>IRC P2705.1 fixes the geometry: no water closet, lavatory or bidet set closer than 15 inches from its center to any side wall, partition or vanity, no closer than 30 inches center to center between adjacent fixtures, and no less than 21 inches of clear space in front, per the <a href="http://media.iccsafe.org/code-development/group-a/IRC-P.pdf">ICC plumbing provisions</a>. A wall-hung lavatory that replaces a vanity changes the 15-inch measurement because the obstruction disappears. A wider vanity replacing a narrow one changes it the other way. Both are worth checking with a tape before the cabinet or the fixture is ordered rather than after.</p>
<h2>The parts worth paying for</h2>
<p>Brass or stainless valve bodies over plastic. Ceramic disc cartridges from a manufacturer whose parts are still sold. Quarter-turn angle stops instead of multi-turn stops that seize. Braided stainless supply connectors rather than plastic ones. A tub or shower valve with an accessible service plate. None of that shows in a photograph and all of it decides whether the fixture is still working in fifteen years. For the enclosure and drain work that a valve change often uncovers, see <a href="../tub-to-shower-conversion/">tub-to-shower conversion</a>; the <a href="../services/">services list</a> shows how fixture work fits into the larger scopes.</p>

## svc_bathroom_tile_installation_lede

Tile is the part everyone chooses and the last part that fails. What decides whether a Bellevue tile job holds up for twenty years is the substrate under it, the plane it was set on, and whether an exterior wall behind it was insulated to the state's energy code before anyone reached for thinset.

## svc_bathroom_tile_installation_body

<h2>Substrate first, pattern second</h2>
<p>A tile assembly is a stack: framing, backer, waterproofing where the code demands it, mortar bed, tile, grout. Every failure mode lives in the lower half of that stack. Deflection in the floor cracks grout lines and then tiles. A backer that absorbs water swells and lets the tile release. A wall that is out of plane by half an inch across eight feet forces lippage that no grout width hides. So the sequence on a real job is: verify the framing, correct the plane, install the code-listed backer, waterproof, then lay out. Selecting a tile before that inspection happens is how a good-looking sample ends up on a bad substrate. The <a href="../services/">full service list</a> separates the surface work from the assembly work behind it for the same reason.</p>
<h2>The backer list is short and the code is specific</h2>
<p>Bellevue builds residential work to the 2021 International Residential Code, adopted at <a href="https://codelibrary.amlegal.com/codes/bellevuene/latest/bellevue_ne/0-0-0-2638">§ 8-17 of the city code</a> as Chapters 2 through 24 plus Chapter 44. IRC Table R702.4.2 lists what may back wall tile in tub and shower areas: glass mat gypsum backing panel to ASTM C1178, fiber-reinforced gypsum panels to ASTM C1278, nonasbestos fiber-cement backer board to ASTM C1288 or ISO 8336 Category C, and nonasbestos fiber mat-reinforced cementitious backer units to ASTM C1325. R702.3.7.1 then states that water-resistant gypsum backing board "shall not be used where there will be direct exposure to water, or in areas subject to continuous high humidity," and R702.3.7 bars water-resistant gypsum board over a Class I or II vapor retarder inside a shower or tub compartment while requiring cut and exposed edges to be sealed as the manufacturer directs. All of that text is in <a href="https://up.codes/viewer/new_jersey/irc-2021/chapter/7/wall-covering">IRC Chapter 7</a>. Practically: green board can back the tile on the dry wall beside the vanity and cannot back the tile inside the wet enclosure.</p>
<h2>Where the wet zone actually ends</h2>
<p>IRC R307.2 sets the nonabsorbent finish requirement at 72 inches above the floor on shower walls and on tub walls where a showerhead is installed, and in a shower with no curb the height is measured from the drain inlet, per <a href="https://www.chippy.tools/building-codes/us/wet-areas/waterproofing-requirements/">this reading of R307.2</a>. That 72-inch line is a minimum, not a design intent. Tile that stops at 72 inches in a room with an eight-foot ceiling leaves a horizontal joint at eye level where the membrane terminates, and that joint is the first thing to fail if the fan is undersized. Running tile to the ceiling on the wet walls is a durability decision, not a luxury one, and it changes the quantity takeoff by enough that it belongs in the estimate rather than in a change order.</p>
<h2>Exterior walls, the energy code, and Nebraska cold</h2>
<p>Sarpy County's 99 percent heating design temperature is 2 degrees Fahrenheit, per the <a href="https://www.energystar.gov/ia/partners/bldrs_lenders_raters/downloads/County%20Level%20Design%20Temperature%20Reference%20Guide%20-%202015-06-24.pdf">county-level design temperature guide</a>, and Omaha's coldest reading in 2024 was 16 below zero on January 14, per the <a href="https://www.weather.gov/oax/2024climatesummary">National Weather Service annual summary</a>. Nebraska sits in Climate Zone 5, where the 2018 energy code Bellevue adopted asks for R-20 or R-13 plus R-5 continuous in a wood frame wall, R-49 at the ceiling and R-19 or R-15 continuous at a basement wall, per the <a href="https://insulationinstitute.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/N130-NE-Energy-Code-0425.pdf">state energy code summary</a>. Bellevue adopted the 2018 International Energy Conservation Code in the same ordinance that adopted the IRC. When a shower wall is also an exterior wall — common in the compact base-adjacent baths and in Olde Towne — the studs are open exactly once in the life of that room. Insulating and air sealing then costs a fraction of what it costs after the tile is set, and it is the difference between a cold tile surface that condenses and one that does not.</p>
<h2>Layout decisions that survive a wet floor</h2>
<p>Set the layout off the plumbing wall and the most visible sightline rather than off a corner, because a cut of less than half a tile at the entry reads as an error from the doorway while the same cut in a back corner disappears. On floors, slope governs before pattern does: a linear drain lets a large-format tile run in one direction on a single plane, while a center drain under a 12 by 24 tile forces radial slope and cuts. Movement joints belong at changes of plane and at the perimeter, not wherever the tile runs out.</p>
<h2>What the older half of the city adds</h2>
<p>Bellevue's 2023 housing plan filed with the Legislature counted 26,976 housing units in 2022, roughly 18,234 owner and 8,742 rental, and proposed a code inspection program aimed at structures 60 or more years old, per the <a href="https://nebraskalegislature.gov/pdf/reports/committee/urban/Bellevue_2023_Affordable_Housing_Action_Plan.pdf">Affordable Housing Action Plan</a>. In that stock the walls are rarely flat and a previous owner has often tiled over tile, so correcting the plane with furring is a real line item. For the membrane detailing under all of this, see <a href="../bathroom-waterproofing/">bathroom waterproofing</a>.</p>

## svc_bathroom_waterproofing_lede

Waterproofing is the only part of a bathroom that has to work perfectly and is impossible to inspect once the tile is on. It is also the layer most often reduced to a coat of paint on a board that was never rated for water.

## svc_bathroom_waterproofing_body

<h2>Water resistance and waterproofing are different products</h2>
<p>The distinction the code draws is worth stating exactly. IRC R702.3.7.1 says water-resistant gypsum backing board "shall not be used where there will be direct exposure to water, or in areas subject to continuous high humidity," and R702.3.7 adds that water-resistant gypsum board shall not be installed over a Class I or II vapor retarder in a shower or tub compartment, with cut and exposed edges — including those at wall intersections — sealed as the manufacturer recommends, per the <a href="https://up.codes/viewer/new_jersey/irc-2021/chapter/7/wall-covering">IRC wall covering chapter</a>. Water resistant means it tolerates humidity. Waterproof means liquid water cannot pass. A shower needs the second thing, and green board is the first thing. Bellevue enforces the 2021 IRC, Chapters 2 through 24 plus Chapter 44, per <a href="https://codelibrary.amlegal.com/codes/bellevuene/latest/bellevue_ne/0-0-0-2638">§ 8-17 of the city code</a>.</p>
<h2>The approved backer list</h2>
<p>IRC Table R702.4.2 names what may back wall tile in tub and shower areas: glass mat gypsum backing panel to ASTM C1178, fiber-reinforced gypsum panels to ASTM C1278, nonasbestos fiber-cement backer board to ASTM C1288 or ISO 8336 Category C, and nonasbestos fiber mat-reinforced cementitious backer units to ASTM C1325, per the <a href="https://up.codes/viewer/new_jersey/irc-2021/chapter/7/wall-covering">IRC wall covering chapter</a>. Those are substrates, not membranes. A cement board wall without a membrane or a surface-applied waterproofing layer still passes water into the cavity; cement board simply does not degrade when it happens. The assembly that performs pairs a listed backer with either a sheet membrane, a bonded liquid membrane applied to the manufacturer's wet-film thickness, or a foam board system with sealed seams.</p>
<h2>How high, and from where</h2>
<p>IRC R307.2 requires a nonabsorbent finish on shower walls, and on bathtub walls where a showerhead is installed, to a height of 72 inches above the floor, and in a shower with no curb that 72 inches is measured from the drain inlet rather than the floor, per <a href="https://www.chippy.tools/building-codes/us/wet-areas/waterproofing-requirements/">this reading of R307.2</a>. That second clause is the one people miss on a curbless build, where the floor plane slopes away and the reference point moves. Seventy-two inches is a minimum. In a room with an eight-foot ceiling and a rain head mounted overhead, carrying the membrane and the tile to the ceiling removes the horizontal termination joint that would otherwise sit right where the wettest air collects.</p>
<h2>Corners, changes of plane, and penetrations</h2>
<p>Flat field area almost never fails. Failures happen at inside corners, at the wall-to-pan transition, at the curb, at the drain flange, at valve and showerhead penetrations, at niche openings and at fastener heads. Every one of those gets treated as a detail rather than as part of the field: preformed corners or reinforcing band bedded in the membrane, a bonding flange drain integrated with the sheet or liquid system, sealant compatible with the membrane at pipe penetrations, and a niche waterproofed as its own small room with a sloped sill. Mixing manufacturers across a single assembly is where warranties and performance both end.</p>
<h2>Test before anything covers it</h2>
<p>A flood test is the only real verification available. Plug the drain, fill the pan to just below the top of the curb or the perimeter, mark the water level, and leave it for 24 hours with a look at the ceiling or the joist bay below. Do that before the tile is set, because after the tile is set the test tells you a leak exists without telling you where. Bellevue's permit division states that when a trap, drainpipe, soil, waste or vent pipe is removed and replaced with new material the job is new work for which "a permit shall be procured and inspections made as provided," per the <a href="https://www.bellevue.net/departments/permits-inspections/faqs?QuestionID=31&amp;AFMID=831">City's permit FAQ</a>. The City's Permits and Inspection Division runs six inspectors and two administrative personnel and in 2009 received a second number one rating from the Insurance Services Office in the Building Code Effectiveness Grading Classifications, a rating awarded to only five jurisdictions in the country, per the <a href="https://www.bellevue.net/331/Permits-Inspections">City's permits and inspections page</a>. Concealed work is worth showing before it is concealed.</p>
<h2>Vapor, insulation, and the Nebraska cold side</h2>
<p>On an exterior wall the waterproofing decision interacts with the vapor decision, which is why R702.3.7 bars water-resistant gypsum over a Class I or II vapor retarder inside a shower compartment. Nebraska is Climate Zone 5, where the 2018 energy code prescribes R-20 or R-13 plus R-5 continuous for a wood frame wall and 3 air changes per hour at 50 pascals, per the <a href="https://insulationinstitute.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/N130-NE-Energy-Code-0425.pdf">state energy code summary</a>, and Sarpy County's 99 percent heating design temperature is 2 degrees Fahrenheit, per the <a href="https://www.energystar.gov/ia/partners/bldrs_lenders_raters/downloads/County%20Level%20Design%20Temperature%20Reference%20Guide%20-%202015-06-24.pdf">county design temperature guide</a>. A shower wall on the outside of the building needs one continuous water control layer on the room side and enough insulation that the cavity does not sit at dew point. Two membranes with a cold cavity between them is how a wall stays wet. For the tile that goes over all of this, see <a href="../bathroom-tile-installation/">bathroom tile installation</a>.</p>

## svc_guest_bathroom_remodel_lede

A hall bath serves people who did not choose the fixtures, and in a city with Offutt Air Force Base next door it often serves a tenant or a buyer on a schedule. That changes the specification: durable, standard-sized, fast to finish, cheap to maintain.

## svc_guest_bathroom_remodel_body

<h2>Why turnover shapes this specification in Bellevue</h2>
<p>Offutt's 2024 economic impact report lists 22,876 total personnel and 8,096 dependents inside a 75-mile radius, per the <a href="https://www.offutt.af.mil/Portals/97/55th%20Wing/PA/2024%20Economic%20Impact%20Report%20Final.pdf">55th Wing report</a>, and Military OneSource puts the installation's total population at 44,914 with roughly 12,000 individuals and more than 22,000 family members served, per the <a href="https://installations.militaryonesource.mil/in-depth-overview/offutt-afb">Offutt installation overview</a>. Bellevue holds roughly 8,742 rental units out of 26,976 total, per the <a href="https://nebraskalegislature.gov/pdf/reports/committee/urban/Bellevue_2023_Affordable_Housing_Action_Plan.pdf">2023 Affordable Housing Action Plan</a>. Together that is a market with a real supply of houses changing occupants on someone else's calendar. A secondary bathroom in that context is not a design showcase. It is a room that has to look current, survive strangers, and be finished before a closing or a lease start.</p>
<h2>Choices that hold up under other people's use</h2>
<p>Porcelain floor tile with a rated slip surface, a one-piece or single-seam wall surface behind the tub, solid-surface or quartz on the vanity top, and a chrome or brushed-nickel trim line that will still be stocked in five years. Skip the finishes with maintenance built in: unsealed natural stone, oil-rubbed bronze that wears at the handle, flat paint in a wet room, and glass with a coating that needs a specific cleaner. A single-lever valve with a metal cartridge outlasts a two-handle assembly with plastic stems by a wide margin in a household where nobody reports a drip. The <a href="../services/">services list</a> separates the pieces that are worth spending on from the ones that are not.</p>
<h2>Keep the fixtures where they are</h2>
<p>Nothing raises the cost of a secondary bathroom faster than moving the toilet. Bellevue's permit division treats replacing a defective trap, drainpipe, soil, waste or vent pipe with new material as new work requiring a permit and inspections, while cleaning stoppages and repairing leaks that need no rearrangement of pipes, valves or fixtures require no permit, per the <a href="https://www.bellevue.net/departments/permits-inspections/faqs?QuestionID=31&amp;AFMID=831">City permit FAQ</a>. A like-for-like refit that reuses the existing trap locations stays on the cheap side of that line. Even so, the fixture clearances still get verified: 15 inches from a fixture centerline to any wall, partition or vanity, 30 inches between adjacent fixture centerlines, 21 inches clear in front, per the <a href="http://media.iccsafe.org/code-development/group-a/IRC-P.pdf">ICC plumbing provisions</a>. Plenty of 1960s hall baths were built with a 13-inch offset and stay that way until someone remodels.</p>
<h2>Ventilation is the item that decides how the room ages</h2>
<p>A guest bath gets used in bursts and then closed. That is the worst possible pattern for humidity. IRC M1505.4.4 requires 50 cfm intermittent or 20 cfm continuous local exhaust, and natural ventilation under R303.3 requires at least 3 square feet of glazing with half of it openable, per <a href="https://buildingcodetrainer.com/bathroom-ventilation/">this account of the ventilation code</a>. In a rental the fan also has to be something a tenant will actually run, which in practice means quiet and on a timer or a humidity sensor rather than tied to the light. Duct it to the exterior in insulated duct, because at Sarpy County's 2 degree Fahrenheit heating design temperature, per the <a href="https://www.energystar.gov/ia/partners/bldrs_lenders_raters/downloads/County%20Level%20Design%20Temperature%20Reference%20Guide%20-%202015-06-24.pdf">county design temperature guide</a>, uninsulated duct in an attic condenses and drips back at the housing.</p>
<h2>Electrical brought up to the current edition</h2>
<p>Bellevue adopted the 2023 National Electrical Code and started enforcing it on January 1, 2025, per the <a href="https://www.bellevue.net/331/Permits-Inspections">permits and inspections page</a>. For a secondary bathroom that means ground-fault protection for receptacles under 210.8(A)(1), which in the 2023 edition covers 125 through 250 volt receptacles, per <a href="https://wireref.com/gfci/bathroom/">this note on the change</a>, at least one receptacle within 36 inches of the basin edge under 210.52(D), and at least one dedicated 20-amp circuit for the bathroom receptacles under 210.11(C)(3), per the <a href="https://www.naffainc.com/x/CB2/Elect/Bathrooms.htm">bathroom electrical reference</a>. Any luminaire inside the tub or shower zone has to be listed for damp or wet locations under NEC 410.10(D). Older hall baths frequently have a single ungrounded receptacle in a light fixture, and that is the item an inspection catches.</p>
<h2>Scope that fits a short window</h2>
<p>Where the room has to be back in service quickly, the honest sequence is: keep the layout, replace the tub surround and valve, retile the floor, replace the vanity and top, replace the toilet, upgrade the fan and the receptacle, repaint. That list touches every visible surface and every failure point without opening a wall for pipe relocation. If the tub itself is sound and only the surround has failed, the <a href="../bathtub-replacement/">bathtub replacement page</a> explains when a tub is worth keeping.</p>

## svc_half_bath_remodel_lede

A powder room has two fixtures and no shower, and that single absence changes the code, the cost and the schedule. No shower means no waterproofed enclosure, no scald valve, and no 72-inch nonabsorbent wall requirement — which is why this is the most predictable job in the house.

## svc_half_bath_remodel_body

<h2>What the missing shower removes from the scope</h2>
<p>IRC R307.2's requirement for a nonabsorbent finish to 72 inches applies to shower walls and to bathtub walls where a showerhead is installed, per <a href="https://www.chippy.tools/building-codes/us/wet-areas/waterproofing-requirements/">this reading of R307.2</a>. A half bath has neither, so the wall assembly is ordinary drywall and paint or tile set as a finish rather than as part of a waterproof system. UPC 408.3's pressure balance and thermostatic valve requirement with its 120 degree handle stop applies to shower and tub-shower valves, per the <a href="https://forms.iapmo.org/email_marketing/codespotlight/2018/may17.htm">UPC 408.3 spotlight</a>, and a lavatory faucet is not one. UPC 408.7's 1,024 square inch compartment rule has nothing to act on either, per the <a href="https://eofficial.org/uniform-codes-spotlight-mar-13-2025/">Uniform Codes spotlight</a>. The result is a room where nearly the whole budget goes to visible finish rather than to concealed assembly.</p>
<h2>The clearances still bind, and they bind harder</h2>
<p>Powder rooms are the smallest rooms in the house, and IRC P2705.1 does not scale down for them: no water closet or lavatory closer than 15 inches from its center to any side wall, partition or vanity, no closer than 30 inches center to center between adjacent fixtures, and not less than 21 inches of clear space in front, per the <a href="http://media.iccsafe.org/code-development/group-a/IRC-P.pdf">ICC plumbing provisions</a>. A great many under-stair and closet-conversion powder rooms in older Bellevue housing were built to less than that. Housing in Olde Towne spans build years from 1890 through 1955, per a <a href="https://derekcolwell.realtor/bellevue-ne/guide">Bellevue neighborhood guide</a>, and the City's 2023 plan to the Legislature asked for a code inspection program covering structures 60 or more years old, per the <a href="https://nebraskalegislature.gov/pdf/reports/committee/urban/Bellevue_2023_Affordable_Housing_Action_Plan.pdf">Affordable Housing Action Plan</a>. Where the existing room cannot meet the 15-inch rule, the usual fix is a narrower or wall-hung lavatory, since the toilet's drain location is the expensive thing to move.</p>
<h2>What a two-fixture permit actually costs</h2>
<p>Because the fixture count is small, the plumbing permit math is easy to state. Bellevue charges $23.50 for permit issuance plus $9.80 per fixture or trap, adding $4.75 per fixture where drainage or vent piping is altered, per the <a href="https://www.bellevue.net/DocumentCenter/View/952/Plumbing-Fees-PDF">plumbing fee schedule</a>. Two fixtures at the base rate is $19.60 on top of the $23.50 issuance charge. A failed plumbing reinspection is $49.50, which is the largest avoidable number on the list. Under <a href="https://codelibrary.amlegal.com/codes/bellevuene/latest/bellevue_ne/0-0-0-7761">§ 27-58</a> the permit is applied for and paid for by the master plumber, and the City's own language is that when a defective trap or drainpipe has to be replaced with new material the work counts as new work for which "a permit shall be procured and inspections made as provided," per the <a href="https://www.bellevue.net/departments/permits-inspections/faqs?QuestionID=31&amp;AFMID=831">permit FAQ</a>.</p>
<h2>Ventilation is still required</h2>
<p>Losing the shower does not remove the exhaust obligation. IRC M1505.4.4 requires 50 cfm intermittent or 20 cfm continuous local exhaust, with natural ventilation permitted under R303.3 only where openable glazing of at least 3 square feet is provided and at least half of that area opens, per <a href="https://buildingcodetrainer.com/bathroom-ventilation/">this account of the ventilation code</a>. Interior powder rooms have no window at all, so a fan is the only compliant option, and it has to be ducted to the exterior. Because these rooms sit off a hallway or a dining room, fan noise is a real specification item rather than a preference — a loud unit simply does not get switched on.</p>
<h2>Electrical in a two-fixture room</h2>
<p>Bellevue adopted the 2023 National Electrical Code with enforcement beginning January 1, 2025, per the <a href="https://www.bellevue.net/331/Permits-Inspections">City's permits and inspections page</a>. NEC 210.52(D) requires at least one receptacle outlet within 36 inches of the outside edge of the basin, NEC 210.11(C)(3) requires at least one 20-amp branch circuit for bathroom receptacles with no other outlets on it though it may serve more than one bathroom, and 210.8(A)(1) requires ground-fault protection, per the <a href="https://www.naffainc.com/x/CB2/Elect/Bathrooms.htm">bathroom electrical reference</a>. Older powder rooms frequently have no receptacle at all, or one in the light fixture. Bellevue exempts reinstallation of attachment plug receptacles "but not the outlets," per the <a href="https://www.bellevue.net/departments/permits-inspections/faqs?QuestionID=31&amp;AFMID=831">permit FAQ</a>, so adding the box is permitted work.</p>
<h2>Where the money is well spent</h2>
<p>With no concealed assembly to fund, a powder room is the one bathroom where the finish budget goes further than anywhere else in the house. A wall-hung or pedestal lavatory recovers floor area and reads intentional. A full-height tile or paneled wainscot on one wall gives the room a subject. A single well-chosen light at the mirror does more than four recessed cans. Because the room is small, quantities are small, so an upgraded material costs tens of dollars rather than thousands. For the fixture itself see <a href="../toilet-installation/">toilet installation</a>; the <a href="../services/">services list</a> shows how a powder room compares to the larger scopes.</p>

## svc_heated_bathroom_floor_lede

Electric floor heat is the only bathroom upgrade that changes how the room feels every single winter morning, and it is governed by a short list of electrical requirements: a dedicated circuit, a listed system, and ground-fault protection that the code makes mandatory rather than optional.

## svc_heated_bathroom_floor_body

<h2>Why this matters in Sarpy County specifically</h2>
<p>Sarpy County's 99 percent heating design temperature is 2 degrees Fahrenheit, per the <a href="https://www.energystar.gov/ia/partners/bldrs_lenders_raters/downloads/County%20Level%20Design%20Temperature%20Reference%20Guide%20-%202015-06-24.pdf">county-level design temperature reference</a>, and Omaha recorded 16 below zero on January 14, 2024 with a January mean of 20.9 degrees, 3.5 degrees below normal, per the <a href="https://www.weather.gov/oax/2024climatesummary">National Weather Service annual climate summary</a>. A tile floor over an unheated crawl space, over a garage, or on a basement slab sits close to the temperature of what is beneath it, and forced-air heat delivered through a ceiling or high wall register does nothing for it. Floor heat addresses the surface directly, which is why it reads as a comfort upgrade out of proportion to its energy use.</p>
<h2>The electrical requirement is explicit</h2>
<p>NEC 424.99(C) requires ground-fault interrupter protection for all heating panels and heating panel sets installed under floor covering, per this <a href="https://www.ecmag.com/magazine/articles/article-detail/codes-standards-2017-nec-equipment-general-use-significant-changes-2017-nec-part-6">account of the 424 requirements</a>. Bellevue has adopted the 2023 National Electrical Code, with enforcement beginning January 1, 2025, per the <a href="https://www.bellevue.net/331/Permits-Inspections">City's permits and inspections page</a>. In practice that protection lives either in the breaker or in the thermostat, and pairing a GFCI thermostat behind a GFCI breaker causes nuisance trips, so the system is designed as one or the other. The system also gets its own circuit sized to the installed load, because the 20-amp receptacle circuit required by NEC 210.11(C)(3) may have no other outlets on it, per the <a href="https://www.naffainc.com/x/CB2/Elect/Bathrooms.htm">bathroom electrical reference</a>. A heated floor is not something to tap off the vanity receptacle.</p>
<h2>Cable, mat, and what fits the room</h2>
<p>Loose cable on the correct spacing gives full coverage of an irregular room and takes longer to install. A prefabricated mat installs faster and suits a rectangular floor with few obstructions. Either way the heated area excludes the footprint of the toilet, the vanity cabinet and any permanent fixture, so the usable heated area is smaller than the room and the load calculation follows that reduced area. Never cut a heating cable to fit. The circuit length is fixed at manufacture and cutting it destroys the system. Measure and lay out the field, including the cold-lead route to the thermostat, before any thinset is mixed.</p>
<h2>Build-up height and the doors</h2>
<p>An electric mat plus its embedment adds roughly a quarter to three eighths of an inch under the tile, and an uncoupling membrane designed to carry cable adds a little more. That change propagates: the door has to clear the new surface, the transition at the hall becomes a step if it is not detailed, and the toilet flange elevation changes relative to the finished floor. The <a href="../toilet-installation/">toilet installation page</a> covers what a recessed flange does to the seal, and it is the failure most often caused by a floor that got taller. Where the bathroom door swings inward in a tight room, half an inch of build-up can force a door change.</p>
<h2>Permits and the exemption that does not apply</h2>
<p>Bellevue's permit division exempts a short list of electrical work, including repair or replacement of a fixed approved appliance of the same type and rating in the same location, and reinstallation of attachment plug receptacles "but not the outlets," per the <a href="https://www.bellevue.net/departments/permits-inspections/faqs?QuestionID=31&amp;AFMID=831">City's permit FAQ</a>. A new circuit, a new thermostat location and a new heating system under the floor are none of those things. The same page states that unless otherwise exempted, separate plumbing, mechanical and electrical permits are required for regulated work within the city and its two-mile jurisdiction. The Permits and Inspection Division operates with six inspectors and two administrative personnel and holds a number one Building Code Effectiveness Grading rating from the Insurance Services Office awarded in 2009 to only five jurisdictions in the country, per the <a href="https://www.bellevue.net/331/Permits-Inspections">City's permits and inspections page</a>.</p>
<h2>Insulation is what makes it economical</h2>
<p>A heated floor over an uninsulated basement ceiling, an unheated garage, or a bare slab spends much of its output heating the space below. Nebraska is Climate Zone 5, where the 2018 energy code Bellevue adopted with the IRC at <a href="https://codelibrary.amlegal.com/codes/bellevuene/latest/bellevue_ne/0-0-0-2638">§ 8-17</a> prescribes R-19 or R-15 continuous at a basement wall and R-49 at the ceiling, per the <a href="https://insulationinstitute.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/N130-NE-Energy-Code-0425.pdf">state energy code summary</a>. On a slab the standard approach is a layer of rigid insulation under the heating element so the output goes up into the tile rather than down into the ground. On a wood-framed floor over unconditioned space, insulating the joist bays below does the same work. Do that first, and the thermostat runs a fraction of the hours it otherwise would. For a below-grade room where this pairing matters most, see <a href="../basement-bathroom-addition/">basement bathroom addition</a>.</p>

<h2>What the newer subdivisions actually give you</h2>
<p>The housing built across the southwest side of the city clusters between the late 1990s and the 2010s, per a <a href="https://derekcolwell.realtor/bellevue-ne/guide">Bellevue neighborhood guide</a>, and that vintage brings a predictable set of conditions: a large footprint, engineered floor framing, copper or PEX supply, a plastic drain system, and a bathroom laid out around a corner soaker tub nobody uses. <h2>Replacing the garden tub with usable shower volume</h2>
<p>The most common request in this vintage is deleting the corner tub and spending the footprint on the shower. <h2>Two basins means two sets of code obligations</h2>
<p>NEC 210.52(D) requires at least one receptacle outlet within 36 inches of the outside edge of each basin, on a wall or partition adjacent to that basin or its countertop, per the <a href="https://www.naffainc.com/x/CB2/Elect/Bathrooms.htm">bathroom electrical summary</a>. Adding a second basin therefore adds a receptacle, not just a faucet. <p>Primary bathrooms in these subdivisions frequently sit on a corner with two exterior walls and a large window. <h2>Sequence and living arrangements</h2>
<p>A full primary bath rebuild takes the room out of service for weeks, not days, and in a house where it is the only shower that changes the plan. Where the scope extends past the bathroom itself, the <a href="../bathroom-remodel/">whole bathroom remodel page</a> is the better place to start.</p>

Glass is the last item installed and the first one to expose every error underneath it. A panel is cut to the opening that exists, not the opening on the plan, so the walls, the curb and the plumb of the jamb all get measured after the tile is set.

## svc_shower_door_and_glass_body

<h2>The code calls this a hazardous location</h2>
<p>IRC R308.4.5 treats glazing in walls, enclosures or fences containing or facing bathtubs, showers, saunas, hot tubs and whirlpools as a hazardous location where the bottom exposed edge of the glazing is less than 60 inches measured vertically above any standing or walking surface, with an exception for glazing more than 60 inches measured horizontally from the water's edge or the edge of a shower, per this <a href="https://www.co.routt.co.us/DocumentCenter/View/15198">reproduction of R308.4.5</a>. Bellevue enforces the 2021 IRC, Chapters 2 through 24 plus Chapter 44, adopted at <a href="https://codelibrary.amlegal.com/codes/bellevuene/latest/bellevue_ne/0-0-0-2638">§ 8-17</a>. In plain terms, an enclosure panel or a door within reach of a shower has to be safety glazing, which means fully tempered in practically every shower application. Tempered glass fails into small blunt fragments instead of shards, which is the whole point of the requirement.</p>
<h2>Frameless is a structural choice, not a style choice</h2>
<p>A frameless enclosure carries its own weight and the door's swing through a small number of clamps and hinges anchored into the wall. Those anchors need blocking behind the tile, positioned before the backer goes up. Retrofitting them into a finished tile wall means either hitting a stud by luck or accepting a toggle in half-inch backer, which is how a hinge works loose and a door drops out of square. Semi-frameless and framed enclosures spread the load along a jamb channel and tolerate far more wall variation. That tolerance is the real reason framed units still dominate in older housing: the walls are not straight enough for anything else without corrective work.</p>
<h2>Out-of-plumb walls are the Bellevue variable</h2>
<p>The City's 2023 plan filed with the Legislature counted 26,976 housing units, proposed a systematic code inspection program for structures 60 or more years old, and put the deteriorated pockets near the downtown core and the oldest neighborhoods to the north and south, per the <a href="https://nebraskalegislature.gov/pdf/reports/committee/urban/Bellevue_2023_Affordable_Housing_Action_Plan.pdf">Affordable Housing Action Plan</a>. Housing in Olde Towne spans build years from 1890 through 1955, per a <a href="https://derekcolwell.realtor/bellevue-ne/guide">Bellevue neighborhood guide</a>. Framing that old has settled, and a shower opening out of plumb by three eighths of an inch top to bottom is ordinary rather than remarkable. Framed enclosures absorb that in the channel. A frameless panel does not, so either the wall gets corrected during the tile work or the glass gets cut on a taper — a real fabrication option that has to be decided at the measure, not at the install.</p>
<h2>The curb, the slope, and where the water goes</h2>
<p>Glass does not make a shower watertight; the pan and the slope do. A curb has to slope inward so water running down the inside face of the glass returns to the pan rather than sitting on the curb top and wicking into the joint. On a curbless installation there is no curb to slope, so the door swings over a level surface and the entire containment depends on the floor slope and the membrane. That is where the waterproofing height rule changes: IRC R307.2 requires a nonabsorbent finish to 72 inches, and in a shower with no curb that height is measured from the drain inlet rather than the floor, per <a href="https://www.chippy.tools/building-codes/us/wet-areas/waterproofing-requirements/">this reading of R307.2</a>. See <a href="../bathroom-waterproofing/">bathroom waterproofing</a> for the membrane detail that sits under the glass.</p>
<h2>Clear volume the glass has to respect</h2>
<p>Bellevue enforces the 2021 Uniform Plumbing Code under <a href="https://codelibrary.amlegal.com/codes/bellevuene/latest/bellevue_ne/0-0-0-7799">§ 27-85</a>. UPC 408.7 requires the shower compartment's finished interior to be not less than 1,024 square inches and capable of encompassing a 30-inch circle measured at the top of the threshold, maintained to a point not less than 70 inches above the shower drain outlet, with valves, showerheads, soap dishes, shelves and grab bars as the only permitted intrusions, per the <a href="https://eofficial.org/uniform-codes-spotlight-mar-13-2025/">Uniform Codes spotlight on 408.7</a>. A header, a transom bar or a return panel set inside the compartment line can compromise that clear volume, and a door that opens inward into a 32-inch base can too. Confirm the swing direction and the clear opening against the fixture layout before ordering.</p>
<h2>Hardware, coatings, and what actually stays clean</h2>
<p>Thicker glass at three eighths or half an inch is stiffer and moves less at the hinge, which keeps the seal strip aligned. A factory-applied hydrophobic coating slows mineral buildup but does not eliminate squeegee duty, and it can be voided by abrasive cleaners. Choose hinge and handle finishes that are solid brass or stainless rather than plated zinc, because the hardware sits in the wettest air in the house. Fixed panels with a stabilizing bar are less prone to loosening than a wide unsupported door. Where the room is tight enough that a swinging door does not work, a bypass slider or a single fixed panel with an open entry is the alternative, and the <a href="../small-bathroom-remodel/">small bathroom page</a> works through the geometry that drives that decision.</p>

A small bathroom is a dimensional problem before it is a design problem. Five feet by eight feet is the standard hall bath in most of Bellevue's older housing, and every fixture in it is governed by a clearance the plumbing code fixes in inches.

## svc_small_bathroom_remodel_body

<h2>The arithmetic that decides what fits</h2>
<p>Start with three numbers from IRC P2705.1: no toilet, lavatory or bidet closer than 15 inches from its center to any side wall, partition or vanity; no less than 30 inches center to center between adjacent fixtures; and no less than 21 inches of clear floor space in front, per the <a href="http://media.iccsafe.org/code-development/group-a/IRC-P.pdf">ICC plumbing provisions</a>. In a 60-inch wall those numbers eat the room quickly. Then add UPC 408.7, which Bellevue enforces under the 2021 Uniform Plumbing Code adopted at <a href="https://codelibrary.amlegal.com/codes/bellevuene/latest/bellevue_ne/0-0-0-7799">§ 27-85</a>: a shower compartment needs a finished interior of at least 1,024 square inches able to encompass a 30-inch circle, measured at the threshold and held to 70 inches above the drain outlet, though a receptor of 30 by 60 inches is separately accepted, per the <a href="https://eofficial.org/uniform-codes-spotlight-mar-13-2025/">Uniform Codes spotlight</a>. A 32-inch square base is 1,024 square inches exactly. That is why so many small-bath plans stall at the same fixture.</p>
<h2>What actually buys space</h2>
<p>Moving a wall is rarely the cheapest way to gain room. Four changes usually do more. Swapping a swing door for a pocket or barn slider recovers the entire door arc, which in a 5 by 8 room is most of the usable floor. Replacing a 22-inch-deep vanity with an 18-inch model gives back four inches of walking width along the longest dimension. Trading a tub for a 32 by 60 shower with a low curb removes the visual and physical bulk of the apron. Recessing the medicine storage into the stud bay rather than hanging a cabinet takes three inches off the wall. None of those touch structure or plumbing rough-in locations, which keeps them out of the expensive half of the estimate.</p>
<h2>Why the older half of the city has so many of these rooms</h2>

It also means any plan that relocates the toilet is a stack conversation, not a fixture order.</p>
<h2>The single wet wall is the hinge of the budget</h2>
<p>Keeping the toilet, lavatory and tub or shower on the wall they already occupy holds the plumbing scope to fixtures, valves and stub-outs. <p>Small bathrooms are the ones that grow mold, because the moisture load per cubic foot is higher and the window is usually a 2 by 2 unit over the tub. ## svc_toilet_installation_lede

A toilet either sits level on a solid floor with the flange at the right height, or it rocks, leaks slowly, and rots the subfloor around it. Nearly every failed installation traces to flange elevation, and the flange is the cheapest part of the job.

## svc_toilet_installation_body

<h2>Flange height governs the seal</h2>
<p>The closet flange should finish flush with or slightly above the finished floor, so the wax or foam seal compresses between a fixed horn and a fixed surface. When a new floor is added over an old one without resetting or extending the flange, the flange ends up recessed and the seal has to bridge a gap it was never designed to bridge. It leaks slowly, from below, invisibly, and the first symptom is a soft spot in the subfloor or a stain on a basement ceiling. Extension rings solve it properly when they are stacked with a compatible gasket and fastened into sound material. Two wax rings piled up is not a solution; it is a deferred failure. Any change of floor build-up therefore belongs in the same conversation as the toilet, which is why the <a href="../bathroom-flooring/">bathroom flooring page</a> covers the height arithmetic alongside the finish.</p>
<h2>The subfloor under the flange is usually the real job</h2>
<p>Pull an old toilet in a house of a certain age and the sheathing around the flange is often soft. Bellevue has a large stock of that age: the City's 2023 plan to the Legislature counted 26,976 housing units, sought a systematic code inspection program for structures 60 or more years old, and located deteriorated pockets near the downtown core and in the oldest neighborhoods north and south of it, per the <a href="https://nebraskalegislature.gov/pdf/reports/committee/urban/Bellevue_2023_Affordable_Housing_Action_Plan.pdf">Affordable Housing Action Plan</a>. The repair is to cut back to sound sheathing, block or sister the framing as needed, patch to the same thickness, then set the flange to the new finished height. A shim under the base with caulk around it hides the symptom and leaves the cause under the fixture.</p>
<h2>Rough-in dimensions and clearances</h2>
<p>The rough-in is measured from the finished wall to the center of the closet bend, and standard bowls are made for 12 inches with 10-inch and 14-inch versions available. Get it wrong and either the tank will not clear the wall or a gap opens behind it. Clearances then come from IRC P2705.1: no water closet set closer than 15 inches from its center to any side wall, partition or vanity, no closer than 30 inches center to center between adjacent fixtures, and not less than 21 inches of clear space in front, per the <a href="http://media.iccsafe.org/code-development/group-a/IRC-P.pdf">ICC plumbing provisions</a>. Those numbers are what rule out a wider vanity beside an existing toilet, and they get checked with a tape before anything is ordered.</p>
<h2>When a swap becomes permitted work</h2>
<p>Bellevue's permit division is specific that no permit is required for clearing stoppages or repairing leaks in pipes, valves or fixtures where the repair does not involve replacing or rearranging valves, pipes or fixtures — but that should any trap, drainpipe, soil, waste or vent pipe become defective and need removal and replacement with new material, the work is considered new work and "a permit shall be procured and inspections made as provided," per the <a href="https://www.bellevue.net/departments/permits-inspections/faqs?QuestionID=31&amp;AFMID=831">City's permit FAQ</a>. Replacing a cracked closet bend or a broken flange collar on the pipe crosses that line. Under <a href="https://codelibrary.amlegal.com/codes/bellevuene/latest/bellevue_ne/0-0-0-7761">§ 27-58</a> the plumbing permit is applied for, signed and paid for by the master plumber, and <a href="https://nebraskalegislature.gov/laws/statutes.php?statute=18-1917">Nebraska statute 18-1917</a> requires that license to come from the city or village nearest the site. The fee is $23.50 for issuance plus $9.80 per fixture or trap, per the <a href="https://www.bellevue.net/DocumentCenter/View/952/Plumbing-Fees-PDF">plumbing fee schedule</a>.</p>
<h2>What is under the flange in older housing</h2>
<p>Cast iron closet bends and stacks are common in the city's older neighborhoods. Extra heavy cast iron and ductile iron waste lines carry an expected service life of 75 to 100 years, per <a href="https://www.corrview.com/technical-bulletin-pd-02">CorrView's piping bulletin</a>, which puts pre-war installations at the far end of that range. Cast iron fails from the inside and from the bottom of the pipe, so a hub can look sound while the barrel below is thin. If the flange is leaded into a cast iron hub, replacing it is a cutting and joining operation rather than four screws, and it changes both the schedule and the permit picture. Knowing which of those two jobs is in front of you before demolition starts is the difference between a morning and a day.</p>
<h2>Fixture selection that is actually about maintenance</h2>
<p>A skirted or one-piece bowl has no crevice at the trapway and cleans in one pass. A dual-flush or a modern single-flush at 1.28 gallons clears a Bellevue drain fine as long as the drain has correct fall — low-flow fixtures get blamed for slope problems they did not create. Choose a model whose flush valve and fill valve are commodity parts, because those two components are the ones that fail. A slow-close seat with removable hinges is a small cost that makes the fixture cleanable. For a two-fixture room where the toilet is half the scope, see <a href="../half-bath-remodel/">half bath remodel</a>.</p>

## svc_tub_to_shower_conversion_lede

A conversion is not a swap. Taking a tub out and leaving a shower in its place moves the drain, changes the trap arm, and rebuilds the waterproof assembly from the subfloor up, which is why it prices and schedules like a small rebuild rather than like a fixture change.

## svc_tub_to_shower_conversion_body

<h2>The line between this page and bathtub replacement</h2>
<p>Read the <a href="../bathtub-replacement/">bathtub replacement page</a> beside this one, because the two jobs diverge at the drain. A replacement puts a tub back in a tub's footprint: the waste and overflow lands in the same spot at one end of the alcove, the trap below stays where it is, and the wall assembly gets rebuilt only to the height the new tub flange demands. A conversion deletes the tub entirely. The drain has to come to the middle of the new floor, or wherever the base you selected puts it, so the trap and trap arm get cut out and re-run. The alcove floor becomes a graded, waterproofed surface instead of a shelf holding a self-contained vessel. Two jobs, one alcove, and almost nothing shared behind the tile. Everything else on this site follows from that split, so the <a href="../services/">services list</a> sorts the two apart rather than filing them together.</p>
<h2>Moving the drain is the whole job</h2>
<p>Under a Bellevue tub the waste tees into a trap near the end wall, usually inside a joist bay if the bathroom sits over a basement. A shower base wants its outlet near the center of the pan, and manufactured bases publish an outlet location you either match or reject the base. That means opening the floor, cutting the existing trap out, setting a new trap at the depth the base thickness requires, and re-running the trap arm to the stack at the fall the code demands. In older parts of the city that stack may be cast iron, and extra heavy cast iron waste lines are generally credited with a service life of 75 to 100 years, per <a href="https://www.corrview.com/technical-bulletin-pd-02">CorrView's piping bulletin</a> — old enough that a hub connection can be sound and the fitting beside it soft.</p>
<h2>The City treats it as new work, and the master plumber files it</h2>
<p>Bellevue's Permits and Inspection Division is explicit that clearing a stoppage or fixing a leak needs no permit, but once a trap, drainpipe, soil, waste or vent pipe has to be removed and replaced with new material, that becomes new work for which "a permit shall be procured and inspections made as provided," per the <a href="https://www.bellevue.net/departments/permits-inspections/faqs?QuestionID=31&amp;AFMID=831">City's permit division</a>. A conversion cuts out a trap by definition, so there is no version of this job that sits inside the exemption. The filing is not yours to do either. Under <a href="https://codelibrary.amlegal.com/codes/bellevuene/latest/bellevue_ne/0-0-0-7761">Bellevue city code § 27-58</a>, the plumbing permit is applied for by the master plumber, and once approved it is signed and paid for by that license holder. The City's fee schedule charges $9.80 per fixture or trap plus a $23.50 issuance charge, and adds $4.75 per fixture for altering drainage or vent piping — which is exactly the line a conversion triggers and a like-for-like tub swap does not, per the <a href="https://www.bellevue.net/DocumentCenter/View/952/Plumbing-Fees-PDF">plumbing fee table</a>.</p>
<h2>Size the compartment before anything is ordered</h2>
<p>Bellevue enforces the 2021 Uniform Plumbing Code, adopted at <a href="https://codelibrary.amlegal.com/codes/bellevuene/latest/bellevue_ne/0-0-0-7799">§ 27-85 of the city code</a>. UPC 408.7 requires a shower compartment with a finished interior of not less than 1,024 square inches that is also capable of encompassing a 30-inch circle, measured at the top of the threshold, and that clear volume has to be maintained to a point not less than 70 inches above the shower drain outlet, per the <a href="https://eofficial.org/uniform-codes-spotlight-mar-13-2025/">Uniform Codes spotlight on 408.7</a>. Valves, showerheads, soap dishes, shelves and grab bars are the only permitted intrusions. A 30 by 60 receptor is called out separately as acceptable, which matters because a standard alcove tub is 30 by 60. A sloped ceiling, a soffit, or a bulkhead hiding old pipe can eat the 70-inch dimension even when the floor plan measures fine.</p>
<h2>Waterproofing gets built new, not patched</h2>
<p>A tub carries its own watertight floor. Delete the tub and the room loses it, so the pan, the curb or the ramp, the corners and the drain flange all become site-built assemblies. IRC R307.2 requires a nonabsorbent finish on shower walls to 72 inches above the floor, and in a curbless shower that measurement starts at the drain inlet, per <a href="https://www.chippy.tools/building-codes/us/wet-areas/waterproofing-requirements/">this reading of R307.2</a>. Old greenboard salvaged off the studs is not a shortcut here; water-resistant gypsum board is barred where there is direct exposure to water, per <a href="https://up.codes/viewer/new_jersey/irc-2021/chapter/7/wall-covering">the R702 wall covering text</a>.</p>
<h2>What Bellevue's older stock adds to the scope</h2>
<p>The 2023 affordable housing plan Bellevue filed with the Legislature proposes a systematic code inspection program for every housing structure 60 or more years old, and locates the city's deteriorated pockets near the downtown core and in the oldest neighborhoods north and south of it, per the <a href="https://nebraskalegislature.gov/pdf/reports/committee/urban/Bellevue_2023_Affordable_Housing_Action_Plan.pdf">Bellevue Affordable Housing Action Plan</a>. In Olde Towne that reads as a single vent stack, a tub against an exterior wall, and no room to shift the drain sideways without touching a joist. In the late-1990s and newer subdivisions the alcove is wider and the supply lines are copper or PEX, so the conversion is mostly a floor and waterproofing exercise. If the goal is a level entry rather than simply losing the tub, the <a href="../walk-in-shower-installation/">walk-in shower page</a> covers that.</p>

## svc_vanity_and_countertop_replacement_lede

A vanity swap looks like carpentry and behaves like three trades. The cabinet has to clear the fixture clearances the plumbing code sets, the top has to meet the wall it is actually against, and the receptacle beside it has to satisfy rules the 2023 electrical code tightened.

## svc_vanity_and_countertop_replacement_body

<h2>The clearance numbers decide the cabinet width</h2>
<p>Before a vanity is priced, measure against IRC P2705.1: a water closet, lavatory or bidet may not be set closer than 15 inches from its center to any side wall, partition or vanity, nor closer than 30 inches center to center between adjacent fixtures, and there has to be at least 21 inches of clear space in front of it, per the <a href="http://media.iccsafe.org/code-development/group-a/IRC-P.pdf">ICC plumbing provisions</a>. Those three numbers are what stop a 48-inch vanity from going into a room that looks like it fits one. The 15 inches runs from the toilet centerline to the cabinet side, so a wider vanity does not just crowd the toilet, it puts the room out of compliance. In a small bath the honest answer is often a narrower cabinet with a deeper drawer bank, and the <a href="../small-bathroom-remodel/">small bathroom page</a> works through the rest of that arithmetic. The <a href="../services/">services list</a> sorts the cabinet work from the fixture work it depends on.</p>
<h2>The wall you are mounting to is rarely flat</h2>
<p>Stock vanity tops arrive straight. Bellevue walls in the older half of the city do not. A top scribed to a wall that bows a quarter inch across 60 inches either gets caulked into a gap that collects water or gets cut to follow the wall. Undermount sinks make this worse because the cutout is fixed before the top reaches the room. On a plaster wall the back edge is usually the problem; on a wall that has been skim-coated over old tile the problem is thickness that changes at the height where the backsplash lands. The fix is measuring the wall in three places at counter height before ordering, and building a shim plane behind the cabinet rather than fighting it at the top.</p>
<h2>Electrical: the 2023 code changed the answer</h2>
<p>Bellevue adopted the 2023 National Electrical Code and began enforcing it on January 1, 2025, per the <a href="https://www.bellevue.net/331/Permits-Inspections">City's permits and inspections page</a>. NEC 210.52(D) requires at least one receptacle outlet within 36 inches of the outside edge of each basin, on a wall or partition adjacent to the basin or its countertop, and NEC 210.11(C)(3) requires at least one 20-ampere branch circuit serving bathroom receptacles with no other outlets on it, though it may serve more than one bathroom, per this <a href="https://www.naffainc.com/x/CB2/Elect/Bathrooms.htm">summary of the bathroom electrical rules</a>. NEC 210.8(A)(1) requires ground-fault protection for bathroom receptacles, and in the 2023 edition that scope widened to cover receptacles from 125 volts through 250 volts rather than 125 volts alone, per <a href="https://wireref.com/gfci/bathroom/">this reference on 210.8(A)(1)</a>. A double vanity that adds a second basin therefore adds a receptacle requirement, not just a second faucet.</p>
<h2>Where the permit exemption stops</h2>
<p>Bellevue's permit division lists a set of electrical work that needs no permit, and two items on it get misread constantly: repair or replacement of a fixed approved appliance "of the same type and rating in the same location," and "reinstallation of attachment plug receptacles, but not the outlets," per the <a href="https://www.bellevue.net/departments/permits-inspections/faqs?QuestionID=31&amp;AFMID=831">City's permit exemption list</a>. Putting the same receptacle back in the same box is exempt. Adding a box for a second basin is not. On the plumbing side the same page states that replacing a defective trap or drainpipe with new material is new work requiring a permit and inspections, which is what a vanity swap turns into the moment the old P-trap or the old supply stubs are past saving. Under <a href="https://nebraskalegislature.gov/laws/statutes.php?statute=18-1917">Nebraska Revised Statute 18-1917</a> that pipe work has to be done by a master plumber licensed by the nearest city or village, with journeymen licensed by that same city doing the installation.</p>
<h2>Supply lines in the older stock</h2>
<p>Galvanized steel supply piping was common in houses built up to roughly 1950 and typically lasts 40 to 60 years, per <a href="https://inspectapedia.com/plumbing/Galvanized_Iron_Pipes.php">InspectApedia's account of galvanized pipe</a>, which puts every surviving run in Bellevue's oldest neighborhoods past its expected life. Galvanized announces itself when the old angle stops are opened: the flow drops because corrosion has narrowed the bore, and the threads at the stub-out are frequently the weakest point in the run. A vanity replacement is the cheapest moment in the life of that room to change the stub-outs and valves, because the wall behind the cabinet is open and the finish work is already being done. Skipping it means opening a finished cabinet back later.</p>
<h2>Tops, edges, and what the room can carry</h2>
<p>Quartz and granite tops arrive heavy enough that the cabinet has to be level and fully bearing before they land, and a 3-centimeter slab on a cabinet shimmed at two points will telegraph a crack at the sink cutout. Sink style follows maintenance rather than fashion: an integrated top has no seam to fail, an undermount has a silicone joint that eventually needs redoing, and a vessel sink raises the effective counter height, which matters in a household where the bathroom is shared across a wide age range. If the countertop change is part of a larger reconfiguration rather than a like-for-like swap, the <a href="../bathroom-remodel/">full bathroom remodel page</a> is the better starting point.</p>
