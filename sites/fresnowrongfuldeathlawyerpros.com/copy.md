## qa_1_question

Who can bring a wrongful death claim in California?

## qa_1_answer

California Code of Civil Procedure section 377.60 names the usual starting group: a surviving spouse, registered domestic partner, children, and the issue of deceased children. When there is no surviving issue, people who would inherit under intestate succession can be included. The statute also has rules for certain dependents, including a putative spouse, stepchildren, parents, and some household minors. The facts matter. A relationship that feels obvious in the family may require legal proof, especially with a blended family, a separated spouse, an estate, or a claimed dependency. The safest approach is to list every close relative and the estate status at the outset rather than deciding informally who is “the” claimant.

## qa_2_question

What is the difference between wrongful death and a survival action?

## qa_2_answer

They are related but distinct. A wrongful death claim seeks the losses eligible survivors suffer because their family member died. A survival action carries forward a claim the person who died could have brought if they had lived; it is pursued by the personal representative or, if there is none, a successor in interest. California’s general rule limits the damages in a survival action differently from the heirs’ losses and excludes the decedent’s pain, suffering, and disfigurement, subject to statutory exceptions that can turn on dates and procedural facts. That is why it is important not to use the two terms interchangeably or let an insurer treat them as the same claim.

## svc_fatal_car_accident_claim_lede

A fatal crash on a Central Valley state route or a two-lane county road leaves a paper trail split across agencies and a physical record that thins within days. This page covers what to secure and the two claims California recognizes.

## svc_fatal_car_accident_claim_body

### The report is where the file starts, not where it ends

The number written on a collision report is a way into the record, not the record itself. Behind it sit scene photographs, radio and dispatch logs, emergency call audio, roadway measurements, and the officer's field notes. Around it sit things nobody wrote down: camera footage on a nearby building that overwrites itself, event data stored in a modern vehicle, phone records, and a commercial driver's logs and dispatch instructions. A tow yard can release a vehicle for salvage while a family is still arranging a funeral, and the crushed structure of that vehicle is often the clearest surviving proof of speed and impact angle.

### What the state's own counts say about these roads

Caltrans reported 130 fatal crashes on state highways in its District 6 during 2024, with 142 people killed, and 60 of those fatal crashes happened on rural two- and three-lane highways rather than freeways. Districtwide, non-freeway roads accounted for 75 fatal crashes to the freeway network's 55. Inside city limits the pattern shifts: the city's collision analysis counted 217 fatal-injury crashes between 2019 and 2023, and found 82 percent of the city's killed-or-severe-injury crashes occurred within 250 feet of an intersection, against 65 percent countywide. Across Fresno County over those same five years, 38 percent of the worst crashes involved a vehicle leaving its lane.

### Which parties the facts can add

A collision between two cars near Sunnyside is one investigation. A collision involving a produce trailer, a labor transport van, a delivery route vehicle, or a truck registered to a packing operation is several. Ownership, hiring, maintenance, and the reason the driver was on that road at that hour all become discoverable. Roadway design enters when the geometry itself is at issue. On State Route 41 between Excelsior and Elkhorn avenues, the state added signs, then converted the centerline to a no-passing double yellow in January 2021, and planned a median barrier to physically separate opposing traffic. Where a public entity is a potential defendant, a different and much shorter claim track applies.

### The first week, in a sensible order

Nothing useful requires a decision in the first hours. What helps is narrow: collect certified copies of the death certificate, keep every letter and claim number together, photograph what still exists, and ask the tow yard in writing to hold the vehicle. Identify who in the family may need to act, and get the estate question answered before anyone signs an authorization. A recorded statement given to an adjuster in week one can lock in a version of events assembled before the reports arrive.

### Two claims that travel together

The heirs' claim under Code of Civil Procedure section 377.60 and the decedent's own surviving claim under section 377.30 are separate causes of action with separate owners. Section 377.62 allows them to be brought together when they arise from the same wrongful act. Where no estate is being administered, section 377.32 lets a successor in interest proceed by declaration, which must state that no California administration proceeding is pending and that no other person has a superior right, with a certified death certificate attached.

### The deadlines that decide whether any of it matters

The general limit for a death caused by another's wrongful act or neglect is two years under section 335.1. Against a public entity the real deadline arrives far earlier: Government Code section 911.2 requires a claim for death to be presented no later than six months after the cause of action accrues. If that claim is rejected, the state courts' self-help guidance explains that a lawsuit must follow within six months of the date the rejection was mailed, while an agency's silence past 45 days generally leaves the ordinary two-year window.

### What the other side is doing meanwhile

An insurer with a fatality file moves quickly on two things: a statement, and a release. Both are cheap early and expensive later. Where several heirs exist, an adjuster may also try to settle with whichever one answers the phone. California hands that allocation question to the court instead, since section 377.61 directs the court to determine the respective rights of the people entitled to assert the claim in any award. Fees sit under the same disclosure rules described on the fees page, including the written contract requirements of Business and Professions Code section 6147.

## svc_fatal_work_accident_claim_lede

A death at work opens a compensation claim, a state safety investigation, and sometimes a lawsuit against a company that was never the employer. The three run on separate tracks. This page explains how they differ and what to preserve while they are still open.

## svc_fatal_work_accident_claim_body

### The first question is who was not the employer

Labor Code section 3600 makes workers' compensation the exclusive remedy against an employer for a work injury or death, without regard to fault, subject to narrow statutory exceptions. That is where most families stop reading, and it is the wrong place to stop. Labor Code section 3852 says a compensation claim does not affect the right of action for damages against any person other than the employer. On a Valley worksite that other person is often a real one: a labor contractor, a grower who controlled the property, a trucking company, a machinery manufacturer, a chemical supplier, or a maintenance contractor. The same statute also lets the employer or its carrier pursue the third party, and it leaves the respective rights of the heirs and the employer to be sorted out by the court.

### What the fatality data looks like in this economy

Federal counts put California's fatal work injuries at 419 in 2024. Agriculture, forestry, fishing and hunting accounted for 31 of them, and transportation incidents caused 16 of those 31, with fruit and tree nut farming alone at 10. Truck transportation recorded 43 deaths statewide that year. None of that is abstract in a county whose own economic study attributed 63,103 direct jobs to agricultural production and processing in 2023. A fatal event here frequently sits at the seam between farming and hauling, which is exactly where the employer of record and the company that actually controlled the work turn out to be different entities.

### The eight-hour report, and what it preserves

Title 8 of the California Code of Regulations, section 342, requires an employer to report a work-connected death to the Division of Occupational Safety and Health immediately. The regulation defines that word as no longer than eight hours after the employer knew or with diligent inquiry would have known. Exigent circumstances stretch that to no more than 24 hours. The report has to describe the accident and state whether the scene or the instrumentality has been altered. Ask early whether that report was made, because its answer to the alteration question can matter more than anything said later about equipment that has since been repaired.

### Heat, hours, and the standard that carries a name

Section 3395 of the same title is titled the Maria Isabel Vasquez Jimenez heat illness standard, and it applies to all outdoor places of employment. Agriculture, construction, landscaping, oil and gas extraction, and the transportation of agricultural products or heavy materials are the industries it singles out for high-heat procedures. The standard also states that acclimatization peaks in most people within four to fourteen days of regular work in the heat for at least two hours a day. That is why a new hire, a returning seasonal worker, and the first hot week of summer keep appearing together in these files.

### A citation is evidence of a problem, not proof of a case

Cal/OSHA's enforcement branch investigates reports of accidents causing death, and it can issue citations, special orders, and orders prohibiting use of equipment. That process answers a regulatory question about the employer, not a civil question about who owes a family money. An employer has 15 working days from receiving a citation to notify the Occupational Safety and Health Appeals Board of an appeal, and the citation becomes a final order if nobody does. A cited violation may line up with negligence and may not, and an uncited employer is not thereby cleared.

### What compensation pays, and what it leaves out

The state's own benefit tables set death benefits for injuries on or after January 1, 2013 at $250,000 for one total dependent, $290,000 for two, and $320,000 for three or more, with burial expenses up to $10,000. Labor Code section 4702 pays that in installments at the temporary disability rate but not less than $224 per week, and directs $250,000 to the estate where there are no dependents. Nothing in that system pays for the loss of a parent's guidance or a spouse's companionship. Those are damages only the wrongful death claim reaches.

### What to hold on to now

Keep pay records, schedules, safety meeting sheets, training documents, text messages about the task, badge or gate logs, and the carrier's letters. Write down the names of everyone present and what equipment was in use. Deadlines diverge here, and the death claim against a third party runs on the two-year limit in Code of Civil Procedure section 335.1 rather than on any compensation timeline.

## svc_medical_negligence_death_claim_lede

When a death follows care, the question is rarely whether the chart is complete. It is whether the timeline shows a choice that fell below the standard. This page covers the records, the causation problem, and the fee statute that applies only to these claims.

## svc_medical_negligence_death_claim_body

### How a bad outcome is told apart from a claim

Medicine produces deaths that nobody could have prevented, deaths from a known risk that was properly disclosed, and deaths that trace to a decision or an omission. Only the third supports a claim, and no family can sort those three from memory. What separates them is a sequenced timeline: when symptoms started, what was reported and to whom, and which tests were ordered. It also records who was on shift, what the vital signs did overnight, and how long passed between a change in condition and a response to it. Building that timeline is ordinary work, and it usually reveals the gaps before any expert opinion is sought.

### Ask for the chart, not the summary

An after-visit summary is a courtesy document. The record that matters includes physician and nursing notes, medication administration entries, telemetry and monitor data, imaging with the radiologist's readings, laboratory results with their timestamps, consultation requests, transfer paperwork, and the audit trail showing who opened the chart and when. Request from every facility involved, including the ambulance service, an urgent care visit that preceded the hospital, a specialist's office, a skilled nursing facility, and any imaging provider billed separately. Records from before the final admission often carry the decisive entry, because a delayed diagnosis leaves its evidence in earlier visits rather than in the last one.

### The estate's claim, and the deadline that just changed

Two claims can arise from the same death. The heirs hold the wrongful death claim under Code of Civil Procedure section 377.60. The estate holds whatever claim the person could have brought while alive, which passes to a personal representative or a successor in interest under section 377.30. Section 377.34 normally limits the estate's damages to losses sustained before death and expressly excludes pain, suffering, and disfigurement. A temporary exception allowed those predeath damages in actions filed on or after January 1, 2022 and before January 1, 2026. The statute ties that window to the filing date rather than the date of death, and a legislative proposal to remove the sunset did not pass in 2025. Anyone weighing an estate claim should have the current text checked against their own dates before assuming either result.

### The fee statute that only applies here

These claims are the one corner of California injury law where a percentage is actually fixed by statute. Business and Professions Code section 6146(a) covers damages sought against a health care provider based on that provider's alleged professional negligence. It limits the fee to 25 percent of the amount recovered when the matter resolves by a settlement and release signed before a complaint or arbitration demand is filed, then 33 percent once a case has been filed. Every other death claim in Fresno County falls outside that section, which is why the fees page explains what the contract has to disclose instead of quoting a number. Limitations rules for professional negligence also differ from the general two-year death rule, and the applicable one has to be identified from the dates.

### What a defense does with the same records

Hospital and carrier defense teams read the chart first and read it well. They will locate every entry that suggests the outcome was a recognized complication, every note about a missed appointment or an unfilled prescription, and every place the family's later account and the contemporaneous record diverge. They will retain a specialist in the same field who will testify that the care met the standard. None of that is a reason to avoid the review. It is the reason the timeline should be built from documents rather than recollection, and the reason nothing should be signed while the chart is still outstanding.

### What happens after contact is made

A first review is an organizing exercise. Facilities get identified, authorizations get drafted so that the requests actually capture the full record, and the estate question gets answered, since a survival claim needs someone with authority to bring it. Where no will exists, the courts' guidance describes the priority order for appointing a personal representative, beginning with a surviving spouse or domestic partner, then a child, a grandchild, a parent, and a sibling, with the judge deciding among them. In Fresno County that petition is filed with the probate clerk at the B. F. Sisk Courthouse, and a probate examiner reviews the papers for compliance and posts notes on any deficiency before the hearing. Those notes have to be cured by declaration, amendment, or supplement, and email to the examiner does not do it.

## svc_wrongful_death_damages_claim_lede

California draws a hard line through the losses a family feels. Grief itself is outside the claim, while the loss of a person's companionship, guidance, and support is inside it. This page covers who may sue, what counts, and who approves a child's share.

## svc_wrongful_death_damages_claim_body

### Three classes of people, not one list

Code of Civil Procedure section 377.60 divides eligible claimants into groups that behave differently. Subdivision (a) covers the surviving spouse, domestic partner, children, and the issue of deceased children, and where no issue survives it reaches the people who would take by intestate succession. Subdivision (b) adds a putative spouse, that spouse's children, stepchildren, and parents, but only if they were dependent on the person who died. A putative spouse is defined there as the survivor of a void or voidable marriage who the court finds believed in good faith that the marriage was valid. Subdivision (c) reaches a minor who lived in the household for the 180 days before the death and depended on the decedent for one half or more of their support.

### One action, and what happens when a relative will not join

California treats this as a single indivisible claim rather than a set of parallel lawsuits. Known heirs are necessary parties who belong in the same case, which is why the identity of every eligible relative has to be settled before anything is filed. A relative who does not want to participate does not get to stall the rest, and the usual mechanism is to name that person as a defendant so the court has everyone before it. Section 377.61 then directs the court to determine the respective rights of the people entitled to assert the cause of action in any award, so a share is not decided by whoever spoke to an adjuster first.

### What the jury is told it may count

The pattern instruction on death damages, CACI 3921, sets out the economic side in four parts. They are the financial support the decedent would have contributed during the shorter of the two life expectancies, the loss of gifts or benefits that could have been expected, funeral and burial expenses, and the reasonable value of household services. The noneconomic side is the loss of the decedent's love, companionship, comfort, care, assistance, protection, affection, society, and moral support, along with the loss of enjoyment of sexual relations and the loss of training and guidance. The instruction states that no fixed standard exists for deciding that amount.

### And what it is told to leave out

The same instruction forbids three things. Jurors may not consider the plaintiff's grief, sorrow, or mental anguish. They may not consider the pain and suffering of the person who died. They may not consider the poverty or the wealth of the plaintiff. That first exclusion surprises nearly everyone, and it explains why the proof in these cases looks so domestic. Calendars showing who drove a child to practice, a household budget, tax returns, caregiving notes, photographs, and testimony from neighbors and coaches all speak to companionship and services. Testimony about how much a family cries does not.

### The estate's separate claim and its closing window

Damages belonging to the estate follow a different rule. Section 377.34 limits them to loss the person sustained before death, including any penalties or punitive damages, and it excludes pain, suffering, and disfigurement. An exception applied to actions granted a preference under section 36 before January 1, 2022, and to actions filed on or after January 1, 2022 and before January 1, 2026. Because that exception keys to the filing date, two families with identical facts can land on opposite sides of it. Section 377.61 also states that the heirs' award may not include damages recoverable under section 377.34, which keeps the two claims from paying twice for the same thing.

### A child's share stops at the judge's desk

Where part of a recovery belongs to a minor or a person with a disability, approval is not optional. Rule 7.950 of the California Rules of Court requires a verified petition containing a full disclosure of all information bearing on the reasonableness of the compromise, filed on Judicial Council form MC-350. Rule 7.952 requires the petitioner and the minor to attend the hearing unless the court dispenses with the appearance for good cause, and it lets the court require testimony from the attending or examining physician. Fees paid from that money are measured against a reasonable fee standard under rule 7.955 rather than left to the contract.

### How the numbers get built

Future economic support is reduced to present cash value, while noneconomic damages are not discounted a second time. The work is therefore arithmetic as much as advocacy. It draws on earnings history, the trajectory the person was on, benefits and retirement contributions, the hours of household work someone else now performs, and life expectancy figures. Where the death happened at work or in a crash with a public entity involved, other systems pay some of these costs and their offsets have to be mapped before a settlement is structured rather than afterward.

## pricing_lede

California sets no percentage on what a lawyer may take out of a wrongful death recovery. It protects families instead through what the contract has to disclose and what a judge has to approve. This page lays out both, who the Code of Civil Procedure lets sue, and what the courthouse on O Street charges to carry a case to trial.

## pricing_body

### Nothing in California law fixes the number

Rule 1.5(a) of the California Rules of Professional Conduct forbids an agreement for, a charge of, or collection of an unconscionable or illegal fee, and no statute anywhere in California supplies a percentage ceiling for a wrongful death claim. The percentage on a Fresno family's contract came from the lawyer who drafted it. The law's answer is not to cap that figure but to force it into the open and, where a child is involved, hand it to a judge.

### How unconscionability actually gets tested

Rule 1.5(b) measures the fee against all facts and circumstances existing when the agreement was made, unless the parties contemplated that later events would affect it. The enumerated considerations include fraud or overreaching in negotiating or setting the fee, and material facts left undisclosed. They also include the amount of the fee in proportion to the value of the services performed, the relative sophistication of the lawyer and the client, and the results obtained. A grieving family is rarely a sophisticated buyer, and that asymmetry is on the list.

### What the contract has to say out loud

Business and Professions Code section 6147 governs the paperwork. Subdivision (a) requires a written contract signed by both the attorney and the client, or the client's guardian or representative, with a duplicate copy provided at the time it is entered into. It must state the agreed rate, explain how disbursements and costs will affect both the fee and the client's recovery, and disclose any compensation owed for related matters outside the contract. Then, at (a)(4), unless the claim falls under section 6146, the contract must state that no law sets this fee and that its amount is open to negotiation. If any of that is missing, subdivision (b) makes the agreement voidable at the client's option and limits the attorney to a reasonable fee.

### The 25 and 33 percent figures belong somewhere else

Those two numbers circulate as though they were general injury law. They are not. Section 6146(a) applies only where a person seeks damages against a health care provider based on that provider's alleged professional negligence. It caps the fee at 25 percent of the amount recovered where the case resolves by settlement and release signed before a civil complaint or arbitration demand is filed, and 33 percent where recovery follows the filing. A death caused by a truck driver, a defective machine, or unsafe premises here sits outside that section, and a contract applying those percentages has borrowed the wrong statute.

### Who California lets bring the claim

Section 377.60 of the Code of Civil Procedure decides standing. Subdivision (a) covers the surviving spouse, domestic partner, children and the issue of deceased children. Where no issue survives it reaches the people who would take the decedent's property by intestate succession, and it lets legal guardians step in where the parents are themselves deceased. Subdivision (b) adds, whether or not they qualify under (a), a dependent putative spouse and that spouse's children, stepchildren, parents, or legal guardians. Subdivision (c) reaches a minor who lived in the household for the 180 days before the death and depended on the decedent for at least half of their support. Any of them may sue, or the personal representative may sue on their behalf, and a percentage attaches to each resulting share rather than one lump.

### A child's money goes past the judge

Where part of a recovery belongs to a minor or a person with a disability, the fee stops being purely contractual. Rule 7.955(a)(1) requires the court to use a reasonable fee standard in approving attorney fees payable from that money, unless it approved the agreement in advance. Subdivision (b) gives fourteen nonexclusive factors, including at (b)(9) the informed consent of the minor's representative to the fee and at (b)(13) the risk of loss borne by the attorney, the costs advanced, and the delay in payment. Subdivision (c) requires a declaration addressing the applicable factors. Under rule 7.952(a) the petitioner and the minor both attend the hearing unless excused for good cause, and the court may require the examining physician to testify.

### What the courthouse on O Street costs to use

The fee schedule the Fresno court publishes puts a first paper in an unlimited civil case at $435, and the same amount is due from each other party filing an answer. Summary judgment is $500 under Government Code 70617(d), and a complex designation adds $1,000 for all plaintiffs plus $1,000 per defendant up to $18,000 for the case. The advance jury fee is $150 and nonrefundable. Court reporting runs $30 for a proceeding of an hour or less, then $440 per half day or $880 per day. On the probate side, a petition to compromise a minor's claim where no civil action is pending costs $435 under 70655(c)(1), a petition for appointment of a guardian of the estate is $435, and guardianship of the person alone is $225. Each is a case cost rather than a fee, and the contract should state who fronts them and what happens if the case ends with no recovery.

## svc_agricultural_workplace_fatalities_lede

Fresno County's largest industry produces its own pattern of fatal events: equipment, transport, confined spaces, chemicals, and heat, spread across employers, labor contractors, and property owners. This page describes how those cases are structured. Nothing here is legal advice or an offer to represent anyone.

## svc_agricultural_workplace_fatalities_body

### The scale of the industry, from the county's own report

The Fresno County Agricultural Commissioner reported that agricultural production values in the county totaled $9,029,122,000 in 2024, calling it the highest value recorded and describing Fresno County as a top agricultural producing county in California and the nation. That figure is context rather than a legal fact, but it explains why a fatal event here is rarely confined to one employer. A single field operation can involve a grower, a management company, a farm labor contractor, a custom harvester, a hauler, a chemical applicator, and an equipment vendor, each with its own records and its own insurance.

Source: [Fresno County Annual Crop & Livestock Report](https://www.fresnocountyca.gov/Departments/Agricultural-Commissioner/Annual-Crop-Livestock-Report)

### What federal counts show for this sector

The Bureau of Labor Statistics reported California fatal work injuries by industry for 2024. Within agriculture, forestry, fishing and hunting, crop production accounted for 13 deaths and support activities for agriculture accounted for 13, and the sector total fell from the prior year's figure of 45. Statewide, falls, slips and trips rose to 94 deaths from 79 the year before, with falls to a lower level making up 81 of them. Exposure to harmful substances or environments accounted for 83 deaths, down from 106 in 2023, with 74 of those involving exposure to harmful substances. Support activities for agriculture is the category that most often means a labor contractor rather than a farm.

Source: [BLS, Fatal work injuries in California, 2024](https://www.bls.gov/regions/west/news-release/fatalworkinjuries_california.htm)

### Where the employer defense stops

The compensation system generally bars a negligence suit against the employer, and that bar has statutory edges. Labor Code section 3706 provides that where an employer fails to secure the payment of compensation, an injured employee may bring an action at law against the employer for damages, as if the compensation statutes did not apply. Labor Code section 3602 keeps a separate set of doors open, including a claim where the injury is aggravated by the employer's fraudulent concealment of the injury and its connection with the employment, and a claim where the injury is caused by a defective product manufactured by the employer and sold or otherwise transferred for valuable consideration to an independent third person. Whether any of that fits a given death is a records question first.

Sources: [Lab. Code § 3706](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=LAB&sectionNum=3706.) · [Lab. Code § 3602](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=LAB&sectionNum=3602.)

### Labor contractors and layered employment

The most consequential question in a Valley field death is often who directed the work. A crew may be paid by a contractor, supervised by a foreman the contractor employs, working on ground controlled by a grower, using equipment owned by a third company, and transported in a vehicle registered to someone else. Each of those relationships generates paper: contracts, invoices, insurance certificates, safety plan acknowledgments, payroll records, and gate or scale logs. The identity of the entity that owed a duty is decided by what those documents show about control, not by whose name was on the paycheck.

### The regulator that arrives first

Cal/OSHA operates a Fresno District Office at 2550 Mariposa Street, Room 4000, in downtown Fresno, and that office is where a local investigation is typically opened. An investigation produces interviews, photographs, measurements, and sometimes citations. It answers a regulatory question about compliance, not the civil question of who owes a family anything, and a family cannot assume the two will reach the same conclusion. Requesting the investigation file, once it closes, is nonetheless one of the highest-value steps available, because it captures a worksite as it existed on the day.

Source: [Cal/OSHA district offices](https://www.dir.ca.gov/dosh/districtoffices.htm)

### The coroner's parallel duty

Government Code section 27491 gives the coroner a duty to inquire into and determine the circumstances, manner, and cause of certain deaths, and the categories it lists include deaths related to or following known or suspected accident or injury, occupational diseases and occupational hazards, and drownings. On a farm death, that means an official file is created independently of the employer's own reporting, and it can preserve details the employer's version omits.

Source: [Gov. Code § 27491](https://codes.findlaw.com/ca/government-code/gov-sect-27491/)

### What to collect while the season is still running

Field operations change quickly. The crop is harvested, the equipment is moved, the crew disperses, and a labor contractor's roster from that week may not be reproducible in a month. Useful items are mundane: the daily assignment, the names and phone numbers of coworkers, photographs of the machine and the ground, the training documents the worker signed, the vehicle used for transport, and the timeline of when supervisors were told what. Written preservation requests to the employer, the contractor, and the property owner cost nothing and shift the burden of an unexplained gap.

## svc_autopsy_and_evidence_preservation_lede

In the first days after a sudden death, two things happen at once: an official investigation begins, and the physical evidence a family might later need starts to disappear. This page describes both processes as general information rather than legal advice.

## svc_autopsy_and_evidence_preservation_body

### When the coroner takes the case

Government Code section 27491 gives the coroner a duty to inquire into and determine the circumstances, manner, and cause of specified deaths. The categories include violent, sudden, or unusual deaths, deaths related to or following known or suspected accident or injury, occupational diseases and occupational hazards, and drownings. Where a death falls in one of those categories, the investigation proceeds as a matter of statutory duty rather than at anyone's request, and its findings become an official record independent of any employer, hospital, or property owner.

Source: [Gov. Code § 27491](https://codes.findlaw.com/ca/government-code/gov-sect-27491/)

### The authority to perform an examination

Section 27491.4 addresses the coroner's authority in connection with those inquiries, including the performance of autopsies and the examinations needed to determine cause of death. Because that authority is statutory, an examination may occur without a family's agreement in cases within the coroner's jurisdiction. Understanding that in advance changes how families approach funeral scheduling, second opinions, and requests for retained samples or tissue.

Source: [Gov. Code § 27491.4](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=GOV&sectionNum=27491.4.)

### What the local office does, in its own words

The Fresno County Sheriff-Coroner describes the office as responsible for investigating reportable deaths in the county and conducting medico-legal investigations, and it lists among the coroner's duties identifying the deceased, notifying next of kin, and protecting the property of the deceased. That last duty is easy to overlook and practically useful, because personal effects, a phone, clothing, and vehicle contents can end up in the coroner's custody rather than at the scene.

Source: [Fresno County Sheriff-Coroner FAQs](https://www.fresnosheriff.org/community/questions/coroner-faqs.html)

### How selective the process is

The office's 2024 statistics show how the volume is filtered. Of 9,185 deaths in the county that year, 4,350 were reported to the coroner and 1,138 became case investigations with a report produced. Forensic pathologist examinations numbered 996, of which 319 were autopsy examinations and 677 were external examinations, toxicology, or medical record review. Those figures make an important point for families: a reported death does not automatically produce an autopsy, and the kind of examination performed determines what information will exist later.

Source: [2024 Fresno County Coroner Statistics](https://www.fresnosheriff.org/images/pdfs/2024%20Coroner%20Statistics%20Final%20-%20Updated.pdf)

### Records generated in the first week

Several separate files exist almost immediately: the coroner's investigative report and any examination findings, law enforcement reports, emergency dispatch recordings and computer-aided dispatch logs, ambulance or air transport records, and hospital emergency department charting. Each is held by a different custodian with a different request process and a different retention period, and audio and telemetry records tend to have the shortest retention of the group. Requesting them in writing, and keeping the request, is what establishes that a family asked while the material still existed.

### Physical evidence has its own clock

Vehicles are released for salvage, damaged equipment is repaired or replaced, a trench is backfilled, a machine guard is reinstalled, surveillance video is overwritten, and a work site changes with the next shift. None of that requires anyone to act in bad faith; it is what ordinarily happens. A short written request identifying the specific item, the location, and the date, asking that it be preserved and not altered or released, is the single most useful document a family can send in the first week. Keeping a copy with the date sent is part of the point.

### Toxicology, timing, and second opinions

Toxicology and final cause-of-death determinations often take weeks or months, and the interval frequently pushes past the point at which practical decisions have to be made. A family that wants an independent examination has a narrow window in which that is physically possible. A family that wants only the records has more time, but not unlimited time. Either way, the useful early step is to ask the coroner's office directly what stage the case is at and what will be produced.

### Where the estate's authority becomes necessary

Some records cannot be released to a relative without documented authority, particularly medical records and certain personal effects. The mechanism for establishing that authority is described on the survival actions page, and it takes time to complete. Beginning it while other preservation requests are going out keeps the two tracks from delaying one another.

## svc_construction_fatality_claims_lede

Construction deaths tend to involve several companies on one site and a set of written safety orders that state exactly what should have been in place. This page outlines how those pieces fit together and is offered as general information rather than legal advice.

## svc_construction_fatality_claims_body

### The rules are specific, which helps

California's construction safety orders are not vague standards of care. On fall exposure, the orders require that approved personal fall arrest, personal fall restraint, or positioning systems be worn by employees whose work exposes them to falling in excess of seven and a half feet from the perimeter of a structure, unprotected sides and edges, leading edges, through shaftways and openings, sloped roof surfaces steeper than 7:12, or other sloped surfaces steeper than 40 degrees, where not otherwise adequately protected. A measurement and a piece of equipment are both verifiable after the fact, which is why photographs of the structure and inventory of the harnesses on site matter.

Source: [8 CCR § 1670](https://www.dir.ca.gov/title8/1670.html)

### Excavation work has its own written duties

The excavation orders address the conditions that kill people in trenches. Where the stability of adjoining buildings, walls, or other structures is endangered by excavation operations, support systems such as shoring, bracing, or underpinning must be provided. Excavation below the level of the base or footing of a foundation or retaining wall is not permitted unless a support system such as underpinning is provided, the excavation is in stable rock, or a registered professional engineer has approved a determination that the work will not pose a hazard. Employees must be protected from loose rock or soil falling or rolling from an excavation face, and excavated material or equipment must be kept at least two feet from the edge or restrained.

Source: [8 CCR § 1541](https://www.dir.ca.gov/title8/1541.html)

### Statewide fall data, for perspective

Federal figures put falls, slips and trips at 94 fatal work injuries in California during 2024, up from 79 the previous year, with falls to a lower level accounting for 81 of them. Those counts cover all industries, not construction alone, but they describe the mechanism that drives most of the written rules above. They also make a practical point: the fatal event is usually a short fall in an ordinary situation, not a spectacular one, which is exactly the kind of scene that gets cleaned up before anyone photographs it.

Source: [BLS, Fatal work injuries in California, 2024](https://www.bls.gov/regions/west/news-release/fatalworkinjuries_california.htm)

### Several employers, one site

A commercial project involves an owner, a general contractor, subcontractors in sequence, equipment lessors, and sometimes a construction manager and a design professional. The compensation bar applies to the deceased worker's own employer. It does not by itself resolve whether another company on the site owed a duty, and the answer depends on contracts, scopes of work, site control, and who supplied and maintained the equipment involved. Subcontract agreements, daily reports, safety meeting sign-in sheets, inspection logs, and rental agreements are the documents that map those relationships.

### The general duty statute underneath it all

Civil Code section 1714 states the baseline: everyone is responsible not only for the result of willful acts but also for an injury occasioned to another by a want of ordinary care. Safety orders give that principle content on a construction site, but the orders are enforced by the state, and a violation is not automatically the same thing as civil liability. It is evidence about what was required and what was done, weighed alongside everything else in the record.

Source: [Civ. Code § 1714](https://codes.findlaw.com/ca/civil-code/civ-sect-1714/)

### Public projects change the clock

Where the project owner is a city, county, school district, or state agency, an early administrative claim step can apply before any lawsuit, and its deadline is measured in months. A private jobsite death and a public-works death can look identical on the ground and run on entirely different calendars. Identifying the owner and the contracting entity in the first weeks is therefore a scheduling decision, not a detail, and it is covered on the deadlines page.

### Preserving a site that is still being built

Construction sites are designed to change. The trench is backfilled, the scaffold comes down, the equipment goes back to the rental yard, and the crew moves to the next job. Photographs and video from any phone on site, the names of everyone present, the rental tags on equipment, the weather that day, and the sequence of work are all recoverable in week one and mostly gone by month three. Written requests asking the general contractor and the equipment lessor to preserve specific items are worth sending even when a family has not decided anything else.

## svc_damages_a_family_may_recover_lede

California divides what can be recovered after a death between two separate claims, and a judge rather than the family decides how a recovery is split. This page describes those categories and the approval steps that apply to some of them. It is general information and not legal advice.

## svc_damages_a_family_may_recover_body

### Two ledgers, not one

The survivors' claim and the decedent's own surviving claim measure different things. The survivors' claim addresses what the people entitled to bring it lost by the death. The estate's claim addresses what the person lost or was billed for while alive, and the governing section keeps pain, suffering, and disfigurement outside that measure, subject to a limited statutory window described on the survival actions page. Understanding which ledger a particular item belongs to prevents the common error of expecting one claim to pay for everything.

Source: [Cal. Code Civ. Proc. § 377.34](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=CCP&sectionNum=377.34.)

### The court, not the claimants, divides a recovery

Code of Civil Procedure section 377.61 assigns the court the job of deciding what each qualifying claimant is entitled to out of any award in a death action. That single sentence has large practical effects. It means an adjuster cannot lawfully resolve the whole matter with whichever relative answers the phone, it means a dispute among survivors about shares gets decided by a judge on evidence about dependency and relationship, and it means documentation of who relied on the decedent financially and in daily life is evidence about allocation, not just about liability.

Source: [Cal. Code Civ. Proc. § 377.61](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=CCP&sectionNum=377.61.)

### The categories in practical terms

Economic items are the ones with paper behind them: the financial support the household would have received, the value of services the person performed at home, funeral and burial expenses, and, on the estate's side, medical charges and lost earnings between injury and death. The non-economic side addresses the loss of the relationship itself, which has no invoice and is proven through testimony and the ordinary record of a shared life. Because the two are proven differently, the strongest files are usually the ones where the mundane economic documentation was gathered early rather than reconstructed under time pressure.

### One statutory cap, in one kind of case

Where the claim is against health care providers or health care institutions based on professional negligence, a statutory limit applies to noneconomic damages, and it increases annually on a schedule the statute sets, with the amount in effect at judgment, arbitration award, or settlement being the applicable one. That limit does not apply to ordinary negligence cases, and it does not reach economic damages in any case. The details are on the medical negligence page.

Source: [Civ. Code § 3333.2](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=CIV&sectionNum=3333.2.)

### Where a child's share is involved

Money recovered for a minor is not simply handed over. California's rules of court govern petitions to compromise a minor's claim, require attendance at the hearing by the petitioner and the minor unless the court excuses it for good cause, and apply a reasonable fee standard to money paid for a minor's benefit unless the court approved the agreement in advance. Those requirements are procedural, and they add time to the end of a case that families are rarely told about at the beginning. Fresno's own filing costs for such petitions appear on the fees page.

### Interaction with benefits already received

A family may have already received workers' compensation death benefits, health insurance payments, or public benefits before any civil claim is resolved. Those systems have their own recovery and credit mechanisms, and the Labor Code specifically contemplates that a court will sort out the respective interests of the survivors and an employer or carrier where a third-party recovery exists. Practically, that means the gross figure discussed in a settlement conversation is not the figure a family receives, and asking early about liens and credits avoids a very unpleasant late surprise.

Source: [Lab. Code § 3852](https://codes.findlaw.com/ca/labor-code/lab-sect-3852/)

### Local procedure adds its own timeline

Even a straightforward case carries local scheduling obligations. Fresno County Superior Court's local rules require that all named defendants be served and proof of service filed within sixty days of the filing of the complaint. Deadlines like that one are invisible from outside the courthouse and are exactly the sort of requirement that determines whether a case moves or stalls in its first months.

Source: [Fresno Superior Court civil FAQs](https://www.fresno.courts.ca.gov/divisions/civil/civil-faqs)

## svc_drowning_and_canal_fatalities_lede

Fresno County is threaded with irrigation canals that move water fast, look shallow, and sit next to neighborhoods and farm roads. Deaths in them raise questions about public property immunity that do not arise in ordinary cases. This page is general information about that framework and not legal advice.

## svc_drowning_and_canal_fatalities_body

### How much of this water there is

The Fresno Irrigation District describes its system as nearly 700 miles of canals and pipeline throughout the district, delivering water to over 200,000 acres of farmland in Fresno County, along with roughly 980 acres of groundwater recharge facilities. That is an unusual amount of open, moving water inside and around a metropolitan area, and it is why the hazard is a routine local subject rather than an occasional one.

Source: [Fresno Irrigation District](https://www.fresnoirrigation.com/about)

### What the local drowning record shows

The Water Safety Council of Fresno County, which publishes drowning-prevention material for the county, reports that 35 drownings, about 17 percent of all drownings in the county, occurred in Fresno County canals between 2005 and 2020. Drawing on sheriff's department reporting for 2016 through 2022, it counts 88 instances of sheriff's department participation in the rescue or retrieval of suspected drownings in canals. The same material describes why the hazard is deceptive: fast-moving water in a narrow channel can knock a person off their feet even at a depth of about a foot, canals can carry undertows and turbulence capable of pulling a strong swimmer under, sides can be steep and slippery, debris collects in the channel, and a dry canal is not safe because water may be released without warning.

Source: [Water Safety Council of Fresno County](https://www.watersafe.org/canals)

### The immunity that governs most of these cases

Government Code section 831.8 gives public entities and districts that operate flood control channels, water conservation or storage facilities, canals, and their related works substantial immunity from liability for injuries occurring in those facilities or on adjacent public property. The statute is not absolute. It withdraws the immunity for a person who did not commit a criminal trespass where the injury was caused by a dangerous condition that created a substantial and unreasonable risk of death or serious injury, where the condition was not reasonably apparent to and would not have been anticipated by a person using the property with due care, and where the entity had actual knowledge of the condition and sufficient time to protect against it. A separate exception applies where the injured person is under twelve years of age, subject to its own written conditions.

Source: [Gov. Code § 831.8](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=GOV&sectionNum=831.8.)

### When the ordinary dangerous-condition rule applies instead

Not every water death is a canal case. A pool, a retention basin, a construction excavation that filled, a work site adjacent to a channel, or a privately maintained ditch can each be governed by different rules, and where the property is public but the immunity does not apply, the analysis returns to the general dangerous-condition statute and its elements of notice and foreseeability. Identifying the owner and the operator of the specific structure, and whether a district, a city, a grower, or a homeowners association maintained it, is the first factual question.

Source: [Gov. Code § 835](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=GOV&sectionNum=835.)

### The coroner's file, and why it matters here

The Government Code lists drowning among the death categories the coroner has a duty to investigate. That means an official record of the circumstances, manner, and cause is created independently of any property owner or agency, and it typically includes scene information a family never sees firsthand. Requesting that file, and any law enforcement report referencing the same incident, is a concrete early step.

Source: [Gov. Code § 27491](https://codes.findlaw.com/ca/government-code/gov-sect-27491/)

### Local ordinance and physical condition

The City of Fresno's municipal code contains a dedicated article on canals and ponding basins, and it makes it unlawful for a person to swim, wade, or enter the waters of any posted canal or ponding basin operated by a public agency within the city without that agency's express consent. That kind of provision can bear on the immunity analysis above, because the statutory exception for a person who did not commit a criminal trespass turns on the lawfulness of the entry. Whether fencing, signage, ladder rungs, gates, or barriers existed, and in what condition, is a question of physical fact recorded best by immediate photographs of the site from several angles, including the approach a person would have taken.

Source: [Fresno Municipal Code, canals and ponding basins](https://library.municode.com/CA/fresno/codes/code_of_ordinances?nodeId=MUCOFR_CH10REREPUNUREPRCOUS_ART9CAPOBA)

### What the compressed timeline demands

A claim involving a district, a city, or the county runs on an administrative deadline measured in months rather than the ordinary limitation period, and the identity of the correct entity is not always obvious from a map. Water systems here are operated by multiple districts and municipalities whose boundaries overlap in ways residents have no reason to know. The practical sequence is to fix the location precisely, photograph it, identify the operator in writing, and treat the claims-presentation calendar as the governing constraint from the first week.

## svc_fatal_truck_and_farm_equipment_crashes_lede

Farm equipment on a public road, a produce trailer running to a packing house, and an interstate carrier operate under different rules, and after a fatal crash those differences decide which records exist. This page explains the framework in general terms rather than as legal advice.

## svc_fatal_truck_and_farm_equipment_crashes_body

### Slow vehicles on fast roads

California requires that a vehicle or combination designed to be and operated at 25 miles per hour or less display a slow-moving vehicle emblem on the rearmost vehicle, mounted base down between three and five feet above the ground. The Vehicle Code also describes the emblem's geometry: a cut-off triangle whose height must reach 14 inches or more, bordered in red reflective material at least an inch and three-quarters across, with fluorescent orange in the middle. Narrow exceptions exist for certain utility and highway maintenance vehicles working at a jobsite. Whether the emblem was present, clean, and correctly mounted is a factual question with a written standard behind it, and it is one of the first things a family should try to document.

Source: [Cal. Veh. Code § 24615](https://law.justia.com/codes/california/code-veh/division-12/chapter-2/article-3/section-24615/)

### Farm vehicles occupy their own category

Implements of husbandry are treated differently from ordinary commercial vehicles. Caltrans publishes the exemptions: implements of husbandry only incidentally operated or moved over a highway are exempt from registration, and a list of specific farm vehicles is exempt if they display an identification plate. That list includes farmer-owned water tank vehicles used to service the owner's own implements, cotton module movers, trailers equipped with plenum chambers for drying agricultural commodities, certain trap wagons with fuel tanks capped at 3,000 gallons total capacity, and forklifts operated by a farmer not for compensation, with a hay-squeeze treated as a forklift. Exemption from a registration requirement is not exemption from a duty of care, but it does change which agency holds which record.

Source: [Caltrans, Implements of Husbandry Exemptions](https://dot.ca.gov/programs/traffic-operations/legal-truck-access/exemption-farm-equipment)

### Hours-of-service gaps in agricultural hauling

Federal driving-hour limits contain an agricultural exception. The Federal Motor Carrier Safety Administration explains that where a trip involves no non-agricultural cargo and its sole purpose is to pick up or deliver agricultural commodities as defined in the rules, driving and on-duty time are not limited and the other requirements of the hours-of-service part do not apply. The exception covers transportation during the initial 150 air miles from the source of the commodity regardless of how far the final destination is, and once a driver operates beyond that radius the ordinary limits apply and those hours must be recorded. In a fatigue case, that boundary determines whether a log even exists for the hours in question.

Source: [FMCSA, agricultural commodity exception](https://www.fmcsa.dot.gov/regulations/hours-service/%E2%80%9Cagricultural-commodity%E2%80%9D-exception-49-cfr-3951k1-hours-service-regulations)

### Truck volume is a local variable, not a background fact

A City of Fresno freight study documents how mixed this traffic is. It notes that State Route 99 is designated part of the National Highway Freight Network and appears among the top 25 key corridors by commodity tonnage in federal freight data, that the city's designated truck routes adopted in 2005 restrict where commercial vehicles over 12,000 pounds may travel, and that on one measured section of State Route 41 an estimated 4,700 average annual daily truck trips made up roughly 4 percent of about 117,500 average annual daily vehicles. A separate observation point north of State Route 180 counted 147,834 daily vehicle trips, with a license plate analysis concluding 13 percent of screened plates were trucks.

Source: [City of Fresno truck reroute existing conditions study](https://www.fresno.gov/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/Fresno-Truck-Reroute-Existing-Conditions_August2023-FINAL.pdf)

### Machine safety rules that apply off the highway too

California's construction and general industry safety orders for agricultural operations set expectations for the equipment itself. The rules require that guards and shields be kept in place during operation, prohibit riders on equipment not designed to carry them, and require that power be shut down and the machine secured before servicing, adjusting, or unclogging, with everyone clear before it is restarted. A companion order requires seat belts on agricultural equipment where rollover protective structures are installed, referencing an SAE standard and an anchorage strength requirement. After a fatality, the condition of a guard, a shield, a belt, or a lockout point is physical evidence that a repair can erase within a week.

Sources: [8 CCR § 3441](https://www.dir.ca.gov/title8/3441.html) · [8 CCR § 3653](https://www.dir.ca.gov/title8/3653.html)

### Sorting out who the defendants are

A single load can involve a grower, a labor contractor, a hauling company, a broker, an equipment dealer, a repair shop, and a manufacturer. Registration, insurance filings, bills of lading, dispatch records, maintenance invoices, and the employment relationship all point at different entities. Where the driver was an employee, the compensation system and any third-party claim run on separate tracks, which is covered on the workplace pages. The useful early step is documentary: photograph plates, placards, decals, trailer numbers, and any visible damage before the equipment is moved or repaired.

## svc_fatal_vehicle_collision_claims_lede

A fatal collision in Fresno County can happen on a freeway built in the 1960s, on a two-lane county road, or at a rural intersection an hour from a trauma center. This page describes how those settings change the record and the claim. It is general information, not advice about a specific crash.

## svc_fatal_vehicle_collision_claims_body

### What the county's own death investigation shows

The Fresno County Sheriff-Coroner publishes annual case statistics. For 2024 the office recorded 9,185 total deaths in the county, of which 4,350 were reported to the coroner and 1,138 became coroner case investigations with a report produced. Within those investigations, 589 were classified as accidents, and motor vehicle accidents accounted for 193 of them. Bodies transported to the coroner's morgue numbered 1,400. ### Roads that were not designed for current traffic

Some of the county's fatality risk is structural. Caltrans describes the South Fresno stretch of State Route 99 as an alignment constructed in 1965 with pavement that is old and highly deteriorated, and notes that only two lanes, one in each direction, cross the existing structures at several interchanges, some of which are half or split configurations. On State Route 41 further west, the department has proposed expanding a 7.1-mile segment in Fresno and Kings counties to four lanes with a 46-foot-wide median, citing congestion, high collision rates, and a lack of passing opportunities on that segment. Design and maintenance history is discoverable, and it is the kind of record that only exists in an agency's files.

Sources: [Caltrans, South Fresno SR-99 Corridor](https://dot.ca.gov/caltrans-near-me/district-6/district-6-projects/06-0h240) · [Caltrans, Excelsior Expressway II](https://dot.ca.gov/caltrans-near-me/district-6/district-6-projects/06-0s370)

### Distance to definitive care

Community Regional Medical Center in downtown Fresno describes itself as the only Level I Trauma Center between Los Angeles and Sacramento. For a family, that fact usually surfaces as a timeline: where the injury happened, how long transport took, and which facility made which decision. Those intervals are documented in dispatch logs, air and ground ambulance records, and emergency department charting, and they are worth requesting early because retention schedules for audio and telemetry are shorter than for written charts.

Source: [Community Regional Medical Center trauma center](https://www.communitymedical.org/specialties-and-departments/trauma-center)

### When the defendant might be a public entity

If the condition of a road, a shoulder, a signal, or a crossing is part of the story, the analysis shifts to Government Code section 835, which exposes an agency to liability where its property was in a dangerous condition, provided the claimant proves each element the section lists: causation, a reasonably foreseeable risk of that type of harm, and either creation of the hazard by an employee acting negligently or notice early enough to have guarded against it. The section also demands a much earlier administrative step, described on the deadlines page, that runs on months rather than years.

Source: [Gov. Code § 835](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=GOV&sectionNum=835.)

### The two-year rule, and what it does not cover

Where someone else's wrongful conduct or carelessness caused the death, the ordinary window is two years, set by Code of Civil Procedure section 335.1. That is the outer boundary for a private defendant. It is not the operative deadline where a public agency, a government vehicle, or a public road condition is involved, and it is not the deadline for a claim against a health care provider. Assuming the two-year figure applies to every possible defendant in a crash is one of the more expensive assumptions available.

Source: [Section 335.1, text published by FindLaw](https://codes.findlaw.com/ca/code-of-civil-procedure/ccp-sect-335-1/)

### Evidence that disappears on its own schedule

Different parts of a collision record decay at different speeds. Private camera footage overwrites in days. A vehicle held at a tow yard can be released for salvage or auction while a family is still arranging a service. Skid and gouge marks weather. Commercial dispatch data, electronic logging records, and maintenance files exist but are held by the company that generated them. Written requests to preserve, sent early and kept as copies, are what convert those items from likely to available. Photographs of the roadway, the sightlines, and any temporary conditions such as signage, harvest traffic, or dust are cheap to take and impossible to recreate later.

## svc_filing_deadlines_and_government_claims_lede

The most common way a valid California death claim ends is a missed deadline, and the shortest deadlines apply when a public agency is involved. This page sets out the statutory periods and Fresno's local filing addresses as general information, not legal advice.

## svc_filing_deadlines_and_government_claims_body

### The general period

When a death is traced to another party's wrongful conduct or carelessness, two years is the ordinary limitation period. That is the default for a private defendant and the number most families are told. It is not the applicable deadline against a public entity, and it is not the applicable deadline against a health care provider for professional negligence, where a three-year and one-year structure with limited tolling applies instead.

Sources: [the two-year death provision, as FindLaw publishes it](https://codes.findlaw.com/ca/code-of-civil-procedure/ccp-sect-335-1/) and [the professional negligence provision](https://codes.findlaw.com/ca/code-of-civil-procedure/ccp-sect-340-5/)

### Six months, when a public entity is involved

Government Code section 911.2 gives a family six months from accrual to present a claim where the cause of action involves a death, bodily injury, personal property damage, or damage to growing crops, and one year for anything else. That presentation requirement comes before any lawsuit. A public entity here can mean a city, the county, a school or hospital district, a transit agency, an irrigation or water district, or the state, and a family often cannot tell from the facts of an incident that an agency is involved at all.

Source: [Gov. Code § 911.2](https://codes.findlaw.com/ca/government-code/gov-sect-911-2/)

### What happens after a claim is acted on

Section 945.6 governs the suit deadline once a claim has been presented. Where written notice of rejection is given and served as the statute requires, the action must be commenced not later than six months after the notice is personally delivered or deposited in the mail. Where no such notice is given, the period is two years from accrual of the cause of action. Because that six-month period runs from a mailing date, the envelope and the date stamp on an agency letter are worth keeping as carefully as the letter itself.

Source: [Gov. Code § 945.6](https://codes.findlaw.com/ca/government-code/gov-sect-945-6/)

### If the six months has already passed

Two provisions address late claims. Section 911.4 allows an application to the public entity for leave to present a late claim, which must be presented within a reasonable time not to exceed one year after the cause of action accrued. Section 946.6 allows a petition to the court for relief from the claim requirement where the application was denied or deemed denied, and it requires that the petition be filed within six months after the application is denied or deemed denied. These are relief mechanisms with their own deadlines, not extensions of the original one.

Sources: [Gov. Code § 911.4](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=GOV&sectionNum=911.4.) · [§ 946.6](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=GOV&sectionNum=946.6.)

### Where a Fresno County claim is filed

The County of Fresno states that claims against the county must be filed with the Clerk of the Board of Supervisors, and it restates the statutory periods: six months for bodily injury or death, damage to personal property, or damage to growing crops, and one year for all other claims. The county's Claim for Damages form is available from that office, bills and estimates are to be attached, and claims must be submitted by mail or in person to the Clerk of the Board of Supervisors, Hall of Records, 2281 Tulare Street, Room 301, Fresno, CA 93721. The county states that claims are not accepted by email or fax.

Source: [County of Fresno, Claims for Damages](https://www.fresnocountyca.gov/Departments/Clerk-of-the-Board-of-Supervisors/Claims-for-Damages)

### Where a City of Fresno claim is filed

The City of Fresno directs claims to its Risk Management Division and states that a Claim for Damages form must be filed in duplicate, either in person or by mail, at 2600 Fresno Street, Room 1030, Fresno, CA 93721-3612. The city reproduces the same six-month statutory period covering death, personal injury, personal property, and growing-crop claims. Two agencies operating in the same city, with different offices and different filing requirements, is the ordinary situation rather than an unusual one.

Source: [City of Fresno Risk Management](https://www.fresno.gov/personnel/risk-management/)

### Claims involving the courts themselves

A claim against a judicial branch entity uses its own form, which Fresno County Superior Court publishes. It is a reminder that the correct procedure depends on the identity of the entity, and that identifying the right one is the first substantive task rather than a formality.

Source: [Claim against judicial branch entity form](https://www.fresno.courts.ca.gov/system/files?file=government-claim-judicial-branch.pdf)

### A workable calendar

Treat six months as the operative planning horizon whenever a road, a public building, a public vehicle, a district facility, or a public employee might be involved, and treat the two-year figure as the outer limit rather than the target. Keep a written list of every entity that could conceivably be responsible, with the date of the incident at the top. Where the estate's own claim is involved, the authority to bring it takes time to establish, and that time comes out of the same period.

## svc_heat_illness_fatalities_lede

California regulates outdoor heat with a written standard that sets specific numbers: temperature triggers, water quantities, rest intervals, and a supervision period for new workers. This page explains what those provisions require. It is general information about the regulation, not legal advice.

## svc_heat_illness_fatalities_body

### The numbers the standard actually sets

Section 3395 of the state's occupational safety orders requires that employees have access to potable drinking water in quantities of at least one quart per employee per hour for the entire shift, provided free of charge. Shade must be present and available when the temperature exceeds 80 degrees Fahrenheit, in an amount and location the regulation specifies. Employees are to be allowed and encouraged to take a preventative cool-down rest in the shade when they feel the need, for a period of no less than five minutes, and an employee taking such a rest is to be monitored and asked whether they are experiencing symptoms of heat illness. These are quantified duties, which is what makes compliance provable or disprovable after the fact.

Source: [8 CCR § 3395](https://www.dir.ca.gov/title8/3395.html)

### High-heat procedures, and the agricultural rule

At temperatures of 95 degrees and above, the standard adds high-heat procedures. Those include effective means of observation and communication, a mandatory buddy system or regular contact where a supervisor observes 20 or fewer employees, designating one or more employees on each worksite authorized to call for emergency medical services, reminding employees throughout the shift to drink water, and pre-shift meetings to review the high-heat procedures. For agriculture specifically, the regulation requires a minimum ten-minute net preventative cool-down rest period every two hours once the temperature reaches 95 degrees. A gap between the written plan and the day's actual schedule is a documentary problem, and payroll and break records are where it appears.

### Acclimatization and the first two weeks

The standard treats new exposure as the highest-risk period. It states that acclimatization peaks in most people within four to fourteen days of regular work for at least two hours per day in the heat, requires close observation of all employees by a supervisor or designee during a heat wave, and requires that an employee newly assigned to a high heat area be closely observed by a supervisor or designee for the first fourteen days of employment. The regulation defines a heat wave as any day in which the predicted high temperature will be at least 80 degrees and at least ten degrees higher than the average high daily temperature in the preceding five days. Hire dates and assignment dates therefore matter as much as the thermometer.

### Emergency response, and the rural directions problem

The standard also addresses what happens after someone shows symptoms. Its emergency response procedures require effective communication, responding to signs and symptoms, contacting emergency medical services, and ensuring that clear and precise directions to the work site can and will be provided to emergency responders. On a Valley worksite reached by an unmarked farm road, that last requirement carries real weight, and after a death the question of how long it took anyone to describe the location becomes central. Dispatch recordings and cell records are the sources that answer it.

### What enforcement in this area looks like

Cal/OSHA has publicized heat enforcement actions with figures attached. In one 2024 action the division cited a landscaping company based in Van Nuys $276,425 for willful-serious heat violations, describing failures to provide access to water and shaded areas and proper training, and a lack of written procedures for high-temperature work, in conditions that often exceeded 95 degrees. In another, a farm labor contractor in Dixon was cited $17,550 for three serious violations of the heat standard, including failing to implement high heat or emergency response procedures and failing to follow its own written plan for acclimatizing employees during the first fourteen days of work in temperatures over 95 degrees. Both employers' cases followed complaint-based inspections, and one appealed.

Sources: [Cal/OSHA, willful-serious heat citation](https://www.dir.ca.gov/DIRNews/2024/2024-105.html) · [Cal/OSHA, farm labor contractor citation](https://www.dir.ca.gov/DIRNews/2024/2024-85.html)

### Local climate is the background condition

The National Weather Service office serving the region publishes Fresno climate normals, including an average July high temperature of 97.7 degrees. A figure at that level means the regulation's 95-degree threshold is not an outlier in a Fresno summer, and that high-heat procedures are routinely in scope rather than exceptional. What decides an individual case is narrower: recorded temperatures at that location on that day, the shift schedule, the water and shade actually available, and how a worker's symptoms were handled.

Source: [NWS Hanford, Fresno July normals](https://www.weather.gov/hnx/fat07nrm)

### Evidence that a family can still reach

Heat cases are won or lost on ordinary records. Time and payroll data show shift length and break timing. Text messages and radio logs show what supervisors said. Photographs show whether shade structures existed and where. Coworkers remember whether water was carried to the crew or left at a truck a quarter mile away. Medical records show core temperature on arrival. Where the employer is shielded by the compensation system, a labor contractor, property owner, or equipment provider may not be, which is a separate analysis covered on the exclusivity page.

## svc_medical_negligence_deaths_lede

A death that follows treatment runs on its own statutory track in California: a shorter and differently structured deadline, a notice requirement before filing, and a cap on one category of damages. This page describes those rules generally and is not legal advice.

## svc_medical_negligence_deaths_body

### The deadline is not the ordinary one

For an action against a health care provider based on professional negligence, Code of Civil Procedure section 340.5 fixes the outer boundary at three years from the injury date, and a shorter one year measured from when the claimant found out about the injury or, using reasonable diligence, ought to have found out, whichever of the two arrives sooner. The three-year period is tolled only for proof of fraud, intentional concealment, or the presence of a foreign body left in a person with no diagnostic or therapeutic purpose. For a minor the statute provides a period of three years from the date of the wrongful act, and for a child under the age of six a period of three years or until the eighth birthday, whichever provides the longer time. Applying the general two-year death limitation here is a mistake with no remedy.

Source: [Cal. Code Civ. Proc. § 340.5](https://codes.findlaw.com/ca/code-of-civil-procedure/ccp-sect-340-5/)

### The notice that must come first

Section 364 prohibits an action for professional negligence against a health care provider unless the defendant has been given at least ninety days' prior notice of the intention to commence the action. The notice does not require the pleading of a specific dollar amount, and where it is served within ninety days of the expiration of the applicable limitation period, the statute extends the time to file by ninety days from the date of service. That interaction between the notice and the deadline is technical, and it is one of the reasons cases in this category are prepared before they are announced.

Source: [Cal. Code Civ. Proc. § 364](https://codes.findlaw.com/ca/code-of-civil-procedure/ccp-sect-364/)

### One category of damages is capped

Civil Code section 3333.2 limits noneconomic damages in these cases. For a wrongful death action against health care providers or health care institutions based on professional negligence, the section sets a separate limit of $500,000 for providers collectively, another $500,000 for institutions collectively, and a further $500,000 for certain unaffiliated providers or institutions whose separate and independent negligence occurred at, or in relation to medical transport to, an unaffiliated institution. The statute took effect for cases filed or arbitrations demanded on or after January 1, 2023, and provides that the wrongful death figures increase by $50,000 each January 1 for ten years up to $1,000,000, with the amount in effect at the time of judgment, arbitration award, or settlement being the one that applies. Beginning January 1, 2034, that ceiling is adjusted annually by 2 percent.

Source: [Civ. Code § 3333.2](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=CIV&sectionNum=3333.2.)

### What the cap does not touch

The limit applies to noneconomic losses. Economic losses are a separate calculation, and in a death case they can include the value of financial support the household lost, medical and funeral expenses, and the value of services the person provided. The distinction matters at settlement, because a discussion framed entirely around the capped figure omits the part of the claim that is not capped.

### Records are the case

Nothing in a treatment case is visible from outside the chart. Complete records rather than summaries, nursing notes, medication administration records, imaging with the actual image files, laboratory values with times, monitoring data, consult notes, transfer records, and the billing file all carry different information. Requesting them in full and early, with an authorization signed by a person who has authority to make the request, is the entire foundation. Where the estate's representative or successor in interest is the correct requester, that authority question needs to be settled first, which is covered on the survival actions page.

### Institutions and public providers

A hospital district, a county facility, or a university medical center may be a public entity, which introduces an administrative claim requirement and a much shorter presentation deadline on top of the professional negligence rules. Clinics, contracted physician groups, staffing agencies, and transport providers may each be separate entities with separate insurers. Establishing exactly which corporate body employed which clinician is ordinary but necessary work, and the answer is usually in the billing records rather than the clinical ones.

### Where elder and dependent adult standards overlap

Where the patient was an elder or dependent adult and the conduct alleged is neglect rather than professional negligence, a different statutory scheme can apply, with its own proof standard and its own treatment of damages after death. Care facility deaths therefore get analyzed under both frameworks rather than one, and that analysis is set out on the care facility page.

## svc_nursing_home_and_care_facility_deaths_lede

Deaths in skilled nursing and residential care settings are regulated by a licensing system that issues classified citations with published penalty ranges, and they can involve a statutory scheme separate from ordinary medical negligence. This page is general information, not legal advice.

## svc_nursing_home_and_care_facility_deaths_body

### The citation classes, and what they mean

California classifies violations at long-term health care facilities and attaches civil penalties to each class. A class AA violation is one that meets the criteria for a class A violation and that the department determines was a substantial factor in the death of a resident, and it carries a civil penalty of not less than $5,000 and not more than $25,000 for each citation. A class A violation is one the department determines presents either imminent danger of death or serious harm to residents, or a substantial probability that death or serious physical harm would result, and it carries a penalty of not less than $1,000 and not more than $10,000 for each citation. A class B violation has a direct or immediate relationship to resident health, safety, or security without meeting the higher classes. A class A condition must be abated immediately unless the department sets a fixed correction period.

Source: [Health & Safety Code § 1424](https://codes.findlaw.com/ca/health-and-safety-code/hsc-sect-1424/)

### Why the class matters to a family

A citation is a regulatory finding, not a civil judgment, and its existence does not establish anyone's liability to a family. What it does provide is a dated, official statement of what the state found, written by investigators who visited the facility and reviewed records that a family cannot see. The classification also signals what the department concluded about the relationship between the violation and a resident's death, which is information no facility incident report will state in those terms.

### The elder and dependent adult track

Welfare and Institutions Code section 15657 applies where a defendant is liable for physical abuse or neglect of an elder or dependent adult and it is proven by clear and convincing evidence that the defendant acted with recklessness, oppression, fraud, or malice in committing the abuse. In that situation the court awards reasonable attorney's fees and costs in addition to other remedies, and the statute provides that the limitations of Code of Civil Procedure section 377.34 on damages recoverable after the plaintiff's death do not apply, while capping damages for the decedent's predeath pain and suffering by reference to the limit in Civil Code section 3333.2. The proof standard is demanding, and the difference between negligence and recklessness is the whole question.

Sources: [Welf. & Inst. Code § 15657](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=WIC&sectionNum=15657.) · [Civ. Code § 3333.2](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=CIV&sectionNum=3333.2.)

### Two frameworks, one set of facts

The same death can be examined as professional negligence by licensed clinicians and as neglect by a custodial facility. The frameworks differ in deadline, proof standard, damages treatment, and fee exposure, which is why the characterization of the conduct does more work here than in most cases. Charting that shows a pattern over weeks tends to matter more than any single entry, and staffing records, assignment sheets, and call-light or alarm data are where a pattern becomes visible.

### The record set worth requesting

Beyond the clinical chart, useful material includes the admission agreement and all attachments, care plans and their revisions, weight and intake logs, wound and skin assessments, fall and incident reports, medication administration records, physician orders and their timing, transfer and hospital records, and the facility's own investigation. Public licensing history and inspection findings for the facility are separate, and they sit with the state rather than with the operator.

### Deadlines depend on characterization

A claim framed as professional negligence runs on the shorter medical timeline described on the medical negligence page. A claim framed differently may run on the general two-year death limitation. Where the facility is operated by a public entity, an administrative claim requirement can precede any lawsuit. Because the characterization is contested and the deadlines differ, the earliest possible determination of which clock is running is the practical priority.

### What to do while the file is fresh

Keep a dated log of what was observed and who said what, with names and shifts. Ask for records in writing and keep the request and the response. Photograph anything visible during a visit. Preserve phone messages and emails from the facility, which often contain the only contemporaneous account of an event. If a coroner investigation was opened, its file is a separate and independent record of the circumstances.

## svc_premises_and_security_related_deaths_lede

When a death happens on someone else's property, the questions are about the physical condition of the place, what the owner knew, and whether the harm was foreseeable. This page explains the general framework in California and is not legal advice about any specific location.

## svc_premises_and_security_related_deaths_body

### The baseline duty

Civil Code section 1714 states the underlying principle that everyone is responsible not only for the result of willful acts but also for an injury occasioned to another by a want of ordinary care. For a property case, that principle takes its shape from the facts: what the condition was, how long it existed, whether it was inspected, what warnings existed, and who had control. Ownership, occupancy, management, and maintenance are frequently split among different companies, and each split creates a separate set of records.

Source: [Civ. Code § 1714](https://codes.findlaw.com/ca/civil-code/civ-sect-1714/)

### Public property is analyzed under a statute

Where the property belongs to a public entity, liability runs through Government Code section 835, which requires proof that the property was in a dangerous condition at the time of the injury, that the condition proximately caused the injury, that the condition created a reasonably foreseeable risk of the kind of injury which was incurred, and either that a negligent or wrongful act of an entity employee created the condition or that the entity had notice of it a sufficient time in advance to have taken protective measures. Parks, transit facilities, parking structures, school grounds, and public buildings therefore run on a different analysis than a shopping center.

Source: [Gov. Code § 835](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=GOV&sectionNum=835.)

### Deaths involving third-party violence

A death caused by another person's criminal act raises a separate question about the property: what the owner knew about prior incidents, what security measures existed, and whether the harm was foreseeable in that setting. Local context matters to that inquiry. The Fresno County Sheriff-Coroner's 2024 statistics classified 63 of the county's 1,138 coroner case investigations as homicides, alongside 96 suicides and 6 undetermined cases. Those are county-wide counts rather than facts about any location, and a case about a specific property is built from that property's own incident history, calls for service, and security records.

Source: [2024 Fresno County Coroner Statistics](https://www.fresnosheriff.org/images/pdfs/2024%20Coroner%20Statistics%20Final%20-%20Updated.pdf)

### Water features and open channels on private land

A property with a pool, a pond, a retention basin, or an unfenced channel adjacent to it raises overlapping questions of condition, barriers, and access. Where a public water facility is involved, a specific statutory immunity with written exceptions applies instead, and that framework is described on the canal and drowning page. Establishing which entity owned and maintained the exact structure involved is the first step in either direction.

### The records that decide these cases

Property cases turn on documents the owner controls: inspection and maintenance logs, work orders, vendor contracts, lighting and camera specifications, prior incident reports, tenant complaints, and correspondence with insurers or inspectors. Surveillance footage is the single most decisive item and the one most likely to be gone, because many systems overwrite on a short cycle. A written preservation request naming the date, time, camera locations, and system, sent in the first days, is the step that most often determines whether footage still exists when anyone asks for it.

### Two claims, one incident

As in any California death case, the survivors' action and the decedent's own surviving claim are distinct, with different owners and different measures, and they are described on the standing and survival action pages. In a premises case, that distinction affects who may request records and who may sign a release, which is why the authority question is worth resolving before dealing with an insurer.

### Practical steps in the first weeks

Fix the location precisely, including the exact entrance, stairway, aisle, or lot section. Photograph the condition, the lighting, the signage, and the sightlines at the same hour of day as the incident. Identify the owner through county assessor records rather than the sign on the building. Note whether construction, repairs, or an event changed the site. Keep every insurer letter, and avoid giving a recorded statement before the underlying facts are documented, since the earliest version of events tends to be the one that gets quoted later.

## svc_punitive_damages_and_survival_claims_lede

Punitive damages in California require a specific showing about a defendant's state of mind, and after a death they attach to the estate's claim rather than to the survivors' claim. This page explains the statutory requirements as general information, not as legal advice.

## svc_punitive_damages_and_survival_claims_body

### What the statute requires

Civil Code section 3294 permits exemplary damages in an action for breach of an obligation not arising from contract where it is proven by clear and convincing evidence that the defendant has been guilty of oppression, fraud, or malice. Clear and convincing evidence is a higher standard than the ordinary one used to decide liability, and the terms are defined rather than left to argument. The section defines malice as conduct intended to cause injury, or despicable conduct carried on with a willful and conscious disregard of the rights or safety of others. It defines oppression as despicable conduct that subjects a person to cruel and unjust hardship in conscious disregard of that person's rights, and it defines fraud in terms of intentional misrepresentation, deceit, or concealment of a material fact with intent to deprive a person of property or legal rights or otherwise cause injury.

Source: [Civ. Code § 3294](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=CIV&sectionNum=3294.)

### Corporate defendants have an extra requirement

Where the defendant is an employer, the statute adds conditions. An employer is not liable for exemplary damages based on an employee's act unless the employer had advance knowledge of the employee's unfitness and employed that person with a conscious disregard of the safety of others, or authorized or ratified the wrongful conduct, or was itself personally guilty of oppression, fraud, or malice. For a corporate employer, that advance knowledge, authorization, ratification, or act of oppression must be on the part of an officer, director, or managing agent of the corporation. In a case against a company, that requirement moves the inquiry away from the individual at the scene and toward what the organization knew and decided.

### Which claim carries them after a death

Section 377.34 provides that the damages recoverable in an action brought by the decedent's personal representative or successor in interest include any penalties or punitive or exemplary damages that the decedent would have been entitled to recover had death not occurred. The survivors' standing statute contains no comparable provision. The practical consequence is structural: where punitive damages are in play after a death, they generally travel with the estate's surviving claim, which is one more reason the authority to bring that claim needs to be established rather than assumed.

Source: [Cal. Code Civ. Proc. § 377.34](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=CCP&sectionNum=377.34.)

### Elder and dependent adult cases use their own standard

Welfare and Institutions Code section 15657 sets out a related but separate scheme for physical abuse or neglect of an elder or dependent adult. It requires proof by clear and convincing evidence that the defendant is liable for the abuse and acted with recklessness, oppression, fraud, or malice, and it then provides for an award of reasonable attorney's fees and costs and modifies how damages after death are treated. Recklessness appears in that list and not in section 3294, which is why the two frameworks are analyzed side by side in care facility cases.

Source: [Welf. & Inst. Code § 15657](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=WIC&sectionNum=15657.)

### What kind of evidence bears on state of mind

Documents rather than adjectives carry this issue. Prior complaints and warnings, internal audits, regulatory citations and their history, maintenance deferrals, budget decisions about safety equipment, training that was scheduled and cancelled, and communications showing that a decision-maker was told about a hazard before the event all speak to knowledge and choice. Personnel files, board or management meeting materials, and repeat-violation histories can matter more than the incident report. This material is usually obtained in litigation rather than requested informally.

### A high bar, honestly stated

Most negligence cases do not support a punitive claim, and treating one as likely tends to distort a family's expectations of everything else. The clear and convincing standard, the definitional requirements, and the managing-agent rule for corporations each independently narrow the field. The realistic use of section 3294 in the early stages is as a reason to preserve the organizational records that would show what a company knew, because those documents are also relevant to ordinary liability.

### How it interacts with settlement and approval

Where a claim on behalf of a minor or an estate is resolved, court involvement in approval and allocation can follow, as described on the damages page. A punitive component does not change who owns the underlying claim, and it does not remove the requirement that a court determine the respective rights of survivors in any award on the wrongful death side. Keeping the two claims distinct in any settlement document is what prevents an unintended release of the claim that carries this exposure.

## svc_survival_actions_in_california_lede

The claim the person who died owned at the moment of death does not vanish with them. It becomes an asset of the estate, with its own owner, its own damages rules, and its own filing mechanics. This page describes that second claim in general terms and is not legal advice.

## svc_survival_actions_in_california_body

### Two claims, two owners

A survival action is the decedent's own cause of action, continued after death rather than created by it. Code of Civil Procedure section 377.30 provides that a cause of action that survives passes to the decedent's personal representative or, where none exists, to the decedent's successor in interest, and that it may be enforced by that person. Compare that with the survivors' claim: different plaintiff, different injury, different measure. The same event can generate both, and the paperwork for each is filed by a different party even when everyone involved is in the same family.

### Proceeding without a probate estate

Section 377.32 lets a successor in interest bring or continue the action by filing a declaration or affidavit instead of obtaining letters of administration. The statute prescribes what that document contains, including the decedent's name and date and place of death, a statement that no proceeding is now pending in California for administration of the estate, the declarant's basis for succeeding to the claim, and a statement that no other person has a superior right. A certified copy of the death certificate attaches to it. This route saves a family that has no other reason to open probate from opening one, but the declaration is a sworn document and the superior-right statement is the part people get wrong.

Sources: [§ 377.30](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=CCP&sectionNum=377.30.) · [§ 377.32](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=CCP&sectionNum=377.32.)

### What the estate's claim can and cannot collect

Section 377.34 sets the measure. Recovery is limited to what the person lost or spent while still alive, plus any penalty or punitive award that person could have obtained had they survived, and the section keeps pain, suffering, and disfigurement out of that calculation. That single exclusion is the reason two claims arising from one death can be valued so differently. Medical charges, ambulance and hospital billing, lost earnings between injury and death, and property damage belong to the estate's side of the ledger.

### The temporary exception, and its expiry

The section carries a limited exception. For an action that was granted a preference under section 36 before January 1, 2022, or that was filed on or after January 1, 2022 and before January 1, 2026, damages for the decedent's pain, suffering, or disfigurement are recoverable. The Legislature attached a reporting mechanism to that window: a plaintiff who recovers such damages must report the judgment to the Judicial Council within sixty days, with the case details the statute lists. Whether that window applies to a given case depends on its filing date, which is one more reason the calendar governs this area more than the facts do.

Source: [§ 377.34](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=CCP&sectionNum=377.34.)

### Where elder and dependent adult claims diverge

Welfare and Institutions Code section 15657 changes the arithmetic for a narrow class of cases. Where a defendant is found liable for physical abuse or neglect of an elder or dependent adult with recklessness, oppression, fraud, or malice, and the standard of proof in the statute is met, the court awards reasonable attorney's fees and costs, and the statute states that the limitations of section 377.34 on damages recoverable after death do not apply. It then caps damages for the decedent's predeath pain and suffering by reference to the limit in Civil Code section 3333.2. That combination is why care-facility deaths are analyzed on a separate track.

Source: [Welf. & Inst. Code § 15657](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=WIC&sectionNum=15657.)

### Practical consequences worth knowing early

Records that only the estate's representative can request tend to be the records that decide the case. Hospital and billing files, employment and payroll data, phone and vehicle data, and insurance correspondence all move faster once someone holds clear authority. Settling one claim without addressing the other is a recurring problem: a release signed by survivors, or by a successor in interest, can be read more broadly than the person signing intended. Sorting out which claim is which before any money changes hands avoids a fight over what was actually resolved.

## svc_who_may_bring_a_wrongful_death_claim_lede

California decides who is allowed to sue for a death by statute, not by who was closest to the person. This page walks through the list the Legislature wrote, the people it leaves out, and the estate question that sits underneath it. It is general information about California procedure and not legal advice about any particular family.

## svc_who_may_bring_a_wrongful_death_claim_body

### A statutory list, not a family tree

Standing comes from Code of Civil Procedure section 377.60, which names the categories of people who may bring the action. The first group covers a surviving spouse, a domestic partner, children, and the issue of deceased children. Where none of those exist, the statute reaches the persons who would be entitled to the decedent's property by intestate succession. A separate branch of the section covers a putative spouse and that person's children, along with stepchildren, parents, and the legal guardians of the decedent where the parents have died. The section describes a putative spouse as a surviving spouse of a void or voidable marriage who is found to have believed in good faith that the marriage was valid. Reading the statute in order matters, because the later branches only open when the earlier ones are empty.

### Dependency, and the household minor

Two paths in section 377.60 turn on financial reality rather than family status. One allows a claim by parents, or by other listed relatives, when they were dependent on the decedent for support. The other covers a minor who lived in the decedent's household for at least 180 days before the death and depended on the decedent for at least one-half of that minor's support. Both are proof questions. Pay records, tax filings, lease and school documents, shared bank accounts, and the household timeline are what establish them, and they are easier to gather in the first months than in the second year.

Source: [Cal. Code Civ. Proc. § 377.60](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=CCP&sectionNum=377.60.)

### One action, several claimants

California treats this as a single action even when many people qualify. Under section 377.61, it falls to the judge to fix each claimant's share of anything awarded, so the division is a judicial determination rather than a family negotiation. The practical consequence is that a relative who is left out of a filing does not get an independent second lawsuit later, and someone who signs a release for their own share can affect the shape of the whole case.

Source: [Cal. Code Civ. Proc. § 377.61](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=CCP&sectionNum=377.61.)

### The estate question runs alongside it

Standing under section 377.60 belongs to survivors. Authority over the decedent's own claim is a different appointment, and where an estate is opened it runs through probate. Fresno County Superior Court publishes the mechanics for the county: the original will must be deposited with the Probate Clerk within thirty days of the death, the Probate Clerk's Office sits on the third floor of the B. F. Sisk Courthouse at 1130 O Street in Room 300, and where no executor is named the court appoints an administrator following a statutory order of priority. A family that assumes the two questions are the same one often discovers late that nobody has authority to sign for the estate.

Source: [Fresno Superior Court, Decedent's Estate](https://www.fresno.courts.ca.gov/divisions/probate/decedents-estate)

### Situations that complicate the list

Blended families, long separations without a filed dissolution, adult children of an earlier marriage, unmarried partners who were never registered, and adopted or stepchildren all sit at the edges of the statutory categories rather than outside them. So do relatives who supported the household in cash without documentation. None of that makes a claim impossible, and none of it makes one automatic. What resolves it is documentary: marriage and registration records, dissolution files, birth and adoption records, immigration and residency documents, and the money trail showing who actually relied on whom.

### What is worth doing before anyone files

Write out the family as it actually was, with dates, addresses, and dependents, rather than as it appears on one form. Order several certified copies of the death certificate. Locate any will and find out whether probate has been opened by someone else. Keep every letter from an insurer, employer, or public agency together with its claim number and its postmark, because more than one deadline in California starts running from a mailing date. Where a public entity may be involved, the timeline compresses sharply and is covered on the deadlines page.

## svc_workplace_death_and_comp_exclusivity_lede

Families are often told that workers' compensation is the only remedy after a death at work. That is the general rule and it has written exceptions, and it says nothing about companies that were not the employer. This page explains the structure as general information, not legal advice.

## svc_workplace_death_and_comp_exclusivity_body

### What the general rule actually says

Labor Code section 3600 conditions the compensation remedy on a list of requirements: that both employer and employee are subject to the compensation provisions, that the injury arises out of and in the course of the employment, and that the injury is not caused by intoxication, by the employee's own intentional self-infliction, by an altercation the employee initiated, or by certain felonious or off-duty recreational circumstances the statute describes. Where those conditions are met, liability for compensation exists without regard to negligence and in place of any other liability to the employee or the employee's dependents. That last phrase is the exclusivity rule, and its scope is defined by the same statute that creates it.

Source: [Lab. Code § 3600](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=LAB&sectionNum=3600.)

### The written exceptions

Section 3602 states the exclusivity principle and then carves out specific situations. One covers an employee's injury or death proximately caused by a willful physical assault by the employer. Another allows an action where the injury is aggravated by the employer's fraudulent concealment of the existence of the injury and its connection with the employment, with the employer's compensation liability offset against the damages. A third permits a claim where the injury or death is proximately caused by a defective product manufactured by the employer and sold, leased, or otherwise transferred for valuable consideration to an independent third person, where the injured employee then works for a different employer. These are narrow, and each one is a factual showing rather than an argument.

Source: [Lab. Code § 3602](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=LAB&sectionNum=3602.)

### An employer without coverage is in a different position

Where an employer fails to secure the payment of compensation, section 3706 allows an action at law against that employer for damages as if the compensation provisions did not apply. In an economy with layered labor arrangements, whether coverage actually existed for the crew doing that work on that day is a question worth answering rather than assuming. Certificates of insurance, contractor licensing records, and the carrier's own correspondence are the starting documents.

Source: [Lab. Code § 3706](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=LAB&sectionNum=3706.)

### Third parties are outside the bar entirely

Exclusivity protects the employer. It does not protect a property owner, a general contractor, a machinery manufacturer, a chemical supplier, a maintenance company, a trucking firm, or another driver. California's compensation statutes contemplate that a claim for benefits proceeds alongside an action against a third party, and they provide a mechanism for sorting out the employer's or carrier's interest in any third-party recovery. The practical point for a family is that the existence of a compensation claim is not evidence that no other claim exists.

### Two benefit systems, two measures

Compensation death benefits are set by statute and paid according to dependency, with the amounts and installment rules fixed by the Labor Code rather than negotiated. A wrongful death action measures something the compensation system does not attempt to value at all: the survivors' loss of the relationship. Because the two systems overlap in time but not in purpose, correspondence in a compensation file frequently misdescribes what a family's total exposure or recovery might be. Reading a benefit notice as the whole picture is a common and avoidable error.

### Where the claims-presentation statutes fit

If the employer or the third party is a public entity, the government claims process becomes relevant, but the Government Code excludes claims by public employees for fees, salaries, wages, and workers' compensation from its general claim requirements. That exclusion is easy to misread as a general exemption. It is not, and a family with both a compensation claim and a potential claim against a public agency is dealing with two separate procedural tracks running on different calendars.

Source: [Gov. Code § 905](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=GOV&sectionNum=905.)

### What to secure while the compensation file is open

The compensation claim generates useful material: the employer's report, the carrier's investigation correspondence, medical and billing records, and wage statements. So does the state safety investigation. A family that keeps organized copies of both, with dates and claim numbers, preserves the factual record for any other claim that might exist. Signing a compromise and release, or any broad settlement document, without understanding which claims it touches is the step that most often closes doors nobody meant to close.

## q_how_long_does_a_wrongful_death_lawsuit_take_lede

There is no fixed number, and anyone who gives one is guessing. What actually sets the pace is which of three tracks a claim is on, and Fresno's own civil calendar adds a layer none of the general answers online account for.

## q_how_long_does_a_wrongful_death_lawsuit_take_body

### The three tracks run at different speeds

A pre-litigation settlement -- the claim resolves through negotiation with an insurer before a lawsuit is even filed -- is the fastest path, and can close in months when liability isn't seriously disputed and records are complete. A filed lawsuit that settles during litigation, most commonly during or shortly after discovery, is the middle track. A case that goes to trial is the slowest, and in California that includes time the case simply waits for a courtroom and a judge to become available, which is scheduling, not case complexity. This general information describes how these tracks work, not what any specific case will do.

### Where the case actually sits changes what "long" means

Cases naming a public entity add the six-month government-claim presentation period before a lawsuit can even be filed at all, on top of whatever the underlying litigation takes. Medical negligence deaths run against California's shorter three-year-or-one-year discovery structure rather than the ordinary two-year period, which affects when a case has to start, not how long it takes once it does. Both of those are covered in more depth on the filing-deadlines page; the point here is that the clock on presentation and the clock on litigation duration are two different things, and families sometimes only track one of them.

### What actually consumes the time in the middle

Discovery -- written questions, document production, depositions -- is where most of a litigated case's duration lives, and its length depends on how many parties are involved, how contested liability is, and whether expert witnesses are needed on causation or damages. A fatal collision with one clear at-fault driver moves differently than a nursing home death or a workplace fatality where an employer, a contractor, and an equipment manufacturer may all be named separately. More defendants generally means more time, not because any one of them is slow, but because discovery has to run with all of them.

### Fresno County's own calendar is part of the answer

Fresno County Superior Court hears Unlimited Civil cases -- the category most wrongful death claims fall into -- at the B.F. Sisk Courthouse, and like most California counties, trial-setting depends on the court's own calendar load at the time a case is ready, not just how quickly the parties finish discovery. A case that is factually and legally ready for trial can still wait for a courtroom. That scheduling reality is local to where the case is filed and doesn't show up in general national answers to this question.

### The honest range, and why it's still a range

Pre-litigation settlements can close in months. Litigated cases that settle before trial commonly run one to two years from filing. Cases that go the distance to trial can run two years or more once court scheduling is added in. None of those numbers are a prediction for a specific case -- they describe how the three tracks behave in general, which is what this page can responsibly offer. A conversation about where a specific case is likely to fall, given its facts, is what the call is for.
