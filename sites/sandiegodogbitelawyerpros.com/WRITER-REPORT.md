## The three sourced local facts

1. **California strict liability + the two-year deadline** (`strict_liability_statute`)
   Civil Code 3342 makes a dog's owner liable for a bite in a public place, or anywhere the
   bitten person was lawfully present including the owner's own property, regardless of the
   dog's former viciousness or the owner's knowledge. CCP 335.1 gives two years to sue.
   - https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?sectionNum=3342.&lawCode=CIV
   - https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?sectionNum=335.1&lawCode=CCP

2. **San Diego County 24-hour bite reporting and the 10-day quarantine** (`county_bite_reporting`)
   County Code 62.615 puts the duty to notify on the bitten person, the parent of a bitten
   child, the treating physician, the animal's owner and anyone else who knows of the bite,
   and deems a failure to report within 24 hours a violation. County Animal Services states
   the biting dog is quarantined ten days from the date of the bite even with current rabies
   vaccination. (San Diego city and county specific.)
   - https://codelibrary.amlegal.com/codes/san_diego/latest/sandiego_regs/0-0-0-127231
   - https://www.sddac.com/content/sdc/das/license-laws/laws/dog_bites.html

3. **2023-2024 San Diego County Grand Jury dog bite report** (`grand_jury_bite_data`)
   Roughly 2,500 bites a year reported by the county to the California Department of Public
   Health across 2020-2022; about 40% involved dogs unvaccinated or of unknown vaccination
   status; not every city files, so the true count is higher; 9 to 18 Dangerous Dog hearings
   a year, of which 78-90% ended in a dangerous declaration. (Dated, county specific.)
   - https://www.sandiegocounty.gov/content/dam/sdc/grandjury/reports/2023-2024/dog-bites/23-24%20Dog%20Bite%20Report.pdf
