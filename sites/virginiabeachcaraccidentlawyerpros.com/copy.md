### Virginia’s current minimum limits

Virginia Code § 46.2-472 sets different floors by policy effective date. ### What the UM/UIM statute requires

Virginia Code § 38.2-2206 generally requires a bodily-injury or property-damage liability policy issued or delivered in Virginia to include uninsured motorist protection. The UM limits must be at least the statutory requirements and ordinarily equal the liability limits, although a named insured can reject additional UM coverage in the form the statute permits. The same endorsement must provide underinsured motorist coverage equal to the UM limits.

The statute defines an uninsured vehicle broadly enough to include one with no qualifying liability coverage, one whose insurer denies coverage, certain vehicles backed by no bond or self-insurance, and an unknown owner or operator. It defines an underinsured vehicle by comparing applicable, available liability coverage with total damages, up to the UIM coverage afforded. Those definitions are technical. They explain why the policy and statutory language should be read rather than reduced to “the other driver had insurance” or “the other driver did not.”

The statute also addresses whether underinsured coverage is paid with or without credit for liability coverage available for payment. Virginia handles injury fees with a reasonableness rule, a list of terms that must appear in a written contingent agreement, and a set of statutory amounts a clerk and a hospital can claim. This page walks through each of those, with the rule or code section beside it.

## pricing_body

### Virginia's rule requires a reasonable fee and names what that means

Rule 1.5 of the Virginia Rules of Professional Conduct opens with four words that carry the whole standard: a lawyer's fee shall be reasonable. It then lists eight factors for testing one: the time and labor required, the novelty and difficulty of the questions and the skill needed; whether the matter closed off other employment; and the fee customarily charged in the locality for similar services. The list continues with the amount involved and the results obtained; time limits imposed by the client or the circumstances; and the nature and length of the professional relationship. The last two are the experience, reputation and ability of the lawyers, and whether the fee is fixed or contingent.

No percentage appears anywhere in that list. Virginia does not legislate a rate for injury work, so any figure presented as "the Virginia limit" for a car crash claim is somebody's practice rather than the Commonwealth's rule.

### Rule 1.5 also controls the paperwork, and it is specific

Rule 1.5(b) requires the fee to be adequately explained, and where the lawyer has not regularly represented that client, the amount, basis or rate must be communicated preferably in writing before or within a reasonable time after the representation begins. Rule 1.5(c) is stricter for contingent matters. Such an agreement has to state in writing the method by which the fee is determined, along with the percentages that accrue to the lawyer on settlement, trial or appeal. It must also list the litigation and other expenses deducted from the recovery, and say whether those expenses come out before or after the fee is calculated. When the matter concludes, the rule requires a written statement of the outcome and, where there was a recovery, the remittance and how it was computed.

A single percentage for every scenario is not what the rule contemplates, and the before-or-after question about expenses is no technicality: the same percentage applied in the two orders produces different money for the client.

Virginia adds one flat prohibition that not every state has. Nonrefundable advanced legal fees are barred. Contingent fees are prohibited outright in domestic relations matters, except in rare instances, and in criminal defense.

### What the Virginia Beach clerk charges to open a case

Court costs in the Commonwealth are assembled from parts rather than quoted as one figure. Under Va. Code 17.1-275(A)(13) the circuit court clerk's fee in a civil action that includes a claim for money damages is $100 where the recovery sought does not exceed $49,999, $200 up to $100,000, $250 up to $500,000, and $300 above that; $10 of each fee goes to the Courts Technology Fund. A petition to approve a settlement where no action has been filed carries a $50 clerk's fee.

Separately, Va. Code 58.1-1727 imposes a writ tax on commencing a civil action in a court of record: $5 where the debt or damages demanded does not exceed $49,999, $15 where the demand exceeds that but not $100,000, and $25 where it exceeds $100,000.

The Virginia Beach Circuit Court's own civil fee schedule shows how a total is built, listing the components charged alongside the clerk fee and writ tax. Those are a $9 legal aid fee, a $5 technology trust fund fee, a $4 law library fee, a $2 courthouse maintenance charge, a $1 indigent defense assessment, and a $10 court technology line on higher-tier filings. That is why a filing total here ends in an odd figure.

### The claims that reach a recovery before the injured person does

Virginia gives medical providers a statutory lien on an injury claim, with dollar ceilings. Under Va. Code 8.01-66.2 a hospital or nursing home lien for a just and reasonable charge cannot exceed $2,500; each physician, nurse, physical therapist or pharmacy is limited to $750; each emergency medical services provider is limited to $200. Those liens do not attach automatically: Va. Code 8.01-66.5 requires written notice to the party alleged to have caused the injury, the injured person, or that person's lawyer.

Getting the records that prove the claim has its own ceilings. Va. Code 8.01-413 allows a provider to charge no more than fifty cents a page for the first fifty pages of paper copies and twenty-five cents after that, with search and handling capped at twenty dollars plus postage. For electronic production the caps are thirty-seven cents and eighteen cents a page, and the total cannot exceed $160.

### One cap that is often misdescribed

Virginia does cap money in medical malpractice cases, and it is not a fee cap. Va. Code 8.01-581.15 limits the total recoverable for injury to or death of a patient on a sliding annual scale: $2.70 million for acts from July 1, 2025 through June 30, 2026, and $2.75 million the following year. It constrains the judgment, not any percentage, and says nothing about a collision on Shore Drive.

## svc_commercial_truck_collisions_lede

A tractor-trailer collision generates records that no passenger-car crash produces, and federal regulations set the clock on how long several of them have to exist. Two retention periods do most of the damage when they are missed: six months and three years.

## svc_commercial_truck_collisions_body

### The six-month clock on the driver's logs

Under 49 CFR 395.8(k)(1) a motor carrier must keep records of duty status and supporting documents for each driver for at least six months from the date of receipt. Under 395.8(k)(2) the driver has to keep a copy of the previous seven consecutive days of records in his possession and available for inspection while on duty. Neither period was written with an injury claim in mind, and neither extends itself because a claim is being investigated.

Six months from a crash is not a long time relative to how long a serious injury takes to stabilize, which is why the timing of a preservation request is a substantive question rather than a clerical one. Virginia Code 8.01-379.2:1 provides that a party or potential litigant has a duty to preserve evidence that may be relevant to reasonably foreseeable litigation, and directs a court to consider the totality of the circumstances in deciding whether and when that duty arose, including notice that specific litigation was likely and that the evidence would be relevant. Where evidence that should have been preserved is lost, altered, concealed or destroyed and cannot be replaced through additional discovery, the court may order measures no greater than necessary to cure the prejudice, and only on a finding that the party acted recklessly or intended to deprive another party of its use may the court presume the evidence was unfavorable, instruct the jury to that effect, or dismiss or enter default. The section expressly does not create a separate cause of action for spoliation.

### The three-year accident register

49 CFR 390.15(b) requires a motor carrier to maintain an accident register for three years after the date of each accident. The register has to list the date, the city or town nearest the accident and the state, the driver's name, the number of injuries, the number of fatalities, and whether hazardous materials other than fuel from the vehicle's own tanks were released. It must also include copies of all accident reports required by state or other governmental entities or by insurers. Subsection (a) separately requires carriers and intermodal equipment providers to make all records and information pertaining to an accident available to authorized federal, state or local representatives within the time the request specifies, and to give reasonable assistance in an investigation.

The register is useful less for the crash under investigation than for what preceded it, because three years of entries describe a pattern that a single report cannot.

### Hours of service is arithmetic, and the arithmetic is checkable

49 CFR 395.3 sets the limits for property-carrying commercial vehicles. A driver may not drive without first taking ten consecutive hours off duty. Driving is prohibited after the fourteenth consecutive hour following the start of the shift. Within that window, total driving time is capped at eleven hours. Driving is also prohibited once more than eight hours of driving time have passed without at least a consecutive thirty-minute interruption, satisfied by off-duty, sleeper-berth or on-duty-not-driving time or a combination, with short-haul exceptions. The weekly ceilings are sixty on-duty hours in any seven consecutive days for a carrier that does not run every day of the week, or seventy in any eight consecutive days for one that does, and either period may restart with an off-duty break of thirty-four or more consecutive hours.

Because those are numbers rather than judgments, electronic logging data, fuel receipts, weigh-station records, dispatch messages and delivery timestamps can be compared against one another. Requirement 395.8(e) prohibits false duty-status reports and prohibits tampering with an electronic logging device or on-board recorder so that it fails to record and retain required data.

### What a truck's physics adds

Virginia Code 46.2-870 caps trucks, tractor trucks and property-carrying combinations at 45 miles per hour on highways outside the interstate and four-lane categories. The stopping-distance tables that Virginia Code 46.2-880 tells courts to notice show a separate and longer truck column: 155 feet total at 35 miles per hour against 135 for an automobile, 313 feet against 265 at 55, and 411 feet against 344 at 65. Those figures raise no presumption and assume a level, dry, debris-free road and a vehicle in good condition, so brake adjustment and load become factual questions rather than assumptions.

### The Virginia data on large trucks

The 2025 Virginia traffic crash facts report counted 2,670 crashes involving large trucks, which was 2.3 percent of all crashes for the year. Of those, 45 were fatal, 1,095 involved injury and 1,530 involved property damage only. Ten large truck occupants were killed, all of them drivers, and 496 were injured, with 55 seriously injured. The distribution is the point: the occupants of the heavier vehicle are not usually the people counted in the injury column.

### Where Virginia's fault rule intersects

None of the federal recordkeeping above changes the doctrine that governs the claim. Virginia does not compare the negligence of the parties, so a carrier's documented hours-of-service problem sits alongside, rather than above, any argument that the injured person was also careless. The contributory negligence page explains that structure, and the crash reports and evidence page covers what the officer's report can and cannot be used for.

The material here is general information about federal motor carrier regulations and Virginia law rather than legal advice; a specific truck collision should be reviewed by the attorney advertising on this site.

## svc_contributory_negligence_in_virginia_lede

Most states divide a crash recovery in proportion to blame, so a driver found ten percent careless collects ninety percent. Virginia does not divide it. Carelessness by the injured person that proximately contributed to the collision generally ends the claim instead of discounting it.

## svc_contributory_negligence_in_virginia_body

### Virginia courts do not weigh the two shares against each other

The Supreme Court of Virginia has put the point about as plainly as it can be put: the negligence of the parties is not compared. Where a plaintiff has been negligent and that negligence proximately contributed to the injury, recovery is barred, a rule the court traced back through decisions such as *Fein v. Wade* when it decided *Litchford v. Hancock* in 1987. Nothing in that framework asks a jury to assign percentages, because percentages have no work to do. The finding is binary.

That is why a Virginia crash file can turn on a detail that would be a rounding error elsewhere. A rolling stop, a burned-out taillight, a glance at a phone, a few miles per hour over the limit — in a percentage state each of those shaves a recovery. Here, if the conduct is found to be careless and to have helped cause the collision, the analysis can stop there. Reading this page is not a substitute for advice from a licensed attorney about a particular collision.

### The plaintiff's carelessness has to be a proximate cause, not merely a flaw

The doctrine is narrower than its reputation. Virginia decisions distinguish negligence that proximately caused the harm from negligence that was only a remote circumstance, and only the former bars a claim. In *Williams v. Harrison* the court explained that a negligent plaintiff may still recover where his own negligence was remote rather than proximate. The plaintiff's carelessness also has to concur with the other driver's rather than sit at some earlier point in the story, a requirement the court drew from cases including *Eiss v. Lillis* and *Lawrence v. Wirth*.

Nor is the burden on the injured person. *Gravitt v. Ward* holds that the defense carries the burden of showing the plaintiff's negligence was a proximate, direct, efficient contributing cause, unless the plaintiff's own evidence discloses it, and that more than a scintilla of evidence is needed before the issue reaches a jury at all. Those two allocations matter as much as the doctrine itself.

### Usually a jury question, occasionally a matter of law

Contributory negligence and proximate cause are ordinarily left to the finder of fact. They become questions of law only where reasonable minds could not differ, which is a demanding standard rather than a routine one. The duties being measured are familiar: a driver must keep a proper lookout and maintain proper control, standards Virginia courts have restated across decades of collision cases.

There is also a limit on the comfortable assumption that other people will behave. Virginia recognizes a right to assume ordinary care from others, but that assumption expires the moment a driver realizes, or should realize, that the other person is not exercising reasonable care. After that point, continuing as though nothing has changed is its own exposure.

### Statutes that pull in the opposite direction

The General Assembly has carved out specific situations where conduct cannot be treated as the injured person's negligence. Nonuse of a safety belt is the clearest: under Virginia Code 46.2-1094(D) a violation is not negligence, cannot be weighed to reduce damages, is not admissible, and cannot be argued by counsel in a case seeking damages from the operation of a vehicle. A pedestrian crossing at an intersection without marked crosswalks by the most direct route is not negligent as a matter of law under Virginia Code 46.2-923. A bicyclist's failure to move into single file when overtaken is expressly not negligence per se in a civil action under Virginia Code 46.2-905. Virginia Code 8.01-58 exists because the legislature had to write contributory negligence out of the way for injured railroad employees.

Each of those carve-outs is narrow and none of them settles the rest of a fault dispute. They are useful mainly as proof that the doctrine is statutory terrain, not folklore.

### Where the doctrine has nothing to say

Contributory negligence cannot exist without primary negligence by the defendant; if the other driver was not careless, there is no claim for the plaintiff's carelessness to defeat. It is also not a defense to an intentional tort. And it operates alongside, not instead of, the last clear chance doctrine, which the courts treat as a separate inquiry with its own elements — the subject of its own page here.

Willful and wanton conduct raises different questions again. Virginia Code 8.01-44.5 lets a finder of fact award punitive damages in a motor vehicle case on proof of malice or conduct showing conscious disregard for the rights of others, and deems the willful-and-wanton element satisfied where the evidence establishes a blood alcohol concentration of 0.15 percent or more together with knowledge of impairment and proximate causation. Virginia Code 8.01-38.1 then caps the total punitive award at $350,000 and keeps the jury from being told about the cap.

### Why the contemporaneous record carries so much weight here

Because a modest finding of carelessness can be decisive rather than costly, the record made in the first days does disproportionate work. The Virginia Department of Motor Vehicles counted 4,965 crashes in Virginia Beach City in its 2025 crash facts report, with 28 people killed and 3,102 injured, and attributed 610 of those crashes to speed. In a jurisdiction with this rule, the difference between a vague recollection and a documented lane position, signal phase, weather note and witness name is the difference between a live dispute and a closed one.

## svc_crash_reports_and_evidence_lede

The officer's crash report is the document everyone asks for and the one Virginia law will not let a jury see. Understanding that split is the difference between using the report correctly and building a claim on something inadmissible.

## svc_crash_reports_and_evidence_body

### The statute says the report is not evidence

Virginia Code 46.2-379 makes officer crash reports confidential, restricts their use to accident-prevention purposes by the Department of Motor Vehicles and state agencies, and provides that such reports are not to be used as evidence in any civil or criminal trial. It permits the Department to disclose a limited set of items: the date, time and location of the crash, and the names and addresses of the drivers, the owners, the injured persons and the witnesses, along with one investigating officer.

That limited disclosure list is the point. The report is a directory of people and a fixed record of time and place. The officer's opinion about who caused the crash, the diagram and the narrative are not a shortcut around proving the case with testimony and physical evidence.

### It is still available, and the window is thirty-six months

Virginia Code 46.2-380(A) requires the Department to keep crash reports for at least thirty-six months and makes them open to inspection by the persons involved in or injured by the crash or their attorneys, by the owners of vehicles or property damaged, by insurers reasonably anticipating exposure, by the Federal Motor Carrier Safety Administration, by personal representatives, and by the parents or guardians of a minor involved, with copies furnished at the requester's expense.

Locally, the Virginia Beach Police Department directs report requests for incidents from December 2022 forward through its Freedom of Information Act office, with an online portal for submissions, and lists 757-385-5000 as the non-emergency number. A request that identifies the date, time, location, driver names and any report number moves faster than one that describes the crash in prose.

### The evidence that actually decides fault is perishable

Physical evidence has a shorter life than the report. Skid and gouge marks weather, debris is swept, vegetation blocking a sight line is trimmed, a damaged vehicle is repaired or sold for salvage, a private camera overwrites its storage on a fixed cycle, and event data recorder contents can be lost with the module. In a state where any carelessness by the injured person can bar the claim, that evidence is often what establishes the sequence rather than merely illustrating it.

Virginia Code 8.01-379.2:1 gives that concern legal shape. A party or potential litigant has a duty to preserve evidence that may be relevant to reasonably foreseeable litigation, and a court deciding whether and when the duty arose considers the totality of the circumstances, including notice that specific and identifiable litigation was likely and that the evidence would be relevant. Where evidence that should have been preserved is lost through a failure to take reasonable steps, or is disposed of, altered, concealed, destroyed or not preserved, and cannot be restored or replaced through additional discovery, the court on a finding of prejudice may order measures no greater than necessary to cure it. Only on a finding that the party acted recklessly or intended to deprive another party of the evidence's use may the court presume the evidence was unfavorable, instruct the jury to presume that, or dismiss the action or enter default. The section does not create a separate cause of action for spoliation.

### Records held by a carrier have federal clocks

Where a commercial vehicle is involved, the retention periods are set federally and they are short. 49 CFR 395.8(k)(1) requires a carrier to keep records of duty status and supporting documents for at least six months from receipt, and 49 CFR 390.15(b) requires an accident register to be maintained for three years after each accident, listing the date, the nearest city or town and state, the driver's name, the number of injuries, the number of fatalities and whether hazardous materials other than the vehicle's own fuel were released, together with copies of accident reports required by governmental entities or insurers. The commercial truck collisions page covers those obligations in more detail.

### Statements, and the right to a copy

Virginia Code 8.01-417(A) requires a person who takes a signed written statement or a voice recording from an injured person about the injury to furnish a copy of the statement promptly, or a verified typed transcription within thirty days after the recording is made once it is transcribed, and in all cases when the injured person or that person's attorney asks. Subsection B provides that a party who subpoenas documents in a civil proceeding must, on written request, furnish true and full copies to another party or that party's attorney, with the requesting party paying the reasonable cost of copying, unless the documents are returnable to and kept by the clerk or the court orders otherwise for good cause.

Subsection C sets up a pre-suit disclosure route. Before filing a motor vehicle personal injury action, an attorney who has given written notice of representation, or an unrepresented injured person, may request in writing that the insurer disclose the liability limits of any applicable policy and the alleged tortfeasor's physical address where it has not been reported. The request must include the accident date, the tortfeasor's name and last known address if reported, a copy of the accident report if any, and the claim number if available.

### Medical records follow a different statute

Virginia Code 8.01-413 requires health records to be furnished within thirty days after a written request from the patient, the patient's attorney, an executor or administrator or an authorized insurer, makes properly authenticated copies admissible in the same manner as originals, and makes the requesting party liable for the provider's reasonable charges for maintaining, retrieving, reviewing, preparing, copying and mailing them.

This page explains how Virginia treats crash reports and evidence in general and offers no legal advice; a specific evidence problem should be raised with the attorney advertising on this site.

## svc_distracted_driving_crashes_lede

Virginia's handheld device statute is short, the fines are specific, and the crash data behind it is substantial: distracted driving accounted for fifteen percent of all Virginia crashes in the most recent state report. What the statute does not do is decide a civil claim.

## svc_distracted_driving_crashes_body

### The handheld device prohibition

Virginia Code 46.2-818.2 makes it unlawful to hold a handheld personal communications device while driving a motor vehicle on the highways of the Commonwealth. A first offense carries a fine of $125, and a second or subsequent offense carries a fine of $250. A violation in a highway work zone carries a mandatory $250 fine. For a first offense, the statute allows completion of a driver improvement clinic in lieu of the fine.

The exceptions are enumerated rather than general. They cover operators of emergency vehicles performing official duties, a driver who is lawfully parked or stopped, use of a device to report an emergency, operators of amateur or citizens band radio, and Department of Transportation incident-management vehicles. The section also directs that distracted driving be included in the driver's license knowledge examination.

An older provision addressing texting, Virginia Code 46.2-1078.1, was repealed effective January 1, 2021, so a citation or a summary that relies on it is describing law that is no longer in force. Virginia Code 46.2-818.2 has itself been amended several times, including in 2020, 2023 and 2026, which is a reason to read the current text rather than an article about it.

### A traffic fine and a civil claim are different questions

A conviction under the handheld statute is not a finding on a civil claim, and the absence of a citation is not a finding either. A civil case still requires proof of negligence and of causation, and in Virginia it also has to survive the defense that the injured person was careless. The contributory negligence page explains why that defense is a bar rather than a discount here.

That said, phone use is provable independently of any citation. Carrier records, device logs, application activity, message timestamps, hands-free pairing records and the position of the device after the crash all speak to it, and each of those has its own retention period. Virginia Code 8.01-379.2:1 sets out the duty to preserve evidence that may be relevant to reasonably foreseeable litigation and the remedies available where such evidence is lost, altered, concealed or destroyed and cannot be replaced through additional discovery, with the more serious sanctions available only on a finding of recklessness or intent to deprive.

### Distraction is broader than a phone

Virginia Code 46.2-816.1 makes it a Class 1 misdemeanor to operate a vehicle in a careless or distracted manner and to cause serious bodily injury or death to a vulnerable road user, a class the section defines to include pedestrians, bicyclists, users of electric personal assistive mobility devices, people in wheelchairs and people on skateboards, scooters or animal-drawn conveyances. Prosecution under that section is mutually exclusive with prosecution for reckless driving under Virginia Code 46.2-852, which addresses driving in a manner endangering life, limb or property. Virginia Code 46.2-861 separately makes it reckless driving to travel at a speed unreasonable for the conditions regardless of the posted limit.

### The state data

The 2025 Virginia traffic crash facts report counted 17,357 crashes involving distracted driving, which was 15.0 percent of all traffic crashes for the year. Of those, 104 were fatal, 7,706 involved injury and 9,547 involved property damage only. The report recorded 116 people killed in those crashes, 14.1 percent of all traffic deaths, made up of 79 drivers, 26 passengers and 11 pedestrians. It counted 12,256 people injured, or 16.5 percent of all traffic injuries, and 1,097 seriously injured, comprising 825 drivers, 205 passengers and 67 pedestrians.

For scale, the same report counted 115,858 crashes statewide with 825 deaths and 74,194 injuries, and 4,965 crashes in Virginia Beach City with 28 people killed and 3,102 injured against 309,904 licensed drivers in the city. The report does not break the distracted driving figures out by jurisdiction, so no Virginia Beach distraction count appears here; that number was left out rather than estimated.

### Where inattention meets the following-distance rule

Distraction usually shows up in a crash file as delayed reaction, which is why the statutory stopping-distance tables are relevant. Virginia Code 46.2-880 directs courts to take notice of those tables while stating that they raise no presumption, and the perception-and-reaction component is built on one and one-half seconds: 77 feet at 35 miles per hour, 121 feet at 55 and 143 feet at 65. A few seconds spent looking away expands that component by a multiple. Virginia Code 46.2-816 separately forbids following at a distance that is not prudent for the two vehicles' speeds and for traffic and highway conditions, and the rear-end collisions page works through how those two provisions interact.

### Building the record

The useful details are the ones that fix time and behavior: the exact time of the collision, the lane and direction, whether the vehicle drifted or braked, what the other driver said at the scene, whether any witness saw a device in use, and whether a business or public camera covered the approach. Virginia Code 8.01-417(A) requires a person who takes a signed statement or a recording from an injured person to provide a copy promptly, or a verified transcription within thirty days once transcribed, which is worth remembering before any recorded interview.

This page describes Virginia's distracted driving statutes and state crash data as general information, not as legal advice about any collision; that discussion belongs with the attorney advertising on this site.

## svc_hit_and_run_and_uninsured_motorist_claims_lede

When the other driver leaves, Virginia does not leave the injured person without a defendant. The uninsured motorist statute allows a suit against an unknown driver under the name John Doe, but it attaches a reporting requirement that a delay can defeat.

## svc_hit_and_run_and_uninsured_motorist_claims_body

### The statutory duty the departing driver ignored

Virginia Code 46.2-894 requires the driver of a vehicle involved in a crash in which a person is injured or killed, or in which an attended vehicle or other property is damaged, to stop as close to the scene as safely possible, report his name, address, driver's license number and vehicle registration number, and render reasonable assistance to any injured person. A violation is a Class 5 felony where injury or death resulted or where property damage exceeded $1,000, and a Class 1 misdemeanor where the damage was $1,000 or less. The criminal exposure is separate from any civil claim and does not resolve one.

### The reporting requirement in a no-contact case

Virginia Code 38.2-2206(D) draws a line that surprises people. Where the owner or operator causing the injury or damage is unknown and the crash involved no contact between that vehicle and the vehicle the insured occupied, or no contact with the insured's person if he was not in a vehicle, recovery under the uninsured motorist endorsement requires that the accident be reported promptly either to the insurer or to a law-enforcement officer with jurisdiction in the county or city where it happened. The statute allows for circumstances by permitting a report as soon as reasonably practicable where prompt reporting was not practicable.

The phantom-vehicle case is exactly the scenario that provision addresses: a driver runs another off the road on Shore Drive or into a barrier on the I-264 approach and never touches the car. In that situation, the call to the police or the carrier is not merely good practice; the statute conditions the coverage on it. The Virginia Beach Police Department lists 757-385-5000 as its non-emergency number and directs report requests for incidents from December 2022 forward through its Freedom of Information Act office.

### Suing a driver nobody can name

Virginia Code 38.2-2206(E) permits an action against an unknown owner or operator as John Doe. Service of process may be made by delivering a copy of the motion for judgment or other pleadings to the clerk of the court where the action is brought, and service on the insurer that issued the policy is made as though the insurer were a party defendant, with Virginia Code 8.01-288 expressly inapplicable to that service. The insurer may file pleadings and take other action allowed by law in the name of John Doe.

Subsection F carries a parallel rule for identified but uninsured or underinsured drivers: an insured who intends to rely on the coverage must serve a copy of the process on the insurer in the same manner. The subsection also disposes of a defense that would otherwise end some claims, providing that where the owner or operator is immune from liability for negligence the immunity does not bar a judgment enforceable against the insurer, is not a defense available to the insurer, and results in judgment entered in the name of "Immune Defendant."

### Property damage has its own floor and its own deductible

The endorsement required by Virginia Code 38.2-2206 must provide at least $20,000 of coverage for damage to or destruction of the insured's property in any one accident. The same section permits the endorsement to exclude the first $200 of loss where the accident involved an unidentifiable owner or operator of an uninsured vehicle. That $200 carve-out applies to the unidentified-driver situation specifically, which is another reason the identification question is worth pursuing rather than conceding.

Whether a vehicle qualifies as uninsured is defined by the statute rather than by intuition, and the definition reaches situations beyond a driver with no policy at all. The underinsured motorist page covers the adjacent problem of a driver whose limits exist but are too low.

### The records that make an unidentified-driver claim provable

A file built on an unknown defendant lives on contemporaneous corroboration. That means the time and place recorded to the lane, the description of the other vehicle including partial plate characters, the names of witnesses, the identity of any nearby business or public camera, and the date of the report to the police or the carrier. Virginia Code 46.2-380 keeps the officer's report open to inspection for at least thirty-six months by people involved in the crash, their attorneys, vehicle owners and insurers reasonably anticipating exposure, while Virginia Code 46.2-379 states that such reports are not to be used as evidence in a civil or criminal trial and limits what the Department may disclose to items such as date, time, location and the names and addresses of drivers, owners, injured persons and witnesses.

Virginia Code 8.01-417(A) requires a person who takes a signed statement or a voice recording from an injured person to furnish a copy promptly, or a verified typed transcription within thirty days once the recording is transcribed. Where a claim is being made under the injured person's own policy, the party taking the statement may be the same company paying the claim, which is a reason to keep a copy of everything said.

### Fault still governs

An uninsured motorist claim is a claim for what the unknown or uninsured driver would have owed, so it carries the same fault analysis. Virginia's refusal to compare the negligence of the parties applies with full force, and the 2025 crash facts report attributed 304 alcohol-related crashes in Virginia Beach City, with 10 deaths and 201 injuries, and 610 speed-related crashes causing 4 deaths and 350 injuries.

This is general background on Virginia's uninsured motorist statute, not legal advice, and it cannot evaluate coverage in any particular case; the attorney advertising on this site is the person to raise it with.

## svc_intersection_and_right_of_way_crashes_lede

Right-of-way disputes are the collisions Virginia's fault rule handles least forgivingly, because both drivers usually had a duty and both usually had a view. Three statutes carry most of the weight, and none of them says whoever entered first wins.

## svc_intersection_and_right_of_way_crashes_body

### Stop and yield signs create a duty that does not end at the sign

Virginia Code 46.2-821 requires a driver approaching an intersection controlled by a stop sign or yield sign to stop or yield as directed before entering, and then to yield the right-of-way to traffic approaching on the intersecting highway from either direction. The second half is the part that generates litigation. A complete stop satisfies the first duty and leaves the second untouched; a driver who stops, looks, and pulls into the path of a vehicle already approaching has complied with the sign and not with the statute.

That structure makes gap judgment the contested fact. Approach speed, sight distance, the presence of a parked vehicle or vegetation at the corner, and whether the approaching vehicle was signaling a turn all bear on it. This page describes the rules generally and cannot evaluate any intersection or any collision.

### Left turns carry the yield

Virginia Code 46.2-825 places the obligation on the driver intending to turn left to yield to oncoming traffic. A green ball is permission to proceed, not permission to turn across a gap that is not there, which is why turn-lane collisions rarely resolve on the color of the light alone. Where a protected arrow is present the analysis changes, and the phase indications become central rather than incidental.

### The signal statute is more detailed than most drivers assume

Virginia Code 46.2-833 defines what each indication requires: steady green, steady red, steady amber, flashing red, flashing amber and arrow indications each carry distinct instructions. The amber provision is the one that decides cases, because it directs a driver to stop before entering the intersection unless stopping cannot be done safely. That is a conditional duty measured against speed and distance at the moment the indication changed, not a grace period.

Signal timing evidence therefore has real value. City Traffic Engineering reviews design, speed limits, signs and signals, and identifies the pedestrian pushbuttons, pedestrian signals and four-section signal heads with arrows that appear across the city's intersections. Cycle length at a busy pushbutton location can run two minutes or more, which is why an estimate of how long a light had been amber is worth checking against the actual phasing.

### When both drivers were arguably careless, Virginia's rule bites

In a percentage state, an intersection case where each driver made a mistake becomes an allocation exercise. In Virginia, a finding that the injured driver was also negligent and that the negligence proximately contributed generally ends the claim, because the Supreme Court of Virginia has held that the negligence of the parties is not compared. Contributory negligence and proximate cause are ordinarily jury questions, becoming questions of law only where reasonable minds could not differ.

Virginia also limits an argument that comes up constantly at intersections. A driver may generally assume that others will use ordinary care, but that assumption ends once the driver realizes, or reasonably should realize, that another person is not exercising reasonable care. A driver who sees a vehicle approaching a stop sign without slowing cannot rely on the sign and then rely on the assumption.

### The evidence that survives, and the evidence that does not

Physical evidence at an intersection disappears quickly. Debris fields get swept, gouge marks fade, a sight-line obstruction gets trimmed and event data recorders are overwritten or the vehicle is scrapped. What tends to persist is the paper: the officer's report, available for inspection for at least thirty-six months under Virginia Code 46.2-380 even though Virginia Code 46.2-379 bars its use as trial evidence, plus any statements taken. Virginia Code 8.01-417(A) requires that an injured person who gives a signed statement or a recording receive a copy promptly, or a verified transcription within thirty days once transcribed.

The city's own safety work is another documented source. Virginia Beach Public Works produced a 2024 Local Road Safety Action Plan built on historical crash data to identify patterns treatable through engineering, and City Council adopted the final plan into the Comprehensive Plan by reference on November 19, 2024. Where a corridor or intersection appears in that analysis, the history is documented rather than anecdotal.

### Pedestrians in the intersection change the statutes involved

Virginia Code 46.2-924 requires drivers to stop for a pedestrian crossing in the driver's lane or an adjacent approaching lane at a marked crosswalk, at the end of a block or at any intersection where the speed limit is not more than 35 miles per hour, prohibits passing a vehicle stopped for that purpose, and makes a violation causing serious bodily injury or death to a lawfully crossing vulnerable road user a Class 1 misdemeanor. Virginia Code 46.2-923 in turn provides that a pedestrian crossing at an intersection without marked crosswalks by the most direct route is not negligent as a matter of law. The page on pedestrian and cyclist collisions develops both provisions.

The 2025 crash facts report counted 1,612 pedestrian crashes statewide, 137 of them fatal, and 138 pedestrians killed, which accounted for 16.7 percent of all Virginia traffic deaths that year. Intersections are where a substantial share of that exposure sits.

## svc_interstate_64_and_highway_collisions_lede

Interstate 64 is the spine of the Hampton Roads network and the reason a Virginia Beach crash file often involves a road that is under reconstruction. Highway collisions bring in the speed statutes, the reckless driving statutes, and a construction record held by the state rather than by either driver.

## svc_interstate_64_and_highway_collisions_body

### The speed limit is statutory before it is posted

Virginia Code 46.2-870 sets 55 miles per hour as the maximum on interstate highways, other limited access highways with divided roadways, non-limited-access highways with four or more lanes, and state primary highways. It then allows 70 miles per hour where the Commonwealth Transportation Board has posted that limit following an engineering study, lists specific United States routes where 60 miles per hour applies, and sets 45 miles per hour for trucks, tractor trucks and property-carrying combinations on highways outside the first category. A collision file that identifies the roadway class establishes the governing limit without needing a photograph of a sign.

Exceeding a limit is not the only speed offense. Virginia Code 46.2-861 makes it reckless driving to travel at a speed unreasonable for the conditions regardless of the posted maximum, which is the provision that reaches fog off the water, standing rain and a stopped queue behind a tunnel backup. Virginia Code 46.2-862 makes it reckless driving to exceed the limit by 20 miles per hour or more, or to drive faster than 85 miles per hour at all. Virginia Code 46.2-852 covers reckless driving generally, in terms of endangering life, limb or property.

### Distance at highway speed is the physical constraint

The statutory tables that Virginia Code 46.2-880 tells courts to notice show why interstate collisions differ in kind rather than degree. At 65 miles per hour the table gives 201 feet of automobile braking distance, 143 feet of perception-and-reaction distance, and a 344-foot total, with 411 feet for a truck. The section states that the tables raise no presumption and assumes a level, dry, debris-free surface and a vehicle in good condition, so wet pavement, a curve or a worn tire moves the real figure. In a work zone with a narrowed shoulder, the room to absorb that distance may not exist.

### The corridor is a construction record, and it is documented

The Hampton Roads Bridge-Tunnel Expansion is the largest highway project in Virginia's history at $3.9 billion, and it covers Interstate 64 between Mallory Street in Hampton and Patrol Road in Norfolk. The scope adds a third travel lane and a drivable shoulder in each direction and builds new twin two-lane bored tunnels, bringing capacity across the water to eight lanes. The Hampton Roads Transportation Accountability Commission is the primary funding agent through regional sales and gas tax collections, with $200 million from the SMART SCALE program and $108 million from the Virginia Department of Transportation.

South of the water, the I-64 Southside Widening and High Rise Bridge Project widened nearly nine miles of Interstate 64 to three full-time travel lanes in each direction, added one express lane between Bowers Hill and Battlefield Boulevard, and built a fixed-span bridge with 100 feet of vertical clearance over the Elizabeth River. The bridge opened to traffic in July 2022, the express lanes opened in February 2024, and VDOT records the project as complete in fall 2024 at an estimated $409.6 million. Lane closures on the corridor are published in the district weekly lane closure report.

Those records matter for a specific reason. A crash description that says which lane, which direction, which taper, which barrier configuration and which date can be matched against the published closure record for that week. A description that says only "on I-64" cannot.

### Work zones carry their own statutory consequences

Virginia Code 46.2-818.2 makes holding a handheld personal communications device while driving unlawful, and sets a mandatory $250 fine for a violation in a highway work zone, above the $125 first-offense fine that applies elsewhere. Work zone geometry also concentrates the very factors that widen stopping distance: temporary striping, shifted lanes, reduced shoulders, and the abrupt speed differentials created when a through lane ends.

### Highway crashes involve state parties and different deadlines

Where a claim runs against the Commonwealth or a transportation district rather than a private driver, the timing changes completely. Virginia Code 8.01-195.6 calls for a written statement describing the claim, fixing when and where the injury happened and naming the responsible agency, filed inside one year of accrual, and directs it either to the Attorney General or to the Division of Risk Management's Director, or instead to the Commissioner of Highways in a matter involving the Department of Transportation. Virginia Code 8.01-195.7 then bars the claim unless suit is brought within eighteen months of filing that notice or within two years after accrual, and allows the action to begin on denial or after six months from filing. Those are shorter and stricter than the ordinary rule described on the statute of limitations page.

### Scale, and what the numbers do not settle

Statewide, the 2025 Virginia traffic crash facts report counted 115,858 crashes, 825 deaths and 74,194 injuries, against 6,080,177 licensed drivers and an estimated 89,098 million vehicle miles, producing a death rate of 0.93 per 100 million miles. Virginia Beach City accounted for 4,965 of those crashes with 28 deaths and 3,102 injuries. Aggregate figures describe exposure across a year; they say nothing about responsibility in one collision, and Virginia's contributory negligence rule means that responsibility question can be decisive rather than proportional.

## svc_last_clear_chance_doctrine_lede

Last clear chance is the exception Virginia keeps for a plaintiff who was careless and still reaches a jury. It is not a general fairness valve. Virginia decisions define two categories of plaintiff, assign the injured person the burden on every element, and refuse the instruction when the proof falls short.

## svc_last_clear_chance_doctrine_body

### The doctrine survives a plaintiff's ongoing carelessness

For years the argument was made that a plaintiff whose negligence continued right up to impact could never invoke last clear chance. The Supreme Court of Virginia addressed that in *Coutlakis v. CSX Transportation*, holding that the continuing character of the plaintiff's negligence does not by itself defeat the doctrine. Once contributory negligence is established, the question becomes whether the defendant was also negligent and which of the two had the last clear chance to avoid the harm.

That is a real doctrine with real limits. The court has also been explicit that the plaintiff bears the burden of producing evidence on each element before an instruction is given at all. A theory offered in argument is not a substitute for testimony, sight-distance measurements or physical evidence about what the other party could have done and when.

### Two categories, and the difference between them decides cases

Virginia has sorted plaintiffs into two groups since *Greear v. Noland Company* in 1955. The helpless plaintiff is one who has negligently placed himself in a position of peril from which he is physically unable to remove himself. For that plaintiff, the other party is answerable if he saw, or should have seen, the plaintiff's situation in time to avert the harm by reasonable care.

The inattentive plaintiff is different. He is physically able to get out of the way but is unconscious of the danger. For that plaintiff, liability arises only if the other party actually saw him and realized, or reasonably should have realized, his peril in time to avoid it. The words carry weight: constructive notice is enough in the first category and not in the second. Which category a case falls into is frequently the whole argument.

### Helplessness means physical incapacity

Because the helpless category is more generous, Virginia courts police its boundary. *Vanlandingham*, decided in 1972, ties helplessness to physical incapacity rather than to distraction or misjudgment. In *Pack v. Doe* the court declined to treat a plaintiff as helpless where the incapacity arose from voluntary intoxication. A person who could have stepped back, braked or looked up is generally an inattentive plaintiff, with the stricter actual-sight requirement that follows.

### Time to avert is a separate element

Even where the category is settled and the other party's awareness is proved, the doctrine still asks whether there was a clear opportunity to prevent the harm through reasonable care. Perception, reaction and braking all consume distance. Virginia Code 46.2-880 directs courts to take notice of statutory speed and stopping-distance tables — the table gives figures such as 265 feet of total stopping distance for an automobile at 55 miles per hour and 313 feet for a truck — while stating that those figures raise no presumption and assuming a level, dry, debris-free road and a vehicle in good condition. The table is a reference point for argument, not an answer.

There is a matching restriction on the other side. Virginia courts have held that where the opportunity to avoid the collision was as available to the plaintiff as to the defendant, the plaintiff's negligence remains a proximate cause and bars recovery, a rule drawn from decisions including *Cook v. Shoulder*. Last clear chance requires that the chance actually have been the defendant's, and the last one.

### It does not displace the general rule

*Williams v. Harrison* states directly that the doctrine does not supersede contributory negligence. It is an exception applied on proof of its elements by a preponderance of the evidence, not a substitute framework. Related doctrine follows the same shape: contributory negligence is no defense to an intentional tort, and where the record shows willful and wanton conduct the question of that conduct has been left to the jury, as the court noted in *Coutlakis* by reference to *Huffman v. Love*.

### The evidence that decides these cases is perishable

Because the doctrine turns on what the other party saw or should have seen and when, the useful proof is about sight lines and timing rather than about damage. Lighting conditions, the height of a median plant, the position of a parked truck, the phase of a signal, the presence of a curve, the location of the first skid mark and the content of any statement taken from the other driver all speak to that question. Virginia Code 8.01-417(A) requires that a person who takes a signed statement or recording from an injured person furnish a copy promptly, or a verified transcription within thirty days once a recording is transcribed.

Pedestrian cases raise these issues most often. Statewide, the 2025 Virginia crash facts report counted 1,612 pedestrian crashes, 138 pedestrians killed and 1,554 injured, with 440 seriously injured, and the report breaks those figures out by lighting condition. On Shore Drive, Atlantic Avenue or Virginia Beach Boulevard after dark, the lighting question is not a technicality.

This page describes Virginia doctrine in general terms and gives no legal advice about any claim; an individual case should be discussed with the attorney advertising here.

## svc_medical_bills_and_health_care_liens_lede

Virginia caps what most providers can assert against a personal injury recovery, and the ceilings are lower than the bills. It also forbids health insurers from taking a personal injury recovery through subrogation, which is a rule several other states do not have.

## svc_medical_bills_and_health_care_liens_body

### The statutory lien ceilings

Virginia Code 8.01-66.2 sets the maximum amounts a provider may claim as a lien against a recovery. A hospital or nursing home is limited to $2,500. Each physician, nurse, physical therapist or pharmacy is limited to $750. Each emergency medical services provider or agency is limited to $200. Those figures are ceilings on the lien, not on the bill, so a five-figure hospital charge does not translate into a five-figure lien under this section.

Virginia Code 8.01-66.3 then subordinates those liens to the injured person's attorney's claim against the recovery. The ordering is statutory rather than negotiable, and it changes what a distribution looks like.

### Notice is a condition, not a courtesy

Virginia Code 8.01-66.5 requires written notice of the lien before a lien under Virginia Code 8.01-66.2 or 8.01-66.9 is created. A provider that never gives the required notice has not perfected the lien the statute would otherwise allow. That single requirement resolves a meaningful share of the lien disputes that arise at the end of a claim, which is why the correspondence file should keep every notice with its date.

The same section carries specific timing for the Commonwealth's medical assistance program. Notice to the Department of Medical Assistance Services must be given within sixty days of filing suit or within twenty-one days of learning of the department's interest, and the department is to respond within sixty days.

### The Commonwealth's lien works differently

Virginia Code 8.01-66.9 gives the Commonwealth a lien for the total amount it paid on behalf of the injured person, and that lien is not subject to the ceilings in Virginia Code 8.01-66.2. It is inferior to attorney fees and costs but superior to the other liens in the chapter, and the court may reduce and apportion it. A file with Medicaid involvement therefore has a different arithmetic than one without, and the reduction and apportionment authority is a reason for the court to see the numbers rather than for the parties to assume them.

### Health insurers cannot take the recovery

Virginia Code 38.2-3405 prohibits a health or medical insurance policy, subscription contract or plan from containing a provision that subrogates the insurer to the insured's recovery for personal injury, and bars requiring the insured to agree to pay back benefits from such a recovery. The section carves out workers' compensation and federal or state programs. That rule removes an entire category of claim against a settlement that would exist in many other states, and it is worth reading the plan documents against rather than assuming a demand letter is enforceable.

Explanations of what a particular plan may or may not recover are legal questions about that plan, and this page does not answer them.

### First-party benefits, where they were purchased

Virginia Code 38.2-2202 requires an auto policy to carry boldface notice of the availability of medical expense benefits up to $2,000 per person for expenses incurred within three years of the accident, and income loss benefits up to $100 per week for up to one year. Those coverages are optional and modest, and whether they were bought is answered by the declarations page rather than by memory. Where they exist, they pay without regard to which driver was at fault, which can matter early while liability is unresolved.

### Getting the bills and records in a usable form

Virginia Code 8.01-413 requires health records to be furnished within thirty days after a written request from the patient, the patient's attorney, an executor or administrator, or an authorized insurer. It makes properly authenticated copies and printouts admissible in the same manner as originals, permits a subpoenaed provider to comply by mailing authenticated copies to the clerk, and makes the requesting party liable for the provider's reasonable charges for maintaining, retrieving, reviewing, preparing, copying and mailing what is produced. Itemized statements are more useful than balance summaries, because a lien analysis depends on which category of provider rendered which service.

### Why the fault rule reaches the bills too

A recovery is what liens attach to, so Virginia's contributory negligence doctrine sits upstream of every calculation on this page. Where the injured person's own carelessness proximately contributed to the collision, the claim can be barred entirely rather than reduced, and there is then nothing for a lien to reach. The contributory negligence page explains that doctrine and the last clear chance page covers the exception.

Settlement structure matters as well. Virginia Code 8.01-35.1 provides that a good-faith release given to one of several persons liable for the same injury does not discharge the others unless it says so, reduces the claim against the others by the stipulated amount or the consideration paid, whichever is greater, keeps the release out of evidence while allowing the court to consider it, and discharges the released party from contribution liability. The underinsured motorist page covers how that reduction interacts with a later claim under the injured person's own policy.

The above is general information about Virginia's lien and subrogation statutes, not legal advice about any bill, lien or settlement; those questions belong with the attorney advertising on this site.

## svc_pedestrian_and_cyclist_collisions_lede

Virginia writes specific protections for people on foot and on bicycles into the traffic code, and two of them are written as limits on the contributory negligence defense. In a state where any carelessness by the injured person can end a claim, those provisions do unusual work.

## svc_pedestrian_and_cyclist_collisions_body

### The driver's duty to stop at a crossing

Virginia Code 46.2-924 requires the driver of a vehicle to stop and yield the right-of-way to a pedestrian crossing a highway at a clearly marked crosswalk, at a regular pedestrian crossing at the end of a block, or at any intersection where the speed limit is not more than 35 miles per hour, where the pedestrian is crossing in the driver's lane or in an adjacent lane approaching the driver's vehicle. It prohibits passing a vehicle stopped for that purpose. A violation causing serious bodily injury or death to a vulnerable road user who is crossing lawfully is a Class 1 misdemeanor. The section also states that pedestrians are not to enter or cross an intersection in disregard of approaching traffic, and gives the pedestrian the right-of-way where a driver is turning.

The 35 mile-per-hour threshold matters in Virginia Beach, where much of the resort area, the Atlantic Avenue grid and residential collectors sit at or below that limit while the arterials that intersect them do not.

### Two provisions that limit the fault defense

Virginia Code 46.2-923 directs pedestrians not to interfere carelessly or maliciously with the orderly passage of vehicles and to cross at intersections or marked crosswalks where possible, but it then provides that at intersections without marked crosswalks a pedestrian is not negligent as a matter of law when crossing by the most direct route. The section also bars an officer from stopping a pedestrian for a violation of it and makes evidence discovered or obtained through such a stop inadmissible.

Virginia Code 46.2-905 requires a bicyclist to ride as close as safely practicable to the right curb or edge, subject to five listed exceptions, and defines a substandard width lane as one too narrow to share safely side by side. It permits riding two abreast and requires moving to single file when overtaken, and then states expressly that a failure to move into single file is not negligence per se in any civil action. In a jurisdiction that does not apportion fault, an express statement that a specific act is not negligence per se is a meaningful shield rather than a technicality.

### The three-foot passing rule

Virginia Code 46.2-839 requires the driver of a vehicle overtaking a bicycle, moped or electric personal assistive mobility device to pass at a reasonable speed and at least three feet to the left, and to change lanes where the lane is too narrow to do that. The rule makes lane position and lane width measurable facts rather than impressions, which is why the width of the travel lane and the presence or absence of a bicycle lane belong in the description of a collision.

### The vulnerable road user statute

Virginia Code 46.2-816.1 makes it a Class 1 misdemeanor to operate a vehicle in a careless or distracted manner and to cause serious bodily injury or death to a vulnerable road user. The section defines that class to include pedestrians, bicyclists, users of electric personal assistive mobility devices, people in wheelchairs, and people on skateboards, scooters or animal-drawn conveyances. Prosecution under that section and prosecution for reckless driving under Virginia Code 46.2-852 are mutually exclusive.

### What the Virginia data shows

The 2025 Virginia traffic crash facts report counted 1,612 pedestrian crashes, which was 1.4 percent of all traffic crashes, of which 137 were fatal and 1,475 involved injury. It recorded 138 pedestrians killed, amounting to 16.7 percent of all traffic deaths for the year, along with 1,554 pedestrians injured and 440 seriously injured. Bicycle figures were smaller and similarly skewed: 627 crashes, 15 of them fatal and 593 involving injury, with 15 riders killed, 600 injured and 129 seriously injured.

The pattern in both sets is the same. People outside a vehicle make up a small share of crashes and a much larger share of deaths, which is the arithmetic behind statutes written specifically for them. The report also breaks pedestrian outcomes out by lighting condition and by pedestrian action, which is a reason a collision description should record whether it was dark, whether the street was lit, and where the person was in the crossing.

### Local context and the records that matter

Virginia Beach Public Works produced a 2024 Local Road Safety Action Plan built on historical crash data to find patterns treatable through engineering work and strategic initiatives, and City Council adopted the final plan into the Comprehensive Plan by reference on November 19, 2024. City Traffic Engineering also states plainly that crosswalks are not safety devices, and that they direct pedestrians along the safest route rather than stopping traffic. Pushbutton intersections can take two minutes or more to cycle, and waits over three minutes can be reported by service request or at 757-385-3111.

For a claim, the useful record is specific: the intersection or block, the direction of travel, the lane, the posted limit, whether the crosswalk was marked, the lighting, the signal indication and the pedestrian or bicycle signal phase. Virginia Code 46.2-380 keeps the officer's report open to inspection for at least thirty-six months, though Virginia Code 46.2-379 provides that such reports are not to be used as trial evidence, a distinction the crash reports and evidence page develops.

Fault analysis still governs the outcome. The contributory negligence page explains the general rule, and the last clear chance page covers the exception that pedestrian cases raise more often than any other category.

## svc_rear_end_collisions_lede

Virginia has no car-length rule and no statute that makes the trailing driver automatically responsible. What it has is a following-distance standard written in terms of what is reasonable, and a stopping-distance table courts may notice without treating as proof.

## svc_rear_end_collisions_body

### The statute sets a judgment standard, not a measurement

Under Virginia Code 46.2-816 a driver may not follow more closely than is prudent and reasonable, judged against how fast each vehicle is moving and against the traffic and the condition of that particular highway. There is no number in it. A distance that is prudent at 25 miles per hour on a dry Kempsville side street is not prudent at 55 on a wet stretch of Independence Boulevard behind a loaded truck, and the statute is written to take that variation into account rather than to freeze one figure.

The practical consequence is that a rear impact opens a factual inquiry instead of closing one. Speed, sight distance, road surface, gradient, the load and braking capacity of both vehicles, the sequence of brake applications and whether the lead vehicle was slowing, stopping, turning, merging or reversing all feed the same question. General explanation cannot answer that question for a particular collision, and nothing here should be read as advice about one.

### The stopping-distance table is a reference, and the statute says so

Virginia Code 46.2-880 directs every court to take notice of statutory tables of speed and stopping distance, then states that those tables raise no presumption. The distinction is important because the numbers look authoritative. At 35 miles per hour the table gives 58 feet of automobile braking distance, 77 feet of perception-and-reaction distance based on one and one-half seconds, and 135 feet in total, rising to 155 feet for a truck. At 55 miles per hour the figures become 144 feet of braking, 121 feet of reaction and 265 feet total, or 313 feet for a truck. At 65 miles per hour the total reaches 344 feet, and 411 feet for a truck.

The table also carries assumptions that a real Virginia Beach roadway may not satisfy: a vehicle in good condition and a level, dry highway free of loose material. Rain off the Atlantic, sand tracked from Sandbridge, standing water on a flat road and a worn brake system each push the actual distance away from the printed one. That is an argument for measurement, not for citing the table as though it settled the matter.

### Why the position of the vehicles is not the end of it in Virginia

In a state that reduces recovery by percentage, an argument that the lead driver contributed something is a discount negotiation. In Virginia it is a defense. So the question of whether the lead vehicle stopped without cause, changed lanes late, drove with inoperative brake lamps or traveled so slowly as to impede the normal and reasonable movement of traffic, which Virginia Code 46.2-877 addresses, is not a side issue. It is the issue the defense will develop.

That asymmetry also shapes what the trailing driver is asked. Following distance, phone use, cruise control, glare, the last time brakes were serviced and how long the lead vehicle's lamps had been visible are all fair territory. The page on contributory negligence explains why a single admission on any of those points carries more weight here than in most states.

### The Virginia Beach traffic picture behind the statistics

The 2025 Virginia traffic crash facts report from the Department of Motor Vehicles recorded 4,965 crashes in Virginia Beach City against 309,904 licensed drivers, with 610 of those crashes speed-related, producing 4 deaths and 350 injuries. Speed is the single most common contributing factor listed for the city after the raw crash count itself, and speed is exactly the variable that stretches the distance figures above.

City Traffic Engineering describes the criteria it uses to set limits and signal timing, including roadway classification, accident history, vehicle speed, vehicle volume, pedestrian and bicycle traffic, sidewalks, curves, driveways and traffic control. It also notes that a busy signalized intersection with a pedestrian pushbutton can take two minutes or more to complete a cycle, and asks that waits over three minutes be reported through a service request or by calling 757-385-3111. Cycle length matters in a queue-and-release corridor, because the platoon behavior that produces chain impacts is a function of it.

### Injury records and one statutory protection

Soft-tissue complaints often surface hours or days after a low-speed impact, and the page on whiplash and soft-tissue injuries covers the documentation problem in detail. The short version is that gaps in treatment records are argued as evidence about severity, so the dates of every visit, the discharge instructions, the imaging and the work restrictions belong in one place from the beginning.

One statute limits what can be made of the occupant's own conduct. Virginia Code 46.2-1094(D) provides that a safety-belt violation is not negligence, cannot be considered in mitigation of damages, is not admissible in evidence, and cannot be commented on by counsel in an action for damages arising from the operation of a motor vehicle. The same section sets a $25 civil penalty with no demerit points and bars a stop made solely for that violation.

### The police report is not the proof

Virginia Code 46.2-379 makes officer crash reports confidential, restricts their use to accident-prevention work by the Department and state agencies, and states that they are not to be used as evidence in a civil or criminal trial. Virginia Code 46.2-380 nevertheless keeps those reports available for inspection for at least thirty-six months by the people involved, their attorneys, vehicle owners, insurers with anticipated exposure and personal representatives. The report is a research tool and a source of names, not a substitute for testimony, and the page on crash reports and evidence works through the distinction.

## svc_spinal_injury_claims_lede

A spinal claim differs from a soft-tissue claim in what has to be proved about the future. Virginia gives courts a statutory life-expectancy table and a specific route for expert testimony, and both become central once permanence is in issue.

## svc_spinal_injury_claims_body

### Permanence changes the structure of the claim

A herniation, fracture, fusion or cord injury raises questions a strain does not: whether the condition is permanent, whether further surgery is likely, what the restrictions will be, and what the person will be able to earn. Each of those is a projection, and a projection requires a method rather than an assertion. The imaging, the operative reports, the treating physicians' opinions and the functional testing become the raw material for that method.

Pre-existing degeneration is the standard counterargument, particularly for adults with prior imaging. Records that pre-date the collision are usually the most useful evidence on that point, in either direction. This page explains the framework and cannot evaluate any diagnosis.

### The life-expectancy table is statutory

Virginia Code 8.01-419 provides a table of average life expectancy to be received in all courts as evidence, along with other evidence, of the life expectancy of a person of a given age. The table gives figures such as 77.4 years at birth for both sexes combined, 44.1 remaining years at age 35, and 18.4 remaining years at age 65. Because the table is statutory it does not have to be established through a witness, which removes one dispute from a case where permanence is at issue.

The figure is an average. It sets a reference for the period over which a permanent restriction operates, not a finding about a particular person's health.

### Expert proof runs through Virginia Code 8.01-401.1

The same section that governs expert testimony generally does specific work in a spinal case. An expert may testify and draw inferences from facts, circumstances or data made known at or before trial, and the underlying material need not be independently admissible if it is of a type normally relied on in that field, which is how imaging interpretations, therapy notes and diagnostic testing performed by others enter an opinion. The expert may state an opinion without first disclosing the underlying facts unless the court requires disclosure, and may be required to disclose them on cross-examination.

The treatise provision matters where surgical outcomes or the natural history of a disc injury is disputed. Statements in published treatises, periodicals or pamphlets on medicine may be read into evidence, but not received as exhibits, where the publication is established as reliable authority and the statements are relied on during direct examination or raised on cross. Where a party intends to introduce them through an expert on direct, copies of the specific statements must be designated and provided to opposing parties thirty days before trial unless the court orders otherwise.

### Physician testimony has a stated ceiling

Virginia Code 8.01-399 permits disclosure of privileged physician-patient communications where the patient's condition is at issue, through discovery or trial testimony, and limits the admissible opinion to a diagnosis stated to a reasonable degree of medical probability. The section also provides that no order may compel a party to sign a release for records held by a provider located in Virginia. In a spinal case the phrase "reasonable degree of medical probability" is not decoration; it is the standard a treating surgeon's causation opinion has to meet.

### Getting the records, and paying for them

Virginia Code 8.01-413 requires health records to be furnished within thirty days after a written request from the patient, the patient's attorney, an executor or administrator, or an authorized insurer, and makes properly authenticated copies admissible in the same manner as originals. The requesting party is liable for the provider's reasonable charges for maintaining, retrieving, reviewing, preparing, copying and mailing the material produced. A spinal file typically spans an emergency department, an imaging provider, a surgeon, an anesthesia group, a hospital, a therapy practice and possibly a pain management clinic, so the thirty-day clock runs several times over and the copying charges accumulate.

### Bills, liens and the amount that actually arrives

Spinal treatment produces the largest bills in a car crash file, and Virginia limits what providers may claim against a recovery. Virginia Code 8.01-66.2 caps a hospital or nursing home lien at $2,500, each physician, nurse, physical therapist or pharmacy at $750, and each emergency medical services provider or agency at $200. Virginia Code 8.01-66.3 makes those liens inferior to the injured person's attorney's claim, and Virginia Code 8.01-66.5 requires written notice before such a lien is created. The medical bills and health care liens page works through the sequence in more detail.

### Settlements for someone who cannot consent

Where the injured person is a minor or otherwise under a disability, Virginia Code 8.01-424 requires court involvement. The court where an action is pending may approve a compromise, including claims under a liability policy, if it determines the compromise is in that person's interest, and an approving order binds the person except where set aside for fraud. Where no action is pending, the motion may be made to any circuit court, with reasonable notice to all parties and anyone the court finds interested. The court must then direct payment of the proceeds by one of the statutory routes, which include payment into court or to the general receiver, payment to a qualified fiduciary after inquiry into the adequacy of the bond, or, for a minor, investment in a college savings trust account under restrictions the statute specifies.

Virginia's contributory negligence rule applies to a catastrophic claim exactly as it applies to a modest one, which is the subject of its own page here.

## svc_statute_of_limitations_virginia_lede

Two years is the number most people have heard, and it is the right number for an ordinary personal injury action. It is also the wrong number in several situations that arise in Virginia Beach, because a claim touching a city or the Commonwealth carries a notice deadline that runs first.

## svc_statute_of_limitations_virginia_body

### The ordinary rule

Virginia Code 8.01-243(A) requires an action for personal injuries, whatever the theory of recovery, to be brought within two years after the cause of action accrues. Subsection B sets five years for damage to property, which is why a vehicle claim and an injury claim from the same collision do not necessarily expire together. Subsection C extends certain medical malpractice periods and sets a ten-year outer limit for those actions.

Accrual is a legal determination rather than a calendar entry, and the date a person first felt a symptom is not automatically the date the clock started. This page describes the framework generally and cannot fix a deadline in any particular matter.

### Death changes the section and the starting point

Virginia Code 8.01-244(B) requires a wrongful death action to be brought within two years after the death, and the action is brought by the decedent's personal representative rather than by family members individually. The two-year period therefore runs from the death rather than from the collision, which can be a different date entirely where a person survives an injury for a period.

### Tolling exists, and it is statutory

Virginia Code 8.01-229 addresses the circumstances in which the running of a limitation period is suspended, including infancy and incapacity, and it addresses the effect of the death of a party. Those provisions are specific, and they are not a general fairness doctrine. Where a claim involves a minor or a person whose capacity was affected by the injury, the interaction between the tolling provisions and the two-year rule is a question worth raising early rather than after the ordinary date has passed. The brain injury page notes the same interaction.

### A claim against the city runs on six months

Virginia Code 15.2-209(A) bars a claim for negligence against a city or county unless the claimant files a written statement of the nature of the claim, including the time and the place at which the injury is alleged to have occurred, within six months after the cause of action accrues. The statement is filed with the city or county attorney, or with the chief executive or mayor where there is no such attorney, and the claimant carries the burden of proving that the statement was received.

Six months is less than a third of the ordinary period, and the proof-of-receipt requirement means the method of delivery matters. A collision involving a municipal vehicle, a signal or a roadway condition can put that section in play before anyone has finished treatment.

### A claim against the Commonwealth runs on one year, then eighteen months

For a claim against the Commonwealth or a transportation district, Virginia Code 8.01-195.6 calls for a written statement describing the claim, identifying when and where the injury happened and naming the agency said to be responsible, filed inside one year of accrual. The filing goes to the Attorney General or to the Division of Risk Management's Director, and instead to the Commissioner of Highways in a matter naming the Department of Transportation. Failure to file does not bar the claim where the required official or entity had actual knowledge of the claim, the time and place of the injury and the agency alleged to be liable within the deadline. Notice is deemed filed when received in the office to which it is directed.

Virginia Code 8.01-195.7 then bars the claim unless the action is commenced within eighteen months of filing the notice, or within two years after accrual. The action may be brought on denial of the claim by the Attorney General or the Director of the Division of Risk Management, or on the expiration of six months from the filing of the notice unless the claim has been compromised in the meantime. Both periods are subject to the tolling provisions of Virginia Code 8.01-229. Because Interstate 64 and much of the arterial network in the region is state right-of-way, this is not an exotic path; the highway collisions page covers the corridor itself.

### Filing in the wrong forum is a different problem

Virginia Code 16.1-77 caps the general district court's civil jurisdiction at $50,000 exclusive of interest, and the Virginia Beach Circuit Court states a goal of concluding civil cases within eighteen months of filing, except in exceptional cases brought to its attention. The circuit court also requires a pretrial conference in all civil jury trials under Rules 1:19 and 4:13 of the Rules of the Supreme Court of Virginia, requires a completed civil cover sheet with the initial pleading, and requires counsel to file a praecipe for the setting of a trial date or to contact the court to set an agreed date.

### The nonsuit is not an extension

Virginia Code 8.01-380 allows a party to take a nonsuit as to a cause of action or party, but only before a motion to strike the evidence has been sustained, before the jury retires from the bar, or before the action has been submitted to the court for decision. Only one nonsuit is available as a matter of right against the same party, and additional nonsuits require reasonable notice to counsel of record, an attempt to notify unrepresented parties, or a stipulation. A party taking a nonsuit must tell the court about any prior nonsuit of that cause of action, and the order must reflect each prior nonsuit with its date and court. Where notice of a nonsuit of right is given within seven days of trial or during trial, the court may assess reasonable expert witness fees and travel costs actually incurred by the opposing party because earlier notice was not given.

Deadlines are the one part of a claim that cannot be repaired later, and this page is general information rather than legal advice about any date; a specific date should be confirmed with the attorney advertising on this site.

## svc_traumatic_brain_injury_claims_lede

A brain injury claim runs into a proof problem and a capacity problem at the same time. Virginia's tolling statute, its rules on compromises for a person under a disability, and its limit on what a physician may state as a diagnosis all become operative once cognition is affected.

## svc_traumatic_brain_injury_claims_body

### The injury that is not on the imaging

A concussion or mild traumatic brain injury frequently produces normal computed tomography results, so the record is assembled from observation instead of from a picture. Emergency department notes about disorientation or amnesia, statements from people who saw the person before and after, neuropsychological testing, symptom inventories, return-to-work records, missed appointments and changes in driving or medication habits carry the description. Where imaging does show hemorrhage or contusion, the dispute usually shifts to the extent of lasting deficit rather than to whether an injury occurred.

The defense argument in these cases is rarely that nothing happened. It is that the deficits are attributable to something else, or that they have resolved. That is why the documented timeline matters more than any single test result. This page is general explanation and cannot assess an individual injury.

### What a physician is permitted to state

Virginia Code 8.01-399 keeps physician-patient communications privileged, allows disclosure where the patient's physical or mental condition is at issue through discovery or trial testimony, and limits the admissible opinion to a diagnosis stated to a reasonable degree of medical probability. The section also provides that no order may compel a party to sign a release for the records of a provider located in Virginia. In a brain injury case the mental-condition trigger is squarely engaged, so the scope of what becomes discoverable is broader than in an orthopedic case, including records that discuss mood, cognition, medication and prior treatment.

That breadth is a reason to think about authorizations carefully rather than to sign whatever arrives. Nothing here says what should be signed in any matter.

### Expert testimony carries the causation link

Virginia Code 8.01-401.1 allows an expert to testify from facts, circumstances or data made known at or before trial and permits reliance on material that would not itself be admissible where it is of a type normally relied upon in the field. Neuropsychological test batteries, vestibular assessments and imaging read by a different physician all reach the opinion by that route. The section also allows statements from a published treatise, periodical or pamphlet established as reliable authority to be read into evidence but not received as an exhibit, and requires copies of statements to be introduced through an expert on direct examination to be designated and provided to opposing parties thirty days before trial unless the court directs otherwise.

### Capacity changes the deadline

Virginia Code 8.01-243(A) generally requires an action for personal injury to be brought within two years after the cause of action accrues. Virginia Code 8.01-229 addresses what happens when the injured person cannot manage the claim, providing for tolling in cases of infancy and incapacity and addressing the effect of the death of a party. Where a brain injury impairs the capacity to bring suit, the interaction of those two sections is a legal question with consequences that cannot be reconstructed later, and it is the reason a date should be identified early rather than assumed.

The statute of limitations page sets out the ordinary two-year rule together with the separate notice deadlines that apply where a city or the Commonwealth is involved, which are considerably shorter.

### Settling a claim for someone who cannot evaluate it

Where the injured person is a minor or otherwise under a disability, Virginia Code 8.01-424 requires the court to approve any compromise, including claims under a liability insurance policy, and to find that the compromise is in the interest of the person under the disability. An order approving the compromise binds that person except that it may be set aside for fraud. If an action is pending the motion goes to the court where it is pending; if not, it may be made to any circuit court, with reasonable notice to all parties and to anyone the court finds interested in the compromise.

The statute then controls where the money goes. The court must direct payment into court or to the general receiver, or to a qualified fiduciary after inquiry into the adequacy of the bond, or, for a minor, into a college savings trust account restricted to target enrollment portfolios with the parent as custodian and with court approval required before any transaction other than a distribution for qualified higher education expenses. Structured payments over time are addressed as well. These are protective mechanisms, and they take time, which is a scheduling fact rather than an obstacle.

### The Virginia data on serious injury

The 2025 Virginia traffic crash facts report counted 74,194 injuries statewide from 115,858 crashes, and reported serious injuries as a separate category throughout. Distracted driving alone accounted for 1,097 seriously injured persons that year, 825 of them drivers, 205 passengers and 67 pedestrians. Virginia Beach City recorded 4,965 crashes with 3,102 people injured and 28 killed. Those totals do not identify head injuries specifically, and no figure in this paragraph describes any individual case.

### Fault remains the threshold question

Virginia's contributory negligence doctrine applies without adjustment for the severity of the injury. Carelessness by the injured person that proximately contributed to the collision generally bars the claim rather than reducing it, and the narrow last clear chance exception has its own elements and its own page. A brain injury can also make the injured person a poor historian about the moments before impact, which is another reason independent evidence of the sequence carries weight.

## svc_underinsured_motorist_claims_lede

An underinsured motorist claim is a contract question layered on a fault question. Whether the coverage adds to the other driver's limits or merely fills them in usually depends on a single election form signed years before the collision.

## svc_underinsured_motorist_claims_body

### Coverage that tracks the liability limits unless someone opted out

Virginia Code 38.2-2206(A) requires uninsured and underinsured motorist coverage in an amount equal to, but not exceeding, the liability limits of the policy, unless a named insured rejects the additional coverage in the manner Virginia Code 38.2-2202(B) prescribes. The practical effect is that a policyholder who raised liability limits without thinking about it generally raised this coverage too, and one who signed a rejection may have less protection than the declarations page suggests at a glance.

Virginia Code 38.2-2202 also requires specific boldface notices in a motor vehicle policy. It requires notice of the availability of medical expense benefits of up to $2,000 per person for expenses incurred within three years of the accident, and income loss benefits of up to $100 per week for a period of up to one year. Those optional first-party benefits are modest by design, and whether they were purchased is a question the declarations page answers.

### The credit question is the one worth finding

The same statute sets out how the underinsured payment interacts with what the at-fault driver's insurer pays. Underinsured motorist benefits are paid without a credit for the liability payment unless a named insured has signed an election to reduce the coverage by amounts recoverable from other sources. Two policies with identical limits can therefore produce very different totals depending on whether that election exists.

Locating the election form, the rejection form and the declarations page for the policy period in effect on the date of the crash is accordingly a first step rather than a formality. General explanation cannot determine what any particular policy provides.

### Minimum limits changed, and the policy date decides which apply

Virginia Code 46.2-472 sets the minimum liability amounts, and the figures depend on when the policy took effect. Policies effective between January 1, 2022 and December 31, 2024 carry minimums of $30,000 where one person is injured or killed, $60,000 where two or more are, and $20,000 for property damage. Policies effective on or after January 1, 2025 carry $50,000, $100,000 and $25,000 instead.

That transition matters to an underinsured analysis in both directions. It raises the floor on what a minimally insured at-fault driver carries, and because uninsured and underinsured limits track liability limits absent a rejection, it can raise the injured person's own coverage as well. It also means two crashes six months apart can be governed by different numbers.

### The insurer becomes a participant in the lawsuit

Virginia Code 38.2-2206(F) requires an insured who brings an action against the owner or operator of an uninsured or underinsured vehicle, and who intends to rely on the coverage, to serve a copy of the process on the insurer in the manner prescribed as though the insurer were a party defendant, with Virginia Code 8.01-288 not applying to that service. The insurer may then file pleadings and take other action in the name of the owner or operator, or in its own name.

The same subsection removes sovereign or other personal immunity as an obstacle: where the owner or operator is immune from liability for negligence, that immunity does not bar a judgment enforceable against the insurer, is not a defense the insurer may raise, and produces a judgment entered in the name of "Immune Defendant" that is enforceable against the insurer and any non-immune defendant.

### Releases can close doors that were still open

Because an underinsured claim usually follows a settlement with the at-fault driver's carrier, the terms of that first settlement matter. Virginia Code 8.01-35.1 provides that a release or covenant not to sue given in good faith to one of two or more persons liable for the same injury does not discharge the others unless its terms so provide, and reduces the claim against the others by the amount stipulated or the consideration paid, whichever is greater. The release is not admitted into evidence at trial but is considered by the court in reducing the verdict, and it discharges the released party from liability for contribution. The section also covers agreements limiting a defendant's liability to an agreed maximum and minimum.

That reduction mechanism is why the sequence and the language of a settlement are substantive terms rather than paperwork. Nothing here indicates whether a particular release should be signed.

### The fault question does not soften

An underinsured claim still requires proof that the other driver was negligent and that the negligence caused the harm, and Virginia's contributory negligence rule means carelessness by the injured person that proximately contributed can bar the claim outright rather than reducing it by a share. The contributory negligence page sets out that doctrine, and the last clear chance page covers the narrow exception.

Context on exposure: the 2025 Virginia traffic crash facts report recorded 4,965 crashes in Virginia Beach City with 28 people killed and 3,102 injured, against 309,904 licensed drivers in the city. Statewide the report counted 115,858 crashes, 825 deaths and 74,194 injuries. Those totals describe a year of collisions; they do not describe what any policy will pay.

The above is general information about Virginia's uninsured and underinsured motorist statutes and is not legal advice on any claim; coverage questions in a real case belong with the attorney advertising on this site.

## svc_whiplash_and_soft_tissue_injuries_lede

Soft-tissue injury claims are disputed on documentation rather than on diagnosis. Virginia's rules about what a physician may say, when records must be produced, and what a jury may hear about a seat belt all shape the record before anyone argues about value.

## svc_whiplash_and_soft_tissue_injuries_body

### The injury is real and the proof is indirect

Cervical strain and related soft-tissue injuries usually do not appear on an X-ray, so the evidence is a sequence: mechanism of impact, onset and progression of symptoms, examination findings, range-of-motion measurements, prescribed treatment, response to treatment, and functional restrictions. Each element is documented by somebody other than the injured person, which is precisely why a delay in seeking care or a three-week gap between visits becomes an argument rather than a detail.

None of that makes a complaint less genuine. It means the file is built from records, and records exist only if they were created. This page describes how Virginia handles that material generally and gives no advice about an individual injury.

### Virginia's medical privilege is narrower than a blanket authorization implies

Virginia Code 8.01-399 makes communications between a physician and patient privileged, subject to release at the patient's request or with consent. Where the patient's physical or mental condition is at issue in a civil action, the section permits disclosure through discovery or through testimony at trial, and it limits the physician's admissible opinion to a diagnosis stated to a reasonable degree of medical probability. It also provides that no order may compel a party to sign a release for the physician's records where the provider is located in the Commonwealth.

That structure has a practical consequence. A broad authorization handed over early can reach material the statute would have channeled through discovery, including records of conditions unrelated to the collision. A person deciding what to sign is making a substantive choice, and the choice is worth discussing with counsel rather than with the adjuster requesting it.

### Records have a deadline and a price

Virginia Code 8.01-413 requires copies of health records to be furnished within thirty days after a written request from the patient, the patient's attorney, the patient's executor or administrator, or an authorized insurer, subject to the statute's conditions. The same section makes properly authenticated copies, printouts and other reproductions admissible in the same manner as originals, allows a subpoenaed provider to comply by mailing authenticated copies to the clerk, and makes the requesting party liable for the provider's reasonable charges for maintaining, retrieving, reviewing, preparing, copying and mailing what is produced.

Thirty days is worth planning around. A treatment history spread across an emergency department, a primary care office, an imaging center, a physical therapy practice and a chiropractic office means several thirty-day windows running in parallel, not one.

### Statements taken early become fixed

Virginia Code 8.01-417(A) requires that a person who takes a signed written statement or a voice recording from an injured person about the injury furnish a copy of the statement promptly, or a verified typed transcription within thirty days after the recording was made once it is transcribed, and in all cases on request by the injured person or that person's attorney. A recorded description of symptoms given three days after a crash, before a delayed onset has developed, tends to be quoted back later. Having the copy is the minimum protection.

Subsection C of the same section addresses information flowing the other direction. Before filing suit for personal injuries from a motor vehicle accident, an attorney who has given written notice of representation, or an unrepresented injured person, may request in writing that the insurer disclose the liability limits of any applicable policy and the alleged tortfeasor's physical address where it has not previously been reported. The request has to supply the accident date, the tortfeasor's name and last known address if reported, a copy of the accident report if any, and the claim number if available.

### One statute keeps the seat belt out of the argument

Virginia Code 46.2-1094(D) provides that a safety-belt violation does not constitute negligence, may not be considered in mitigation of damages, may not be admitted into evidence, and may not be commented on by counsel in an action for damages arising from the operation, ownership or maintenance of a motor vehicle. Subsection C sets the penalty for the violation at a $25 civil penalty with no demerit points and no court costs, and subsection F bars an officer from stopping a vehicle solely for that violation and makes evidence obtained from such a stop inadmissible.

This is a genuine protection in a state where a plaintiff's own conduct can otherwise end a claim entirely. It is also narrow: it addresses the belt and nothing else, so posture, headrest position and pre-existing degeneration remain fair subjects.

### Expert testimony and the treatise rule

Virginia Code 8.01-401.1 allows an expert in a civil action to testify and draw inferences from facts, circumstances or data made known at or before trial, and provides that the underlying material need not itself be admissible if it is of a type normally relied upon in that field. The section permits opinion testimony without prior disclosure of the underlying facts unless the court requires it, subject to cross-examination. It also allows statements from published medical treatises, periodicals or pamphlets established as reliable authority to be read into evidence, though not received as exhibits, and requires that copies of statements to be introduced through an expert on direct examination be provided to opposing parties thirty days before trial unless the court orders otherwise.

Soft-tissue cases are often where that literature does the most work, because the mechanism of injury has to be explained rather than displayed.

### Where the fault rule reappears

Documentation does not answer liability. Virginia does not compare the negligence of the parties, so an argument that the injured person contributed to the collision is a defense to the whole claim rather than a discount on the medical specials. The contributory negligence page and the rear-end collision page cover that interaction, and the medical bills and health care liens page covers what happens to the bills themselves.
