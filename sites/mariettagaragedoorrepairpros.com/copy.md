## symptom_1_title
A loud snap, then a door that is suddenly too heavy

## symptom_1
A sharp bang from the garage followed by a door that barely lifts usually means the counterbalance spring has broken. The opener did not create the weight problem. Its job is only to move a balanced door; the spring normally carries most of the load. On a torsion system, stand clear and look for a visible gap in the coil wrapped around the steel shaft above the opening. On an extension-spring system, one long spring may hang slack beside the horizontal rail. Do not put hands near either setup.

Leave the door closed if it is closed. If it is open or partly open, keep people and vehicles out of the opening and do not try to pull it down. Disconnecting power to the opener prevents someone from pressing the wall button by habit, but it does not make the door light. A failed spring can also let a cable slip from a drum, so an apparently simple spring failure deserves an on-site inspection of both sides of the door.

In Marietta's long wet seasons, surface rust on bearing plates, cables, and the spring shaft can make the door drag before the spring finally gives up. The technician measures the existing spring by wire size, inside diameter, length, and the door's actual weight. A two-spring door is normally evaluated as a pair because both springs have done the same work for the same number of cycles. After replacement, the useful test is balance: with the opener disconnected, the door should stay near waist height instead of racing down or shooting upward. That is the point at which the opener can be put back into service.

## symptom_2_title
The door starts down and then reverses

## symptom_2
A downward cycle that aborts and returns upward usually means the protection circuit detected trouble. Begin at floor level. Each photo sensor is mounted beside a jamb; its light has to reach the matching sensor across the garage before a closing cycle is allowed. Fallen leaves, a rake handle, webs, or stored equipment often cut that path. Clean each lens with a soft cloth and make sure the brackets face one another squarely. On most systems, a solid status light at both sensors indicates alignment.

Marietta's humidity gives this problem a local wrinkle. Condensation and pollen collect on the lenses and in the small sensor brackets, while damp rollers and hinges can make the door travel stiffly. The opener then interprets that resistance as an obstruction and reverses. Raising the close-force setting may make the symptom disappear for a day, but it removes a safety margin and leaves the mechanical drag in place. Do not bypass the sensors or keep increasing force to get the door shut.

If the sensors are clear and lit, the technician checks whether the door moves freely by hand with the opener disconnected. A door that binds at one spot may have a dry roller, a bent hinge, swelling at the bottom weather seal, or track alignment that has drifted. If the door runs smoothly by hand, attention shifts to the opener's travel limits, wiring, and control board. The repair is often smaller than a new opener: a sensor adjustment, roller set, hinge, or limit reset. The decision comes after the moving door is inspected, not from the symptom alone.

## symptom_3_title
One side is low, a roller left the track, or a panel took a limb hit

## symptom_3
A garage door that hangs lower on one side, has a roller outside the track, or will not clear a bent panel is not a door to test repeatedly. Stop the opener, keep clear of the opening, and leave the door in the position where it stopped. The cables, rollers, and remaining track may be holding a great deal of weight in an unstable arrangement. Pressing the button again can turn a slipped cable into a collapsed door or bend additional sections.

This is worth treating seriously in a city with mature trees and frequent rain. A falling limb may leave a dent that looks cosmetic from the driveway while also bending a horizontal strut, pinching the track, or shifting the top section out of square. A vehicle nudge has a similar pattern. The visible mark does not tell the whole story; the tracks need to be checked for plumb, the rollers for cracks or seized bearings, and the lift cables for fraying or loose wraps at the drums.

The repair begins by securing the door so it cannot travel. Rollers are guided back into undamaged track or replaced, cables are reset to equal tension, and bent sections of track are straightened only when their shape and mounting holes remain sound. A badly creased rail is replaced rather than forced into a shape that will bind again. The door is then hand-balanced before the opener is reconnected. For wood doors, moisture matters too: a swollen or deteriorated lower edge can make the door bind and overload otherwise healthy hardware. The repair plan should separate a workable track-and-roller fix from panel or door replacement when the structure itself has been compromised.

## symptom_4_title
You hear the opener, but the garage door has no movement

## symptom_4
Motor sound without door travel is not automatically a failed opener. Watch the operator track first. A moving belt or chain paired with a stationary door often means the sliding connector has separated from the door arm. Someone may have used the red emergency release, or the cord may have been caught by stored items. When the door is firmly on the floor and operating normally by hand, the connector can often be latched again through the release and one controlled cycle. Do not try that while the door is elevated, skewed, or unusually heavy; solve the spring or track issue first.

A grinding sound with no chain or belt movement points to a worn drive gear inside the opener. A brief hum followed by silence can be a capacitor, motor issue, or control-board fault. Georgia moisture also finds its way into old wall-button connections, sensor wiring, and unsealed housings. That does not mean humidity alone is the diagnosis, but corrosion at low-voltage terminals is something worth checking before replacing an entire opener.

The technician first releases the opener and tests whether the door is balanced and rolls freely. An opener cannot be judged fairly while it is trying to lift a door with a broken spring, seized roller, or swollen wood section. Once the door itself is sound, the rail, trolley, gear, limits, safety sensors, remote receiver, and wiring are checked in order. A stripped gear or failed sensor is usually a focused repair. Replacement makes more sense when critical parts are unavailable, the housing is damaged, or repeated repairs have made the old unit unreliable. In either case, the new or repaired opener is tested through a full open-and-close cycle with its reversal system working.

## qa_1_question
Does Marietta require a permit for a garage door replacement?

## qa_1_answer
The City of Marietta says a permit is required when an owner or contractor constructs, enlarges, alters, repairs, moves, or demolishes a building or structure. Its public page does not give a garage-door-only exception, so do not assume a full door swap or an altered opening is permit-free. A spring, roller, cable, or opener repair is different from changing the opening or replacing the door assembly, but the scope and address control. Confirm the project with Marietta Building Inspections before ordering a new door. Work outside the city limits can fall under Cobb County rather than the city, so verify the jurisdiction instead of filing with the wrong office.

## qa_2_question
Why is a garage door replacement different in the Downtown Historic District?

## qa_2_answer
The City of Marietta's Certificate of Approval form says any exterior work in the Downtown Historic District goes to the Historic Board of Review, and a building permit will not be issued without an approved certificate when one is required. That means a visible garage door is not merely a hardware choice there. The historic guidance treats garages and other accessory buildings as important elements, calls for original significant garages to be retained and repaired, and asks that visible structures receive the same visual care as the house. Before choosing windows, a carriage-style face, color, or a different door material, confirm the review path and provide the proposed materials and photographs the application calls for.

## qa_3_question
Why do steel hardware and wood doors act up after humid weather?

## qa_3_answer
North Georgia has long warm, humid periods and about 50 to 55 inches of rain in a typical year. That moisture does not break a good door by itself, but it helps surface corrosion form on shafts, cables, bearings, and fasteners when water remains on the hardware. It also lets pollen and grit stick to old lubricant. Wood absorbs moisture, so a wood door or lower wood section can swell, soften at an unprotected edge, and begin rubbing a track it once cleared. The right response is inspection and targeted lubrication, not more opener force. Persistent drag needs the door balanced and the hardware checked before the opener is asked to compensate.
