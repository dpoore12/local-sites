## symptom_1_title

It is a first arrest, but Arizona is still calling it a DUI

## symptom_1

A first arrest does not mean a minor charge in Arizona. The usual alcohol-count statute covers a result of 0.08 or above within two hours of driving, but it also covers driving while impaired “to the slightest degree.” That second route matters. A reported number below 0.08 does not automatically end the case when the state alleges impairment from alcohol, drugs, medication, or a combination. The citation may list more than one subsection, and the evidence supporting each one can differ.

For a first standard DUI conviction, Arizona law states a minimum ten consecutive days in jail. The statute also allows a judge, at sentencing, to suspend all but one day if court-ordered screening, education, treatment, or psychotherapy is completed. That is not the same thing as a promise that the case will end that way. The charging allegation, prior record, facts of the stop, test evidence, and the court’s rulings all matter before sentencing is even at issue.

The immediate task is to identify exactly what was charged and gather the evidence behind it. An attorney may look at the reason for the stop, driving observations, field-sobriety instructions and conditions, statements, video, timing of drinking and driving, and the collection and analysis of a breath or blood sample. Keep every sheet issued at release and write down the timeline while it is fresh. If the ticket names Tempe Municipal Court, do not treat its date as informal. A missed court setting can create a separate problem even while the DUI allegation is still being sorted out.

## symptom_2_title

The result is 0.15 or higher: extreme and super extreme DUI

## symptom_2

Arizona’s alcohol tiers are unusually explicit. A reported alcohol concentration from 0.08 through 0.149 is the standard per-se DUI range. At 0.15 through 0.199, the charge moves into the statute labeled extreme DUI. At 0.20 or more, it is commonly called super extreme DUI. The names are not just labels. The statute assigns different minimum jail terms, assessments, and ignition-interlock consequences.

For a first conviction in the 0.15–0.199 range, the stated minimum is thirty consecutive days in jail. For 0.20 or above, the stated minimum is forty-five consecutive days. The same law includes a limited sentencing provision that may allow a judge to suspend all but nine days in the first tier, or all but fourteen days in the higher tier, when the statutory ignition-interlock requirement is met. Eligibility, compliance, the precise conviction, and the court’s sentence matter; the headline number alone is not a personal prediction.

Check the document carefully. The relevant statutory language measures alcohol concentration within two hours of driving or actual physical control, and says it must result from alcohol consumed before or while driving. A defense review is therefore not just a glance at a printout. It can include when the sample was taken, which test is alleged, chain-of-custody documents for blood, maintenance and procedural records where applicable, video, and the state’s proof connecting the reported result to the statute. If the paperwork says 0.15 or 0.20, treat the line between tiers as important and get the actual records preserved quickly.

## qa_1_question

Which Arizona DUI tier applies to a 0.08, 0.15, or 0.20 result?

## qa_1_answer

Arizona’s basic alcohol DUI provision uses 0.08 or more within two hours of driving. A reported 0.08 through 0.149 generally falls in that standard per-se range. Under A.R.S. § 28-1382, 0.15 through 0.199 is the extreme-DUI tier and 0.20 or above is the higher tier often called super extreme DUI. A result below 0.08 is not automatically the end of a DUI allegation because A.R.S. § 28-1381 also prohibits driving while impaired to the slightest degree. The charge and evidence—not a nickname for the number—control what has to be addressed.
