## qa_1_question
What is the difference between a wrongful-death claim and a survival action in Texas?

## qa_1_answer
They address different losses. A wrongful-death claim concerns losses caused to the people left behind by a death. A survival action concerns the personal-injury claim that belonged to the person who was injured before death. Texas Civil Practice and Remedies Code section 71.021 states that a personal-injury cause of action does not abate because the injured person died and that it survives to and in favor of the heirs, legal representatives, and estate.

That is why an early review should not reduce the situation to one label. Medical bills, conscious pain, lost earnings before death, funeral-related issues, and the family’s own losses can raise different questions. The presence of an estate, the available records, and the facts of the injury all matter. This page cannot decide which claims apply to a particular family, but it can make one point clear: preserving records now gives the family room to understand the difference later.

## svc_fatal_car_accident_claim_lede
A fatal collision produces a report, an adjuster, and a deadline that depends on something few families think to ask. Which government owns the road where it happened can compress the time to act down to forty-five days.

## svc_fatal_car_accident_claim_body
### Who Texas allows to bring the action

[Section 71.004(a)](https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/civil-practice-and-remedies-code/title-4/chapter-71/subchapter-a/section-71-004/) of the Civil Practice and Remedies Code gives the wrongful death action to three groups and to no others. The surviving spouse. The children. The parents. Subsection (b) lets any one of them file for the benefit of all, so the family does not need to agree before the claim is protected. A brother or a sister is not on that list, and neither is a grandparent or a grandchild. [Section 71.003(a)](https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/civil-practice-and-remedies-code/title-4/chapter-71/subchapter-a/section-71-003/) adds a quiet threshold: the action exists only if the person who died could have sued for that injury had they lived.

### Three calendar months, and a duty that shifts

Subsection (c) of the same section is the part almost nobody is told about. If none of those relatives has begun an action within three calendar months after the death, the executor or administrator of the estate is directed to bring and prosecute it, unless all of them ask that it not be filed. This is not a cutoff that erases the family's rights. It is a duty placed on whoever represents the estate, and it becomes real as soon as an estate has been opened. Read alongside the ordinary two-year period for a death claim, it explains why the calendar matters early rather than in year two.

### Which government owns the pavement

Austin reported 99 traffic deaths in 2025 and estimates that roughly 65 to 75 percent of the city's traffic fatalities happen on [roads owned by the state](https://www.austintexas.gov/transportation-public-works/mobility-matters/severe-crashes-show-downward-trend-2025). The city's own [Vision Zero performance review](https://www.austintexas.gov/sites/default/files/files/TPW/VisionZero/VZ-Analytics-Austin-Safety-Project-Performance-10.02.25.pdf) is blunter: deaths on city-owned streets have stayed relatively flat while deaths on state and other agencies' roadways rose. Across Travis County, [TxDOT counted](https://www.txdot.gov/content/dam/docs/division/trf/crash-records/2024/13.pdf) 139 fatal crashes and 155 traffic deaths in 2024. Ownership is not trivia in a claim. A crash on a state highway points toward a state agency, while a crash at a Bouldin Creek or East Cesar Chavez intersection may point toward the city, and each carries a different clock.

### Forty-five days, written into the city charter

Article XII, section 3 of the [Austin city charter](https://services.austintexas.gov/edims/document.cfm?id=292113) requires written notice, verified by affidavit, to the city council or the city manager within forty-five days. The notice must state when, where and how the death occurred, the apparent extent of the injury, the damages claimed, the claimant's residence, and the names and addresses of the witnesses being relied on. In the charter's own words, failure to give that notice exonerates and exempts the city from any liability whatsoever. The city's [claims instructions](https://www.austintexas.gov/services/file-claim) repeat the same 45-day window. The Tort Claims Act default in [section 101.101](https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/civil-practice-and-remedies-code/title-5/chapter-101/subchapter-d/section-101-101/) is six months, but subsection (b) ratifies charter periods, which is how six months becomes six weeks here.

### Naming the wrong defendant can end it

[Section 101.106](https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/civil-practice-and-remedies-code/title-5/chapter-101/subchapter-d/section-101-106/) treats the first filing as an irrevocable election. Suing the government employee immediately and forever bars any recovery from the governmental unit unless it consents, and suing the unit bars any recovery from the employee. Where a suit names both, the employee is dismissed on the unit's motion. Two other provisions narrow the target further. [Section 101.021](https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/civil-practice-and-remedies-code/title-5/chapter-101/subchapter-b/section-101-021/) waives immunity mainly for the operation or use of motor-driven vehicles and equipment, and for a condition or use of tangible property. [Section 101.056](https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/civil-practice-and-remedies-code/title-5/chapter-101/subchapter-c/section-101-056/) removes discretionary decisions from the chapter altogether, which is why a design choice and a maintenance failure are treated differently.

### What a public defendant can be made to pay

[Section 101.023](https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/civil-practice-and-remedies-code/title-5/chapter-101/subchapter-b/section-101-023/) sets the ceilings. A municipality is liable for death or bodily injury up to $250,000 for each person and $500,000 for each single occurrence. The state government carries the same two figures. A unit of local government that is not a municipality drops to $100,000 for each person and $300,000 for each occurrence. Those numbers are the outer edge of what a public entity can be ordered to pay under the chapter, whatever a jury might otherwise have found.

### The first two weeks, in practical terms

Record the agency that investigated, the report number, and the medical examiner case number if there is one. Do not let the vehicle be repaired, sold or scrapped while its condition is still a question. Save the adjuster's letters with their dates, because the date a request arrived can matter as much as its content. On fees, Texas puts no percentage limit on a death recovery. The protection sits in [Rule 1.04(d)](https://www.legalethicstexas.com/resources/rules/texas-disciplinary-rules-of-professional-conduct/fees/), which requires a signed writing stating separate percentages for settlement, trial and appeal. It must also list the expenses deducted and say whether they come off before or after the percentage.

## svc_fatal_work_accident_claim_lede
A death on the job opens two files at once. One is a benefit claim under the Labor Code, with its own list of relatives and its own one-year deadline. The other is the wrongful death action, and its list is shorter.

## svc_fatal_work_accident_claim_body
### Two lists of relatives, and they do not match

The wrongful death action belongs to a spouse, children and parents under [section 71.004](https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/civil-practice-and-remedies-code/title-4/chapter-71/subchapter-a/section-71-004/). Death benefits follow a different map. [Labor Code section 408.182](https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/labor-code/title-5/subtitle-a/chapter-408/subchapter-j/section-408-182/) pays an eligible spouse and eligible children first, then eligible grandchildren, and where none of those exist it pays equal shares to surviving dependents who are parents, stepparents, siblings or grandparents. A dependent brother can therefore draw benefits from the carrier and still hold no wrongful death claim of his own. Families discover that mismatch in the middle of the process, usually after someone has already been told they are covered.

### The one-year benefit deadline

The Division of Workers' Compensation asks a beneficiary to file [DWC Form-042](https://www.tdi.texas.gov/forms/dwc/dwc042benclm.pdf) with the division or the insurance carrier within one year of the employee's death. After that year the form itself lists the narrow openings: a claim for someone seventeen or younger, a claim for a person unable to make it, or a showing of good reason for the delay. Benefits are set at [75 percent](https://www.tdi.texas.gov/wc/employee/deathben.html) of the deceased employee's average weekly wage, subject to statutory limits, and burial expenses may be requested up to $10,000. Non-dependent eligible parents have their own rule under subsection (d-1), including one payment per household and a total that cannot exceed 104 weeks.

### Exemplary damages come from the constitution here

[Article XVI, section 26](https://law.justia.com/constitution/texas/sections/cn001600-002600.html) of the Texas Constitution reaches any person, corporation or company that commits a homicide through a willful act, an omission or gross neglect. Such a defendant is responsible in exemplary damages to the surviving husband, widow, or heirs of his or her body, and a criminal proceeding is beside the point. That wording is older than the modern statutes and narrower in one respect worth noticing. It names the spouse and the heirs of the body. It does not name parents, though [section 71.009](https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/civil-practice-and-remedies-code/title-4/chapter-71/subchapter-a/section-71-009/) separately allows exemplary damages in a death caused by a willful act or omission or by gross negligence.

### What the survival claim needs before it moves

[Section 71.021](https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/civil-practice-and-remedies-code/title-4/chapter-71/subchapter-b/section-71-021/) keeps the injured person's own claim alive and passes it to the heirs, legal representatives and estate. Somebody usually has to be appointed before that claim can be pursued, and in this county that means the [Travis County probate courts](https://www.traviscountytx.gov/probate), two statutory courts served by the County Clerk's probate division. Timing has a twist. [Section 16.062](https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/civil-practice-and-remedies-code/title-2/subtitle-b/chapter-16/subchapter-d/section-16-062/) suspends the running of an applicable limitations period for twelve months after a death, but if an executor or administrator qualifies before that year is out, the period starts running at the qualification instead.

### When the employer is a public body

A worker who died for the city, the county, a school district or the hospital district is inside the governmental-immunity system, not outside it. The city's forty-five day charter notice can apply, other units fall back on the six-month rule in [section 101.101](https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/civil-practice-and-remedies-code/title-5/chapter-101/subchapter-d/section-101-101/), and the ceilings in [section 101.023](https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/civil-practice-and-remedies-code/title-5/chapter-101/subchapter-b/section-101-023/) apply whatever the loss. A suit under the chapter also has to be brought where the cause of action arose and must name the unit itself as the defendant, under [section 101.102](https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/civil-practice-and-remedies-code/title-5/chapter-101/subchapter-d/section-101-102/).

### What to hold on to, and what not to sign

Ask for the incident report, the training file, the equipment inspection records and the schedule for the shift. Photograph the machine or the vehicle if it is still accessible, and do not let anyone modify it. Keep every letter from the carrier with its envelope. A recorded statement or a benefit election signed early can shape the record before the family knows which of the two systems it is standing in, so a document that arrives in the first week deserves a slower reading than it invites.

### How the fee side is regulated

Texas states no maximum percentage for a death case. [Rule 1.04](https://www.legalethicstexas.com/resources/rules/texas-disciplinary-rules-of-professional-conduct/fees/) instead forbids an unconscionable fee. For a contingent arrangement it requires a signed written agreement that separates the percentage on settlement, on trial and on appeal. The same agreement lists the expenses deducted and says whether they are subtracted before or after the percentage is applied.

## svc_medical_negligence_death_claim_lede
When a death follows treatment, two claims can arise from one chart. One belongs to the family. The other belongs to the estate, and it usually cannot move until a probate court has appointed somebody to hold it.

## svc_medical_negligence_death_claim_body
### The two claims, and who owns each one

The family's claim rests on [section 71.004](https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/civil-practice-and-remedies-code/title-4/chapter-71/subchapter-a/section-71-004/), which limits it to a surviving spouse, children and parents. The other claim is the patient's own, and [section 71.021](https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/civil-practice-and-remedies-code/title-4/chapter-71/subchapter-b/section-71-021/) keeps it from dying with them, passing it to the heirs, legal representatives and estate. Both are gated by [section 71.003(a)](https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/civil-practice-and-remedies-code/title-4/chapter-71/subchapter-a/section-71-003/), which applies the subchapter only where the patient could have brought the injury action had they lived. A death that follows care may also be a health care liability claim under chapter 74, which layers presuit notice and expert-report requirements on top of everything described here.

### Why an estate often has to be opened

The survival claim needs a legal representative, and that appointment happens in probate. Travis County runs [two statutory probate courts](https://www.traviscountytx.gov/probate), and the County Clerk's probate division handles the filing procedures, court costs, citations and the issuance of letters. Where there was no will, an application to determine heirship can run alongside the application for administration. None of this decides the medical question. It decides who is allowed to ask it on the patient's behalf.

### The twelve months that can quietly expire

[Section 16.062](https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/civil-practice-and-remedies-code/title-2/subtitle-b/chapter-16/subchapter-d/section-16-062/) suspends an applicable limitations period for twelve months after a death. Read the second sentence too. Once an executor or administrator qualifies, the period begins to run from that qualification, so appointing someone can start a clock rather than pause it. [Section 16.001](https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/civil-practice-and-remedies-code/title-2/subtitle-b/chapter-16/subchapter-a/section-16-001/) is the other narrow provision families ask about: time under a legal disability, meaning under eighteen or of unsound mind, is excluded from a limitations period, disabilities cannot be tacked together, and one arising after the period starts does not suspend it.

### When the medical examiner has the case

The Travis County Medical Examiner works under [article 49.25](https://www.traviscountytx.gov/medical-examiner/faq) of the Code of Criminal Procedure. That authority reaches unattended deaths, suspicious or unnatural deaths, and deaths where the cause is unknown. It also reaches children under six and deaths occurring less than twenty-four hours after admission to a hospital or another institution. That last category catches many hospital deaths that families assume the office would never see. The examination itself generally takes 12 to 24 hours. The office states that an autopsy report is public information under the Public Information Act unless an exception applies.

### Getting the autopsy report and the examiner's file

Requests go to the office at 7723 Springdale Road, or by mail and e-mail, using the [autopsy report request form](https://www.traviscountytx.gov/medical-examiner/records-request), which asks for the case number, the decedent's name, the date of death and the requester's relationship. The published [fee schedule](https://www.traviscountytx.gov/medical-examiner/fee-schedule) charges $25 for an autopsy report and $5 per investigation of death report, exempts the county legal next of kin from the autopsy report charge, and charges $50 to certify a record and $30 per disc of digital images. A certified copy is the version that a court file usually needs.

### A public provider moves the deadline forward

Central Health is [Travis County's taxpayer-funded hospital district](https://www.centralhealth.net/about/), created by voters in 2004, and it operates a clinic network across the county. A hospital district is a governmental unit, which puts the six-month notice rule in [section 101.101](https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/civil-practice-and-remedies-code/title-5/chapter-101/subchapter-d/section-101-101/) and the ceilings in [section 101.023](https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/civil-practice-and-remedies-code/title-5/chapter-101/subchapter-b/section-101-023/) in front of the ordinary rules. For a unit of local government other than a municipality those ceilings are $100,000 for each person and $300,000 for each single occurrence. Naming an individual clinician first can also trigger the election in [section 101.106](https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/civil-practice-and-remedies-code/title-5/chapter-101/subchapter-d/section-101-106/) and bar the claim against the district.

### Records to gather, in a workable order

Start with the complete chart from each facility rather than a discharge summary, then the medication administration record, the orders, the imaging, the monitoring data and the nursing notes. Add the ambulance run report and any transfer paperwork. Keep a simple log of what was requested, from whom, on what date and what came back. On fees, [Rule 1.04](https://www.legalethicstexas.com/resources/rules/texas-disciplinary-rules-of-professional-conduct/fees/) sets no percentage limit and instead requires the written terms and the expense treatment to be spelled out before anyone signs.

## svc_wrongful_death_damages_claim_lede
Damages in a Texas death case is not one number. It is a set of shares, decided relative by relative, sitting under different ceilings depending on who the defendant turns out to be.

## svc_wrongful_death_damages_claim_body
### One verdict, separate shares

[Section 71.010](https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/civil-practice-and-remedies-code/title-4/chapter-71/subchapter-a/section-71-010/) lets the jury award damages in an amount proportionate to the injury resulting from the death, then divides that award in the shares the jury finds among the individuals entitled to recover who are alive at that time. The practical effect is that a spouse, a minor child, an adult child and a parent each stand on their own proof. Age, dependency, the shape of the relationship and what the household actually lost are examined separately. A single settlement figure still has to be broken into those parts before anyone can say what it means for an individual family member.

### The relatives Texas leaves out

Because [section 71.004](https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/civil-practice-and-remedies-code/title-4/chapter-71/subchapter-a/section-71-004/) makes the action the exclusive property of a spouse, children and parents, everyone else is outside it. Siblings are the most common surprise, and grandchildren and grandparents are in the same position. Under subsection (b) one qualifying relative may bring the action for the benefit of all. Subsection (c) then directs the executor or administrator to bring it where none of them has done so within three calendar months of the death, unless all of them ask otherwise. A relative outside the statutory list may still matter as a witness, and may still be an heir for the separate estate claim under [section 71.021](https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/civil-practice-and-remedies-code/title-4/chapter-71/subchapter-b/section-71-021/), but they are not a wrongful death beneficiary.

### Exemplary damages, and an argument that is not settled

[Section 71.009](https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/civil-practice-and-remedies-code/title-4/chapter-71/subchapter-a/section-71-009/) allows exemplary as well as actual damages where the death was caused by a willful act or omission or by gross negligence. [Article XVI, section 26](https://law.justia.com/constitution/texas/sections/cn001600-002600.html) of the state constitution states the same right in older language. It runs to the surviving husband, widow, or heirs of the body, without regard to any criminal case. [Section 41.008(b)](https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/civil-practice-and-remedies-code/title-2/subtitle-c/chapter-41/section-41-008/) then caps exemplary damages at the greater of $200,000, or twice the economic damages plus any noneconomic damages the jury found, with that second component capped at $750,000. Whether a constitutional right can be limited by a statutory cap in a death case has been argued for decades. The honest answer is that Texas courts have not adopted it. One court of appeals [declined to hold the cap unconstitutional](https://caselaw.findlaw.com/court/tx-court-of-appeals/1092508.html) and noted that another court had approved applying it in wrongful death actions. In a 2005 [gross negligence death case](https://law.justia.com/cases/texas/supreme-court/2005/2000547.html) the Supreme Court of Texas resolved the appeal on the evidence, leaving untouched the court of appeals holding that the cap was not unconstitutional as applied.

### How the cap is now calculated

A [2026 opinion of the Supreme Court of Texas](https://www.txcourts.gov/media/1462759/240045.pdf) tightened the arithmetic. The cap is measured defendant by defendant, comparing the exemplary award against the economic damages attributable to that defendant's conduct based on its percentage of responsibility, not against the total economic damages awarded against everyone. In a case with a trucking company, a driver and a maintenance contractor, that holding can move the ceiling substantially, which is why responsibility percentages are not just a liability question.

### When the defendant is a government

[Section 101.023](https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/civil-practice-and-remedies-code/title-5/chapter-101/subchapter-b/section-101-023/) caps liability for death or bodily injury at $250,000 per person and $500,000 per occurrence against a municipality and against the state, and at $100,000 per person and $300,000 per occurrence against a unit of local government that is not a municipality. Notice comes first, though. Austin's charter demands written notice, verified by affidavit, within forty-five days, and [section 101.101](https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/civil-practice-and-remedies-code/title-5/chapter-101/subchapter-d/section-101-101/) expressly ratifies a charter period while setting six months as the default elsewhere. [Section 101.056](https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/civil-practice-and-remedies-code/title-5/chapter-101/subchapter-c/section-101-056/) also keeps discretionary decisions outside the chapter, which limits what a road design or a policy choice can support.

### The deadline, and the narrow ways it bends

An action for injury resulting in death is generally due within two years, and the clock starts on the death itself rather than on the day a report or a chart arrives. The tolling provisions are narrow. [Section 16.001](https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/civil-practice-and-remedies-code/title-2/subtitle-b/chapter-16/subchapter-a/section-16-001/) excludes time spent under a legal disability, [section 16.062](https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/civil-practice-and-remedies-code/title-2/subtitle-b/chapter-16/subchapter-d/section-16-062/) suspends an applicable period for twelve months after a death until a representative qualifies, and a public defendant's notice period can expire long before either of them matters. None of these is a reason to assume there is spare time.

### What a fee agreement has to disclose

No Texas rule caps the percentage taken from a death recovery. [Rule 1.04](https://www.legalethicstexas.com/resources/rules/texas-disciplinary-rules-of-professional-conduct/fees/) prohibits an unconscionable fee, lists eight factors for measuring reasonableness including the fee customarily charged locally for similar work, and requires a contingent agreement to be written, signed, and specific about the separate percentages for settlement, trial and appeal. It must also say which expenses are deducted and whether they come off before or after the percentage is applied. Since [section 71.010](https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/civil-practice-and-remedies-code/title-4/chapter-71/subchapter-a/section-71-010/) divides the recovery into shares, that percentage lands on each share rather than on one undivided sum.

## pricing_lede

Texas puts no percentage limit on a fee taken out of a death recovery, and it is Rule 1.04 rather than a rule numbered 1.5 that governs the arrangement. This page walks through what that rule demands of the writing, who chapter 71 lets bring the claim, how a jury's split of the damages sets each share, and what the Travis County clerks charge along the way.

## pricing_body

### The rule number is 1.04, and it names no percentage

Fee conduct in Texas sits in Rule 1.04 of the Texas Disciplinary Rules of Professional Conduct, not in a rule numbered 1.5 as it is in most states. Subdivision (a) prohibits an illegal fee and an unconscionable one, defining the latter in a single sentence: a fee is unconscionable if a competent lawyer could not form a reasonable belief that it is reasonable. No maximum percentage for a death claim appears in the rule or in the Civil Practice and Remedies Code. Two agreements offered to an Austin family are two proposals, judged against a professional judgment test rather than an arithmetic limit.

### The eight measures of a reasonable fee

Rule 1.04(b) sets out what goes into that judgment. The labor and hours a case demands, its novelty and difficulty, and the skill required. Whether the matter keeps the lawyer from other employment. The fee customarily charged in the locality for similar legal services, which is why an Austin comparison is not a Houston comparison. The amount involved and the results obtained. Time limits imposed by the client or by events. The length of the professional relationship, and whether the fee is fixed or contingent.

### What has to be on paper

Under Rule 1.04(c) the basis or rate of the fee has to be communicated to the client, preferably in writing, before or within a reasonable time after the representation begins. For a contingent arrangement, 1.04(d) goes further. The agreement must be written, signed by the client, and it must state the method for determining the fee, including the percentage accruing on settlement, on trial and on appeal. Those three numbers are frequently different. It must state the expenses deducted from the recovery and whether they come off before or after the percentage is figured. When the case closes, the client is owed a written accounting of the result and, where funds came in, the remittance and the calculation behind it.

### Who owns the claim, and what happens if nobody files

Section 71.004(a) of the Civil Practice and Remedies Code makes the wrongful death action the exclusive property of the surviving spouse, children and parents of the deceased. Under (b) any one of them, or all of them together, may bring it for the benefit of everyone entitled. Subsection (c) is the deadline most families never hear about. If none of those individuals has begun an action within three calendar months of the death, the executor or administrator must bring it. The exception is a request from all the beneficiaries that it not be filed. Section 71.021 keeps the deceased person's own injury claim alive separately, passing it to the heirs, legal representatives and estate.

### The jury splits the money before the fee is applied

Section 71.010 lets the jury award damages in a proportion it finds and then divides the recovery in the shares the jury sets. A verdict resolves into separate shares belonging to separate people, each with a different age, dependency and loss, and the percentage lands on each share rather than on one undivided pot. Where one of those people is a minor, that share cannot simply be handed over.

### A minor's share and the chapter 142 route

Property Code chapter 142 governs money recovered for a child with no legal guardian, who appears through a next friend or a guardian ad litem. Section 142.001 lets the court, on application and after a hearing, decree how the funds are invested. Section 142.004 limits the vehicles to a qualified tuition or ABLE account and federally insured interest-bearing time deposits, widening only slightly where the clerk of court invests on written order. Section 142.005 allows a court-created trust instead, and (b) fixes its terms, including that a minor's trust ends at death, at a stated age, or at the twenty-fifth birthday, whichever comes first. Subdivision (l) caps what the petitioning party may be reimbursed in attorney fees for setting the trust up at $1,000, and (m) and (n) restrict who may serve as trustee depending on whether the principal exceeds $50,000.

### What the Travis County clerks charge

The District Clerk's schedule effective January 1, 2026 puts the total base fee for a civil suit at $350, being a $213 local consolidated fee and a $137 state consolidated fee. Issuing a citation, subpoena or writ is $8. Service by certified mail through the District Clerk is $85, while a Travis County constable serving citations and notices charges $90 plus a separate $10 witness payment fee. A jury demand under Rule 216 costs $10. Later filings in the same case, including an intervention, a counterclaim, a third party petition or a motion for new trial, are $80 each. Money held in the court registry carries an administrative charge of 5 percent capped at $50 where it earns no interest, or 10 percent of the interest where it does. On the probate side the County Clerk charges $360 to apply for letters of administration, for a determination of heirship, or for a management trust. Add a $525 ad litem deposit in the situations the Clerk identifies, $2 to issue letters, and $25 to file an annual account.
