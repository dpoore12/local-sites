## qa_1_question
What is the difference between a wrongful-death claim and a survival action in Texas?

## qa_1_answer
They address different losses. A wrongful-death claim concerns losses caused to the people left behind by a death. A survival action concerns the personal-injury claim that belonged to the person who was injured before death. Texas Civil Practice and Remedies Code section 71.021 states that a personal-injury cause of action does not abate because the injured person died and that it survives to and in favor of the heirs, legal representatives, and estate.

That is why an early review should not reduce the situation to one label. Medical bills, conscious pain, lost earnings before death, funeral-related issues, and the family’s own losses can raise different questions. The presence of an estate, the available records, and the facts of the injury all matter. This page cannot decide which claims apply to a particular family, but it can make one point clear: preserving records now gives the family room to understand the difference later.

## svc_fatal_car_accident_claim_lede
A fatal collision produces a report, an adjuster, and a deadline that depends on something few families think to ask. Which government owns the road where it happened can compress the time to act down to forty-five days.

## svc_fatal_car_accident_claim_body
### Who Texas allows to bring the action

[Section 71.004(a)](https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/civil-practice-and-remedies-code/title-4/chapter-71/subchapter-a/section-71-004/) of the Civil Practice and Remedies Code gives the wrongful death action to three groups and to no others. The surviving spouse. The children. The parents. Subsection (b) lets any one of them file for the benefit of all, so the family does not need to agree before the claim is protected. A brother or a sister is not on that list, and neither is a grandparent or a grandchild. [Section 71.003(a)](https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/civil-practice-and-remedies-code/title-4/chapter-71/subchapter-a/section-71-003/) adds a quiet threshold: the action exists only if the person who died could have sued for that injury had they lived.

### Three calendar months, and a duty that shifts

Subsection (c) of the same section is the part almost nobody is told about. If none of those relatives has begun an action within three calendar months after the death, the executor or administrator of the estate is directed to bring and prosecute it, unless all of them ask that it not be filed. This is not a cutoff that erases the family's rights. It is a duty placed on whoever represents the estate, and it becomes real as soon as an estate has been opened. Read alongside the ordinary two-year period for a death claim, it explains why the calendar matters early rather than in year two.

### Which government owns the pavement

Austin reported 99 traffic deaths in 2025 and estimates that roughly 65 to 75 percent of the city's traffic fatalities happen on [roads owned by the state](https://www.austintexas.gov/transportation-public-works/mobility-matters/severe-crashes-show-downward-trend-2025). The city's own [Vision Zero performance review](https://www.austintexas.gov/sites/default/files/files/TPW/VisionZero/VZ-Analytics-Austin-Safety-Project-Performance-10.02.25.pdf) is blunter: deaths on city-owned streets have stayed relatively flat while deaths on state and other agencies' roadways rose. Across Travis County, [TxDOT counted](https://www.txdot.gov/content/dam/docs/division/trf/crash-records/2024/13.pdf) 139 fatal crashes and 155 traffic deaths in 2024. Ownership is not trivia in a claim. A crash on a state highway points toward a state agency, while a crash at a Bouldin Creek or East Cesar Chavez intersection may point toward the city, and each carries a different clock.

### Forty-five days, written into the city charter

Article XII, section 3 of the [Austin city charter](https://services.austintexas.gov/edims/document.cfm?id=292113) requires written notice, verified by affidavit, to the city council or the city manager within forty-five days. The notice must state when, where and how the death occurred, the apparent extent of the injury, the damages claimed, the claimant's residence, and the names and addresses of the witnesses being relied on. In the charter's own words, failure to give that notice exonerates and exempts the city from any liability whatsoever. The city's [claims instructions](https://www.austintexas.gov/services/file-claim) repeat the same 45-day window. The Tort Claims Act default in [section 101.101](https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/civil-practice-and-remedies-code/title-5/chapter-101/subchapter-d/section-101-101/) is six months, but subsection (b) ratifies charter periods, which is how six months becomes six weeks here.

### Naming the wrong defendant can end it

[Section 101.106](https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/civil-practice-and-remedies-code/title-5/chapter-101/subchapter-d/section-101-106/) treats the first filing as an irrevocable election. Suing the government employee immediately and forever bars any recovery from the governmental unit unless it consents, and suing the unit bars any recovery from the employee. Where a suit names both, the employee is dismissed on the unit's motion. Two other provisions narrow the target further. [Section 101.021](https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/civil-practice-and-remedies-code/title-5/chapter-101/subchapter-b/section-101-021/) waives immunity mainly for the operation or use of motor-driven vehicles and equipment, and for a condition or use of tangible property. [Section 101.056](https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/civil-practice-and-remedies-code/title-5/chapter-101/subchapter-c/section-101-056/) removes discretionary decisions from the chapter altogether, which is why a design choice and a maintenance failure are treated differently.

### What a public defendant can be made to pay

[Section 101.023](https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/civil-practice-and-remedies-code/title-5/chapter-101/subchapter-b/section-101-023/) sets the ceilings. A municipality is liable for death or bodily injury up to $250,000 for each person and $500,000 for each single occurrence. The state government carries the same two figures. A unit of local government that is not a municipality drops to $100,000 for each person and $300,000 for each occurrence. Those numbers are the outer edge of what a public entity can be ordered to pay under the chapter, whatever a jury might otherwise have found.

### The first two weeks, in practical terms

Record the agency that investigated, the report number, and the medical examiner case number if there is one. Do not let the vehicle be repaired, sold or scrapped while its condition is still a question. Save the adjuster's letters with their dates, because the date a request arrived can matter as much as its content. On fees, Texas puts no percentage limit on a death recovery. The protection sits in [Rule 1.04(d)](https://www.legalethicstexas.com/resources/rules/texas-disciplinary-rules-of-professional-conduct/fees/), which requires a signed writing stating separate percentages for settlement, trial and appeal. It must also list the expenses deducted and say whether they come off before or after the percentage.

## svc_fatal_work_accident_claim_lede
A death on the job opens two files at once. One is a benefit claim under the Labor Code, with its own list of relatives and its own one-year deadline. The other is the wrongful death action, and its list is shorter.

## svc_fatal_work_accident_claim_body
### Two lists of relatives, and they do not match

The wrongful death action belongs to a spouse, children and parents under [section 71.004](https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/civil-practice-and-remedies-code/title-4/chapter-71/subchapter-a/section-71-004/). Death benefits follow a different map. [Labor Code section 408.182](https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/labor-code/title-5/subtitle-a/chapter-408/subchapter-j/section-408-182/) pays an eligible spouse and eligible children first, then eligible grandchildren, and where none of those exist it pays equal shares to surviving dependents who are parents, stepparents, siblings or grandparents. A dependent brother can therefore draw benefits from the carrier and still hold no wrongful death claim of his own. Families discover that mismatch in the middle of the process, usually after someone has already been told they are covered.

### The one-year benefit deadline

The Division of Workers' Compensation asks a beneficiary to file [DWC Form-042](https://www.tdi.texas.gov/forms/dwc/dwc042benclm.pdf) with the division or the insurance carrier within one year of the employee's death. After that year the form itself lists the narrow openings: a claim for someone seventeen or younger, a claim for a person unable to make it, or a showing of good reason for the delay. Benefits are set at [75 percent](https://www.tdi.texas.gov/wc/employee/deathben.html) of the deceased employee's average weekly wage, subject to statutory limits, and burial expenses may be requested up to $10,000. Non-dependent eligible parents have their own rule under subsection (d-1), including one payment per household and a total that cannot exceed 104 weeks.

### Exemplary damages come from the constitution here

[Article XVI, section 26](https://law.justia.com/constitution/texas/sections/cn001600-002600.html) of the Texas Constitution reaches any person, corporation or company that commits a homicide through a willful act, an omission or gross neglect. Such a defendant is responsible in exemplary damages to the surviving husband, widow, or heirs of his or her body, and a criminal proceeding is beside the point. That wording is older than the modern statutes and narrower in one respect worth noticing. It names the spouse and the heirs of the body. It does not name parents, though [section 71.009](https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/civil-practice-and-remedies-code/title-4/chapter-71/subchapter-a/section-71-009/) separately allows exemplary damages in a death caused by a willful act or omission or by gross negligence.

### What the survival claim needs before it moves

[Section 71.021](https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/civil-practice-and-remedies-code/title-4/chapter-71/subchapter-b/section-71-021/) keeps the injured person's own claim alive and passes it to the heirs, legal representatives and estate. Somebody usually has to be appointed before that claim can be pursued, and in this county that means the [Travis County probate courts](https://www.traviscountytx.gov/probate), two statutory courts served by the County Clerk's probate division. Timing has a twist. [Section 16.062](https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/civil-practice-and-remedies-code/title-2/subtitle-b/chapter-16/subchapter-d/section-16-062/) suspends the running of an applicable limitations period for twelve months after a death, but if an executor or administrator qualifies before that year is out, the period starts running at the qualification instead.

### When the employer is a public body

A worker who died for the city, the county, a school district or the hospital district is inside the governmental-immunity system, not outside it. The city's forty-five day charter notice can apply, other units fall back on the six-month rule in [section 101.101](https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/civil-practice-and-remedies-code/title-5/chapter-101/subchapter-d/section-101-101/), and the ceilings in [section 101.023](https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/civil-practice-and-remedies-code/title-5/chapter-101/subchapter-b/section-101-023/) apply whatever the loss. A suit under the chapter also has to be brought where the cause of action arose and must name the unit itself as the defendant, under [section 101.102](https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/civil-practice-and-remedies-code/title-5/chapter-101/subchapter-d/section-101-102/).

### What to hold on to, and what not to sign

Ask for the incident report, the training file, the equipment inspection records and the schedule for the shift. Photograph the machine or the vehicle if it is still accessible, and do not let anyone modify it. Keep every letter from the carrier with its envelope. A recorded statement or a benefit election signed early can shape the record before the family knows which of the two systems it is standing in, so a document that arrives in the first week deserves a slower reading than it invites.

### How the fee side is regulated

Texas states no maximum percentage for a death case. [Rule 1.04](https://www.legalethicstexas.com/resources/rules/texas-disciplinary-rules-of-professional-conduct/fees/) instead forbids an unconscionable fee. For a contingent arrangement it requires a signed written agreement that separates the percentage on settlement, on trial and on appeal. The same agreement lists the expenses deducted and says whether they are subtracted before or after the percentage is applied.

## svc_medical_negligence_death_claim_lede
When a death follows treatment, two claims can arise from one chart. One belongs to the family. The other belongs to the estate, and it usually cannot move until a probate court has appointed somebody to hold it.

## svc_medical_negligence_death_claim_body
### The two claims, and who owns each one

The family's claim rests on [section 71.004](https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/civil-practice-and-remedies-code/title-4/chapter-71/subchapter-a/section-71-004/), which limits it to a surviving spouse, children and parents. The other claim is the patient's own, and [section 71.021](https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/civil-practice-and-remedies-code/title-4/chapter-71/subchapter-b/section-71-021/) keeps it from dying with them, passing it to the heirs, legal representatives and estate. Both are gated by [section 71.003(a)](https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/civil-practice-and-remedies-code/title-4/chapter-71/subchapter-a/section-71-003/), which applies the subchapter only where the patient could have brought the injury action had they lived. A death that follows care may also be a health care liability claim under chapter 74, which layers presuit notice and expert-report requirements on top of everything described here.

### Why an estate often has to be opened

The survival claim needs a legal representative, and that appointment happens in probate. Travis County runs [two statutory probate courts](https://www.traviscountytx.gov/probate), and the County Clerk's probate division handles the filing procedures, court costs, citations and the issuance of letters. Where there was no will, an application to determine heirship can run alongside the application for administration. None of this decides the medical question. It decides who is allowed to ask it on the patient's behalf.

### The twelve months that can quietly expire

[Section 16.062](https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/civil-practice-and-remedies-code/title-2/subtitle-b/chapter-16/subchapter-d/section-16-062/) suspends an applicable limitations period for twelve months after a death. Read the second sentence too. Once an executor or administrator qualifies, the period begins to run from that qualification, so appointing someone can start a clock rather than pause it. [Section 16.001](https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/civil-practice-and-remedies-code/title-2/subtitle-b/chapter-16/subchapter-a/section-16-001/) is the other narrow provision families ask about: time under a legal disability, meaning under eighteen or of unsound mind, is excluded from a limitations period, disabilities cannot be tacked together, and one arising after the period starts does not suspend it.

### When the medical examiner has the case

The Travis County Medical Examiner works under [article 49.25](https://www.traviscountytx.gov/medical-examiner/faq) of the Code of Criminal Procedure. That authority reaches unattended deaths, suspicious or unnatural deaths, and deaths where the cause is unknown. It also reaches children under six and deaths occurring less than twenty-four hours after admission to a hospital or another institution. That last category catches many hospital deaths that families assume the office would never see. The examination itself generally takes 12 to 24 hours. The office states that an autopsy report is public information under the Public Information Act unless an exception applies.

### Getting the autopsy report and the examiner's file

Requests go to the office at 7723 Springdale Road, or by mail and e-mail, using the [autopsy report request form](https://www.traviscountytx.gov/medical-examiner/records-request), which asks for the case number, the decedent's name, the date of death and the requester's relationship. The published [fee schedule](https://www.traviscountytx.gov/medical-examiner/fee-schedule) charges $25 for an autopsy report and $5 per investigation of death report, exempts the county legal next of kin from the autopsy report charge, and charges $50 to certify a record and $30 per disc of digital images. A certified copy is the version that a court file usually needs.

### A public provider moves the deadline forward

Central Health is [Travis County's taxpayer-funded hospital district](https://www.centralhealth.net/about/), created by voters in 2004, and it operates a clinic network across the county. A hospital district is a governmental unit, which puts the six-month notice rule in [section 101.101](https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/civil-practice-and-remedies-code/title-5/chapter-101/subchapter-d/section-101-101/) and the ceilings in [section 101.023](https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/civil-practice-and-remedies-code/title-5/chapter-101/subchapter-b/section-101-023/) in front of the ordinary rules. For a unit of local government other than a municipality those ceilings are $100,000 for each person and $300,000 for each single occurrence. Naming an individual clinician first can also trigger the election in [section 101.106](https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/civil-practice-and-remedies-code/title-5/chapter-101/subchapter-d/section-101-106/) and bar the claim against the district.

### Records to gather, in a workable order

Start with the complete chart from each facility rather than a discharge summary, then the medication administration record, the orders, the imaging, the monitoring data and the nursing notes. Add the ambulance run report and any transfer paperwork. Keep a simple log of what was requested, from whom, on what date and what came back. On fees, [Rule 1.04](https://www.legalethicstexas.com/resources/rules/texas-disciplinary-rules-of-professional-conduct/fees/) sets no percentage limit and instead requires the written terms and the expense treatment to be spelled out before anyone signs.

## svc_wrongful_death_damages_claim_lede
Damages in a Texas death case is not one number. It is a set of shares, decided relative by relative, sitting under different ceilings depending on who the defendant turns out to be.

## svc_wrongful_death_damages_claim_body
### One verdict, separate shares

[Section 71.010](https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/civil-practice-and-remedies-code/title-4/chapter-71/subchapter-a/section-71-010/) lets the jury award damages in an amount proportionate to the injury resulting from the death, then divides that award in the shares the jury finds among the individuals entitled to recover who are alive at that time. The practical effect is that a spouse, a minor child, an adult child and a parent each stand on their own proof. Age, dependency, the shape of the relationship and what the household actually lost are examined separately. A single settlement figure still has to be broken into those parts before anyone can say what it means for an individual family member.

### The relatives Texas leaves out

Because [section 71.004](https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/civil-practice-and-remedies-code/title-4/chapter-71/subchapter-a/section-71-004/) makes the action the exclusive property of a spouse, children and parents, everyone else is outside it. Siblings are the most common surprise, and grandchildren and grandparents are in the same position. Under subsection (b) one qualifying relative may bring the action for the benefit of all. Subsection (c) then directs the executor or administrator to bring it where none of them has done so within three calendar months of the death, unless all of them ask otherwise. A relative outside the statutory list may still matter as a witness, and may still be an heir for the separate estate claim under [section 71.021](https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/civil-practice-and-remedies-code/title-4/chapter-71/subchapter-b/section-71-021/), but they are not a wrongful death beneficiary.

### Exemplary damages, and an argument that is not settled

[Section 71.009](https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/civil-practice-and-remedies-code/title-4/chapter-71/subchapter-a/section-71-009/) allows exemplary as well as actual damages where the death was caused by a willful act or omission or by gross negligence. [Article XVI, section 26](https://law.justia.com/constitution/texas/sections/cn001600-002600.html) of the state constitution states the same right in older language. It runs to the surviving husband, widow, or heirs of the body, without regard to any criminal case. [Section 41.008(b)](https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/civil-practice-and-remedies-code/title-2/subtitle-c/chapter-41/section-41-008/) then caps exemplary damages at the greater of $200,000, or twice the economic damages plus any noneconomic damages the jury found, with that second component capped at $750,000. Whether a constitutional right can be limited by a statutory cap in a death case has been argued for decades. The honest answer is that Texas courts have not adopted it. One court of appeals [declined to hold the cap unconstitutional](https://caselaw.findlaw.com/court/tx-court-of-appeals/1092508.html) and noted that another court had approved applying it in wrongful death actions. In a 2005 [gross negligence death case](https://law.justia.com/cases/texas/supreme-court/2005/2000547.html) the Supreme Court of Texas resolved the appeal on the evidence, leaving untouched the court of appeals holding that the cap was not unconstitutional as applied.

### How the cap is now calculated

A [2026 opinion of the Supreme Court of Texas](https://www.txcourts.gov/media/1462759/240045.pdf) tightened the arithmetic. The cap is measured defendant by defendant, comparing the exemplary award against the economic damages attributable to that defendant's conduct based on its percentage of responsibility, not against the total economic damages awarded against everyone. In a case with a trucking company, a driver and a maintenance contractor, that holding can move the ceiling substantially, which is why responsibility percentages are not just a liability question.

### When the defendant is a government

[Section 101.023](https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/civil-practice-and-remedies-code/title-5/chapter-101/subchapter-b/section-101-023/) caps liability for death or bodily injury at $250,000 per person and $500,000 per occurrence against a municipality and against the state, and at $100,000 per person and $300,000 per occurrence against a unit of local government that is not a municipality. Notice comes first, though. Austin's charter demands written notice, verified by affidavit, within forty-five days, and [section 101.101](https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/civil-practice-and-remedies-code/title-5/chapter-101/subchapter-d/section-101-101/) expressly ratifies a charter period while setting six months as the default elsewhere. [Section 101.056](https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/civil-practice-and-remedies-code/title-5/chapter-101/subchapter-c/section-101-056/) also keeps discretionary decisions outside the chapter, which limits what a road design or a policy choice can support.

### The deadline, and the narrow ways it bends

An action for injury resulting in death is generally due within two years, and the clock starts on the death itself rather than on the day a report or a chart arrives. The tolling provisions are narrow. [Section 16.001](https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/civil-practice-and-remedies-code/title-2/subtitle-b/chapter-16/subchapter-a/section-16-001/) excludes time spent under a legal disability, [section 16.062](https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/civil-practice-and-remedies-code/title-2/subtitle-b/chapter-16/subchapter-d/section-16-062/) suspends an applicable period for twelve months after a death until a representative qualifies, and a public defendant's notice period can expire long before either of them matters. None of these is a reason to assume there is spare time.

### What a fee agreement has to disclose

No Texas rule caps the percentage taken from a death recovery. [Rule 1.04](https://www.legalethicstexas.com/resources/rules/texas-disciplinary-rules-of-professional-conduct/fees/) prohibits an unconscionable fee, lists eight factors for measuring reasonableness including the fee customarily charged locally for similar work, and requires a contingent agreement to be written, signed, and specific about the separate percentages for settlement, trial and appeal. It must also say which expenses are deducted and whether they come off before or after the percentage is applied. Since [section 71.010](https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/civil-practice-and-remedies-code/title-4/chapter-71/subchapter-a/section-71-010/) divides the recovery into shares, that percentage lands on each share rather than on one undivided sum.

## pricing_lede

Texas puts no percentage limit on a fee taken out of a death recovery, and it is Rule 1.04 rather than a rule numbered 1.5 that governs the arrangement. This page walks through what that rule demands of the writing, who chapter 71 lets bring the claim, how a jury's split of the damages sets each share, and what the Travis County clerks charge along the way.

## pricing_body

### The rule number is 1.04, and it names no percentage

Fee conduct in Texas sits in Rule 1.04 of the Texas Disciplinary Rules of Professional Conduct, not in a rule numbered 1.5 as it is in most states. Subdivision (a) prohibits an illegal fee and an unconscionable one, defining the latter in a single sentence: a fee is unconscionable if a competent lawyer could not form a reasonable belief that it is reasonable. No maximum percentage for a death claim appears in the rule or in the Civil Practice and Remedies Code. Two agreements offered to an Austin family are two proposals, judged against a professional judgment test rather than an arithmetic limit.

### The eight measures of a reasonable fee

Rule 1.04(b) sets out what goes into that judgment. The labor and hours a case demands, its novelty and difficulty, and the skill required. Whether the matter keeps the lawyer from other employment. The fee customarily charged in the locality for similar legal services, which is why an Austin comparison is not a Houston comparison. The amount involved and the results obtained. Time limits imposed by the client or by events. The length of the professional relationship, and whether the fee is fixed or contingent.

### What has to be on paper

Under Rule 1.04(c) the basis or rate of the fee has to be communicated to the client, preferably in writing, before or within a reasonable time after the representation begins. For a contingent arrangement, 1.04(d) goes further. The agreement must be written, signed by the client, and it must state the method for determining the fee, including the percentage accruing on settlement, on trial and on appeal. Those three numbers are frequently different. It must state the expenses deducted from the recovery and whether they come off before or after the percentage is figured. When the case closes, the client is owed a written accounting of the result and, where funds came in, the remittance and the calculation behind it.

### Who owns the claim, and what happens if nobody files

Section 71.004(a) of the Civil Practice and Remedies Code makes the wrongful death action the exclusive property of the surviving spouse, children and parents of the deceased. Under (b) any one of them, or all of them together, may bring it for the benefit of everyone entitled. Subsection (c) is the deadline most families never hear about. If none of those individuals has begun an action within three calendar months of the death, the executor or administrator must bring it. The exception is a request from all the beneficiaries that it not be filed. Section 71.021 keeps the deceased person's own injury claim alive separately, passing it to the heirs, legal representatives and estate.

### The jury splits the money before the fee is applied

Section 71.010 lets the jury award damages in a proportion it finds and then divides the recovery in the shares the jury sets. A verdict resolves into separate shares belonging to separate people, each with a different age, dependency and loss, and the percentage lands on each share rather than on one undivided pot. Where one of those people is a minor, that share cannot simply be handed over.

### A minor's share and the chapter 142 route

Property Code chapter 142 governs money recovered for a child with no legal guardian, who appears through a next friend or a guardian ad litem. Section 142.001 lets the court, on application and after a hearing, decree how the funds are invested. Section 142.004 limits the vehicles to a qualified tuition or ABLE account and federally insured interest-bearing time deposits, widening only slightly where the clerk of court invests on written order. Section 142.005 allows a court-created trust instead, and (b) fixes its terms, including that a minor's trust ends at death, at a stated age, or at the twenty-fifth birthday, whichever comes first. Subdivision (l) caps what the petitioning party may be reimbursed in attorney fees for setting the trust up at $1,000, and (m) and (n) restrict who may serve as trustee depending on whether the principal exceeds $50,000.

### What the Travis County clerks charge

The District Clerk's schedule effective January 1, 2026 puts the total base fee for a civil suit at $350, being a $213 local consolidated fee and a $137 state consolidated fee. Issuing a citation, subpoena or writ is $8. Service by certified mail through the District Clerk is $85, while a Travis County constable serving citations and notices charges $90 plus a separate $10 witness payment fee. A jury demand under Rule 216 costs $10. Later filings in the same case, including an intervention, a counterclaim, a third party petition or a motion for new trial, are $80 each. Money held in the court registry carries an administrative charge of 5 percent capped at $50 where it earns no interest, or 10 percent of the interest where it does. On the probate side the County Clerk charges $360 to apply for letters of administration, for a determination of heirship, or for a management trust. Add a $525 ad litem deposit in the situations the Clerk identifies, $2 to issue letters, and $25 to file an annual account.

## svc_accident_reconstruction_evidence_lede

Physical evidence in a fatal collision has a short life. Vehicles get repaired, video overwrites, and roadway conditions change, particularly along a corridor under active reconstruction. Preservation duties and access rules are both statutory.

## svc_accident_reconstruction_evidence_body

<h2>Vehicle recorder data is access-controlled</h2>
<p><a href="https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/transportation-code/title-7/subtitle-c/chapter-547/subchapter-k/section-547-615/">Section 547.615</a> of the Transportation Code addresses manufacturer-installed recording devices. Subsection (b) requires a manufacturer of a new vehicle sold or leased in Texas that is equipped with a recording device to disclose that fact in the owner's manual. Subsection (c) prohibits retrieval of recorded information by anyone other than the vehicle's owner except on court order, with the owner's consent, for the purpose of improving vehicle safety where the owner and driver are not identified, or to determine the need for or facilitate emergency medical response. Subsection (a) defines the owner as the person holding all incidents of ownership, including legal title, regardless of lending, renting or a security interest.</p>
<h2>A court order has a showing requirement</h2>
<p>Subsection (d) provides that for information from the categories described in subsection (a)(2)(B), a court order may issue only after a showing that retrieval is necessary to protect the public safety, or that the information is evidence of an offense or evidence that a particular person committed an offense. Subsection (f) treats subscription telematics differently: where a recording device is part of a subscription service, the agreement must disclose the recording, and the subsection (c) restriction does not apply. In practice, this means the download plan is a legal question before it is a technical one.</p>
<h2>The crash report and the officer's file</h2>
<p><a href="https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/transportation-code/title-7/subtitle-c/chapter-550/section-550-062/">Section 550.062</a> requires an investigating officer's written report of a crash resulting in injury, death, or apparent property damage of $1,000 or more, filed electronically not later than the tenth day after the crash. <a href="https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/transportation-code/title-7/subtitle-c/chapter-550/section-550-065/">Section 550.065</a> makes that information privileged while requiring release on written request to persons directly concerned, a definition that includes any person who may sue because of a death resulting from the collision, at a $6 fee or $8 certified.</p>
<h2>The spoliation standard, from the Supreme Court's own text</h2>
<p>The Texas Supreme Court set out the framework in Brookshire Brothers v. Aldridge, whose <a href="https://cases.justia.com/texas/supreme-court/2014-10-0846-1.pdf">opinion is published in full</a>. The court held that spoliation analysis is a two-step judicial process: the trial court determines as a question of law whether a party spoliated evidence, and if so assesses an appropriate remedy. To find spoliation, the court must find that the party had a duty to reasonably preserve evidence and that it intentionally or negligently breached that duty. Findings are made by the judge outside the jury's presence, and evidence bearing on spoliation is not presented to the jury except as it relates to the substance of the lawsuit.</p>
<h2>Why the harshest remedy is rare</h2>
<p>The same opinion holds that a spoliation instruction is warranted only where the trial court finds the spoliating party acted with the specific intent of concealing discoverable evidence and that a lesser remedy would be insufficient to reduce the prejudice. A negligent mental state can support an instruction only in the rare situation where the nonspoliating party has been irreparably deprived of any meaningful ability to present a claim or defense. Remedies must be proportionate, and the court identified culpability and prejudice as the key considerations. Applying that standard, the court held an instruction was an abuse of discretion where a store preserved the requested footage of a fall but allowed additional footage to be automatically erased. The lesson for a family is that a preservation letter sent early is worth far more than a sanctions motion later.</p>
<h2>A corridor that changes month to month</h2>
<p>Reconstruction in central Austin is complicated by the fact that the roadway itself is being rebuilt. TxDOT's <a href="https://www.txdot.gov/mobility35/projects/capex-central.html">Capital Express Central project</a> is removing the I-35 upper decks, lowering the mainlanes downtown and rebuilding the Lady Bird Lake bridge, with the limits <a href="https://www.txdot.gov/content/dam/project-sites/mobility35/documents/capex-central/capex-central-fact-sheet.pdf">described from US 290 East to SH 71 and Ben White Boulevard</a>. A scene photographed two months after a death may no longer show the lane taper, barrier position or signage that existed on the night in question, so contemporaneous imagery and the traffic control plan in effect that week carry disproportionate weight.</p>
<h2>The examiner's file is part of the reconstruction</h2>
<p>Where the Travis County Medical Examiner took the case, the office's findings bear directly on occupant position, impact mechanics and survivability. Its <a href="https://www.traviscountytx.gov/medical-examiner/fee-schedule">fee schedule</a> lists $25 for an autopsy report, $30 for a disc of digital images or of x-ray and CT files, $50 for certification and $500 per hour for testimony in a Travis County civil matter, and its <a href="https://www.traviscountytx.gov/medical-examiner/faq">FAQ</a> notes a report may take up to 90 days where laboratory work is pending and that an autopsy report is public under the Public Information Act. Also relevant: <a href="https://topics.txcourts.gov/LocalRulesPublic/PreviewAttachment/2121">Local Rule 13.3(a)</a> automatically refers jury-docket cases to pre-trial mediation, so the evidence record is usually tested at mediation before trial. This page is general information rather than legal advice about any crash, and the attorney who advertises on this site is the right person for a specific evidence question.</p>

## svc_apartment_and_premises_deaths_lede

A death inside a rented unit or on shared property raises duties that are written into the lease relationship itself. Texas puts several of them in statute, with written notice as the trigger.

## svc_apartment_and_premises_deaths_body

<h2>Smoke alarms are a statutory duty, not a courtesy</h2>
<p>Subchapter F of chapter 92 of the Property Code governs smoke alarms in rental housing. <a href="https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/property-code/title-8/chapter-92/subchapter-f/section-92-251/">Section 92.251</a> opens the subchapter, and <a href="https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/property-code/title-8/chapter-92/subchapter-f/section-92-258/">section 92.258</a> requires the landlord to inspect and repair a smoke alarm in accordance with the subchapter. <a href="https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/property-code/title-8/chapter-92/subchapter-f/section-92-259/">Section 92.259</a> sets out the landlord's liability where the duty to install, inspect or repair is not met after the tenant's request, and <a href="https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/property-code/title-8/chapter-92/subchapter-f/section-92-260/">section 92.260</a> preserves other rights and remedies. After a fatal fire, the alarm history, the maintenance ticket record and the written requests in the file become central evidence.</p>
<h2>Written notice, and what it starts</h2>
<p>The remedies in this subchapter are structured around a tenant's request and the landlord's response window, which is why the surviving family's copies of maintenance requests, portal tickets, emails and dated letters matter so much. Verbal complaints to an on-site office are the hardest facts to prove after the fact and the easiest to dispute. Where a unit changed hands, the prior management company's records may hold the notice that the current company's system does not.</p>
<h2>An ordinary premises claim still has to be proved</h2>
<p>Outside the smoke alarm provisions, a death on residential property is analyzed as a premises case: what the condition was, how long it existed, what the owner or manager knew or should have known, and what was done about it. Lighting, gate and lock function, stair and railing condition, pool access, balcony structure and prior similar incidents at the property are the usual subjects. Work orders, vendor invoices, inspection reports and prior complaint logs establish the timeline. Those records sit with the management company and its vendors, not with the family.</p>
<h2>Comparative responsibility applies here too</h2>
<p><a href="https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/civil-practice-and-remedies-code/title-2/subtitle-c/chapter-33/section-33-003/">Section 33.003</a> requires the trier of fact to assign percentages of responsibility in whole numbers among claimants, defendants, settling persons and designated responsible third parties. <a href="https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/civil-practice-and-remedies-code/title-2/subtitle-c/chapter-33/section-33-001/">Section 33.001</a> bars recovery only where the claimant's percentage exceeds 50 percent, and <a href="https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/civil-practice-and-remedies-code/title-2/subtitle-c/chapter-33/section-33-013/">section 33.013</a> generally limits each defendant to its own share. A third party who caused the death, such as an assailant or a contractor, may be designated, which redistributes percentages rather than eliminating the premises claim.</p>
<h2>Public housing and public property change the analysis</h2>
<p>Where the property is owned or operated by a governmental unit, <a href="https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/civil-practice-and-remedies-code/title-5/chapter-101/subchapter-b/section-101-021/">chapter 101</a> governs. <a href="https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/civil-practice-and-remedies-code/title-5/chapter-101/subchapter-c/section-101-056/">Section 101.056</a> keeps discretionary decisions outside the waiver, <a href="https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/civil-practice-and-remedies-code/title-5/chapter-101/subchapter-b/section-101-023/">section 101.023</a> caps recovery, and the deadline shortens sharply: <a href="https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/civil-practice-and-remedies-code/title-5/chapter-101/subchapter-d/section-101-101/">section 101.101(b)</a> ratifies a shorter charter period, and the City of Austin's <a href="https://www.austintexas.gov/services/file-claim">claim instructions</a> require a written claim within 45 days of the incident describing the date, location, occurrence and damages.</p>
<h2>Who brings the claim, and the estate's separate one</h2>
<p><a href="https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/civil-practice-and-remedies-code/title-4/chapter-71/subchapter-a/section-71-004/">Section 71.004</a> confines the death claim to the surviving spouse, children and parents and, after three calendar months of inaction, obliges the executor or administrator to bring it unless all beneficiaries request otherwise. <a href="https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/civil-practice-and-remedies-code/title-4/chapter-71/subchapter-b/section-71-021/">Section 71.021</a> carries the deceased person's own injury claim to the heirs, legal representatives and estate. Where the Travis County Medical Examiner investigated, that office <a href="https://www.traviscountytx.gov/medical-examiner/records-request">holds records</a> obtainable separately from the fire or police file.</p>

<p>No figure describing Austin's rental housing stock or apartment population appears on this page, because no primary source for one was verified in preparing it. This page is general information about Texas premises and rental-housing law, not legal advice, and it evaluates no property and no death. The attorney who advertises on this site is the appropriate person for a specific question.</p>

## svc_construction_fatality_claims_lede

Austin is rebuilding its central highway corridor and much else besides, and construction is the deadliest sector in Texas by a wide margin. A death on a jobsite runs into chapter 95 before it reaches ordinary premises law.

## svc_construction_fatality_claims_body

<h2>The statewide picture, from the state's own report</h2>
<p>The Texas Department of Insurance's report on <a href="https://www.tdi.texas.gov/wc/safety/sis/documents/2024fatalrpt.pdf">2024 fatal occupational injuries</a> counts 557 work-related fatalities in Texas at a rate of 3.9 per 100,000 full-time equivalent workers. Construction accounted for 128 of those incidents, roughly 23 percent of the total, and 125 private-sector construction deaths, about 24 percent of private-sector fatalities. Specialty trade contractors fell from 79 to 70. Construction and maintenance sites made up 15 percent of fatality locations. Falls, slips and trips accounted for 14 percent of all fatalities, and 34 percent of fatal falls involved a distance between 6 and 30 feet. Contact-with-object deaths were dominated by struck-by events at 56 percent.</p>
<h2>Chapter 95 raises the bar against a property owner</h2>
<p>Where the person who died worked for a contractor or subcontractor, <a href="https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/civil-practice-and-remedies-code/title-4/chapter-95/section-95-002/">section 95.002</a> applies the chapter to claims against a property owner, contractor or subcontractor arising from the condition or use of an improvement to real property that the contractor was constructing, repairing, renovating or modifying. <a href="https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/civil-practice-and-remedies-code/title-4/chapter-95/section-95-003/">Section 95.003</a> then bars owner liability, including liability for failing to provide a safe workplace, unless the owner exercised or retained some control over the manner in which the work was performed and had actual knowledge of the danger or condition and failed to adequately warn. The statute expressly excludes from control the mere right to order work to start or stop or to inspect progress and receive reports.</p>
<h2>Control and actual knowledge are documentary questions</h2>
<p>Because those two elements are conjunctive, a chapter 95 case is built from paper. Contract scope language, safety plan responsibilities, daily reports, toolbox talk records, inspection notes, correspondence about a hazard and prior incident reports are what establish retained control and prior awareness. Photographs and text messages sent by workers in the days before a death frequently carry more weight than the written safety manual. Those materials degrade quickly on an active project, which is why the request goes out early rather than after the site changes.</p>
<h2>The I-35 corridor is a live example</h2>
<p>TxDOT's <a href="https://www.txdot.gov/mobility35/projects/capex-central.html">Capital Express Central project</a> is removing the I-35 upper decks, lowering the mainlanes downtown and rebuilding the Lady Bird Lake bridge, with the work <a href="https://www.txdot.gov/content/dam/project-sites/mobility35/documents/capex-central/capex-central-fact-sheet.pdf">described from US 290 East to SH 71 and Ben White Boulevard</a>. Work of that character means multiple prime contractors, staged demolition, night operations and traffic control adjacent to the work face. The relevant question after a fatality is which entity controlled the specific operation at that hour, which is rarely the entity whose name appears on the project signage.</p>
<h2>The comp system frequently sits in the middle</h2>
<p>Where the employer carried coverage, <a href="https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/labor-code/title-5/subtitle-a/chapter-408/subchapter-a/section-408-001/">section 408.001(a)</a> of the Labor Code makes workers' compensation benefits the exclusive remedy of a legal beneficiary against that employer for the death. Subsection (b) preserves exemplary damages by a surviving spouse or heirs of the body where the death was caused by an intentional act or omission of the employer or by the employer's gross negligence, and subsection (c) borrows the <a href="https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/civil-practice-and-remedies-code/title-2/subtitle-c/chapter-41/subchapter-a/section-41-001/">section 41.001</a> definition of gross negligence. None of that bars claims against other entities on the site, which is where a general contractor, an equipment supplier or another trade may remain in play.</p>
<h2>A non-subscribing employer loses standard defenses</h2>
<p>Where the employer did not carry coverage, <a href="https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/labor-code/title-5/subtitle-a/chapter-406/subchapter-b/section-406-033/">section 406.033</a> removes contributory negligence, assumption of the risk and the negligence of a fellow employee as defenses, while subsection (d) still requires the plaintiff to prove the employer's negligence. Subsection (c) leaves two defenses available: an act of the employee intended to bring about the injury, and intoxication. Subsection (e) voids any pre-injury waiver. The state estimates non-subscription is concentrated among smaller firms, with <a href="https://www.tdi.texas.gov/wc/reg/nonsub2022.html">31 percent of employers with one to four employees outside the system</a> and 16 percent non-subscription in the mining, utilities and construction grouping.</p>

## svc_damages_a_family_may_recover_lede

Texas divides a death recovery into categories, allocates it in shares rather than as a family fund, and then applies statutory ceilings in specific kinds of cases. Knowing which category a loss falls into comes first.

## svc_damages_a_family_may_recover_body

<h2>The jury awards an amount, then divides it</h2>
<p><a href="https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/civil-practice-and-remedies-code/title-4/chapter-71/subchapter-a/section-71-010/">Section 71.010</a> directs the jury to award an amount proportionate to the injury resulting from the death and to divide that award into shares among the entitled individuals who are alive at the time. Each beneficiary's share rests on that person's own proof, which is why a spouse, an adult child living out of state and a dependent parent may receive very different amounts from the same verdict. <a href="https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/civil-practice-and-remedies-code/title-4/chapter-71/subchapter-a/section-71-004/">Section 71.004</a> confines those beneficiaries to the surviving spouse, children and parents.</p>
<h2>The vocabulary the statute uses</h2>
<p><a href="https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/civil-practice-and-remedies-code/title-2/subtitle-c/chapter-41/subchapter-a/section-41-001/">Section 41.001</a> defines economic damages as compensatory damages for actual economic or pecuniary loss, and then enumerates noneconomic damages in a list that runs from physical pain through mental or emotional anguish, consortium, disfigurement and impairment, on to companionship and society, inconvenience, enjoyment of life and reputation, closing with all other nonpecuniary losses of any kind. The same section defines future loss of earnings to include loss of income, wages or earning capacity and loss of inheritance. Exemplary damages are neither economic nor noneconomic under subsection (5), which is why they are analyzed separately.</p>
<h2>The estate's losses are a different column</h2>
<p>A recovery for the deceased person's own pre-death losses belongs to the survival action under <a href="https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/civil-practice-and-remedies-code/title-4/chapter-71/subchapter-b/section-71-021/">section 71.021</a>, which carries the injury claim to the heirs, legal representatives and estate. Medical charges incurred before death, conscious pain and mental anguish suffered by the person, and lost earnings during the injury period sit there rather than in the family's claim. Because the two claims answer to different owners, a settlement that does not allocate between them creates problems later in probate.</p>
<h2>Creditor protection, and where it does not reach</h2>
<p><a href="https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/civil-practice-and-remedies-code/title-4/chapter-71/subchapter-a/section-71-011/">Section 71.011</a> provides that damages recovered in a wrongful death action are not subject to the debts of the deceased. A survival recovery, by contrast, is an estate asset and moves through the estate's claims process. Where a hospital lien, a funeral balance or credit card debt exists, that distinction determines how much actually reaches the family, and it is one reason allocation is negotiated rather than left implicit.</p>
<h2>Statutory ceilings that override the evidence</h2>
<p>Three ceilings recur. Against a governmental unit, <a href="https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/civil-practice-and-remedies-code/title-5/chapter-101/subchapter-b/section-101-023/">section 101.023</a> limits recovery to $250,000 per person and $500,000 per occurrence for the state and a municipality, and $100,000 per person and $300,000 per occurrence for other local government units. In a health care death claim, <a href="https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/civil-practice-and-remedies-code/title-4/chapter-74/subchapter-g/section-74-303/">section 74.303</a> caps all damages at $500,000 per claimant, indexed to the CPI-W from August 29, 1977, while exempting necessary medical, hospital and custodial care expenses, and <a href="https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/civil-practice-and-remedies-code/title-4/chapter-74/subchapter-g/section-74-301/">section 74.301</a> caps noneconomic damages at $250,000 per claimant per defendant category.</p>
<h2>Fault percentages reduce what is collectible</h2>
<p><a href="https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/civil-practice-and-remedies-code/title-2/subtitle-c/chapter-33/section-33-013/">Section 33.013</a> generally holds a liable defendant responsible only for the percentage of damages matching its percentage of responsibility, with joint and several liability where that percentage exceeds 50 percent or the conduct falls within the narrow felony list. <a href="https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/civil-practice-and-remedies-code/title-2/subtitle-c/chapter-33/section-33-003/">Section 33.003</a> has percentages assigned in whole numbers to claimants, defendants, settling persons and designated responsible third parties, and <a href="https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/civil-practice-and-remedies-code/title-2/subtitle-c/chapter-33/section-33-001/">section 33.001</a> bars recovery where a claimant exceeds 50 percent.</p>
<h2>What a defendant may not argue about a spouse</h2>
<p><a href="https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/civil-practice-and-remedies-code/title-4/chapter-71/subchapter-a/section-71-005/">Section 71.005</a> allows evidence of an actual ceremonial remarriage of a surviving spouse where true, while prohibiting the defense from directly or indirectly mentioning or alluding to a common-law marriage, an extramarital relationship or the surviving spouse's marital prospects. Where a share is payable to a minor, chapter 142 of the Property Code and the county's own rules on withdrawing registry funds come into play. This page is general information rather than legal advice about any family's losses, and the attorney who advertises here is the person to ask.</p>

## svc_drowning_and_pool_fatalities_lede

Water is central to how Austin spends its summers, and drowning claims here run through two separate bodies of law: enclosure rules written for rental complexes, and a recreational-use chapter that limits what a public operator owes.

## svc_drowning_and_pool_fatalities_body

<h2>Enclosure standards written in inches</h2>
<p>Chapter 757 of the Health and Safety Code sets pool-yard enclosure standards, and <a href="https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/health-and-safety-code/title-9/subtitle-a/chapter-757/section-757-002/">section 757.002</a> states which properties it reaches. <a href="https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/health-and-safety-code/title-9/subtitle-a/chapter-757/section-757-003/">Section 757.003</a> requires the owner of a multiunit rental complex with a pool, or a property owners association that owns, controls or maintains a pool, to completely enclose the pool yard. The enclosure must stand at least 48 inches high measured from the ground on the side away from the pool. Openings underneath may not allow a four-inch sphere to pass. Where horizontal and vertical members are spaced at least 45 inches apart, openings may not pass a four-inch sphere; where that spacing is under 45 inches, the limit drops to 1-3/4 inches.</p>
<h2>Materials and cutouts are regulated as well</h2>
<p>Subsection (f) prohibits chain link fencing materials entirely for a new pool yard enclosure constructed after January 1, 1994, and prohibits diagonal fencing members lower than 49 inches above the ground on such enclosures. Subsection (g) limits decorative designs or cutouts to openings no greater than 1-3/4 inches in any direction. After a child's death, these measurements convert an argument about supervision into a measurable question about the barrier, which is why photographs with a tape measure in frame and a prompt inspection of gate latches and self-closing hardware matter more than recollection.</p>
<h2>The recreational use chapter shields public operators</h2>
<p>Austin operates public swimming water, including <a href="https://www.austintexas.gov/department/barton-springs-pool">Barton Springs Pool</a>, a three-acre spring-fed pool in Zilker Park with depths ranging to 18 feet and visitation approaching 800,000 in recent years. <a href="https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/civil-practice-and-remedies-code/title-4/chapter-75/section-75-001/">Section 75.001</a> of the Civil Practice and Remedies Code defines recreation to include swimming. <a href="https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/civil-practice-and-remedies-code/title-4/chapter-75/section-75-002/">Section 75.002(f)</a> then provides that where a person enters premises owned, operated or maintained by a governmental unit and engages in recreation, the governmental unit owes no greater degree of care than is owed to a trespasser. <a href="https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/civil-practice-and-remedies-code/title-4/chapter-75/section-75-003/">Section 75.003(g)</a> states that where the chapter limits a governmental unit's liability under circumstances in which chapter 101 would impose it, chapter 75 controls.</p>
<h2>What that standard leaves open</h2>
<p>The trespasser standard is not absolute immunity. Section 75.002 preserves liability for willful or wanton acts and gross negligence in the provisions addressing trespassers, and <a href="https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/civil-practice-and-remedies-code/title-2/subtitle-c/chapter-41/subchapter-a/section-41-001/">section 41.001(11)</a> supplies the gross negligence definition: an act or omission involving an extreme degree of risk viewed objectively, with the actor's actual subjective awareness of that risk and conscious indifference to it. Section 75.003(c) also confines the chapter, outside governmental units, to owners who do not charge for entry or whose recreational charges in the previous calendar year did not exceed 20 times the ad valorem taxes on the premises.</p>
<h2>Tort Claims Act limits still apply to a public defendant</h2>
<p>Where a claim against a governmental unit survives chapter 75, the <a href="https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/civil-practice-and-remedies-code/title-5/chapter-101/subchapter-b/section-101-021/">chapter 101</a> framework governs the waiver, <a href="https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/civil-practice-and-remedies-code/title-5/chapter-101/subchapter-c/section-101-056/">section 101.056</a> excludes discretionary decisions, and <a href="https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/civil-practice-and-remedies-code/title-5/chapter-101/subchapter-b/section-101-023/">section 101.023</a> caps damages at $250,000 per person and $500,000 per occurrence for a municipality. The notice period is the trap. The City of Austin's <a href="https://www.austintexas.gov/services/file-claim">claim instructions</a> call for a written claim within 45 days, which <a href="https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/civil-practice-and-remedies-code/title-5/chapter-101/subchapter-d/section-101-101/">section 101.101(b)</a> ratifies in place of the statute's six-month default.</p>
<h2>The county examiner takes drownings of young children</h2>
<p>The Travis County Medical Examiner's <a href="https://www.traviscountytx.gov/medical-examiner/faq">published guidance</a> lists the death categories the office accepts under article 49.25 of the Code of Criminal Procedure, including deaths of children under the age of six and deaths that are unexpected or from an unnatural cause. The office reports conducting its examination generally within 12 to 24 hours and notes a report may take up to 90 days to finalize where laboratory results are pending. Its <a href="https://www.traviscountytx.gov/medical-examiner/fee-schedule">fee schedule</a> charges $25 for the autopsy report and $30 for a disc of digital images, and the <a href="https://www.traviscountytx.gov/medical-examiner/records-request">records request page</a> describes how to ask.</p>
<h2>Who may bring the claim</h2>
<p><a href="https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/civil-practice-and-remedies-code/title-4/chapter-71/subchapter-a/section-71-004/">Section 71.004</a> restricts the death action to the surviving spouse, children and parents, and after three calendar months without a filing it obliges the estate's representative to bring it unless all beneficiaries request otherwise. Where a child died, <a href="https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/civil-practice-and-remedies-code/title-2/subtitle-b/chapter-16/subchapter-a/section-16-001/">section 16.001</a> governs how minority affects a limitations period for a surviving minor sibling's own claim. This page is general legal information rather than advice, and it reaches no view about any pool, lake or death; a specific question belongs with the attorney advertising on this site.</p>

In Austin the ownership of the pavement is often the first substantive question in a fatal crash, and it is not a formality. A state highway, a city arterial and an active work zone lead to different defendants, different notice periods and different records.

## svc_fatal_highway_crash_claims_body

<h2>The scale of the problem, in county numbers</h2>
<p>TxDOT's 2024 crash table for Travis County records <a href="https://www.txdot.gov/content/dam/docs/division/trf/crash-records/2024/13.pdf">139 fatal crashes and 155 traffic fatalities</a>, alongside 511 crashes producing suspected serious injuries, 610 suspected serious injuries and 15,872 reported crashes in total. The city's own <a href="https://www.austintexas.gov/transportation-public-works/mobility-matters/severe-crashes-show-downward-trend-2025">2025 review</a> reports 99 traffic deaths and 301 serious injuries within Austin, a 28 percent drop in serious injuries against 2024 and a slight increase in deaths, with 2022 still the high mark at 117 deaths and 540 serious injuries. The same review estimates that roughly 65 to 75 percent of Austin traffic fatalities occur on roads the state owns.</p>
<h2>An active reconstruction corridor changes the evidence</h2>
<p>The I-35 corridor through central Austin is a construction site rather than a settled roadway. TxDOT's <a href="https://www.txdot.gov/mobility35/projects/capex-central.html">Capital Express Central project</a> is removing the upper decks, lowering the mainlanes through downtown, rebuilding the Lady Bird Lake bridge and adding two non-tolled managed lanes in each direction, with the project <a href="https://www.txdot.gov/content/dam/project-sites/mobility35/documents/capex-central/capex-central-fact-sheet.pdf">described from US 290 East to SH 71 and Ben White Boulevard</a>. A death in a shifting work zone raises questions a routine crash does not: which contractor controlled that traffic-control plan on that date, what the lane configuration looked like that week, and whether the barrier, signage and taper matched the approved layout. Those records live with agencies and contractors, not with the family.</p>
<h2>The crash report, and who may obtain it</h2>
<p><a href="https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/transportation-code/title-7/subtitle-c/chapter-550/section-550-062/">Section 550.062</a> of the Transportation Code requires an investigating officer to report a crash that results in injury, death, or apparent property damage of $1,000 or more, and to file the report electronically not later than the tenth day after the crash. <a href="https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/transportation-code/title-7/subtitle-c/chapter-550/section-550-065/">Section 550.065</a> makes that report privileged but releases it on written request to persons directly concerned, a category the statute defines to include any person who may sue because of a death resulting from the collision. The fee is $6, or $8 with certification, and a redacted version is available to any requester.</p>
<h2>Suing a governmental road authority is a different exercise</h2>
<p>Where a public entity is implicated, immunity rules apply before negligence does. <a href="https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/civil-practice-and-remedies-code/title-5/chapter-101/subchapter-b/section-101-021/">Section 101.021</a> waives immunity principally for property damage, injury or death arising from the operation or use of a motor-driven vehicle or equipment, and for a condition or use of tangible personal or real property. <a href="https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/civil-practice-and-remedies-code/title-5/chapter-101/subchapter-c/section-101-056/">Section 101.056</a> keeps discretionary decisions outside the chapter entirely, which is the line between a design choice and a maintenance failure. <a href="https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/civil-practice-and-remedies-code/title-5/chapter-101/subchapter-d/section-101-106/">Section 101.106</a> makes the first filing an irrevocable election between the employee and the governmental unit.</p>
<h2>Notice periods that can expire before the file is built</h2>
<p>The Tort Claims Act default in <a href="https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/civil-practice-and-remedies-code/title-5/chapter-101/subchapter-d/section-101-101/">section 101.101</a> is six months, and subsection (b) expressly ratifies a shorter charter provision. Austin has one: the city's <a href="https://www.austintexas.gov/services/file-claim">claim instructions</a> require a written claim letter within 45 days of the incident, stating date, time, location, what occurred, the damages and supporting documents. Subsection (c) preserves a claim where the unit had actual notice of the death. Ceilings apply as well, and <a href="https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/civil-practice-and-remedies-code/title-5/chapter-101/subchapter-b/section-101-023/">section 101.023</a> sets them at $250,000 per person and $500,000 per occurrence for the state and for a municipality, and $100,000 per person and $300,000 per occurrence for other units of local government.</p>
<h2>Fault is apportioned in whole numbers</h2>
<p><a href="https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/civil-practice-and-remedies-code/title-2/subtitle-c/chapter-33/section-33-003/">Section 33.003</a> has the trier of fact assign a percentage of responsibility, in whole numbers, to each claimant, defendant, settling person and designated responsible third party. <a href="https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/civil-practice-and-remedies-code/title-2/subtitle-c/chapter-33/section-33-001/">Section 33.001</a> bars recovery where the claimant's own percentage is greater than 50 percent, and <a href="https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/civil-practice-and-remedies-code/title-2/subtitle-c/chapter-33/section-33-013/">section 33.013</a> limits each defendant to its own percentage unless that percentage exceeds 50 percent or the conduct falls into the narrow felony category the statute lists.</p>

## svc_filing_deadlines_in_texas_lede

Two years is the number families hear, and it is the number that ends the most claims when relied on alone. Several shorter clocks run at the same time, and one Travis County local rule can close a filed case.

## svc_filing_deadlines_in_texas_body

<h2>The two-year rule, and the date it starts</h2>
<p><a href="https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/civil-practice-and-remedies-code/title-2/subtitle-b/chapter-16/subchapter-a/section-16-003/">Section 16.003(b)</a> requires an action for injury resulting in death to be brought not later than two years after the day of the death. That is a different anchor from an ordinary injury claim, which runs from the injury. Where a person was hurt in one year and died in the next, the survival claim and the death claim can therefore carry different expiration dates on the same facts.</p>
<h2>Twelve months of suspension that can end early</h2>
<p><a href="https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/civil-practice-and-remedies-code/title-2/subtitle-b/chapter-16/subchapter-d/section-16-062/">Section 16.062</a> suspends the running of an applicable limitations period for twelve months after a death, and then supplies the exception: if an executor or administrator qualifies before that twelve months expires, limitations begins to run at the qualification date. Opening an estate to pursue a survival claim can accordingly shorten the very period the family was relying on. <a href="https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/civil-practice-and-remedies-code/title-2/subtitle-b/chapter-16/subchapter-a/section-16-001/">Section 16.001</a> excludes time during which a person is under a legal disability, meaning younger than 18 or of unsound mind, while providing that disabilities may not be tacked and that a disability arising after a period begins does not suspend it.</p>
<h2>Refiling after a jurisdictional dismissal</h2>
<p><a href="https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/civil-practice-and-remedies-code/title-2/subtitle-b/chapter-16/subchapter-d/section-16-064/">Section 16.064</a> suspends limitations between the first filing and a second filing of the same action in a different court where the first court lacked jurisdiction and the refiling happens within 60 days of the date the dismissal or other disposition becomes final. Subsection (b) removes that protection where the adverse party shows the first filing was made with intentional disregard of proper jurisdiction.</p>
<h2>Health care claims add their own timetable</h2>
<p><a href="https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/civil-practice-and-remedies-code/title-4/chapter-74/subchapter-h/section-74-251/">Section 74.251</a> sets a two-year health care liability period measured from the occurrence of the breach or tort, or from the last date of the relevant course of treatment or hospitalization. <a href="https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/civil-practice-and-remedies-code/title-4/chapter-74/subchapter-b/section-74-051/">Section 74.051</a> requires 60 days of written pre-suit notice by certified mail with the statutory authorization and tolls limitations for 75 days. Once suit is filed, <a href="https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/civil-practice-and-remedies-code/title-4/chapter-74/subchapter-i/section-74-351/">section 74.351</a> imposes a 120-day expert report deadline measured from each defendant's original answer.</p>
<h2>Government claims compress everything</h2>
<p><a href="https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/civil-practice-and-remedies-code/title-5/chapter-101/subchapter-d/section-101-101/">Section 101.101</a> requires notice of a claim against a governmental unit within six months of the incident, and subsection (b) expressly ratifies a shorter charter or ordinance provision. The City of Austin's <a href="https://www.austintexas.gov/services/file-claim">claim instructions</a> set a 45-day written claim requirement describing the date, time, location, occurrence, damages and supporting documents. Subsection (c) preserves a claim where the unit had actual notice of the death. <a href="https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/civil-practice-and-remedies-code/title-5/chapter-101/subchapter-d/section-101-106/">Section 101.106</a> makes the choice of filing against the employee or the unit an irrevocable election.</p>
<h2>The benefit claim has its own year</h2>
<p>The Division of Workers' Compensation's <a href="https://www.tdi.texas.gov/wc/employee/deathben.html">death benefits page</a> directs beneficiaries to file a claim, and <a href="https://www.tdi.texas.gov/forms/dwc/dwc042benclm.pdf">DWC Form-042</a> is the form used. That one-year benefit deadline is unconnected to the two-year civil period, runs in a different forum and is easy to lose while a family is still deciding whether to sue anyone.</p>
<h2>A Travis County rule that closes filed cases</h2>
<p>Filing is not the last deadline. Under <a href="https://topics.txcourts.gov/LocalRulesPublic/PreviewAttachment/2121">Local Rule 8.1</a> of the Travis County civil district courts, cases on file more than 180 days in which no answer has been filed are eligible for dismissal for want of prosecution on the court's own motion, as are cases on file more than 18 months that are not set for trial and have had no filings or settings within 180 days. Rule 8.3 provides that the Court Administrator gives notice and that such matters are dismissed summarily on the stated date unless a party acts. Under <a href="https://topics.txcourts.gov/LocalRulesPublic/PreviewAttachment/2121">Local Rule 13.3(a)</a>, all cases set for trial on the jury docket or the non-jury long docket are automatically referred to pre-trial mediation.</p>
<h2>Read this as background only</h2>
<p>Deadline analysis depends on dates, defendants and records that vary case by case, and a missed period is generally not curable. This page is general information about Texas and Travis County timing rules rather than legal advice about any claim, and a specific date question belongs with the attorney advertising on this site.</p>

## svc_medical_and_hospital_negligence_deaths_lede

A death claim against a hospital or physician in Texas is governed by chapter 74, which imposes pre-suit notice, an early expert report and statutory ceilings. Missing any one of those steps ends the claim regardless of the medicine.

## svc_medical_and_hospital_negligence_deaths_body

<h2>Sixty days of notice, and 75 days of tolling</h2>
<p><a href="https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/civil-practice-and-remedies-code/title-4/chapter-74/subchapter-b/section-74-051/">Section 74.051</a> requires written notice by certified mail to each physician or health care provider at least 60 days before filing suit, accompanied by the authorization form for release of protected health information. Subsection (c) tolls the applicable limitations period for 75 days following that notice. Subsection (d) requires the provider to furnish unaltered copies of requested records within 45 days. Subsection (e) is the provision that matters after a death: a request for records of a deceased person is valid where the authorization is signed by a parent, spouse or adult child.</p>
<h2>The expert report deadline is unforgiving</h2>
<p><a href="https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/civil-practice-and-remedies-code/title-4/chapter-74/subchapter-i/section-74-351/">Section 74.351(a)</a> requires service of one or more expert reports, with each expert's curriculum vitae, not later than the 120th day after each defendant's original answer is filed, absent written agreement to extend. A defendant must object to sufficiency within 21 days of service or the answer, whichever is later, or waive objections. Where no report is served in time, subsection (b) requires the court to act on motion, and subsection (c) allows a single 30-day extension to cure a deficient report. Subsection (s) stays discovery until the report is served, apart from records acquisition, and subsection (u) permits no more than two depositions before service.</p>
<h2>Two limitations rules, running side by side</h2>
<p><a href="https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/civil-practice-and-remedies-code/title-4/chapter-74/subchapter-h/section-74-251/">Section 74.251</a> sets the health care liability period at two years from the occurrence of the breach or tort, or from the last date of the relevant course of treatment or hospitalization. <a href="https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/civil-practice-and-remedies-code/title-2/subtitle-b/chapter-16/subchapter-a/section-16-003/">Section 16.003(b)</a> runs a death action from the date of death, and <a href="https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/civil-practice-and-remedies-code/title-2/subtitle-b/chapter-16/subchapter-d/section-16-062/">section 16.062</a> suspends limitations for twelve months after death, ending earlier if an executor or administrator qualifies within that period. A death following a long hospitalization can therefore present two different anchor dates in the same file.</p>
<h2>Ceilings that apply specifically to death cases</h2>
<p><a href="https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/civil-practice-and-remedies-code/title-4/chapter-74/subchapter-g/section-74-301/">Section 74.301</a> caps noneconomic damages at $250,000 per claimant against physicians and providers other than a health care institution, $250,000 against a single institution, and $250,000 per institution with a $500,000 aggregate where more than one institution is liable. <a href="https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/civil-practice-and-remedies-code/title-4/chapter-74/subchapter-g/section-74-303/">Section 74.303</a> is the wrongful death and survival provision: all damages, including exemplary damages, are limited to $500,000 per claimant, adjusted up or down by the change in the CPI-W since August 29, 1977, and subsection (c) exempts past and future medical, hospital and custodial care expenses from that limit.</p>
<h2>Who counts as a health care provider</h2>
<p><a href="https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/civil-practice-and-remedies-code/title-4/chapter-74/subchapter-a/section-74-001/">Section 74.001</a> supplies the definitions that decide whether chapter 74 applies at all, including the definitions of health care institution and health care provider. That classification determines which cap in section 74.301 applies, whether the expert report requirement is triggered and how the notice provision operates. Claims that look like ordinary negligence, such as a fall or a security failure inside a facility, are frequently recharacterized as health care liability claims, and that recharacterization brings the chapter 74 machinery with it.</p>
<h2>The Austin provider landscape adds public defendants</h2>
<p>Travis County has a hospital district: <a href="https://www.centralhealth.net/about/">Central Health</a> was created by county voters in 2004 to fund and coordinate care for residents with low incomes. A district, a public teaching program or a county facility can put governmental immunity into a medical case, which pulls in <a href="https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/civil-practice-and-remedies-code/title-5/chapter-101/subchapter-b/section-101-021/">chapter 101</a> with its caps in <a href="https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/civil-practice-and-remedies-code/title-5/chapter-101/subchapter-b/section-101-023/">section 101.023</a>, its notice provision in <a href="https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/civil-practice-and-remedies-code/title-5/chapter-101/subchapter-d/section-101-101/">section 101.101</a> and the irrevocable election in <a href="https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/civil-practice-and-remedies-code/title-5/chapter-101/subchapter-d/section-101-106/">section 101.106</a>. Chapter 74's requirements do not disappear because the defendant is public.</p>
<h2>An in-hospital death may still be an examiner case</h2>
<p>The Travis County Medical Examiner's <a href="https://www.traviscountytx.gov/medical-examiner/faq">guidance</a> explains that deaths occurring less than 24 hours after admission to a hospital fall within the categories the office accepts, along with deaths that are unexpected or attributable to an unnatural cause. An examiner file can therefore exist alongside the chart, with its own toxicology and its own timeline. Nothing here is legal advice about any treatment or death, and a specific medical file belongs with the attorney who advertises on this site.</p>

## svc_minor_beneficiaries_and_court_approval_lede

Where a child is among the beneficiaries, a Texas death recovery does not simply get paid. The court supervises how a minor's share is held, invested and later released, and Travis County adds its own procedure.

## svc_minor_beneficiaries_and_court_approval_body

<h2>The share belongs to the child, not the household</h2>
<p><a href="https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/civil-practice-and-remedies-code/title-4/chapter-71/subchapter-a/section-71-010/">Section 71.010</a> has the jury award an amount proportionate to the injury resulting from the death and divide that award into shares among the entitled individuals living at the time. A minor child's share is that child's property. A surviving parent who is also a beneficiary holds a share of their own, and the potential for that overlap to become a conflict is why courts appoint a guardian ad litem or require a next friend arrangement before approving anything.</p>
<h2>A bonded manager, at double the value</h2>
<p><a href="https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/property-code/title-10/subtitle-a/chapter-142/section-142-002/">Section 142.002</a> of the Property Code permits the court, in a suit where a minor or incapacitated person without a legal guardian is represented by a next friend or appointed guardian ad litem, to authorize that person to take possession of the recovery. Possession may not be taken until a bond is executed as principal in an amount at least double the value of the property, or at least equal to the value where a solvent authorized surety company signs, payable to the county judge. <a href="https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/property-code/title-10/subtitle-a/chapter-142/section-142-001/">Section 142.001</a> governs the decree that authorizes the arrangement.</p>
<h2>Where the money may be placed</h2>
<p><a href="https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/property-code/title-10/subtitle-a/chapter-142/section-142-004/">Section 142.004</a> lists permitted investments where funds are not otherwise managed under the chapter, including a higher education savings plan or prepaid tuition program under chapter 54 of the Education Code, an ABLE account under that chapter's Texas Achieving a Better Life Experience program, and interest-bearing time deposits in a Texas financial institution insured by the Federal Deposit Insurance Corporation. The list is a constraint rather than a menu of suggestions.</p>
<h2>The section 142.005 trust and its end date</h2>
<p><a href="https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/property-code/title-10/subtitle-a/chapter-142/section-142-005/">Section 142.005</a> allows a court of record, on application and a finding that a trust serves the beneficiary's best interests, to direct the clerk to deliver funds to a financial institution as trustee. Where the beneficiary is a minor not considered disabled under the federal provisions the statute cites, the trust terminates on the beneficiary's death, on an age stated in the trust, or on the beneficiary's 25th birthday, whichever occurs first. Subsection (l) caps reimbursement from the trust for reasonable attorney's fees incurred in bringing the petition at $1,000. Subsections (m) and (n) allow a non-institutional trustee where principal is $50,000 or less only on a best-interests finding, and impose additional findings above $50,000.</p>
<h2>Funds held in the court registry</h2>
<p>Where a share sits in the court's registry, the county charges for holding it. The District Clerk's <a href="https://www.traviscountytx.gov/images/district_clerk/Docs/Travis-County-District-Clerk-Filing-Fees-2026.pdf">fee schedule effective January 1, 2026</a> lists a court registry administrative fee of 5 percent capped at $50 on funds not earning interest under Local Government Code section 117.055, and 10 percent of interest accrued on funds that do earn interest under section 117.054. The clerk's <a href="https://www.traviscountytx.gov/district-clerk/fees">civil fee page</a> publishes the current schedule.</p>
<h2>The Travis County step families do not expect</h2>
<p>Withdrawing a minor's registry funds later has a specific local requirement. Under <a href="https://topics.txcourts.gov/LocalRulesPublic/PreviewAttachment/2121">Local Rule 7.8</a> of the civil district courts, an applicant requesting withdrawal of funds held in the registry for a minor who has not reached legal age must bring to the court, with the motion and proposed order, a Summary of Minor's Bank Account obtained from the District Clerk reflecting the account status and indicating previous withdrawals. A motion filed without that summary is not ready to be heard, which is a common source of delay years after a case has closed.</p>
<h2>Approval, mediation and the central docket</h2>
<p>Because <a href="https://topics.txcourts.gov/LocalRulesPublic/PreviewAttachment/2121">Local Rule 13.3(a)</a> automatically refers jury-docket cases to pre-trial mediation, minor allocation issues are frequently addressed in a mediated settlement that is then presented for approval. Under <a href="https://topics.txcourts.gov/LocalRulesPublic/PreviewAttachment/2121">Local Rule 1.3</a> the case number in the style does not identify the judge who will hear the matter, since the county's courts operate a central docket with hearings assigned to available judges. The <a href="https://www.traviscountytx.gov/courts/civil/district">civil courts sit at 1700 Guadalupe Street</a>.</p>

## svc_motorcycle_fatality_claims_lede

A motorcycle death file usually arrives with a helmet question attached, and Texas law answers it more narrowly than most drivers assume. The percentage of responsibility a jury assigns often decides the outcome.

## svc_motorcycle_fatality_claims_body

<h2>The helmet rule, and the adult exception written into it</h2>
<p><a href="https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/transportation-code/title-7/subtitle-g/chapter-661/subchapter-a/section-661-003/">Section 661.003</a> of the Transportation Code creates an offense where a person operates a motorcycle, or rides on one as a passenger, on any public street or highway in Texas without protective headgear meeting department safety standards. Subsection (c) then supplies an exception where the rider was at least 21 years old and either completed a motorcycle operator training and safety course under chapter 662 or was covered by a health insurance plan providing medical benefits for injuries from a motorcycle collision. Subsection (h) sets the penalty for a violation at a fine of not less than $10 or more than $50, which places the rule in the lowest tier of Texas offenses.</p>
<h2>An officer may not stop a rider to check</h2>
<p>Subsection (c-1) prohibits a peace officer from stopping or detaining an operator or passenger for the sole purpose of determining whether the course was completed or insurance is in place. Subsection (c-2) directs the Texas Department of Insurance to prescribe a standard proof of health insurance for riders 21 and over. Those provisions matter after a death because a defense narrative that treats headgear as the central legal issue is describing a $10 to $50 offense subject to an exception the statute itself supplies. Whether the absence of a helmet contributed to this death remains a medical and engineering question rather than a citation question.</p>
<h2>Austin's own trend is not moving in the riders' favor</h2>
<p>The city's <a href="https://www.austintexas.gov/transportation-public-works/mobility-matters/severe-crashes-show-downward-trend-2025">2025 severe crash review</a> reports motorcyclist serious injuries dropping from 62 in 2024 to 44 in 2025, while deaths among motorcyclists rose over the same period even as citywide serious injuries fell 28 percent. Austin closed 2025 with 99 traffic deaths and 301 serious injuries against a 2022 peak of 117 and 540. Across the county, TxDOT logged <a href="https://www.txdot.gov/content/dam/docs/division/trf/crash-records/2024/13.pdf">139 fatal crashes and 155 traffic fatalities in 2024</a>. The city also estimates most of its traffic deaths occur on state-owned roads, which is where lane drops, resurfaced joints and shifting work-zone striping concentrate.</p>
<h2>Left-turn and lane-position facts do the real work</h2>
<p>Most fatal motorcycle collisions here reduce to whether a driver saw the motorcycle and whether the rider had a lane to use. <a href="https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/transportation-code/title-7/subtitle-c/chapter-551/subchapter-c/section-551-101/">Section 551.101</a> is a reminder that a two-wheeled operator carries the rights and duties of a driver of a vehicle, and the same principle informs how a jury weighs positioning within a lane. Sight lines, approach speed, signal phase, the angle of a turn across traffic and roadway surface condition are the facts that decide fault, and they are established through scene evidence and vehicle inspection rather than through assumptions about riders.</p>
<h2>Percentages, in whole numbers, assigned to everyone</h2>
<p><a href="https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/civil-practice-and-remedies-code/title-2/subtitle-c/chapter-33/section-33-003/">Section 33.003</a> directs the trier of fact to determine the percentage of responsibility, stated in whole numbers, for each claimant, each defendant, each settling person and each designated responsible third party. <a href="https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/civil-practice-and-remedies-code/title-2/subtitle-c/chapter-33/section-33-001/">Section 33.001</a> bars recovery only where the claimant's percentage exceeds 50 percent, so a rider found 40 percent responsible does not lose the claim. <a href="https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/civil-practice-and-remedies-code/title-2/subtitle-c/chapter-33/section-33-013/">Section 33.013</a> then confines each defendant to its own percentage of the damages unless that percentage exceeds 50 percent or the conduct falls within the felony list the section sets out.</p>
<h2>The report, and who is entitled to it</h2>
<p><a href="https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/transportation-code/title-7/subtitle-c/chapter-550/section-550-062/">Section 550.062</a> requires the investigating officer's report on a crash involving injury, death or apparent damage of $1,000 or more, filed electronically not later than the tenth day after the crash. <a href="https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/transportation-code/title-7/subtitle-c/chapter-550/section-550-065/">Section 550.065</a> treats the report as privileged but requires release on written request to a person directly concerned, and the statute expressly includes any person who may sue because of a death resulting from the collision. The charge is $6, and $8 where a certified copy is required.</p>
<h2>Preservation steps specific to a motorcycle</h2>
<p>Two vehicles must be preserved rather than one, and a motorcycle is easier to move, part out or scrap than a car. Keep it in storage untouched, with the helmet and the rider's gear, and record where each item was recovered. Some passenger vehicles store crash data, and access to that data is governed by <a href="https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/transportation-code/title-7/subtitle-c/chapter-547/subchapter-m/section-547-615/">section 547.615</a>. Where the Travis County Medical Examiner accepted the case, that office's <a href="https://www.traviscountytx.gov/medical-examiner/records-request">records request process</a> is separate from the hospital's.</p>

<p>The description above is general information about Texas motorcycle and comparative-fault rules and Austin crash data, not legal advice, and it evaluates no rider, no crash and no defense. An attorney advertising on this site can review the specifics of a particular death.</p>

## svc_nursing_home_and_care_facility_deaths_lede

A death in a nursing home sits inside two overlapping regimes: the resident rights written into the Health and Safety Code, and the health care liability rules of chapter 74 that treat the facility as a provider.

## svc_nursing_home_and_care_facility_deaths_body

<h2>Rights the statute grants each resident</h2>
<p><a href="https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/health-and-safety-code/title-4/subtitle-b/chapter-242/subchapter-l/section-242-501/">Section 242.501</a> of the Health and Safety Code directs the licensing agency to adopt rules preserving a list of resident rights and requires each institution to implement them. The list includes the right to be free from physical and mental abuse, the right to be free from chemical or physical restraints except as authorized in writing by a physician for a specified period or in an emergency, the right to have a guardian or family member place an electronic monitoring device in the resident's room, and rights concerning notice, records and complaints. A death claim frequently begins with which of those rights was documented as exercised or denied.</p>
<h2>Consent for psychoactive medication is separately regulated</h2>
<p><a href="https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/health-and-safety-code/title-4/subtitle-b/chapter-242/subchapter-l/section-242-505/">Section 242.505</a> addresses the administration of psychoactive medication to a resident, including the consent requirements and the information the facility must provide. Where sedation, falls or aspiration figure in a death, the medication administration record, the physician orders and the consent documentation are the primary evidence rather than the incident narrative written afterward. Care plans, weight and hydration logs, wound assessments and staffing schedules complete the picture, and each is a distinct document request.</p>
<h2>Chapter 74 treats the facility as a health care provider</h2>
<p><a href="https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/civil-practice-and-remedies-code/title-4/chapter-74/subchapter-a/section-74-001/">Section 74.001</a> of the Civil Practice and Remedies Code defines health care institution and health care provider, and nursing homes fall inside those definitions. The practical consequence is that a claim over a fall, a pressure injury, an elopement or a medication error is a health care liability claim. That brings the <a href="https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/civil-practice-and-remedies-code/title-4/chapter-74/subchapter-b/section-74-051/">section 74.051</a> pre-suit notice with its 60-day period and 75-day tolling, and the <a href="https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/civil-practice-and-remedies-code/title-4/chapter-74/subchapter-i/section-74-351/">section 74.351</a> expert report due within 120 days of each defendant's answer, with one possible 30-day cure period.</p>
<h2>The caps that apply to a facility death</h2>
<p><a href="https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/civil-practice-and-remedies-code/title-4/chapter-74/subchapter-g/section-74-301/">Section 74.301</a> limits noneconomic damages to $250,000 per claimant against a single health care institution, with a $500,000 aggregate where more than one institution is liable. <a href="https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/civil-practice-and-remedies-code/title-4/chapter-74/subchapter-g/section-74-303/">Section 74.303</a> limits all damages in a wrongful death or survival action against a physician or health care provider to $500,000 per claimant, indexed to the CPI-W from August 29, 1977, while exempting necessary medical, hospital and custodial care expenses. Where the resident's own pre-death suffering is at issue, the survival claim under <a href="https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/civil-practice-and-remedies-code/title-4/chapter-71/subchapter-b/section-71-021/">section 71.021</a> carries it.</p>
<h2>Records, and the authorization a family can sign</h2>
<p><a href="https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/civil-practice-and-remedies-code/title-4/chapter-74/subchapter-b/section-74-051/">Section 74.051(d)</a> requires a provider to furnish unaltered copies of requested records within 45 days of a request. Subsection (e) confirms that a request for a deceased person's records is valid where the authorization is signed by a parent, spouse or adult child, which resolves the question families most often get stuck on when a facility declines to release a chart. Requesting the complete record, including the electronic audit trail, rather than a summary is the practical difference between a usable file and a curated one.</p>
<h2>The examiner's jurisdiction reaches recent admissions</h2>
<p>The Travis County Medical Examiner's <a href="https://www.traviscountytx.gov/medical-examiner/faq">FAQ</a> states the office accepts deaths occurring less than 24 hours after admission to a nursing home, along with unexpected deaths and deaths from unnatural causes, under the article 49.25 categories. That means a death shortly after a transfer between facilities may generate an independent investigation. The office's <a href="https://www.traviscountytx.gov/medical-examiner/fee-schedule">fee schedule</a> lists $25 for an autopsy report, $5 for an investigation of death report and $50 to certify a record, and its <a href="https://www.traviscountytx.gov/medical-examiner/records-request">records page</a> explains the request route.</p>
<h2>Deadlines, and the estate question behind them</h2>
<p><a href="https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/civil-practice-and-remedies-code/title-4/chapter-74/subchapter-h/section-74-251/">Section 74.251</a> sets a two-year health care liability period, and <a href="https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/civil-practice-and-remedies-code/title-2/subtitle-b/chapter-16/subchapter-d/section-16-062/">section 16.062</a> suspends limitations for twelve months after a death, or until an executor or administrator qualifies within that window. Because a survival claim usually requires a representative, the county's <a href="https://www.traviscountytx.gov/probate">statutory probate courts</a> often become part of the timeline. This page is general information, not legal advice, and it assesses no facility or resident; that discussion belongs with the attorney advertising on this site.</p>

Texas writes specific duties for drivers around people on foot and on bicycles, and Austin's own data shows where those duties fail. A fatality claim usually turns on the rule that applied at that exact spot.

<h2>What Austin's numbers actually show</h2>
<p>The city's <a href="https://www.austintexas.gov/transportation-public-works/mobility-matters/severe-crashes-show-downward-trend-2025">2025 severe crash review</a> reports pedestrian deaths falling from 39 in 2024 to 30 in 2025, with pedestrian serious injuries down from 59 to 50 and bicyclist serious injuries from 27 to 21. The city also estimates that most of its traffic deaths happen on state-owned roadway rather than city streets, which decides which agency's records and which notice period matter.</p>
<h2>The crosswalk duty, and the passing prohibition beside it</h2>
<p><a href="https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/transportation-code/title-7/subtitle-c/chapter-552/section-552-003/">Section 552.003</a> of the Transportation Code requires an operator to stop and yield the right-of-way to a pedestrian crossing in a crosswalk where no signal is in place or in operation, and it applies where the pedestrian is on the operator's half of the roadway or approaching so closely as to be in danger. Subsection (c) then prohibits passing a vehicle that has stopped at a crosswalk to permit a pedestrian to cross. That second rule is the one behind many multi-lane deaths, where the first driver stops and the driver in the adjacent lane does not.</p>
<h2>The general duty applies even outside a crosswalk</h2>
<p><a href="https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/transportation-code/title-7/subtitle-c/chapter-552/section-552-008/">Section 552.008</a> requires an operator to exercise due care to avoid colliding with a pedestrian, to sound the horn when necessary, and to exercise proper precaution on observing a child or an obviously confused or incapacitated person. A pedestrian outside a marked crossing is therefore not outside the law's protection. Section 552.003(d) adds an enhanced penalty where the offense causes serious bodily injury or death to a pedestrian who is blind or has a disability, including a fine and 30 hours of community service.</p>
<h2>A cyclist is a driver, with lane rules of their own</h2>
<p><a href="https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/transportation-code/title-7/subtitle-c/chapter-551/subchapter-c/section-551-101/">Section 551.101</a> gives a person operating a bicycle the rights and duties applicable to a driver of a vehicle. <a href="https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/transportation-code/title-7/subtitle-c/chapter-551/subchapter-c/section-551-103/">Section 551.103</a> directs a cyclist moving slower than other traffic to ride as near as practicable to the right curb, then lists the exceptions that matter in a claim: passing, preparing to turn left, avoiding an unsafe condition, and riding on a roadway where the outside lane is less than 14 feet wide with no designated bike lane. Persons may also ride two abreast where they do not impede traffic. Those exceptions are frequently the answer to a defense that the cyclist should have been further right.</p>
<h2>Which government owns that intersection</h2>
<p>Ownership drives the deadline. Where the city is implicated, its <a href="https://www.austintexas.gov/services/file-claim">claim process</a> sets a 45-day written notice, and <a href="https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/civil-practice-and-remedies-code/title-5/chapter-101/subchapter-d/section-101-101/">section 101.101(b)</a> ratifies that shorter charter period against the statute's six-month default. Where the roadway belongs to the state, a different unit and a different file are in play. <a href="https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/civil-practice-and-remedies-code/title-5/chapter-101/subchapter-c/section-101-056/">Section 101.056</a> also removes discretionary policy and design decisions from the chapter, so a signal timing choice and a broken signal head are not treated alike.</p>

<p>Because <a href="https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/civil-practice-and-remedies-code/title-2/subtitle-c/chapter-33/section-33-003/">section 33.003</a> requires percentages assigned in whole numbers to every claimant and defendant, a defense in these cases is usually built around the decedent's own share. <a href="https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/civil-practice-and-remedies-code/title-2/subtitle-c/chapter-33/section-33-001/">Section 33.001</a> bars recovery only where that share exceeds 50 percent, and <a href="https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/civil-practice-and-remedies-code/title-2/subtitle-c/chapter-33/section-33-013/">section 33.013</a> then holds each defendant to its own percentage. Lighting, sight lines, crossing distance, signal phase and vehicle speed all feed those numbers, which is why the physical scene is worth documenting before it is altered.</p>

<p>The officer's crash report is filed electronically under <a href="https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/transportation-code/title-7/subtitle-c/chapter-550/section-550-062/">section 550.062</a> not later than the tenth day, and <a href="https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/transportation-code/title-7/subtitle-c/chapter-550/section-550-065/">section 550.065</a> allows a person who may sue because of a death to obtain it for a $6 fee. Nearby business and transit video usually overwrites within days. ## svc_punitive_damages_standard_lede

Exemplary damages in a Texas death case are governed by a statutory standard, a unanimity requirement and a formula that caps the award. Each element is written down, and each one narrows the category.

## svc_punitive_damages_standard_body

<h2>The three grounds, and the burden attached to them</h2>
<p><a href="https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/civil-practice-and-remedies-code/title-2/subtitle-c/chapter-41/subchapter-a/section-41-003/">Section 41.003</a> permits exemplary damages only where the claimant proves by clear and convincing evidence that the harm resulted from fraud, malice or gross negligence. Subsection (b) states that the burden may not be shifted to the defendant or satisfied by evidence of ordinary negligence, bad faith or a deceptive trade practice. Subsection (d) adds that exemplary damages may be awarded only where the jury was unanimous both on liability for and on the amount of those damages. In a death case, <a href="https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/civil-practice-and-remedies-code/title-4/chapter-71/subchapter-a/section-71-009/">section 71.009</a> authorizes exemplary as well as actual damages where the death was caused by a willful act or omission or by gross negligence.</p>
<h2>The definitions do most of the work</h2>
<p><a href="https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/civil-practice-and-remedies-code/title-2/subtitle-c/chapter-41/subchapter-a/section-41-001/">Section 41.001</a> defines clear and convincing as the measure of proof producing a firm belief or conviction as to the truth of the allegations. Malice means a specific intent to cause substantial injury or harm to the claimant. Gross negligence means an act or omission which, viewed objectively from the actor's standpoint at the time, involves an extreme degree of risk considering the probability and magnitude of potential harm, and of which the actor had actual, subjective awareness while proceeding with conscious indifference to the rights, safety or welfare of others. Both prongs must be proved, which is why internal awareness documents dominate this analysis.</p>
<h2>The cap is arithmetic, not discretion</h2>
<p><a href="https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/civil-practice-and-remedies-code/title-2/subtitle-c/chapter-41/subchapter-b/section-41-008/">Section 41.008(b)</a> limits exemplary damages against a defendant to the greater of $200,000, or two times economic damages plus an amount equal to noneconomic damages found by the jury up to $750,000. Subsection (a) requires the trier of fact to determine economic damages separately from other compensatory damages, which is what makes the formula operable. Subsection (c) removes the cap for claims based on conduct described as a felony in the listed Penal Code sections, including murder and capital murder, where, apart from the intoxication assault and manslaughter provisions, the conduct was committed knowingly or intentionally.</p>
<h2>The jury is not told about the ceiling</h2>
<p>Subsection (e) of section 41.008 provides that the section's provisions may not be made known to a jury by any means, including voir dire, introduction into evidence, argument or instruction. The cap is therefore applied by the court after a verdict rather than argued to jurors, and a headline verdict number frequently bears little relation to the judgment that follows. Subsection (f) exempts causes of action arising from methamphetamine manufacture described in chapter 99.</p>
<h2>Net worth is admissible, but only in the second phase</h2>
<p><a href="https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/civil-practice-and-remedies-code/title-2/subtitle-c/chapter-41/subchapter-a/section-41-011/">Section 41.011(a)</a> lists what the trier of fact considers in setting an amount: the nature of the wrong, the character of the conduct, the degree of culpability of the wrongdoer, the situation and sensibilities of the parties, the extent to which the conduct offends a public sense of justice and propriety, and the defendant's net worth. Subsection (b) then makes evidence relevant only to the amount inadmissible during the first phase of a bifurcated trial. <a href="https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/civil-practice-and-remedies-code/title-2/subtitle-c/chapter-41/subchapter-a/section-41-001/">Section 41.001(7-a)</a> defines net worth as total assets minus total liabilities on a date the trial court determines appropriate.</p>
<h2>How the cap is computed with several defendants</h2>
<p>The Texas Supreme Court addressed the computation question in an <a href="https://www.txcourts.gov/media/1462759/240045.pdf">opinion in cause number 24-0045</a>, which is available in full from the court's own site. Reading the opinion rather than a summary is the only reliable way to apply it, because the arithmetic changes materially depending on whether the statutory formula is applied to the damages attributable to each defendant or to the total. This page does not restate the holding.</p>
<h2>The workplace variant of this claim</h2>
<p>Where the person who died was covered by workers' compensation, <a href="https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/labor-code/title-5/subtitle-a/chapter-408/subchapter-a/section-408-001/">section 408.001(a)</a> of the Labor Code makes benefits the exclusive remedy against the employer, while subsection (b) preserves exemplary damages for a surviving spouse or heirs of the body where the death was caused by an intentional act or omission or by the employer's gross negligence, adopting the section 41.001 definition. That is the narrow route through exclusivity, and it is a clear-and-convincing route.</p>

<p>The description above summarizes statutory text and identifies where the controlling opinion can be read. It is general information rather than legal advice, and it offers no view about whether exemplary damages are available in any particular death case. That assessment belongs with the attorney who advertises on this site.</p>

## svc_survival_actions_under_chapter_71_lede

One death can produce two claims. The family's claim looks forward at what the household lost. The survival action looks backward at what was done to the person who died, and it belongs to the estate.

## svc_survival_actions_under_chapter_71_body

<h2>The claim that does not die with the person</h2>
<p><a href="https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/civil-practice-and-remedies-code/title-4/chapter-71/subchapter-b/section-71-021/">Section 71.021</a> provides that a cause of action for personal injury to the health, reputation or person of an injured individual does not abate because of the death, and that it survives in favor of the heirs, the legal representatives and the estate. The injured person's own losses are the subject: the medical charges incurred before death, conscious physical pain and mental anguish, lost earnings during the period of injury, and disfigurement or impairment they lived with. A wrongful death claim under <a href="https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/civil-practice-and-remedies-code/title-4/chapter-71/subchapter-a/section-71-004/">section 71.004</a> covers different ground and reaches a different set of people, so pleading one label for both is how items get quietly dropped.</p>
<h2>Both claims answer to the same gate</h2>
<p><a href="https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/civil-practice-and-remedies-code/title-4/chapter-71/subchapter-a/section-71-003/">Section 71.003(a)</a> conditions the death claim on the injured individual having been entitled to sue had they lived, and the survival action is that very claim carried forward. A release the person signed, a contractual limitation, an expired injury deadline or a statutory defense therefore travels with the claim into the estate. That is why an early review looks at what the person did or signed before death rather than only at how the death occurred.</p>
<h2>Creditors, and the line section 71.011 draws</h2>
<p>The two claims are treated differently once money exists. <a href="https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/civil-practice-and-remedies-code/title-4/chapter-71/subchapter-a/section-71-011/">Section 71.011</a> states that damages recovered in a wrongful death action are not subject to the debts of the deceased. A survival recovery is an asset of the estate, so it moves through estate administration and the claims process that comes with it. Where hospital bills, a funeral balance and other debts are outstanding, the distinction between the two recoveries is not a technicality.</p>
<h2>Somebody has to be appointed first</h2>
<p>A survival claim usually needs a personal representative or a court-recognized set of heirs before it can be pursued. In this county that is probate work: the <a href="https://www.traviscountytx.gov/probate">two statutory probate courts</a> hear applications to probate a will, applications to declare heirs where there was no will, and the ancillary lawsuits brought by or against a personal representative. The <a href="https://countyclerk.traviscountytx.gov/departments/probate/probate-fee-information/">County Clerk's probate division</a> handles filing procedures, citation issuance and the letters that prove authority, and it publishes what each step costs. None of that resolves the underlying liability question. It settles who is allowed to raise it.</p>
<h2>Twelve months that can end early</h2>
<p><a href="https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/civil-practice-and-remedies-code/title-2/subtitle-b/chapter-16/subchapter-d/section-16-062/">Section 16.062</a> suspends the running of an applicable limitations period for twelve months after the death. Read the second half carefully: if an executor or administrator qualifies before that twelve months expires, the period begins running at the qualification. Opening an estate can therefore restart a clock the family thought was paused. <a href="https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/civil-practice-and-remedies-code/title-2/subtitle-b/chapter-16/subchapter-a/section-16-001/">Section 16.001</a> excludes time under a legal disability, meaning under eighteen or of unsound mind, but disabilities may not be tacked together and one arising after a period begins does not suspend it.</p>
<h2>Medical records, and the authorization that unlocks them</h2>
<p>Where the injury involved health care, <a href="https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/civil-practice-and-remedies-code/title-4/chapter-74/subchapter-b/section-74-051/">section 74.051</a> requires written notice by certified mail to each provider at least 60 days before suit, accompanied by the statutory authorization for release of protected health information, and it tolls the applicable limitations period for 75 days. Subsection (e) is the provision that matters for a survival claim: a request for the records of a deceased person is valid if the authorization is signed by a parent, a spouse or an adult child. Subsection (d) obliges the provider to furnish unaltered copies within 45 days of a request.</p>
<h2>The estate file and the examiner's file are separate</h2>
<p>Where the Travis County Medical Examiner took the case, that office holds records the hospital chart does not contain. Its <a href="https://www.traviscountytx.gov/medical-examiner/fee-schedule">published fee schedule</a> charges $25 for an autopsy report and $5 for each investigation of death report, $50 to certify a record, and $30 for a disc of digital images or of x-ray and CT files. The office's <a href="https://www.traviscountytx.gov/medical-examiner/faq">own guidance</a> notes that a report can take up to 90 days to finalize where laboratory work is pending, and that a death certificate may be issued with a pending cause in the meantime.</p>
<h2>The limits of this page</h2>
<p>The material above describes how Texas separates a survival action from a wrongful death claim. It is general information rather than legal advice, and it reaches no conclusion about any estate, chart or death. An attorney advertising on this site can address a specific situation.</p>

## svc_who_may_bring_a_claim_lede

Texas hands the death claim to a short list of relatives and stops there. Understanding which name belongs on a petition, and what happens after three calendar months of silence, comes before any question about value.

## svc_who_may_bring_a_claim_body

<h2>The statute names three classes and closes the list</h2>
<p><a href="https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/civil-practice-and-remedies-code/title-4/chapter-71/subchapter-a/section-71-004/">Section 71.004(a)</a> of the Civil Practice and Remedies Code says the action is for the exclusive benefit of the surviving spouse, the children and the parents of the deceased. Subsection (b) then allows one of those individuals, or all of them together, to bring it for the benefit of everyone entitled. That structure surprises families in two directions at once. A single grieving parent can protect the whole claim without waiting for agreement from an estranged sibling. And an adult brother who handled every arrangement, paid the funeral home and answered the county's calls holds no beneficiary status of his own under this chapter.</p>
<h2>The threshold question sits in section 71.003</h2>
<p>Before any of that matters, <a href="https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/civil-practice-and-remedies-code/title-4/chapter-71/subchapter-a/section-71-003/">section 71.003(a)</a> applies the subchapter only where the injured individual would have been entitled to bring an action for that injury had they lived or been born alive. A defense that would have defeated the injury claim can therefore defeat the death claim built on it. Subsection (b) confirms the chapter reaches an injury occurring outside Texas, and subsection (c) carves out claims for the death of an unborn child brought against the mother or against a physician performing a lawful procedure with the required consent.</p>
<h2>After three calendar months the duty moves</h2>
<p>Subsection (c) of section 71.004 is the provision families almost never hear early. Where none of the spouse, children or parents has begun an action within three calendar months after the death, the executor or administrator of the estate shall bring and prosecute it, unless every one of those beneficiaries requests that it not be filed. This is not a bar that erases anyone's rights at ninety days. It is an affirmative duty landing on whoever represents the estate, which is one reason the probate question and the liability question tend to arrive on the same desk.</p>
<h2>What a defendant may not raise about a widow or widower</h2>
<p><a href="https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/civil-practice-and-remedies-code/title-4/chapter-71/subchapter-a/section-71-005/">Section 71.005</a> is short and unusually blunt. Evidence of an actual ceremonial remarriage of the surviving spouse is admissible if it is true. The defense, however, is prohibited from directly or indirectly mentioning or alluding to a common-law marriage, an extramarital relationship, or the marital prospects of the surviving spouse. Families frequently brace for exactly that line of attack. The statute forecloses most of it, and the boundary is worth knowing before anyone gives a recorded statement about their personal life.</p>
<h2>Recovery arrives as shares, not as a family fund</h2>
<p><a href="https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/civil-practice-and-remedies-code/title-4/chapter-71/subchapter-a/section-71-010/">Section 71.010</a> lets the jury award an amount proportionate to the injury resulting from the death and then divides that award into the shares the jury finds among the entitled individuals who are alive at the time. Each share stands on its own proof. <a href="https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/civil-practice-and-remedies-code/title-4/chapter-71/subchapter-a/section-71-011/">Section 71.011</a> adds a protection that matters where an estate is insolvent: damages recovered under the subchapter are not subject to the debts of the deceased. A separate claim, the survival action under <a href="https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/civil-practice-and-remedies-code/title-4/chapter-71/subchapter-b/section-71-021/">section 71.021</a>, belongs to the heirs, legal representatives and estate instead, and it does not follow the same beneficiary list.</p>
<h2>Filing in Travis County does not mean drawing a judge</h2>
<p>Local practice here has a feature that catches out-of-town counsel. Travis County's civil district courts run a central docket: under <a href="https://topics.txcourts.gov/LocalRulesPublic/PreviewAttachment/2121">Local Rule 1.3</a> the District Clerk files cases by distributing them equally on a rotating basis among the district courts, yet hearings and trials are assigned to available judges without regard to the court in which the case was filed. The rule states plainly that the court number in the style of the case does not mean the judge of that court will conduct the hearings or trial. Those <a href="https://www.traviscountytx.gov/courts/civil/district">civil and family courts sit at 1700 Guadalupe Street</a>, and the county's <a href="https://www.traviscountytx.gov/probate">two statutory probate courts</a> handle the estate side, including lawsuits by or against a personal representative.</p>
<h2>A nonresident estate and an out-of-state death</h2>
<p>Two provisions handle families who do not live here. <a href="https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/civil-practice-and-remedies-code/title-4/chapter-71/subchapter-a/section-71-012/">Section 71.012</a> says a foreign personal representative who has met the Estates Code chapter 503 requirements for probating a foreign will need not apply for ancillary letters testamentary to bring the action. <a href="https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/civil-practice-and-remedies-code/title-4/chapter-71/subchapter-c/section-71-031/">Section 71.031</a> allows a death or injury caused by an out-of-state act to be enforced in Texas courts where a law of that place or of Texas gives the right, and where the suit is begun inside the Texas period and, for a resident of the other state, inside that state's period as well.</p>
<h2>Where this page stops</h2>
<p>This is background on how chapter 71 allocates a Texas death claim. It is not legal advice, and it does not decide whether any particular relative is a beneficiary of any particular claim. Those questions turn on records, dates and family facts, and they belong with the attorney who advertises on this site.</p>

## svc_workplace_death_and_comp_exclusivity_lede

Whether an employer carried workers' compensation coverage on the day of a workplace death changes almost everything that follows: the remedy, the deadline, the proof and the identity of the payer.

## svc_workplace_death_and_comp_exclusivity_body

<h2>Exclusive remedy, stated plainly</h2>
<p><a href="https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/labor-code/title-5/subtitle-a/chapter-408/subchapter-a/section-408-001/">Section 408.001(a)</a> of the Labor Code makes recovery of workers' compensation benefits the exclusive remedy of a covered employee or a legal beneficiary against the employer, or an agent or employee of the employer, for a work-related death. Subsection (d) adds that a determination of noncompensability under sections 406.032, 409.002 or 409.004 does not disturb that exclusivity. In practice a family may hold a valid claim against a third party while having no negligence claim against the employer at all, and sorting that out is the first analytical step rather than the last.</p>
<h2>The gross negligence opening, and its narrow width</h2>
<p>Subsection (b) permits recovery of exemplary damages by the surviving spouse or heirs of the body of a deceased employee where the death was caused by an intentional act or omission of the employer or by the employer's gross negligence. Subsection (c) adopts the <a href="https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/civil-practice-and-remedies-code/title-2/subtitle-c/chapter-41/subchapter-a/section-41-001/">section 41.001</a> definition, requiring an act or omission involving an extreme degree of risk viewed objectively at the time, of which the actor had actual, subjective awareness while proceeding with conscious indifference. <a href="https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/civil-practice-and-remedies-code/title-2/subtitle-c/chapter-41/subchapter-a/section-41-003/">Section 41.003</a> requires clear and convincing evidence and a unanimous jury finding on both liability and amount. This is not the standard for an ordinary safety lapse.</p>
<h2>What the benefit system actually pays</h2>
<p>Death benefits under <a href="https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/labor-code/title-5/subtitle-a/chapter-408/subchapter-d/section-408-181/">section 408.181</a> equal 75 percent of the employee's average weekly wage, subject to section 408.061 caps. <a href="https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/labor-code/title-5/subtitle-a/chapter-408/subchapter-d/section-408-186/">Section 408.186</a> pays burial costs to the person who incurred them, capped at the lesser of actual reasonable expenses or $10,000, with body transportation covered where the death occurred away from the usual workplace. <a href="https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/labor-code/title-5/subtitle-a/chapter-408/subchapter-d/section-408-182/">Section 408.182</a> splits benefits half to an eligible spouse and half among eligible children where both exist, all to the spouse where there is no eligible child or grandchild, and to dependent parents, stepparents, siblings or grandparents where there is no spouse, child or grandchild.</p>
<h2>A one-year filing window, separate from any lawsuit</h2>
<p>The Division of Workers' Compensation's <a href="https://www.tdi.texas.gov/wc/employee/deathben.html">death benefits guidance</a> directs a beneficiary to file a claim, and <a href="https://www.tdi.texas.gov/forms/dwc/dwc042benclm.pdf">DWC Form-042</a> is the beneficiary claim form. The one-year period for filing runs independently of the two-year civil limitations period in <a href="https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/civil-practice-and-remedies-code/title-2/subtitle-b/chapter-16/subchapter-a/section-16-003/">section 16.003(b)</a>. Families who spend the first year deciding whether to sue can therefore lose a benefit entitlement that was never contested. The two tracks are handled in different forums, on different paperwork, with different proof.</p>
<h2>Non-subscription is more common than families expect</h2>
<p>The state's <a href="https://www.tdi.texas.gov/wc/reg/nonsub2022.html">2022 non-subscriber estimates</a> put private-sector subscription at 75 percent of employers covering 83 percent of employees, leaving roughly a quarter of employers outside the system. Non-subscription reached 31 percent among employers with one to four employees and fell to 7 percent among those with 50 to 99. The mining, utilities and construction grouping showed 16 percent non-subscription. Where an employer is a non-subscriber, <a href="https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/labor-code/title-5/subtitle-a/chapter-406/subchapter-b/section-406-033/">section 406.033</a> strips contributory negligence, assumption of risk and fellow-servant defenses, retains intentional self-injury and intoxication as defenses, and voids pre-injury waivers.</p>
<h2>Public employers are a large share of this market</h2>
<p>Austin's employment base is unusually weighted toward government: the state capital, a public university, a county government and a hospital district sit inside the same county. The state's 2024 report attributes <a href="https://www.tdi.texas.gov/wc/safety/sis/documents/2024fatalrpt.pdf">44 fatalities to governmental employers, with local government accounting for 66 percent of those</a>. A death involving a public employer adds immunity analysis under <a href="https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/civil-practice-and-remedies-code/title-5/chapter-101/subchapter-b/section-101-021/">chapter 101</a>, the damages ceilings in <a href="https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/civil-practice-and-remedies-code/title-5/chapter-101/subchapter-b/section-101-023/">section 101.023</a>, and, for a claim against the city, the <a href="https://www.austintexas.gov/services/file-claim">45-day written notice</a> its charter requires.</p>
<h2>Documents worth requesting immediately</h2>
<p>Ask what carrier, if any, was on the risk that day, and get the coverage confirmation rather than a verbal answer. Preserve the incident report, the training file, the equipment maintenance log and any regulatory inspection that followed. Where a staffing agency was involved, the coverage question may have two answers. This page is general information about Texas workplace death law rather than legal advice, and no conclusion is offered about any employer or event; that discussion belongs with the attorney advertising on this site.</p>
