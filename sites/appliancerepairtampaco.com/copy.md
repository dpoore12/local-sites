## svc_refrigerator_repair_lede

A warm refrigerator is really two repairs wearing one symptom. Either a control, a fan or a defrost part quit, or the sealed system lost its charge. Which one it is decides the price, the certification required, and whether someone else owes the bill.

## svc_refrigerator_repair_body

### Read both compartments before anyone pulls a panel

A freezer that is still hard-frozen while the fresh food section climbs points at air movement rather than at refrigeration. On most current designs the evaporator sits behind the freezer's rear wall, and a fan, a damper, a thermistor or a stalled defrost cycle can leave that coil making cold that never reaches the upper cabinet. When both sections drift up together the list changes: condenser fan, start relay or inverter, main control board, or the sealed system.

A slab of frost across the back freezer panel is evidence about the defrost circuit, and it should be photographed rather than chipped. An ice pick through an aluminum evaporator turns a heater-and-thermostat job into a scrap decision, because the coil is not a repairable part in a household box.

### Sealed system failures behave differently from control failures

The sealed system means the compressor, the condenser, the evaporator, the metering device and the refrigerant charge inside them. When that is the fault, the machine usually runs without stopping and cools weakly, or frost forms on only the first few inches of the evaporator inlet, or nothing gets cold while the compressor stays warm to the hand. Control problems tend to be intermittent instead: they follow a defrost interval, they clear after a power cycle, they leave a stored fault code.

That distinction also decides who is allowed to touch the machine. Under EPA rules for Clean Air Act Section 608, a technician is anyone attaching or detaching hoses and gauges to measure pressure, adding or removing refrigerant, or otherwise violating the integrity of a small appliance, and that person must hold a certification. Type I is the small appliance credential. It does not expire, and apprentices are exempt only while closely and continually supervised.

### Three documents decide who owes the repair

Start with the manufacturer's written warranty that came in the box. Federal law shapes what it must say, because the FTC's disclosure and pre-sale availability rules reach every written warranty on a consumer product costing more than fifteen dollars. Under the pre-sale rule a retailer has to let you read the terms before you buy, not after the box is open.

Next comes the extended service warranty, and in this state that is not a handshake. Section 634.401(13) defines a service warranty as a written promise of at least a year to repair, replace or maintain a consumer product, or to indemnify you for that work, including failure from normal wear and tear or a power surge. Section 634.403 then says nobody may provide or offer to provide service warranties to residents here without a subsisting license from the Office of Insurance Regulation. The license fee is $200 for each license year, and selling without one is a first-degree misdemeanor.

Third comes the home warranty, a separate animal under part II of the same chapter. Section 634.301 defines it as covering a structural component or appliance of a home, and it lists the roof, plumbing, electrical, foundation, basement, walls, ceilings and floors as the structural side.

### A warranty cannot force you to use one repair company

This is the rule almost nobody knows. The FTC's guidance to warrantors is blunt: tie-in sales provisions generally are not allowed, meaning a warranty may not state or imply that you must buy or use an item or service from a particular company to keep coverage alive. The narrow exceptions are items or services the warrantor supplies at no charge under the warranty, or a waiver granted by the Commission.

What a warrantor may still do is refuse to pay for damage that somebody else's parts or work caused. So an independent repair does not void the coverage by itself, but a bad independent repair can carve a hole in it. Keep the invoice and the part number.

### Garage and bayfront placement shortens condenser life

Second refrigerators here live in garages, utility rooms and covered lanais, and that is the worst duty cycle in the house. NOAA engineering weather data for Tampa record 5,093 hours a year at a wet-bulb temperature above 67 degrees, the threshold that marks a humid design climate. A condenser rejecting heat into that air runs longer, and a door gasket that is marginal shows condensation and frost within a season.

Near the water, in Davis Islands or Ballast Point, salt-laden air adds corrosion at exposed terminals, fan motor housings and the condenser fins themselves. Check the manufacturer's ambient rating before assuming a garage installation was ever within specification.

### The second-visit trap

The expensive mistake is a control board sold on a symptom rather than on a measurement. Frost that returns three weeks later usually means the defrost heater, the defrost thermostat or a thermistor was the real fault and the board simply carried the blame. Ask for the measured compartment temperatures, the frost pattern and the resistance readings that pointed at the part.

### What the visit looks like, and when repair stops making sense

Expect a temperature check in both compartments, a look at the evaporator and its frost pattern, condenser and fan condition, a check of the door seals and the defrost circuit, then electrical testing of the start components before the sealed system is ever suspected. The price shape follows that order, because parts-and-labor repairs on fans, heaters, thermistors and boards sit well below sealed system work, which roughly doubles the figure once recovery and recharge are involved. DOE's rulemaking analysis puts the mean lifetime of a standard-size refrigerator or refrigerator-freezer at 14.3 years, so a sealed system quote on a machine near that age deserves a replacement comparison rather than an automatic yes.

## svc_washer_repair_lede

A washer stops for two very different reasons. Either the water cannot leave, which is usually the inexpensive outcome, or the bearing carrying the tub has failed, which is a teardown. The noise it makes on spin tells the two apart faster than any error code.

## svc_washer_repair_body

### Water sitting in the drum is a drain question first

Before a pump is condemned, the path the water takes has to be checked end to end. A coin, a hairpin or a sock in the pump filter, a kinked or crushed drain hose, a standpipe pushed too far down the hose, a clogged air gap, or a drain line shared with a kitchen disposer will all leave a full tub. A pump that hums without moving water is a different finding from a pump that never energizes at all.

Suds are their own trap. Overdosing detergent in mineral-heavy water produces a foam column that the pressure sensor reads as water, so the control keeps rinsing and never spins. That reads on the front panel as a drain failure and is not one.

### The lock and the balance system stop the cycle too

Front-loaders will not drain and spin until the door lock reports closed and latched, and a failed lock switch or a broken handle assembly ends the cycle in a tub full of water. Top-loaders have the reverse problem: worn suspension rods or a failed balance sensor let the basket knock the cabinet, and the control abandons the spin on purpose to protect the machine. Neither is a pump.

### Bearing failure announces itself, if you listen at the right moment

Ignore the wash portion and listen to the spin ramp. A rear tub bearing that has lost its grease and its seal produces a rising metallic roar that tracks the drum speed and disappears the moment the drum coasts. Two other signs confirm it: a rust-colored streak weeping below the outer tub seal, and a drum that can be rocked by hand at the front of the opening with the machine unplugged.

This matters more than any other washer finding because the labor is a full disassembly, and on many sealed outer tubs the bearing is not sold separately at all. That is the point where the repair-versus-replace arithmetic actually bites. DOE's analysis of residential clothes washers uses an average product lifetime of 13.7 years, which is a useful yardstick for a bearing quote on an older pair.

### Hard water reaches parts nobody thinks about

The City of Tampa's 2025 water quality report gives finished-water hardness of 211 milligrams per liter, or 12.3 grains per gallon, drawn primarily from the Hillsborough River. That mineral load builds on inlet valve screens, on the heating element in a heated front-loader, and inside the small rubber bellows folds where it holds moisture and grit against the seal.

Two practical consequences follow. Scale on a valve screen produces slow fill, which some controls report as a fill timeout rather than a water problem. And a valve seat held open by grit keeps trickling after the cycle ends, which is how a washer floods a closet floor while nobody is home.

### Who authorizes the work is a separate question from what failed

Half of this market rents. Tampa's 2022 housing data counted 77,745 renter-occupied homes, 49.5 percent of occupied units, along with 70,201 apartment units, or 40.58 percent of all units. In a stacked laundry closet inside a mid-rise, the person watching the puddle frequently cannot approve the repair, and the building may control the water shutoff, the elevator and the parking.

If a service contract is in play, read it before the visit. Section 634.414 requires that a service warranty contract be delivered, mailed or transmitted electronically to the holder within 45 days of purchase, and that every contract carry a written disclosure that its rate is not regulated by the Office of Insurance Regulation. If nothing was ever sent to you, ask for the document itself before anyone relies on its terms.

### What not to do while it is full

Do not tip a loaded washer to empty it, because water surges over the cabinet lip and into the control area. Skip the drain chemicals as well: caustic liquid sits in the tub, attacks the bellows and the seals, and does nothing to a jammed impeller or an open lock switch. Bail the tub down with a container if you must move it, and leave the door lock alone rather than forcing it.

### What the visit looks like

The technician drains the machine down, opens the filter or coin trap where the design has one, and traces the hose and standpipe. Then the pump is tested electrically and mechanically, the lock circuit and lid switch are checked, and the drum is spun by hand and under power while the bearing and suspension are assessed. Common failures land in a fairly tight parts-and-labor band. Bearing and outer tub work sits far above it, and that quote should always arrive next to the cost of a comparable new machine.

## svc_dryer_repair_lede

A dryer that tumbles but leaves the load damp has either lost heat or lost airflow, and the two feel identical from the laundry room. One is a part. The other is a duct that may also be a fire risk, and the code puts a number on it.

## svc_dryer_repair_body

### One test separates the heater from the vent

Start the machine empty on a heated cycle and go outside to the exterior hood. Strong warm airflow there means heat is being produced and moved, so the fault is more likely a control, a moisture sensor or the load itself. Weak or cool airflow at the hood, with a hot cabinet and a hot laundry room, points at restriction rather than at the heating circuit.

The wall behind the machine is worth a look at the same time. Lint packed at the transition, a hood flap that no longer opens, or moisture staining the drywall around the duct collar all say the same thing.

### The heat circuit differs by fuel, and both are protected

An electric dryer that tumbles without heat can have an open heating element, an open high-limit thermostat, a blown thermal fuse, a failed cycling thermostat, or a burned relay on the control board. It can also be running on a single leg of its 240-volt supply, which lets the drum turn while the element stays cold. A gas dryer without heat can have a failed igniter, a flame sensor out of range, worn gas valve coils, or a tripped thermal cutoff.

Both types cut heat deliberately when airflow drops and the cabinet overheats. That is why a thermal fuse or cutoff must never be bypassed. Replacing one without clearing the restriction that opened it simply arms the same fault again, and it removes the device that was doing the protecting.

### The code caps the vent run at 35 feet, minus the elbows

Florida's mechanical code is specific here. Section 504.9.4.1 sets the maximum exhaust duct length at 35 feet measured from the connection to the transition duct at the dryer to the outlet terminal, and Table 504.9.4.1 charges each fitting an equivalent length against that budget. A four-inch radius mitered 90-degree elbow counts as 5 feet and a mitered 45 counts as 2 feet 6 inches, while a smooth eight-inch radius 90-degree elbow costs only 1 foot 7 inches. Four sharp mitered elbows therefore spend 20 of the 35 feet before the duct has traveled anywhere.

The rest of the section is equally concrete. Ducts must be smooth inside, metal at least 0.016 inch thick and four inches nominal in diameter under 504.9.1. The flexible transition piece must be a single length listed to UL 2158A, no longer than 8 feet, and never concealed in construction under 504.9.3. Terminations need a backdraft damper and no screen at all, and fasteners must not obstruct the flow under 504.4. Where the manufacturer gives no location, 504.4.1 keeps the outlet at least 3 feet from any opening into the building, including a ventilated soffit.

### Why a long run is a fire question and not only a slow-drying one

NFPA research on home fires involving clothes dryers and washing machines found an estimated average of 15,970 such home structure fires a year across 2010 through 2014, with 92 percent of them involving the dryer. In those dryer fires, dust, fiber or lint was the item first ignited in 26 percent of cases, tied with clothing. A duct over its equivalent length runs slower, drops more lint along the way, and holds that lint against a heat source for longer cycles.

Restriction and heat loss travel together, which is why an honest dryer diagnosis measures airflow before it sells a heating part.

### Local conditions that make it worse

Warm, wet intake air carries away less moisture, and there is a great deal of it here. NOAA engineering weather data for Tampa record 5,093 hours a year with a wet-bulb temperature above 67 degrees. A dryer drawing that air, sitting in an unconditioned garage or a closet off a carport, needs a clean and correctly sized duct simply to hit its rated cycle time.

Multifamily layouts push the run longer still. In a mid-rise or a townhome the duct may climb through a chase to a roof or a soffit outlet, and section 504.9.5 requires a permanent label giving the equivalent length within 6 feet of the duct connection whenever it exceeds 35 feet. If that label exists, read it before anyone quotes a heating element.

### What not to attempt, and what the visit covers

Do not run a dryer with a crushed transition, a disconnected duct or a scorched lint smell, and do not vent one into a garage, an attic or a crawlspace. Leave gas connections alone. Once a repair moves the gas line rather than the machine, it becomes permitted work with a City of Tampa trade permit behind it, and that belongs on the estimate in writing.

A full visit reads the heat circuit and its safety devices, measures airflow, inspects the transition and the accessible duct, checks the blower wheel and housing for lint packing, and verifies the moisture sensor and thermostat response through a real cycle. Costs sit in a modest parts-and-labor band for fuses, thermostats, elements and igniters, and rise when the blower housing has to come apart or the duct itself needs replacing. DOE's analysis puts the average consumer clothes dryer lifetime near 14 years, with the distribution stretching to about 30, so age alone rarely settles the repair decision on this machine.

## svc_dishwasher_repair_lede

Where the water shows up is the whole diagnosis. Front corners, the center of the base, and standing water that appears only after the cycle ends are three separate failures, and only one of them is the door everybody blames first.

## svc_dishwasher_repair_body

### Map the water before anything comes out from under the counter

Water at the front corners usually traces to a hardened or fouled door gasket, a warped door, a loose latch, or foam from the wrong detergent riding over the lip. Water under the center or the rear of the machine points instead at the circulation pump seal, the sump gasket, a split hose or the inlet valve fitting. Water that only appears after the cycle ends, sitting in the tub, is a drainage story: filter, drain hose, drain pump, air gap or the disposer connection.

Cabinet damage is what makes the distinction urgent. A slow leak at a rear hose clamp wicks under the toe kick and into the base of the cabinets long before anything shows on the kitchen floor.

### The inlet valve and the pumps fail in different directions

An inlet valve fails three ways, and each one looks like a different appliance problem. It can fail closed, giving no fill at all. It can fail partly open, giving a slow fill that some controls report as a timeout rather than a water fault. Or it can be held open by grit on its seat, which keeps water trickling in after the cycle ends and eventually overflows the tub.

There are usually two pumps to keep straight. The circulation pump drives the spray arms, so its failure shows as dishes coming out dirty with water still moving in and out normally. The drain pump empties the sump, so its failure leaves standing water and often a hum with no flow. A chopper or impeller jammed by a shard of glass or a fruit label sits between the two and mimics both.

### Hard water is a finding, not an excuse

The City of Tampa's 2025 water quality report gives an average finished-water hardness of 211 milligrams per liter, which is 12.3 grains per gallon, drawn mainly from the Hillsborough River. That is enough mineral content to close down spray arm ports, coat the filter screen, crust the heating element and lodge on the inlet valve seat.

The useful test is to separate film from etching. Mineral film wipes off glass with a mild acid and returns; etching is permanent and comes from a detergent and water combination that is too aggressive. Neither one means the machine has failed, and neither one justifies replacing a pump. Scale is also why a small, inexpensive part on this appliance can be back on the invoice within a year.

### Storm season and the control board

Summer here brings a great deal of lightning, and the National Weather Service casualty tabulation by state lists 1,523 for Florida. Surge damage rarely arrives as a dramatic failure. It shows up as a control that no longer drives one output, a touch panel that lights but will not accept a cycle, or a machine that resets partway through.

The statute writers noticed. Section 634.401(13) lists power surge among the causes named in the statutory definition of a service warranty, alongside defects in materials or workmanship and normal wear and tear. If the machine carries such a contract, a surge-damaged board is the kind of claim it was drafted around, so read the coverage before paying for a board yourself.

### Verify the company that says it owes the repair

Anyone offering a service warranty here must be authorized. The Office of Insurance Regulation runs a company search that returns full names, business addresses and identifying information for entities doing business in the state. It is searchable by name, company type and authorized lines of business, and the office also publishes a list of companies in receivership. The same office defines an unlicensed entity as any person or business transacting insurance without the required authorization, and it names service warranty associations and home warranty association products specifically.

Complaints belong with the Department of Financial Services, Division of Consumer Services, which takes concerns about insurance products including warranties through its consumer assistance portal and assigns them to a specialist. Gather four things first: the state you lived in when you bought the plan, the application, the plan documents, and proof of the initial payment. Behind all of it sits section 501.204(1), which declares unfair or deceptive acts in any trade or commerce unlawful, and that reaches the repair company as readily as the warranty seller.

### Nobody in this state licenses appliance repair

It is worth being plain about the gap. Part IX of chapter 559 is titled Repair of Motor Vehicles, and section 559.903 defines a motor vehicle as an automobile, truck, bus, recreational vehicle, motorcycle, motor scooter or other motor powered vehicle. The much-quoted right to a written estimate above $150, in section 559.905, belongs to that act and to shops that repair those vehicles. No equivalent statute governs the person under your sink.

Local rules do not fill it either. The Hillsborough County Tax Collector lists small appliance repair among the tasks that do not require a contractor license. So insist in writing on what the law does not hand you: the diagnosis, the part number, labor separate from parts, and the warranty term on each.

### What the visit looks like

Expect the machine to be leveled and checked at the door seal and latch, then the filter, sump, spray arms and drain path examined, then fill and drain observed under power with the panel off if needed. DOE's dishwasher analysis uses an average product lifetime of 15.2 years, which frames the decision when a pump and a control board are both suspect on the same older machine. Repairs cluster in a moderate parts-and-labor band and climb when the machine has to come out from under a stone countertop.

## pricing_lede

These are the diagnostic fees and repair ranges Tampa Bay appliance companies publish for their own work, set next to the tax, permit and licensing rules the state, the county and the city publish for theirs. Nothing here is a quote for your machine.

## pricing_body

### The diagnosis comes first, and it is usually credited

Four Tampa Bay shops publish the same structure with different numbers: a flat fee to come out, diagnose the fault and put a written estimate in your hand, then that fee waived or deducted once the repair is approved. The published figures are $65, $79, $89 and $120. Read the wording rather than the number, because one company publishes both a $19 service call and a separate $120 diagnostic fee that goes away on approval, which means the trip itself is not free even when the diagnosis becomes free.

The same reading habit sets the realistic floor on a small job. One shop publishes an $89 diagnostic that is deducted from the total, plus a $185 minimum labor fee. Because the diagnostic is deducted rather than added, the smallest invoice once a part is actually fitted is $185 plus the part, not $274. Stacking a credited diagnostic on top of minimum labor overstates the bill by the diagnostic, and it is the most common arithmetic error in comparing two estimates.

### The tax line on the invoice

Florida's rule catches people out. If the shop supplies any part that becomes part of the machine, the whole repair charge is taxable, labor included, even when parts are not listed separately. Labor only work is exempt, but only when the invoice documents that no parts were used. The state rate is 6 percent and Hillsborough County adds 1.5 percent, so a taxable repair invoice in Tampa carries 7.5 percent. On a $500 refrigerator repair that is another $37.50, and no national cost page carries it.

### Nobody licenses appliance repair in this state

There is no state appliance repair license to check. The Hillsborough County Tax Collector lists small appliance repair among tasks that do not require a contractor license, and issues no handyman receipt at all. Screening falls to the homeowner: a written estimate before any part is ordered, the manufacturer part number on the invoice, and the warranty term in writing on both part and labor.

The permitted trades are the bright line. Once a repair turns into moving a water line, replacing a gas range connection or altering a circuit, the City of Tampa trade permit fee schedule applies. It prices a general residential plumbing, electrical or mechanical permit at $120, and natural or LP gas piping at $162. Each carries a state surcharge of 2.5 percent of permit value, with a $4 floor. If someone proposes gas or plumbing alteration as part of an appliance repair with no permit line, that is the moment to ask who is pulling it.

### The fifty percent rule, and what humidity does here

The usual guideline is to repair when the repair costs less than half of a comparable new machine, and one local shop states it in those terms. Two local factors bend it. Tampa water is hard enough to scale heating elements and clog ice makers and dishwasher solenoids early, which makes several of the low-cost repairs here the ones most likely to recur when the water side is ignored. This is also a humid coastal market, hard on dryer transitions, vent runs and control boards in unconditioned closets and garages.

Housing tenure matters too. Roughly half of the county's housing units are renter occupied, so the machine often belongs to a landlord rather than to the person standing in the kitchen. Settling who authorizes the repair first saves paying a diagnostic fee twice.

### Reading the estimate before the part is ordered

A usable estimate names the diagnostic fee and how it is treated, the part number and whether it is a manufacturer or aftermarket part, labor separately from the part, the tax, and the warranty on each. It says whether the sealed system is involved, because that is the row where prices roughly double. It also says what happens if the first part does not fix the fault.

### What the online range leaves out

Brand premiums on diagnosis, and parts that have gone out of production on a ten year old model. The extra labor to pull a stacked laundry pair apart, and second faults found once the panel is off. Then the 7.5 percent that lands on the total the moment a part is involved.

## svc_built_in_refrigerator_repair_lede

A built-in refrigerator is a cabinet-integrated machine with a small refrigerant charge, a front-breathing condenser and a service access that is nothing like a free-standing box. That changes the diagnosis, the parts, and the point at which replacement stops being realistic.

## svc_built_in_refrigerator_repair_body

### The machine breathes through a grille, not through its back

Built-in and column units reject heat through a grille at the top or the toe kick because they are designed to sit flush in a cabinet run with no clearance behind or beside them. Everything about that arrangement makes the condenser the first suspect in a warm-cabinet complaint. Dust and pet hair collect at floor level where the intake is, a cabinet toe kick panel installed slightly wrong blocks part of the opening, and the condenser fan is doing all of the work. A built-in that has never had its grille removed for cleaning will run long cycles, hold a warmer box on hot days, and eventually cost its compressor. Cleaning that coil properly, with the machine powered down and the fan blade checked for wobble, is the least expensive repair on the appliance. The other categories are on [the services index](../services/).

### Small charges make leaks a bigger deal

Environmental Protection Agency risk screening for household refrigeration explains the constraint: isobutane, R-600a, is listed for household refrigerators and freezers subject to use conditions and charge size limits, and under UL 60335-2-24 the maximum allowable charge for an A3 classified refrigerant such as isobutane is 150 grams. Even where a unit uses a conventional refrigerant instead, built-in systems run charges measured in ounces, so a loss that would be trivial in an air conditioner is fatal to cooling here. Two practical consequences: a system that has lost its charge has a leak that must be found rather than topped up, and sealed-system work on these cabinets is skilled, permitted-by-certification work. Under Environmental Protection Agency rules implementing Clean Air Act section 608, anyone attaching gauges to measure pressure or opening the system on a small appliance needs the Type I certification.

### Coastal humidity and salt reach the parts you cannot see

Corrosion is a genuine mechanism on Florida coastal installations, and it has been measured. NASA's corrosion program at Kennedy Space Center monitors chloride deposition at its atmospheric test sites using the wet candle method under ISO 9225, and reports a chloride deposition rate range of 70 to 7,300 milligrams per square meter per day at its beachside site with a weighted mean of 983, against an ISO 9223 classification in which the highest category corresponds to 300 to 1,500 milligrams per square meter per day. Salt-laden air is what those figures describe, and in a built-in refrigerator the vulnerable points are the condenser fins, the fan motor housing, exposed terminals and the ground connections at the compressor. On installations near the water in Davis Islands or Ballast Point, corrosion at a terminal shows first as intermittent behavior rather than as a dead machine, which is why connections are inspected and cleaned before a board is condemned.

### Ambient range, and the cabinet that traps heat

Whirlpool's household refrigeration guidance states that its refrigerators are designed for indoor household use in a location where the temperature ranges from a minimum of 55 degrees to a maximum of 110 degrees, and warns that operating outside that range may produce improper temperatures. Built-in cabinets frequently push the local ambient toward the top of that band: a unit flanked by an oven column, one in a butler's pantry with no return air, or one under a bar counter with the grille half covered. Florida Climate Center normals for Tampa International Airport across 1991 to 2020 give average daily highs of 91.0 degrees in July and 91.2 in August, so a poorly ventilated cabinet in an unconditioned area is not an academic concern. Correcting the air path is sometimes the whole repair.

### Panels, hinges and the ice system are where the labor goes

These units carry custom or stainless overlay panels, soft-close hinge cartridges and door closers under significant weight, and a door that no longer seals is often a hinge or closer problem rather than a gasket problem. Ice and water systems on column units add a filter, a valve, a fill tube and a dispenser control, all of which are affected by mineral content in the supply. Because access to almost everything requires panels off and sometimes the unit slid forward out of a millimeter-tolerance opening, labor on a built-in runs longer than on a free-standing box for the identical part. That should be quoted honestly rather than discovered mid-visit.

### What the visit covers, and when replacement is the answer

Expect temperatures logged in both compartments, the evaporator and frost pattern examined, the condenser and its fan cleaned and checked, the door seals and closers tested along their length, the electrical connections and grounds inspected for corrosion, the defrost circuit and thermistors measured, and the sealed system evaluated only after all of that. Fans, thermistors, heaters, hinges, closers, valves and control boards are ordinary parts-and-labor repairs, though the labor is higher than on a free-standing unit. A leaking evaporator behind cabinet-integrated foam, or a compressor failure on a cabinet whose panels and opening are no longer matched by anything currently made, is where the conversation turns to replacement, and the cost of the cabinetry work belongs in that comparison. For a free-standing machine, start at [refrigerator repair](../refrigerator-repair/); for a wine cabinet in the same run, see [wine cooler repair](../wine-cooler-repair/).

## svc_commercial_appliance_repair_lede

In a commercial kitchen the appliance failure is only half the problem. The other half is a temperature log, an inspection standard and a service window measured in hours, so the repair has to be judged against what the operation is required to prove.

## svc_commercial_appliance_repair_body

### The number the inspector uses is the number the repair has to hit

Florida's Division of Hotels and Restaurants states the cold holding requirement directly: maintain time and temperature control for safety foods, including dairy, seafood, meats, poultry and prepared foods, at 41 degrees or below except during preparation, cooking or cooling, and maintain shell eggs at 45 degrees or below ambient air temperature. Its guidance for operators is equally practical about the equipment, and reads like a service checklist: provide ambient thermometers and place them in the warmest part of the cooler, do not block fans or air circulation, keep gaskets clean and doors in good repair, avoid overfilling containers or stacking above the cold holding line, and calibrate probe thermometers often. A reach-in that holds 43 degrees is not a borderline machine, it is a finding, and the repair target is a documented 41 or below with the doors in normal use. Other appliance categories are on [the services index](../services/).

### Gaskets, doors and airflow before anything else

The three cheapest repairs in a commercial kitchen are the three most often deferred. A gasket that has hardened or torn at a corner lets warm humid air into the cabinet continuously, which shows up as frost on the evaporator, longer run time and a warm zone near the door. A door that no longer self-closes because its cam or hinge spring is worn does the same thing on a larger scale. And a condenser packed with grease and lint, which happens fast in any kitchen with a fryer, raises head pressure until the unit can no longer hold its setpoint on a busy afternoon. None of these needs a refrigeration license to notice, and all of them get checked before a compressor is even discussed.

### Ice machines have published operating limits, and kitchens violate them

A Manitowoc undercounter cuber specification sheet lists an ambient temperature range of 40 to 110 degrees, a water temperature range of 45 to 90 degrees, and ice maker inlet water pressure of 20 psi minimum to 80 psi maximum. It also notes air in the right side and out the left with no side vents, and for water-cooled versions a consumption of 183 gallons per 100 pounds of ice produced. Those figures explain most complaints about a machine that used to make more ice. A cuber tucked into a hot back corner beside a dish machine, or one whose air path is blocked by stacked boxes, is operating outside its own envelope, and production loss is the expected result rather than a fault. Correcting the installation is part of the repair.

### Ybor City and the older commercial stock

Restaurant kitchens in the older commercial buildings around Ybor City and along the historic corridors were carved into shells built for other purposes, which produces a specific set of problems: a mechanical room that shares air with the cook line, condensers discharging into a closed alcove, single-phase supply feeding equipment designed for three, and undersized water service feeding a dish machine and an ice machine at once. That is why a commercial diagnosis starts with the utilities and the room rather than the nameplate. Where the City of Tampa is involved, its trade permit limitation list, updated 11/14/2025, puts residential gas piping under a plumbing trade permit and holds anything beyond the listed limits to a full building permit with plan review, so a kitchen equipment change that alters gas or electrical infrastructure is a permitted project and not a service call.

### Downtime is the real cost, so the sequence matters

For a commercial customer the repair decision is arithmetic on lost service, not just parts. Practically that means diagnosing to a part and a price on the first visit, distinguishing what can be kept running on a temporary measure from what has to stop, and being explicit about lead time on anything not stocked. It also means being honest about equipment near the end of its life: a walk-in condensing unit or a dish machine tank heater on a twenty-year-old installation may be repairable twice more, and the operator deserves the replacement figure alongside the repair figure rather than after it.

### What the visit covers

Expect cabinet and product temperatures logged against the state's holding requirement, gaskets and door closures inspected, condenser and evaporator coils and fans checked, refrigerant-side pressures and superheat read where a sealed system is in question, water pressure and temperature measured at ice equipment, electrical supply verified at the nameplate rating, and a written diagnosis naming the part and the measurement behind it. Gaskets, hinges, fans, thermostats, controls, contactors and water valves are ordinary parts-and-labor repairs. Compressors, evaporators and tank heaters are the items where an operator needs both numbers in front of them. If the failing unit is an ice machine, the diagnostic path is on [ice maker repair](../ice-maker-repair/); for reach-in cold storage, see [refrigerator repair](../refrigerator-repair/).

## svc_cooktop_repair_lede

A cooktop has three completely different technologies behind the same countertop cutout, and each fails in its own way. Radiant glass burns out switches and cracks, induction kills its own electronics with heat, and gas loses ignition. What can be repaired depends entirely on which one is in the counter.

## svc_cooktop_repair_body

### Radiant glass: the switch, the element, the glass

Under a smooth ceramic top each element is fed by an infinite switch that cycles power on and off to produce what the dial calls medium. Those switches wear out contact by contact, so the classic symptom is an element stuck on full or dead at every setting except high. Elements themselves fail open and simply stop. Neither is expensive. The glass is the different case: a crack, a chip through the surface or an impact star means the top is replaced rather than repaired, because the surface is both the cooking vessel support and the electrical isolation. On many models the glass costs enough that a whole new unit competes with it, and a customer deserves that comparison before ordering. The rest of the appliance categories are on [the services index](../services/).

### Induction fails as electronics, not as heating

An induction hob generates a magnetic field in a coil under the glass and puts the heat into the pan itself. There is no element to burn out, so the failures are the coil, its driver board, the temperature sensors that protect the electronics, and above all the cooling fan. Boards under a glass top live in a warm cabinet, and once the fan or its air path clogs, the unit begins shutting a zone down partway through cooking and posting an over-temperature code. A zone that works alone but drops out when three others are running is almost always a thermal-protection event and not a failed coil. The other classic induction complaint is not a fault at all: a pan with too little ferrous content, which the hob refuses to recognize.

### Gas cooktops answer to the fuel code

On a gas top the repairs are ignition and gas flow: a cracked spark electrode, a wet or fouled igniter, a failed spark module, a clogged burner port, or an orifice sized for the wrong fuel after a propane conversion. What the connection looks like is not the technician's preference, it is code. Florida's residential code lists the permitted connection methods in section G2422.1, including a listed and labeled appliance connector complying with ANSI Z21.24 or CSA 6.10 that is located entirely in the same room as the appliance, and section G2422.1.2.1 caps a connector's overall length at 6 feet. Connectors also have to be installed so they are protected against physical damage. Any repair that moves the gas line rather than the appliance belongs on [the gas range page](../gas-range-repair/) with the permit question answered in writing.

### The hood above it is part of the diagnosis

Cooktops installed in island counters and in remodeled older kitchens frequently end up under a hood that either recirculates or is oversized for the house. The model residential code section on makeup air, M1503.6, requires an exhaust system capable of moving more than 400 cubic feet per minute to be provided with makeup air at approximately the exhaust rate, with a damper, wherever fuel-burning appliances that are not direct vent sit inside the same air barrier. That matters at the cooktop because a strong hood without makeup air pulls combustion products the wrong way through other vented appliances and produces lazy yellow flames that get blamed on the burners. If the complaint is smoke, grease film or a hood that runs loud and moves nothing, the work is [range hood repair](../range-hood-repair/) rather than cooktop repair.

### Humidity and the control area

Downdraft units and cooktops mounted over dishwashers or in island cabinets sit in the wettest air in the house, and this is a city with a great deal of it. Corrosion at the spark electrode tips, at the switch terminals under a radiant top, and at ribbon cable connectors on an induction board all show up as intermittent behavior first: a burner that lights on the third try, a zone that works when cold, a display that flickers. Intermittent faults are worth chasing with a meter rather than answering with a board, because a cleaned and re-terminated connection is a repair and a control board on a guess is a return visit.

### What the visit covers

A technician identifies the technology and the model, measures each zone or burner for the fault reported, tests the switches or the driver board rather than assuming, checks the glass for cracks and the seal at the counter for water intrusion, verifies the cooling path on an induction unit, and on gas checks ignition, flame quality, orifice size and the connector installation. Switches, igniters, electrodes, sensors and fans are ordinary repairs. Glass tops, coil assemblies and induction generator boards are the parts that push a decision toward replacement, and the price of both paths belongs on the same page of the estimate. If the oven under the counter is also affected, see [oven and range repair](../oven-and-range-repair/).

## svc_dryer_vent_cleaning_lede

Lint in a dryer duct is not a housekeeping issue, it is the leading contributing factor in dryer fires and the most common reason a working dryer stops drying. Both problems are solved by the same measurement, which is airflow at the exterior hood.

## svc_dryer_vent_cleaning_body

### The federal fire data names the cause precisely

The United States Fire Administration's analysis of clothes dryer fires in residential buildings found an estimated 2,900 such fires reported each year across 2008 to 2010, causing an annual average of 5 deaths, 100 injuries and $35 million in property loss. The contributing factor breakdown is the useful part. Failure to clean accounted for 34 percent of all clothes dryer fires in residential buildings, and 72 percent of the operational deficiency category that led the list at 46.6 percent. Dust, fiber and lint was the item first ignited in 27.6 percent of these fires, essentially tied with clothing at 27.2 percent. Just over half of the fires, 53.5 percent, stayed confined to the object of origin at an average loss of $1,790, while the 8.7 percent that spread to the building of origin averaged $49,500. That is the entire argument for a duct service, in the government's own figures. The rest of the appliance list is on [the services index](../services/).

### Slow drying and fire risk are the same fault

A restricted duct makes the dryer recirculate hot, moist air. Cycle times lengthen, the cabinet and the laundry room get hotter, the high limit thermostat starts cutting the heat, and lint that should have left the building settles along the run instead. Every one of those is a symptom of the same restriction. So when a customer says the dryer takes two cycles, the correct response is a measurement rather than a heating part, because replacing a thermal fuse on a blocked duct simply arms the same fault again while removing the protection that opened it.

### What a real cleaning includes, and what a shortcut leaves behind

Running a brush a few feet into the wall is not the job. A proper service disconnects the machine, removes and inspects the flexible transition duct, cleans the blower housing and the lint chute inside the dryer where a surprising share of the packed lint actually sits, brushes and vacuums the full rigid run from both ends where access allows, clears the exterior hood and confirms its damper flap swings freely, then verifies airflow at the hood before the machine is pushed back. A crushed transition, a duct joined with sheet metal screws that catch lint, a section of plastic or foil flex duct concealed in a wall, or a hood fitted with an insect screen are all findings to correct rather than clean around, since each one re-blocks within months.

### Where the run goes wrong in Tampa's older housing

The Seminole Heights historic district, roughly 215 acres and 703 buildings with a period of significance from 1912 to 1939, is typical of the central-city stock: laundry was added to houses built without it, so the duct was improvised. What that produces is long runs with multiple sharp turns through a converted porch or a rear addition, terminations under a low deck or into a soffit where the discharge cannot escape, and transitions crushed behind a machine pushed tight to a wall. Multifamily and townhome installations push the run further still, up a chase to a roof cap that no one has looked at since construction. The length and the number of turns, not the age of the dryer, are what decide how often this service is actually needed.

### Frequency, and how to tell without guessing

The interval depends on load volume, on whether the run is short and straight or long and turned, and on whether the household dries pet bedding and fleece, which shed heavily. Rather than a calendar rule, use two observable signs: cycle time creeping up on the same load type, and reduced or barely warm airflow at the exterior hood while the machine runs on a heated cycle. A hood flap that no longer opens fully, lint visible on the outside of the cap, or a damp smell in the laundry area are all the same message. Households drying several loads a day through a long run will need service far more often than a two-person household with a six-foot run through an exterior wall.

### What the visit covers

Expect the machine pulled and the transition removed and inspected, the internal lint path and blower housing cleaned, the rigid duct brushed and vacuumed end to end, the exterior hood cleared and its damper verified, airflow measured at the hood before and after, the duct route and its fittings documented with any defects named, and the machine reconnected and run through a heated cycle to confirm the result. If the dryer also has a heating fault, that diagnosis is on [dryer repair](../dryer-repair/), and if a new pair is going in, the duct is corrected as part of [washer and dryer installation](../washer-dryer-installation/).

## svc_freezer_repair_lede

With a freezer the clock matters more than the diagnosis. Federal food safety guidance gives a full freezer 48 hours and a half-full one 24 hours with the door shut, so the first decisions are about the food, and the repair order follows from there.

## svc_freezer_repair_body

### The first hour is about the contents, not the compressor

Centers for Disease Control guidance for power loss and appliance failure is specific: keep the doors closed, and with them closed food stays safe up to 48 hours in a full freezer and 24 hours in a half-full one, while a refrigerator holds for four hours. It also gives the target temperatures, 0 degrees or below for a freezer and 40 degrees or below for a refrigerator, and says to throw out anything that has thawed or risen above 40. Practically that means grouping packages tightly together, not opening the lid to check, and moving what matters into a working box if one is available. A chest freezer packed solid buys nearly twice the time of the same freezer half empty, which is a genuine argument for filling the empty space with jugs of water. The rest of the appliance list is on [the services index](../services/).

### A frost pattern is evidence

Before parts are named, the interior gets read. Even light frost on a full evaporator with a compartment holding temperature means the machine is working. A thick slab of frost on the rear panel of an upright with warm air above it means the defrost circuit stopped: the heater, the defrost thermostat or bimetal, the sensor, or the control's defrost timing. Frost on only the first inches of the evaporator coil with the rest bare means refrigerant flow is short, which is a sealed-system finding rather than a control one. Ice on the floor of a chest freezer under the drain plug means the drain is blocked and meltwater is refreezing where it lands. Chipping at an aluminum evaporator with a screwdriver ends the appliance, so the frost gets photographed and thawed, never attacked.

### Chest and upright freezers fail in different places

A chest freezer is a simple machine: one compartment, cold air falling and staying put, a lid gasket, a thermostat, a start relay and a compressor. Its usual failures are the lid gasket, the thermostat and the start relay, all inexpensive, and its usual complaint is a lid that no longer seals against a warped edge. An upright with an automatic defrost cycle has more to go wrong, since it depends on a fan, a defrost heater and a drain path, and any of those can leave it either frosted solid or slowly warming. Garage models with a self-contained system of ten pounds of refrigerant or less and a motor of one horsepower or less also happen to be the category the City of Tampa's building code explicitly exempts from a mechanical permit, which is why this work stays inside the appliance trade rather than involving an inspection.

### Storm-season outages are the local failure mode

Losing a freezer here is usually not a mechanical event. Florida Public Service Commission outage reporting during Hurricane Milton recorded 484,194 of Hillsborough County's 701,412 accounts without power, 69.03 percent of the county, at 5:44 p.m. on October 11, 2024. Two things follow for the appliance. First, the food clock above starts the moment the lights go out, not when the power comes back. Second, restoration on that scale involves repeated switching, and a compressor that tries to restart against a sag can lose its start relay or its inverter board while the rest of the machine looks fine. A freezer that ran through a storm and quit a week later is the classic version of that, and it is a repair rather than a replacement.

### Ambient temperature decides whether a garage freezer ever worked

Whirlpool's guidance on garage-ready refrigeration lists the factory preset airflow setting as correct for ambient temperatures between 55 and 110 degrees and describes a separate garage mode for ambients consistently between 38 and 55 degrees, and it states plainly that supported ambient ranges vary by product and belong in the product documentation. Florida Climate Center normals for Tampa International Airport from 1991 to 2020 give average daily highs of 91.0 degrees in July and 91.2 in August, and a closed garage runs above the outdoor air on those days. A unit near the top of its rated range runs a longer duty cycle, holds a warmer compartment, and shortens its own compressor life, so the correct question about a struggling garage freezer is whether the model was ever rated for where it sits.

### What the visit covers

Expect compartment temperature logged with an accurate thermometer, the frost pattern and evaporator condition read, the defrost heater and thermostat measured for continuity, the condenser and its fan checked for lint and corrosion, the gasket tested along its whole length, and the start components tested electrically before the sealed system is suspected. Gaskets, thermostats, relays, heaters and fans are ordinary parts-and-labor repairs. A leaking sealed system on an older chest freezer is where replacement usually wins, and the honest quote puts both figures side by side. If the same fault pattern is showing up in the fresh food section of a combination unit, that is [refrigerator repair](../refrigerator-repair/), and a fully integrated cabinet unit is handled as [built-in refrigerator repair](../built-in-refrigerator-repair/).

## svc_front_load_washer_repair_lede

Front-loaders fail in ways top-loaders do not, because everything hangs on a door boot, a drain path and a bearing that carries the whole tub. Odor, a puddle at the front, and a roar on spin are three distinct faults, and only one of them is expensive.

## svc_front_load_washer_repair_body

### The door boot is the part that defines this machine

The flexible bellows between the tub and the door frame holds water in during the wash and grit, hair and detergent residue in its folds forever. Three things go wrong with it. It tears, usually at the bottom where an underwire or a coin worked into the fold, and then it leaks only on high water levels. It builds a biofilm and produces the smell people call mildew. And it holds a foreign object that scrapes the glass or blocks the drain path. LG's own support guidance states the mechanism plainly: if the door is kept closed the moisture from washing cannot dry and mold may form inside the door gasket, and it directs owners to empty the tub, open the door and the dispenser, and let the remaining moisture evaporate, with particular attention in summer when it is hot and humid. Its cleaning procedure is monthly, using 5 grams of oxygen bleach diluted in 5 liters of warm water, wiping the gasket and the door glass dry afterward. It also notes that a gasket cleaning visit is chargeable even in warranty, because a dirty gasket is not a product fault. In a climate like this one, that maintenance is the difference between a clean machine and a service call.

### Detergent dose is a mechanical variable

These machines wash with a few gallons, so an ordinary dose of detergent produces a foam column instead of a wash. The pressure system reads foam as water, the control extends rinses, and the spin never starts, which arrives as a complaint about draining. Suds also carry residue into the bellows folds and the dispenser housing, where it feeds the odor problem. High-efficiency detergent at the marked dose, reduced further for soft loads, is not a preference. Tampa's mineral content works against you here as well: the city's 2025 water quality report gives average hardness of 211 milligrams per liter, or 12.3 grains per gallon, and hard water makes people add more detergent, not less. The full appliance list is on [the services index](../services/).

### Water on the floor comes from four places

Front corners with a clean bellows usually means the door glass is not seating, from a worn hinge or a bent striker. A puddle under the front center is the drain pump housing, its filter cap or the sump hose. Water at the rear is the inlet valve, its hose fittings, or the standpipe backing up. That last one is a plumbing question with a published dimension: the residential code section on standpipes, P2706.1.2, requires them to extend not less than 18 inches and not more than 42 inches above the trap weir, and a standpipe too short or a trap set below the floor is why a machine that drains fine in the shop floods a laundry closet.

### The bearing is the finding that changes the arithmetic

Listen to the spin ramp rather than the wash. A rising metallic roar that tracks drum speed and fades as the drum coasts is the rear tub bearing. Rust weeping below the outer tub, and play when the drum is rocked by hand at the front with the machine unplugged, confirm it. This matters because on many current designs the outer tub is sealed and the bearing is not sold on its own, so the repair is a full teardown or a new tub assembly. That is the point where the quote has to sit next to the price of a comparable new machine instead of being presented alone.

### Older Tampa laundry spaces make the install part of the problem

Front-loaders spin fast enough that the floor becomes part of the machine. In the Seminole Heights historic district, roughly 215 acres and 703 buildings with a period of significance from 1912 to 1939, laundry hookups were added to houses built without them, frequently on wood floors over a crawl space or in a converted back porch. On that kind of structure an out-of-level machine or one still carrying its shipping bolts walks, hammers and eventually damages its own suspension. Leveling with a real level at all four feet, and confirming the floor does not flex under a spin, is part of a repair on this appliance, not an afterthought.

### What the visit covers

Expect the bellows pulled back and inspected all the way around, the drain filter opened, the pump tested electrically and mechanically, the door lock circuit checked, the hoses and standpipe traced, the machine leveled, and the drum spun by hand and under power while the bearing and shocks are assessed. Bellows, pumps, locks, hoses and shock absorbers are ordinary parts-and-labor repairs. Bearing and outer tub work is the one that competes with replacement. If the machine is a top-loader, start at [washer repair](../washer-repair/), and if it lives in a closet under a dryer, see [stackable washer and dryer repair](../stackable-washer-dryer-repair/).

## svc_garbage_disposal_repair_lede

A disposal that hums is jammed, a disposal that is silent has tripped or lost power, and a disposal that leaks is telling you which of its three seals failed. All three get misdiagnosed as a dead motor, and the fix is often a wrench and a reset button.

## svc_garbage_disposal_repair_body

### Hum, silence and leak are three separate diagnoses

A hum with no rotation means the motor is energized and the impeller plate is held. A unit that does nothing at all has an open circuit: the overload has tripped inside the housing, the wall switch or its wiring failed, or the branch circuit is out. Water on the cabinet floor means a seal or a joint, and where it appears names it. Drips at the sink flange point at the mounting gasket and the plumber's putty under the ring. Water at the side of the housing points at the dishwasher inlet connection or the discharge tube gasket. Water from the bottom of the motor housing means the internal seal has failed and the unit gets replaced, because that seal is not a serviceable part. The other kitchen appliances are indexed on [the services index](../services/).

### The manufacturer's own jam procedure, in order

InSinkErator's installation and care instructions are specific. Turn off the disposal and the water. Insert one end of the self-service wrench into the center hole on the bottom of the unit and work it back and forth until it turns one full revolution. Remove the wrench, then use long-handled tongs or pliers, never fingers or hands, to take the object out. Allow the motor to cool for three to five minutes, then lightly push the red reset button on the underside. If the motor still does nothing, the instructions send you to the service panel for a tripped breaker or a blown fuse. That sequence resolves a large share of calls, and skipping the cooling interval is why the reset button appears not to work.

### Drain cleaner is the wrong tool here

Caustic drain chemicals sit in the chamber against the mounting gasket and the discharge gasket, and they do nothing at all to a plate held by a fruit pit or a bottle cap. Worse, the technician who arrives next has to work in the residue. The mechanically useful measure is cold water and a running motor while food goes in, and ice cubes with a short run to knock deposits off the shredder ring. Grease is a different matter: it leaves the disposal as liquid and hardens in the line downstream, and the City of Tampa runs a wastewater grease program precisely because that hardening happens in the sewer as well as under the sink.

### The forgotten cause of a dishwasher that will not drain

A new disposal ships with the dishwasher inlet blocked by a molded plug. The installation instructions describe knocking that plug out with a screwdriver from inside the unit and removing the loose piece before connecting the dishwasher drain hose. When the plug stays in, the dishwasher fills, washes and cannot empty, and the complaint arrives as a broken dishwasher days after a disposal was replaced. When the knockout is removed but the loose plug is left inside the chamber, it circulates and jams the plate. Both are installation errors, not appliance failures, which is why a technician looking at a drainage complaint asks what changed under the sink recently and why the fix sometimes belongs with [dishwasher repair](../dishwasher-repair/).

### The plumbing side is where permit rules start to matter

Swapping a like-for-like unit onto an existing flange and discharge is a fixture replacement. Changing the drain arrangement is not. The City of Tampa's building code exempts a narrow set of minor repairs and replacement parts, and the code text explicitly withholds that allowance from work that includes addition to, alteration of, replacement of or relocation of any waste, vent, sewer, drainage or similar piping, from electric wiring systems, and from mechanical equipment. So a disposal added where none existed, a drain that has to be re-plumbed for a double bowl, or a new dedicated circuit and switch is permitted work with an inspection behind it. Ask which category the quote falls into before anyone starts cutting.

### What the visit covers, and when replacement is honest

A technician confirms voltage at the unit, tests the switch, works the wrench and the reset in the manufacturer's sequence, clears the chamber under a dead circuit, checks all three seal locations for evidence of leaking, verifies the dishwasher inlet is properly opened, and runs water through the discharge to separate a disposal fault from a blocked branch drain. Jams, switches, gaskets and flange resets are inexpensive. A leaking motor housing, a rusted-through grinding chamber, or a stripped mounting ring on an old unit are the three findings where a new disposal costs less than the labor to keep the old one, and that should be said plainly rather than discovered on the second visit.

## svc_gas_range_repair_lede

On a gas range the appliance and the fuel line are two separate jobs with two separate rule sets. Ignition, burner and oven faults are ordinary repairs. Anything that alters the piping is permitted work in Tampa, and the difference belongs on the estimate.

## svc_gas_range_repair_body

### Ignition faults, in the order they occur

A surface burner that clicks without lighting has spark but no fuel at the port, or spark jumping to the wrong place: a wet or cracked electrode, a food-fouled port, or a burner cap seated crooked. A burner that neither clicks nor lights points at the spark module or the switch under that knob. In the oven, a glow-bar igniter draws current to open the gas valve, and as it ages it still glows while drawing too little current to open the valve, so the oven preheats forever and never lights. That specific failure mode is why an igniter is measured under load rather than judged by whether it glows. A sealed oven that lights with a bang has delayed ignition, which is a service item now, not next month. The other appliance categories are on [the services index](../services/).

### Yellow flames and soot are a combustion finding

A properly adjusted burner on natural gas burns mostly blue. Lazy yellow tips, soot on the pan bottoms, or a persistent gas smell during cooking indicate air and fuel are out of balance: an orifice sized for propane on a natural gas supply after a move or a conversion, a blocked air shutter, or a low supply pressure. In houses with a strong kitchen hood the same symptoms appear for a different reason. The model residential code makeup air provision, section M1503.6, requires exhaust capable of more than 400 cubic feet per minute to be given makeup air at approximately the same rate where fuel-burning appliances that are not direct vent sit inside the dwelling's air barrier, because otherwise the exhaust depressurizes the room the burners are breathing from. Testing that means running the hood at full speed and watching the flames.

### What the Florida code says about the connection

Florida's residential code lists the permitted ways to connect an appliance to gas piping in section G2422.1: rigid metallic pipe and fittings, corrugated stainless steel tubing installed per its manufacturer's instructions, or a listed and labeled appliance connector complying with ANSI Z21.24 or CSA 6.10, installed per its instructions and located entirely in the same room as the appliance. Section G2422.1.2.1 limits a connector's overall length to 6 feet measured along its centerline, and G2422.1.1 requires connectors and tubing to be installed so they are protected against physical damage. Where the house has corrugated stainless steel tubing without a listed arc-resistant jacket, section G2411.2 requires the piping system to be electrically continuous and bonded to the electrical service grounding electrode system, with a jumper not smaller than 6 AWG copper and a length not exceeding 75 feet. None of that is optional, and none of it is something to discover during a range swap.

### The Tampa permit line, and where it falls

The City of Tampa's building code exempts a short list of gas work from permits: a portable heating appliance, and replacement of a minor part that neither alters the approval of the equipment nor makes it unsafe. The same code withholds the minor-repair allowance from anything that adds to, alters, replaces or relocates gas, waste, vent or similar piping. On the other side, the city's own trade permit limitation list, updated 11/14/2025, puts residential gas piping excluding tanks under a plumbing trade permit that does not require plan review. So replacing a burner valve or an igniter is appliance work, while moving the shutoff, replacing the flex connector run, or piping a range into a kitchen that never had gas is permitted work. Ask which side of that line a quote sits on before anyone starts.

### Older Tampa kitchens, propane and conversions

Hyde Park's local historic district covers roughly 367 acres and 1,223 buildings with a period of significance from 1886 to 1933, and kitchens in that stock have been reconfigured many times over. Two things recur. Ranges get moved between houses and between fuels, so a unit running on the wrong orifice set is common enough to check first when every burner is yellow. And a connector installed years ago runs behind the range where it has been kinked by the appliance being slid in and out. A connector with a sharp bend, a paint coating, or a splice is replaced, not reused, and the length limit above is the reason a longer one cannot simply be added.

### What the visit covers

A technician verifies gas is present and the shutoff is accessible, tests the spark module and each electrode, measures the oven igniter under load, checks the safety valve, inspects burner ports, caps and air shutters, confirms flame quality on every burner with the hood running, checks the connector for length, routing and damage, and leak-tests every joint disturbed. Igniters, electrodes, spark modules, valves and thermostats are ordinary parts-and-labor repairs. A cracked manifold, a failed safety valve on an old cabinet, or a range needing a full fuel conversion are the findings that make replacement the cheaper path. If the cooking surface alone is the fault, price [cooktop repair](../cooktop-repair/); if the cavity is the problem, see [oven and range repair](../oven-and-range-repair/).

## svc_ice_maker_repair_lede

An ice maker has three inputs, and only one of them is electrical. Water pressure, water quality and freezer temperature each stop production on their own, which is why the valve or the module gets replaced far more often than either one has actually failed.

## svc_ice_maker_repair_body

### Start at the water, because the specifications are published

Manufacturers state the conditions their machines need. General Electric's guidance for incoming ice maker water calls for water pressure between 20 psi minimum and 80 psi maximum, an incoming water temperature between 40 and 90 degrees, and best performance between 50 and 60 degrees. The same guidance requires water conductivity of at least 10 microSiemens per centimeter, says reverse osmosis water is acceptable only while it stays above that figure, and warns that deionized water is not recommended and could damage the machine. A commercial cuber spec sheet from Manitowoc frames the same envelope from the other side, listing an ambient range of 40 to 110 degrees, a water temperature range of 45 to 90 degrees and an inlet pressure range of 20 to 80 psi. Numbers like those turn a vague complaint into a measurement. Other appliance categories sit on [the services index](../services/).

### Hollow cubes, small cubes and no cubes are three different faults

A hollow or partly formed cube means water arrived late or in the wrong amount, which points at the inlet valve, a restricted supply line, a saddle valve half closed, or a clogged filter cartridge. No water at all points at the valve, its solenoid, the supply, or a fill tube frozen shut. Cubes that form but never release point at the ejector mechanism, the mold heater or the module motor. Ice that forms and then fuses into a single block in the bin is nearly always a bin or a door problem letting warm humid air in, not a maker problem at all.

### Mineral content is the local variable

Tampa's finished drinking water carries a substantial mineral load. The city's 2025 water quality report puts average hardness at 211 milligrams per liter, which works out to 12.3 grains per gallon. That much calcium plates out on the inlet valve screen, inside the fill tube and across the mold surface, and each of those deposits produces a different symptom: slow fill read by the control as a fill fault, a fill tube that ices over, and cubes that stick in the mold and stall the ejector. It also explains why a valve replaced without treating the water side can be back on an invoice within a year. Filter changes on the interval the manufacturer prints are cheaper than any of this.

### Freezer temperature is the quiet cause

Production depends on the mold reaching its cutoff temperature within the time the control allows. A freezer sitting warmer than zero degrees, the temperature federal food safety guidance names for frozen storage, slows every harvest cycle and can stop it entirely while the compartment still feels cold to a hand. Anything that raises freezer temperature therefore reads as an ice problem first: a failing defrost cycle, a blocked evaporator, a door left ajar by a packed drawer, or a gasket that no longer seals. That is why a proper visit measures compartment temperature before it ever pulls the ice maker, and why an ice complaint sometimes ends up as [refrigerator repair](../refrigerator-repair/) or [freezer repair](../freezer-repair/).

### Garage and lanai installations sit at the edge of the envelope

Second refrigerators and standalone ice makers in this city live in garages, utility rooms and covered outdoor kitchens. Whirlpool's own installation guidance for household refrigeration puts the factory preset airflow setting at ambient temperatures between 55 and 110 degrees. Florida Climate Center normals for Tampa International Airport across 1991 to 2020 give average daily highs of 91.0 degrees in July and 91.2 in August, and an unventilated garage runs hotter than the outside air on those afternoons. A condenser working against that heat lengthens the freeze cycle, cuts daily production, and makes a perfectly good machine look like it is failing.

### What the visit covers

Expect a measured water pressure reading at the supply, a check of the filter and its age, an inspection of the fill tube and the mold, a resistance check on the mold heater and the thermostat, a harvest cycle forced under observation, and compartment temperatures logged in both sections. The distinction that matters on the invoice is between a water-side repair, a module or valve replacement, and a sealed-system or defrost fault that happens to show up as missing ice. Only the third one is expensive, and it should never be diagnosed by guessing.

## svc_microwave_repair_lede

A microwave is the one appliance in the kitchen that can kill somebody who opens it. The high voltage section stays dangerous with the cord unplugged, so the useful question is not how to open it but which failures are worth paying to repair at all.

## svc_microwave_repair_body

### What is actually inside the cabinet

The transformer steps 120 volts up to roughly 2,000 volts on its main secondary winding, and the capacitor and diode shift that waveform so peaks reach about negative 4,000 volts. Around negative 3,200 volts the magnetron begins producing microwaves. An engineering review of microwave oven safety by Technical Safety BC records components operating at 4,000 to 5,000 volts depending on model, notes that the capacitor may be charged to 2,000 volts and can hold that charge after shutdown depending on its bleed resistor, and points out that most off-the-shelf multimeters are rated for 600 or 1,000 volts and are not rated for these measurements. The same report explains that the transformer is an isolation type, so ground fault protection upstream cannot see a fault on its output. That is the whole argument for leaving the cabinet closed. The rest of the appliance list is on [the services index](../services/).

### The failures that are worth a repair

Three faults account for most service calls and none of them are the magnetron. A door interlock switch that no longer confirms the door is closed stops the oven dead, and it is a small mechanical part. A dead touch panel or membrane keypad shows as buttons that beep for some digits and not others. On over-the-range units the exhaust blower motor, its grease-clogged filter and its damper fail long before the cooking side does, and that job is usually about airflow rather than heat.

### The failures that end the appliance

A magnetron that has lost emission, a burned transformer, or a cavity with rust through the floor or the waveguide cover area all point one direction. Parts and labor on the high voltage section commonly approach or exceed the price of a comparable countertop unit, so the arithmetic rarely favors repair on a small machine. Built-in and over-the-range units are the exception, because the cost is not only the oven but the cabinet opening, the trim kit and the duct connection that a replacement has to match. Arcing inside the cavity, a burning smell, or sparks around the waveguide cover mean stop using it now rather than finish the week.

### Surge damage is a specific local failure, not a general worry

Boards in these machines fail after storm-season voltage events, and Tampa gets those on a scale worth stating. Florida Public Service Commission outage reporting for Hurricane Milton logged 484,194 of Hillsborough County's 701,412 accounts without power, 69.03 percent of the county, as of 5:44 p.m. on October 11, 2024, with 483,981 of those on the Tampa Electric system. Restoration after an event of that size means repeated switching and sags, and a control that survives the outage sometimes fails days later. The signature is a panel that lights but will not accept a cycle, a unit that runs and then resets, or one output that no longer fires while the display looks normal.

### The kitchen that hosts it matters more than the brand

Over-the-range installations do two jobs, and the second one is ventilation. If the unit recirculates through a charcoal filter rather than ducting outside, every bit of cooking moisture stays in the room, which in this climate means a hood that never solves the problem it was bought for. Cabinet depth, the mounting plate pattern and the duct orientation all constrain what will physically replace a failed unit, so measure before shopping. If the complaint is smoke and steam rather than heating, that is really [a range hood question](../range-hood-repair/), and if the cooking surface underneath is also misbehaving, start with [cooktop repair](../cooktop-repair/) instead.

### What the visit covers

A technician verifies power at the receptacle, checks the door interlock chain and the latch board, confirms the panel responds across all keys, listens for the fan and the magnetron under load, and does a timed heating test with a measured volume of water to establish whether the unit still produces useful output. On an over-the-range unit the filter, blower and damper are inspected as part of the same visit. What the customer should get in writing is the failed part, the measured evidence for it, and the labor figure separated from the part, because on this appliance more than any other that is what makes the repair-or-replace decision obvious.

## svc_oven_and_range_repair_lede

An oven that will not reach temperature and an oven that overshoots are opposite faults with the same complaint attached. One is a dead heating element or a failed igniter; the other is a sensor reading low and a control obeying it. The measurement decides which part gets bought.

## svc_oven_and_range_repair_body

### Bake, broil and the burners fail separately

A range is several appliances sharing one cabinet, and the first job is deciding how many of them are broken. If the surface elements or the top burners work while the oven stays cold, the fault is downstream of the control on the oven circuit only. If the oven bakes but will not broil, the second element or the second igniter circuit is open. If nothing on an electric range heats but the clock and lights are alive, the machine is running on one leg of its 240-volt supply and the problem is at the breaker or the feeder, not inside the appliance. Sorting that out at the start is what keeps a fifty-dollar element from being quoted as a control board. The other appliance categories these tests rule out are listed on [the services index](../services/).

### The temperature sensor and the control argue, and the sensor usually wins

Modern ovens hold temperature with a resistance probe mounted through the rear wall. Its resistance climbs as the cavity heats, and the control converts that reading into a setpoint decision. When the probe drifts, the control faithfully heats to the wrong number, so a cake burns at 350 while the display insists nothing is wrong. The honest diagnosis compares a thermometer in the cavity against the display through a full preheat and a full cycle, then measures the probe at room temperature and hot. A control board replaced without that comparison has a good chance of coming back, because the board was reporting what it was told.

### Self-clean is where the expensive failures happen

Pyrolytic cleaning drives the cavity far above cooking temperature with the door locked, and it is the single hardest thing the appliance ever does. The heat routinely takes out the door latch motor, the thermal cutout, the cooling fan and, on some designs, the control board that sits directly above the cavity. A range that died during or right after a clean cycle should be diagnosed in that order rather than as a random control failure. If time allows before an event, the safer path is a manual clean and a working oven instead of a locked door and a dead board.

### The bracket behind the range is not optional

Free-standing ranges tip forward when weight lands on an open door, and the anti-tip bracket that prevents it has been required since 1991. The Consumer Product Safety Commission states that from 1980 through 2008 its staff was aware of 38 fatalities associated with range tipovers, twenty of them, or 53 percent, involving children aged one to five, along with 84 injuries recorded from 1980 through 2006, most of them burns from hot liquid spilling out of pans as the range tilted. Additional marking and installation requirements took effect on February 18, 2012. Any visit that pulls a range out to work on it should end with that bracket engaged again, and a range that has never had one gets one.

### Older Tampa kitchens complicate the electrical side

The Hyde Park local historic district covers roughly 367 acres and 1,223 buildings with a period of significance running from 1886 to 1933, listed on the National Register in 1985 and designated locally in 1988. Kitchens in that stock have been rebuilt repeatedly, and a modern range frequently lands on a circuit that was sized for something smaller. A scorched receptacle, a warm cord, or a breaker that trips only during preheat is a supply problem wearing an appliance costume. That distinction also decides who does the work: the City of Tampa's own trade permit list puts electrical rewiring and panel, meter can and riser replacement under an electrical trade permit, so a range that needs its circuit corrected needs a permitted electrician rather than an appliance part.

### What the visit covers, and where repair stops paying

Expect a check of every element and burner in turn, a resistance reading on the oven probe, a comparison of actual cavity temperature against the display, a look at the door hinges, gasket and latch, and continuity checks on the thermal cutouts before any board is suspected. Elements, igniters, probes, switches and latches are ordinary parts-and-labor repairs. A cracked glass cooktop on a slide-in, a failed dual control assembly, and a cabinet with rusted-through insulation are the three findings that usually move the conversation toward replacement. If the surface is the only broken part, price [cooktop repair](../cooktop-repair/) separately, and if the fuel side is the fault, gas work has its own rules on [the gas range page](../gas-range-repair/).

## svc_range_hood_repair_lede

A range hood has one job, which is moving cooking air outside, and it fails in only a few ways: the blower, the damper, the filter, or a duct that was never right. Whether the hood ever worked depends on where the air comes from as much as where it goes.

## svc_range_hood_repair_body

### Loud and useless is a different fault from silent

A hood that roars and moves nothing is almost always restriction rather than motor failure. A grease-saturated baffle or mesh filter, a damper flap stuck shut by hardened grease, a run of flexible duct crushed above the cabinet, or a roof or wall cap with a screen behind it all produce the same complaint. A hood that is silent has lost the motor, its capacitor, the speed switch or its power. The distinguishing test takes a minute: hold a tissue to the filter opening on high, then check the exterior cap for movement. If air moves at the filter but not outside, the problem is between them. The remaining appliance categories are on [the services index](../services/).

### Recirculating hoods cannot solve a humidity problem

Many hoods, including most over-the-range microwaves, can be configured either to duct outside or to recirculate through a charcoal filter back into the room. A recirculating installation removes some grease and some odor and returns all of the water vapor and all of the combustion products. In a climate where indoor humidity is already the limiting factor for comfort and for appliance condensation, that distinction matters. Before quoting a blower on a hood that has never performed, the first question is whether it is ducted at all, which is answered by looking for a cap outside rather than by reading the model number. Converting a recirculating installation to a ducted one is construction work with a roof or wall penetration, not an appliance repair, and it should be priced that way.

### The makeup air rule that decides whether a big hood works

A powerful hood in a reasonably tight house cannot exhaust air that has nowhere to come from. The model residential code addresses it directly: section M1503.6 requires that where one or more fuel-burning appliances that are neither direct vent nor mechanically drafted sit inside the dwelling's air barrier, each exhaust system capable of exhausting more than 400 cubic feet per minute be provided with makeup air at approximately the exhaust rate, with at least one damper. That is a safety provision about backdrafting, and it is also the practical reason a 600 cubic foot per minute hood installed without makeup air produces a whistling door, a hood that seems weak on high, and yellow-tipped burner flames. A hood complaint on a large unit therefore includes a look at where replacement air enters.

### Grease is the maintenance item and the fire risk

Filters are consumable. Baffle and mesh filters go into a dishwasher or a hot degreasing soak on a schedule set by how often the kitchen fries, and charcoal filters on a recirculating unit are replaced rather than washed. Beyond the filter, grease that gets past it coats the blower wheel, unbalances it and produces a vibration that gets blamed on a bearing, and it accumulates in the first feet of duct. The City of Tampa runs a wastewater grease program for the same reason that grease matters here: it does not stay liquid once it cools, and the deposit it leaves behind is what a hood service is actually removing. Cleaning the wheel and the housing is part of a repair on this appliance, because a new motor spinning a fouled wheel will sound exactly as bad as the old one.

### Older Tampa kitchens and the duct that was never there

In the Hyde Park local historic district, roughly 367 acres and 1,223 buildings with a period of significance from 1886 to 1933, and in the bungalow stock of Seminole Heights, kitchen exhaust was added long after construction. What that produces is predictable: a duct routed through a soffit with several sharp turns, a run that terminates in an attic instead of outside, or a hood over an island fed by a duct in the floor. The number of turns and the length are what determine whether the rated airflow is ever achieved, so the honest first step on a chronically weak hood in that kind of house is tracing the duct rather than replacing the unit. A duct terminating inside the building is a defect to correct in any case, since it deposits moisture and grease in the structure.

### What the visit covers

Expect airflow verified at the filter and at the exterior cap, the filters inspected and cleaned or replaced, the blower wheel and housing examined for grease loading and imbalance, the damper checked for free movement, the motor and its capacitor tested electrically, the speed control and lighting circuit checked, and the duct traced as far as it is accessible. Motors, capacitors, switches, dampers, lights and filters are ordinary parts-and-labor repairs. A hood whose duct is undersized, unterminated or missing is not repaired by parts, and that conclusion belongs in writing. If the unit above the range is a microwave doing hood duty, see [microwave repair](../microwave-repair/); if the burners underneath are also behaving badly, start with [cooktop repair](../cooktop-repair/).

## svc_same_day_appliance_repair_lede

Some appliance failures are urgent and some only feel urgent. Food at risk, water on a floor and a gas smell are same-day problems. A noisy dryer is not, and it is worth knowing which is which before a schedule gets rearranged.

## svc_same_day_appliance_repair_body

### The clock on food is published, so triage starts there

Centers for Disease Control guidance for a power loss or a failed appliance sets the intervals: with the doors kept closed, food stays safe up to four hours in a refrigerator, 48 hours in a full freezer and 24 hours in a half-full one, with 40 degrees or below the refrigerator target and 0 degrees or below the freezer target, and anything thawed or above 40 degrees discarded. Those numbers are the reason a warm refrigerator with a full shelf of perishables outranks almost everything else in a day's schedule, and the reason a freezer that is packed solid has more slack than one that is half empty. They also give a customer something useful to do in the meantime, which is to stop opening the door and to consolidate what matters. The full appliance list is on [the services index](../services/).

### What genuinely earns a same-day visit

Four situations do. A refrigerator or freezer above its target temperature with food in it. Water actively escaping an appliance, because the damage compounds by the hour into cabinet bases and flooring rather than staying on the tile. Any gas odor around a range or a dryer, which is a shutoff-and-leave situation before it is a repair. And an appliance with visible electrical damage, a scorched cord or a burning smell, which should be unplugged and left alone. Everything else, including a dishwasher that cleans badly, a dryer that takes two cycles, a noisy washer or a broken ice maker, is a scheduled repair, and pricing it as an emergency does the customer no favors.

### What can be done before the truck arrives

For a warm refrigerator, check that the condenser is not choked and that a vent inside the cabinet is not blocked by packed food, then leave the door shut. For water, close the appliance's own supply valve, and if that valve is seized close the house valve rather than fighting it. For a washer full of water, bail it down with a container instead of tipping the machine, because tipping sends water over the cabinet lip into the control area. For a dryer, do not run it with a scorched smell or a disconnected duct. None of that is a repair, but each one limits what the repair has to cover.

### Storm season changes the demand pattern in this city

Same-day capacity in Tampa is governed by weather more than by anything else. Florida Public Service Commission outage reporting for Hurricane Milton recorded 484,194 of Hillsborough County's 701,412 accounts without power, 69.03 percent of the county, at 5:44 p.m. on October 11, 2024. Two effects follow. During and immediately after an event, the food clock is running for a very large number of households at once, so the honest answer about scheduling is a real one rather than an optimistic one. And in the days after restoration, failure calls rise sharply, because control boards, start relays and inverter drives that survived the outage fail on the repeated switching and voltage sags that come with bringing a grid back up. A machine that quit a week after a storm is a legitimate surge-related repair, not a coincidence.

### What a same-day visit should actually deliver

The value is a diagnosis and a price on the first visit, not a fast arrival followed by a second appointment. That means the technician measures rather than guesses, names the failed part, and quotes parts and labor separately before work starts. Where the part is on the truck the job finishes that day. Where it is not, the customer should leave the visit knowing the part, the lead time and the total, and with the appliance left in the safest condition available, which sometimes means a water supply isolated or a machine deliberately taken out of service.

### The limits worth stating plainly

Sealed-system refrigeration work, gas piping changes and electrical circuit corrections are not same-day items in the ordinary sense, because they involve recovery equipment, permits or another trade. Under the City of Tampa's building code, emergency equipment repairs may be made and the permit application submitted by the next working business day, which is exactly the framework that covers an urgent failure needing permitted work. Being clear about that on the first call is better than promising a same-day close on a job that legitimately takes two. For the two failures that most often prompt these calls, the diagnostic paths are on [refrigerator repair](../refrigerator-repair/) and [freezer repair](../freezer-repair/).

## svc_stackable_washer_dryer_repair_lede

A stacked pair is two appliances in a closet built for one, and access is the constraint on every repair. The parts fail the same way they do on a full-size set, but reaching them means unstacking, and that changes both the labor and the order of diagnosis.

## svc_stackable_washer_dryer_repair_body

### Two machines, one column, one power problem

Stacked sets come two ways: a true laundry center where washer and dryer share a single cabinet and one control, or a matched front-load pair joined by a stacking kit. The distinction decides everything. On a laundry center a single control board runs both halves, so a dryer that will not start can be a washer-side fault, and a replacement means replacing the whole column. On a stacked pair each machine is independent, each has its own supply, and either can be pulled and serviced on its own. Before anything is unstacked, both supplies get verified, because a dryer sitting dead above a working washer is frequently just its own breaker. The remaining appliance categories are on [the services index](../services/).

### The dryer half needs a 30-amp circuit and a real vent

Whirlpool's electric dryer installation instructions call for a separate 30-amp circuit with a 3 or 4 wire, single phase, 120/240 volt, 60 hertz supply, and they instruct owners not to operate the dryer at temperatures below 45 degrees, because at lower ambient temperatures automatic cycles may not shut off and drying times stretch. In a closet the vent is the bigger issue. The transition duct behind a stacked pair has to make its turn in a few inches of clearance, and the standard failure is a crushed or kinked transition that no one can see with the machines in place. A stacked dryer that heats but dries slowly is nearly always airflow, and the test is at the exterior hood rather than at the drum.

### The washer half has nowhere to move

A front-loader spinning at speed inside a closet has no room to walk, so a suspension problem announces itself as banging against the walls or against the stacking kit rather than as a machine sliding across a floor. Two findings recur. Shipping bolts left in place on an installation, which transmit every vibration into the cabinet and the structure. And a machine leveled on the wrong reference, since a stack is only as square as the washer under it and an out-of-plumb column loads the stacking brackets. The drain side has a published dimension worth knowing: the residential code section for standpipes, P2706.1.2, requires them to extend not less than 18 inches and not more than 42 inches above the trap weir, and closet installations are where that gets ignored, producing siphoning or backflow that looks like a pump fault.

### Older Tampa houses put laundry where it fits

The Seminole Heights historic district covers roughly 215 acres and 703 buildings with a period of significance from 1912 to 1939, and it was designated locally in 1995 after its National Register listing in 1993. Houses of that era were not built with laundry rooms, so the pair usually ends up in a converted closet, a back porch or a rear addition, with a vent run improvised through a wall that was never designed for one. Three consequences follow: a vent that turns immediately and repeatedly, a floor that flexes under spin, and a door swing that means the appliance cannot come out without removing the door frame trim. All three belong in the estimate, because the labor on a stacked repair is mostly the getting-there.

### Unstacking is a procedure, not a lift

A stacked dryer is top-heavy and the kit that holds it is designed to resist vibration, not to carry a machine being tilted. The correct sequence is power and gas or vent disconnected, the dryer lifted straight off with two people, set on cardboard, and the stacking brackets inspected for elongated holes before the column goes back together. Bracket hardware that has worked loose is a real finding on a set that has been banging for months, and it is cheap to correct while the machines are apart. Reusing a bent bracket is how a column ends up out of plumb again a week later.

### What the visit covers, and what it costs to reach

Expect both supplies verified, error codes read from each half, the drain filter and pump checked on the washer, airflow measured at the dryer hood, the transition duct inspected once the machines are separated, the door lock circuit and lid switch tested, the heat circuit and its safety devices measured, and the stacking hardware and leveling corrected on reassembly. The parts are the same parts as on a full-size set. What differs is labor, because a repair that takes forty minutes on a side-by-side pair takes longer when the top machine has to come down first. On a single-cabinet laundry center a failed main control or a bearing is the point where a replacement column competes with the repair, and both figures should appear on the same estimate. For a dryer that heats but will not dry, start with [dryer vent cleaning](../dryer-vent-cleaning/); for a new pair going into a tight space, see [washer and dryer installation](../washer-dryer-installation/).

## svc_washer_dryer_installation_lede

Most of what goes wrong with a new laundry pair in the first year traces to the installation rather than the machines. The drain height, the vent run and the electrical supply each have published requirements, and each one is cheaper to get right on the first day.

## svc_washer_dryer_installation_body

### The drain has a dimension, and closets are where it is missed

The residential code section covering standpipes, P2706.1.2, requires a standpipe to extend not less than 18 inches and not more than 42 inches above the trap weir. Where a laundry tray waste line ties into the same standpipe, section P2706.1.2.1 requires the standpipe to extend not less than 30 inches above its trap weir and above the flood level rim of the tray, with the tray outlet not more than 30 inches horizontally from the standpipe trap. Those numbers exist because a standpipe that is too short lets a modern high-pressure pump push water back out, and one drained into a trap set below the floor siphons the seal. Getting this right at installation prevents a first-year service call that presents as a leaking washer and is really a drain. The other appliance categories are on [the services index](../services/).

### The dryer's supply is a published specification

Whirlpool's electric dryer installation instructions call for a separate 30-amp circuit and a 3 or 4 wire, single phase, 120/240 volt, 60 hertz AC supply, and they instruct owners not to operate the dryer below 45 degrees ambient because automatic cycles may not shut off at lower temperatures. The wiring detail is the one that surprises people: older houses have three-slot dryer receptacles and current machines ship configured for four-wire cords with a separate ground, so the cord and the internal neutral strap have to be matched to what is actually in the wall. A new dryer is not the moment to improvise on that, and a receptacle that is scorched, loose or warm gets corrected by an electrician rather than adapted with a plug.

### Older Tampa services and the panel question

Where the electrical supply itself is undersized, the appliance is not the fix. The National Electrical Code sets minimum ratings for the service disconnecting means in section 230.79, and for a one-family dwelling that minimum is 100 amperes, three-wire, with 60 amperes the minimum for other installations. Housing in the Hyde Park local historic district, roughly 367 acres and 1,223 buildings with a period of significance from 1886 to 1933, and comparable central-city stock, has been rewired in stages over many decades, so a laundry pair frequently lands on whatever circuit was nearest. The City of Tampa's trade permit limitation list, updated 11/14/2025, puts electrical rewiring and panel, meter can and riser replacement under an electrical trade permit, which is the correct route when the answer is a new circuit rather than a different appliance.

### The vent decides whether the dryer performs

A new dryer inherits the duct that is already in the wall, and that duct is the single biggest determinant of how it will dry. What gets checked at installation: that the transition duct is a listed single length rather than a joined run, that it is not crushed behind the machine, that the rigid duct is smooth-walled metal, that joints are sealed without screws projecting into the airflow, that the exterior hood has a working damper and no screen, and that the run is not so long and turned that the machine cannot exhaust. Airflow measured at the exterior hood on the first heated cycle is what confirms all of it, and it takes a few minutes. If the existing duct is the limiting factor, the correct sequence is [dryer vent cleaning](../dryer-vent-cleaning/) or duct correction before the pair is signed off, not after the first complaint.

### Water supply, leveling and the details that cause first-year calls

Supply hoses are the most common source of laundry water damage in any house, and the fix is new hoses with fresh washers on every installation rather than reusing whatever came off the last machine, plus valves that actually close. Leveling is not cosmetic on a front-loader: a machine off level or still carrying its shipping bolts will walk, hammer and damage its own suspension, which in older Tampa houses on wood floors over crawl spaces is a real risk rather than a theoretical one. Clearance behind the machines has to accommodate the hoses and the transition duct without kinking, which frequently means the pair sits an inch further forward than the customer expects.

### What the installation covers

Expect the old pair disconnected and removed, the standpipe height and trap verified, new supply hoses fitted and the valves tested, the electrical supply and receptacle type confirmed against the machine's requirement, the transition duct replaced and the exterior hood checked, both machines leveled and the shipping hardware removed, a fill and drain cycle run on the washer, a heated cycle run on the dryer with airflow verified at the hood, and any code or supply defect found in the process reported in writing rather than worked around. If the pair is going into a closet as a column, see [stackable washer and dryer repair](../stackable-washer-dryer-repair/) for the access constraints that apply.

## svc_wine_cooler_repair_lede

A wine cooler is judged by how steady it holds a narrow band, not by how cold it gets. Most complaints trace to the room it sits in rather than the machine, because these units are rated for a much smaller ambient range than a refrigerator.

## svc_wine_cooler_repair_body

### Thermoelectric and compressor units are not the same appliance

A thermoelectric unit moves heat with a solid-state Peltier module and a pair of fans. It is quiet, it has no refrigerant, and it can only pull the interior a fixed number of degrees below the room, which is why one in a warm space simply never reaches its setpoint even though nothing has failed. A compressor unit is a small sealed refrigeration system and behaves like one: it has a condenser, a fan, a thermostat or control board, and a defrost or cycling pattern. The diagnosis path splits at that identification, so the model number comes first. A thermoelectric unit with a dead module or fan is usually not economically repairable; a compressor unit with a failed fan, thermostat, control board or door seal usually is. The rest of the appliance list is on [the services index](../services/).

### The published ambient limits are narrower than people expect

Manufacturer instructions for compressor-cooled built-in wine coolers are explicit about the room. One Whynter dual-zone manual recommends installation where the ambient temperature is between 72 and 78 degrees, requires a well ventilated area above 60 and below 90 degrees, notes performance may be affected outside those figures, states the front of the appliance must be completely unobstructed, prohibits outdoor installation, and warns against placing the unit next to ovens, grills or other sources of high heat. Set that against Florida Climate Center normals for Tampa International Airport, which give average daily highs of 91.0 degrees in July and 91.2 in August across 1991 to 2020. A cooler in a garage, a lanai or an unconditioned bar area spends the summer above its own rated ceiling, and no repair changes that.

### Built-in versus free-standing is a ventilation question

A free-standing unit rejects its heat out the back and sides and needs clearance to do it. A built-in unit vents through a front grille at the toe kick precisely so it can live in a cabinet run. Installing a free-standing model in a tight cabinet opening is one of the most common causes of a unit that cannot hold temperature, cycles constantly and eventually loses its compressor, and it is not a defect. The tell is a condenser and fan you can only reach from behind, in a cabinet with no rear or upper opening. Where the opening cannot be changed, the honest answer is a true built-in model rather than another repair.

### What the temperature complaint usually turns out to be

Four findings account for most calls that a cooler runs warm. A door seal deformed by heat or by bottles pressing against the shelves, which is checked with a paper drag test around the whole perimeter. A condenser packed with dust in a low cabinet, since the grille sits at floor level where lint collects. A failed condenser or evaporator fan, which leaves a machine that runs and hums but stratifies badly between top and bottom shelves. And a control or thermistor drift, which shows as a display that reads its setpoint while a thermometer on the middle shelf reads six degrees off. Only the last one is diagnosed with a meter, and it should never be assumed first.

### Small charges and flammable refrigerant change the repair rules

Many of these cabinets use isobutane. Environmental Protection Agency risk screening for household refrigeration notes that isobutane is listed as acceptable for household refrigerators and freezers subject to use conditions and charge limits, and that under UL 60335-2-24 the maximum allowable charge for an A3 classified refrigerant such as isobutane is 150 grams. A charge that small means a leak of a few grams destroys performance, so a system that has lost its charge cannot be topped off as a normal service item, and it means any sealed-system work has to follow the flammable-refrigerant handling procedures the standard exists to enforce. For a customer that translates into a plain rule: sealed-system faults on a small wine cabinet usually favor replacement, while fans, seals, thermistors and control boards favor repair.

### What the visit covers

Expect the model identified and its cooling technology confirmed, shelf temperatures logged top and bottom against the display, the ambient in the room measured, the installation checked against the manufacturer's clearance and built-in requirements, the condenser cleaned and its fan verified, the door seal tested along its length, and thermistors and control outputs measured before any board is named. Fans, seals, hinges, thermistors and controls are ordinary parts-and-labor repairs. A dead Peltier stack or a leaking sealed system is where the replacement comparison belongs. If the same customer has a failing kitchen refrigerator, that work is [refrigerator repair](../refrigerator-repair/), and a fully integrated cabinet unit is [built-in refrigerator repair](../built-in-refrigerator-repair/).
