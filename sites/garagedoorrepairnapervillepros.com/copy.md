## svc_garage_door_spring_repair_lede
A snapped spring is the most common garage door failure in Naperville and the one people most often try to handle themselves. It is also the one that hurts people.

## svc_garage_door_spring_repair_body
### What a snapped spring sounds like

Almost everyone describes it the same way: a bang from the garage, loud enough that they thought something fell off a shelf. Then the door will not lift. If you try the opener, the motor strains against a door that suddenly weighs its full one hundred and fifty to two hundred pounds instead of the near-zero it normally feels like. Walk out and look at the bar above the door. On a torsion setup you will see a coiled spring with a visible two-inch gap where it separated. On an older extension-spring door you will see a stretched spring hanging loose along the horizontal track.

### Why the spring type matters in this city

Naperville's garage stock spans about six decades, and the spring system tracks that age closely. The 1960s and 1970s subdivisions on the older side of town were built with extension springs running alongside the tracks, often on one-piece doors with tight headroom above the opening. Everything built from the 1990s onward is nearly always a sectional steel door on a single torsion bar mounted on the header. Those are different parts, different tools, and different failure behavior. A technician who assumes torsion and shows up without extension hardware makes a second trip, which is the whole reason we ask what your street looks like before dispatching.

### Do not lift it and do not touch the winding cones

A torsion spring holds several hundred pounds of stored energy even after it breaks, because the remaining half is still under partial tension. Winding bars are the only correct tool and there is no household substitute. Screwdrivers slip. This is the repair that sends people to Edward Hospital every winter. If the spring is broken: unplug the opener so nobody hits the button, leave the door down, and wait.

### Springs are replaced in pairs

If your door has two springs and one broke, the second one is the same age with the same cycle count and it is going to fail within months. Replacing both at once costs marginally more in parts and saves a second service call. A technician who quotes you one spring on a two-spring door is either not paying attention or is planning on the return visit. Standard residential springs are rated around ten thousand cycles, which for a household that opens the door four times a day works out to somewhere around seven years. High-cycle springs roughly double that for a modest premium and are worth it on the door you actually use as your front entrance.

### What the visit involves

The technician measures wire gauge, inside diameter and overall length off the old spring, or reads the numbers stamped on the cone if it is still legible. That determines the replacement. Both springs come off, the drums get reset, new springs go on and get wound to the correct number of turns for your door's height and weight. Cables get inspected while everything is loose, because a spring failure often frays a cable on the way down. Then the door gets balanced by hand: disconnected from the opener, it should hold still at waist height rather than dropping or flying up. That balance test is what tells you the job was done right, and you can ask to watch it.

### Pricing

You get the number before any work starts. Spring replacement is a parts-and-labor job with a fairly narrow range, and the variables are how many springs, what gauge, and whether the cables and drums also need replacing. Nobody should be quoting you a price for this over the phone without knowing the door height and the spring configuration, but you should absolutely get a firm number before the tools come out.

## svc_garage_door_opener_repair_lede
Two very different complaints get called opener problems: the door that starts down and reverses back up, and the motor that runs while the door sits still. Neither usually means you need a new opener.

## svc_garage_door_opener_repair_body
### The door goes down, then comes back up

This is a safety system doing its job, correctly or incorrectly. Two photo-eye sensors sit about six inches off the floor on either side of the opening, and they have to see each other for the door to close. Sun angle through an open garage in late afternoon can wash one out. A bike, a recycling bin, a snow shovel leaned against the frame will break the beam. Sensors also drift out of alignment on their own over years of the door shaking the frame. Look at the small indicator light on each sensor: on most brands, a steady light on both means aligned, and a flickering or dark light on one means it is not seeing its partner.

If the sensors are clean, aligned and lit, the next suspect is the close-force setting on the motor head. As a door gets stiffer with age, the opener needs more force to push it down, and when it hits the programmed limit it reads that resistance as an obstruction and reverses. Turning the force up is the wrong fix. It masks a door that has developed a mechanical problem, and it defeats the protection that makes the reversal happen in the first place.

### The motor runs but nothing moves

Look up at the rail while someone presses the button. If the chain or belt is moving and the door is not, the trolley has disconnected from the carriage. Usually that is the emergency release cord: somebody pulled it, or it got snagged, and the trolley never re-engaged. Re-seating it is a two-minute fix and you can do it yourself by pulling the cord toward the door and running the opener until it clicks back in.

If the motor hums and the chain does not move at all, it is inside the unit. On chain-drive openers about fifteen years old the plastic drive gear is the usual answer — the nylon teeth wear down and stop grabbing the worm gear. Grinding noise with no movement is the signature. That is a repair, not a replacement, and it is worth asking about before someone quotes you a new opener. A motor that hums for a second and quits without any grinding is more often the capacitor.

### When replacement is actually the right call

If your opener predates 1993 it has no photo-eye sensors, because they were not required yet, and that is a genuine reason to replace rather than repair. Beyond that: parts availability on discontinued models, a cracked motor housing, or a unit that has already had two repairs in two years. Otherwise, most openers on Naperville houses are worth fixing. A quiet belt drive with a battery backup is a nice upgrade and is worth considering on an attached garage under a bedroom, but it is an upgrade, not a repair, and it should be presented to you that way.

### Battery backup and Illinois law

Illinois requires battery backup on new residential opener installations, so if you are replacing the unit entirely you are getting one whether you asked or not. It is genuinely useful here — a summer storm that knocks out power to your block otherwise leaves you lifting a heavy door by hand or parked on the driveway.

### What you will be told

Which of the above it is, what the part costs, and whether the repair is worth doing against the age of the unit. If the honest answer is that a fifteen-year-old opener will need something else within the year, you should hear that, along with the option to fix it anyway.

## svc_off_track_garage_door_repair_lede
A door hanging crooked in the opening is a cable, roller or track problem. It is also the failure mode most likely to get worse in the ten minutes you spend deciding what to do.

## svc_off_track_garage_door_repair_body
### Leave it exactly where it is

A door off its track is held up by whatever is left of the cables and rollers, and that arrangement is not stable. Running the opener to see what happens is how a partial derailment turns into a door lying on a car hood. Unplug the opener, keep everyone out from under it, and do not try to muscle it back onto the rail. This one is genuinely urgent and gets moved to the front of the day.

### The three things that cause it

**One cable came off the drum.** Steel lift cables run from the bottom bracket up to a grooved drum at each end of the torsion bar. If one unspools — often after a spring break drops the door hard — that side loses support and the door racks in the opening. The tell is one side sitting noticeably lower than the other with a slack cable visible along the jamb.

**A roller left the track.** Rollers are the small wheels on the edge of each section. Nylon ones go brittle and crack; steel ones seize when the bearings dry out. A seized roller drags instead of rolling, and eventually climbs out of the track, usually at the curve where the vertical section turns horizontal.

**The track itself is bent.** Backing into it is the classic cause. Around here, so is wind. The August 11 derecho that came through northern Illinois bent a lot of track in this town, and that damage is far more consequential than the dents people call about. A door can look basically fine from the driveway and still be running through a track that has been pushed a quarter inch out of alignment, which will chew through rollers until it is corrected.

### Why the dent is not the problem

This is the single most common misunderstanding after a storm. Homeowners call about a visible dent in a panel, which is cosmetic and can wait, while the real damage is the bent track and bowed strut that are quietly destroying the rollers every cycle. If your door took wind damage, the question to ask is not how the panel looks. It is whether the track is still straight and whether the struts across the back of the sections are still flat.

### Cables and rollers are wear items

Lift cables fray at the bottom bracket where they take the most flex and where road salt collects over an Illinois winter. A frayed cable is a scheduled replacement; a snapped one is an emergency. Rollers on a door that gets daily use are typically good for ten to fifteen years. Both are inexpensive parts, and both are worth replacing as a set while the door is already apart rather than one at a time as they fail.

### The repair

The door gets secured with clamps below the bottom roller so it cannot move. Sections come back into alignment one at a time, rollers get re-seated or replaced, cables get respooled on the drums with equal tension on both sides, and any bent track gets straightened or swapped. Then the same hand-balance check as any other job: disconnected from the opener, the door should hold at waist height on its own. If it does not, the spring tension is off and the rollers will wear out again.

### What it costs

Rollers and cables are cheap. Track sections are moderate. What drives the number is how far the door came off and whether any sections got bent in the process. You get that number before work starts, and if a section is damaged badly enough that replacement makes more sense than repair, you get told that instead of being charged for both.

## svc_garage_door_replacement_lede
Replacing a door is the only garage door job in Naperville that can turn into a permit question, and only when you change the size or the style of the opening.

## svc_garage_door_replacement_body
### One panel or the whole door

A single caved-in section can often be replaced on its own, and that is the cheaper path when it works. Two things determine whether it is available: whether the manufacturer still makes your panel, and how badly the color has aged. A ten-year-old almond door that has been facing south down a Naperville street has faded, and a factory-fresh panel of the same nominal color will not match it. Sometimes that is fine because the new section sits at the bottom where nobody looks closely. Sometimes it looks worse than the dent did. You should get an honest read on that before you commit, not after.

Discontinued panels are the more common blocker. Doors older than roughly fifteen years frequently have no matching section still in production, at which point full replacement is the only real option.

### The permit rule here, specifically

Naperville's Guide to Building Permits requires a permit for door replacement when the work alters the size or the style of the opening, or when it puts a door in a new location. A straight like-for-like swap — same opening, same dimensions, same style — is not on the permit-required list.

That distinction matters more than it sounds. If you are replacing a plain steel double door with another plain steel double door in the same opening, it is usually a same-visit job and there is nothing to file. If you want windows added across the top, or a carriage-house style instead of raised panel, or the opening widened, you are in permit territory and the timeline changes from a day to a few weeks. Confirm your specific job with the City of Naperville before work starts. Worth knowing: the city sits across both DuPage and Will County, so double-check which jurisdiction applies to your address if anything about your project is unusual.

### What to actually choose

**Insulation.** An attached garage sharing a wall with a bedroom or a finished bonus room above it benefits from an insulated door in a way a detached garage does not. R-values on residential doors run roughly from 6 on a basic insulated steel door up to 18 or so on a premium sandwich-construction door. In this climate the insulated option is generally worth it on an attached garage and generally not worth the premium on a detached one.

**Construction.** Two-layer steel doors handle Illinois weather and dents better than single-layer, and they run quieter. Three-layer with a steel back skin is quieter still and stiffer, which matters if your opening is a wide double.

**Wind and struts.** After the storms this area has had, the horizontal struts across the back of each section are worth paying attention to. They are what keeps a wide door from bowing under wind load, and they are also the first thing a cost-cut door leaves out.

### The visit and the timeline

Measurement comes first: opening width and height, headroom above the opening, sideroom on each side, and backroom depth. Older Naperville garages on the 1960s side of town frequently have limited headroom, which rules out standard torsion mounting and calls for a low-headroom track and a different spring position. That has to be caught at measurement, not on install day.

A stock-size like-for-like door on a standard opening can often be ordered and installed inside a week. Custom sizes, custom styles and windows push that out to several weeks, because the door is built to order.

### Pricing

You get an itemized number covering the door, the hardware, the labor and the haul-away of the old door, before anything is ordered. Ask specifically whether new track and new springs are included, because the old spring is sized for the old door's weight and a heavier insulated replacement needs different spring specs to balance correctly.

### A range is not vagueness

Every figure in that table is a band because three things are unknown until somebody looks at the door. Which component failed is the first. Second is the door's weight and width, since a wide insulated double door needs heavier springs, heavier cable and stronger hardware than a light single door on a 1960s garage. Third is what shape the rest of the assembly is in, and that is where Naperville differs from a newer suburb.

The city's median home dates to 1989, but the garage stock spans about six decades. Cress Creek was platted in the 1960s and holds well over a thousand residences. Ashbury filled in during the early and middle 1990s off 95th Street. The Historic District east of downtown is older still. Doors from the 1960s and 1970s are frequently one-piece or extension-spring systems with very little headroom above the opening, while anything built from the 1990s onward is almost certainly sectional steel on a torsion bar. Those two systems need different parts, different tools and different amounts of time, which is why the same phone complaint produces two different prices depending on the address.

### Why this market prices above the national average

Labor. The May 2025 median hourly wage for carpenters across the Chicago-Naperville-Elgin metro is $38.68, against roughly $23 for the same occupation in the large Texas metros. Once a service vehicle, insurance, a stocked spring inventory and drive time are added, the billed hour follows that wage upward. Nothing about the parts is regional; the hours are.

Climate does the rest. Freeze and thaw cycles, road salt carried in on tires and slush standing against the bottom section attack exactly the components that carry the door: cable, bearings, hinge pins and the bottom brackets. A spring rated for 10,000 cycles in a mild climate reaches its end sooner here, and a rusted hinge fastener that has to be cut out rather than unscrewed is quiet, invisible labor time that shows up in the invoice.

### What the city charges, and what it does not

The distinction matters. Naperville's building permit guidance requires a permit when a door replacement alters the size or style of the opening or puts a door in a new location. A straight like-for-like swap is not on the permit-required list, and a spring, cable, roller or opener repair is service on an existing door rather than a replacement. When a permit does apply, the residential fee schedule adopted under Ordinance 18-145 charges an $18 clerical fee plus $25 per plan page for miscellaneous residential permits, with $50 for the inspection and $68 for any reinspection, payable before the return visit is booked. Anyone proposing a wider opening, a taller door or added glass should describe that filing as part of the timeline. Confirm the scope with the city before ordering, because the fee is trivial next to the delay of discovering it afterward.

### Testing a quote before signing it

Ask for the spring specification written out: wire diameter, inside diameter, overall length, and cycle rating. A 25,000-cycle spring costs more than a 10,000-cycle spring and lasts proportionally longer, so the rating is a price factor rather than a detail. Confirm whether the number covers both springs, because a shaft with one new spring and one original is half repaired. On an opener, look for the drive type, the horsepower class, whether battery backup is included, and whether the safety reversing sensors and wall control are new hardware or the old ones reused.

On a replacement, the paperwork should name the manufacturer and model, the gauge of the steel, the insulation value, the window and hardware options, and the color. It should also cover the permit if one applies, jamb and header repair if the framing needs it, removal and disposal of the old door, and a realistic delivery date. Insulated doors in a chosen color are built to order, not pulled from a shelf.

### The traps worth naming

First, the winter emergency upgrade. A spring breaks on the coldest morning of the year with a car trapped inside, and the visit turns into a proposal for a complete new door and opener while the homeowner is standing in the cold. A broken spring on a sound door is a hardware repair, and it stays a hardware repair no matter what the temperature is.

Second, storm damage misread. After the August 2026 derecho that the Weather Service recorded across northern Illinois, a great many doors in this area had visible dents. The dent is not the failure. Bent track, a bowed reinforcement strut and rollers knocked out of the rail are the failure, and correcting those is a fraction of what replacing sections costs.

Third, what the online average omits: the permit and inspection charges when the opening changes, an extension-to-torsion conversion on an older garage, rebuilt wood jambs, a low-headroom kit, and the lead time on an ordered door in the weeks after a regional wind event.

## svc_carriage_house_garage_doors_lede

Carriage house doors come in two very different forms: a modern sectional door with decorative overlay hardware, and a genuine pair of swing-out or slide-to-side doors. They cost different amounts, need different hardware, and in one part of Naperville they need different approvals.

## svc_carriage_house_garage_doors_body

### Overlay sectional versus a true swing-out door

An overlay carriage door is an ordinary sectional door — same torsion bar, same track, same opener — with applied trim boards, decorative hinges and handles, and often a row of windows in the top section, arranged so the door reads as two hinged doors. It gives the appearance without changing how anything works. A true swing-out pair hinges at the jambs and opens outward, and a slide-to-side runs the panels on a bottom track to one side of the opening. Both are real carriage doors and both need swing clearance in the driveway, purpose-made jamb hardware, and a completely different operator — a jamb-mounted arm unit for swing-out doors, not a rail opener. Both appear under [door replacement](../garage-door-replacement/) on the [service list](../services/).

### The Historic District, and the approval that is not automatic

Naperville's local historic district runs roughly from North Avenue on the north to Chicago, Jefferson and Franklin Avenues on the south, and from the east block face of Columbia Street west to Loomis, Ellsworth and the west block face of Center Street, taking in part of the North Central College campus. It covers 322 properties and 310 principal structures, about 36 percent of them built in the 19th century, plus 258 secondary structures — most of which are garages. Exterior alteration to a landmarked property or one inside the district generally needs a Certificate of Appropriateness, reviewed either administratively through the city's fast track process or by the Historic Preservation Commission at a public meeting. The fast track list covers in-kind door replacement in wood or the original material where the opening and style match. Garage doors are not on that list, and the city notes that approval is not needed for work on facades other than the primary facade. In practice, a detached garage at the back of a lot and a garage door facing the street are two different applications. Confirm which category your project is in with the city before ordering a door, because a custom door is built to order and cannot be revised after approval.

### Materials, and what each one costs you later

Steel with a composite overlay: the overlay is a moisture-resistant board rather than solid wood, so it holds paint far better through 56 freeze-thaw cycles a year and does not swell. Insulated versions are available. Solid wood, usually cedar or hemlock: correct on a genuinely historic house, and the only option that can be planed and refinished, but it moves with the seasons and needs refinishing on a schedule rather than when it looks bad.

### Weight, springs and glass

Overlay boards, heavier gauge steel and window inserts all add weight, and the spring has to be sized for the finished door rather than the base panel. A carriage-style door with glass in the top section can run 40 to 80 pounds heavier than the plain door it replaced, which means new springs, sometimes a heavier bar, and occasionally a larger opener. Where the door is going onto an older frame, [hinge, bracket and jamb condition](../garage-door-hinge-repair/) should be checked before the new door is measured.

### Lead time, and what should be on the order

A stock overlay door in a standard size can arrive within a week or two. Custom sizes, custom colors, true swing-out hardware and any door needing a Certificate of Appropriateness run several weeks or longer, and the approval has to be settled first. The order should name the manufacturer and model, panel construction and insulation, overlay material, hardware finish, glass type, exact opening dimensions, headroom and backroom, the track type, the spring specification for the finished weight, and removal of the old door.

## svc_commercial_garage_door_repair_lede

Commercial door failures are priced by downtime, not by parts. A rolling steel service door that will not open shuts a loading bay, a storefront or a service lane until it does.

## svc_commercial_garage_door_repair_body

### The hardware is not residential hardware

Three families of door turn up on commercial buildings here. Rolling steel doors, where interlocking slats coil onto a barrel above the opening on a torsion spring housed inside that barrel. Sectional commercial doors, which look residential but run 24-gauge or heavier steel, 3-inch track, 3-inch stem rollers and much heavier hinges. And high-lift or vertical-lift arrangements in bays with clearance for a truck. Springs on all three are specified in cycles rather than years: a 10,000-cycle spring lasts a household about seven years, and the same spring on a bay opened forty times a day is finished inside nine months. High-cycle springs at 50,000 or 100,000 cycles are the correct specification on a working bay, and they are the item most often missing from a cheap commercial repair. That is why commercial work is listed separately from residential on the [service list](../services/).

### Operators, and what a commercial one has to do

A commercial operator is a jackshaft or trolley unit with a motor rated for continuous duty, and its control is a three-button open-close-stop station rather than a visor remote. Where employees, forklifts or the public pass through, the door needs an external entrapment device — a photo eye, a monitored reversing edge on the bottom slat, or both — and where the door can be started from out of sight, the logic has to require a held button. A residential opener bolted to a commercial door is a genuine hazard and gets flagged during [a safety inspection](../garage-door-safety-inspection/).

### The code the city reviews commercial work under

Under Ordinance 26-010, plans submitted from April 1, 2026 are reviewed against the 2024 International Building Code, the 2024 International Fire Code, the 2024 International Existing Building Code and the 2023 National Electrical Code, plus local amendments in the municipal code. Two practical consequences. A new opening, a widened opening or a change of door type on a commercial building is permitted work rather than a service call. And the electrical for a new three-phase operator is permit work with an inspection attached, while an operator swap on the existing circuit is service. Confirm the scope with the city before a shutdown is scheduled around it, because the inspection sequence is what fixes the date.

### Salt, slush and the doors on the older commercial frontage

The businesses along the East Ogden Avenue corridor, which the city treats as a distinct commercial frontage with its own sign and facade grant programs, front a state highway that is salted heavily all winter. Spray from passing traffic reaches the bottom slats and the guide channels of a rolling door, and rust inside the guides is what makes a curtain bind and jam halfway up. On sectional bays the same brine collects at the bottom bracket, which is where the lift cable is anchored under full spring tension. A frayed cable there does not fail politely; it drops the door. Guides get cleaned and treated with a dry film lubricant rather than grease, because grease holds the grit that caused the problem.

### Planned service instead of emergency service

The economics are different from a house. A quarterly or semiannual interval on a working bay — cycle count logged, springs re-rated to actual use, chain and reduction gears checked, bottom edge and photo eyes tested, guides cleaned — costs a fraction of one unplanned closure. The failures that actually shut a bay are all predictable: a spring at the end of its cycles, a cable frayed at the bracket, a seized roller in a cold guide, a reversing edge that has been jumpered out because it was faulting. When one does let go mid-shift it becomes [emergency response](../emergency-garage-door-repair/), and the first question is whether the opening can be secured while parts come.

## svc_emergency_garage_door_repair_lede

Some door failures can wait for a weekday appointment and some cannot. A car sealed in, a door frozen open overnight in January, or a broken spring at the only entrance a household uses are the ones that get moved to the front of the day.

## svc_emergency_garage_door_repair_body

### What genuinely counts as urgent

Four situations: the door is stuck open and the garage is now an unlocked room on the front of the house; the door is stuck closed with a vehicle behind it and no other way out; a section or a cable has failed in a way that leaves the door hanging unevenly and unsafe to touch; or the door has come down on an object and will not lift. Everything else — noise, a slow open, one dead remote — is a scheduled visit, and there is no reason to pay an after-hours premium for it. The [full service list](../services/) sets out which is which.

### A door stuck open in a Naperville January

This is the one worth acting on quickly for reasons that have nothing to do with the door. January normal snowfall for the area is 11.3 inches, and an open garage in that weather is an open path to the wall between the garage and the house, to whatever plumbing runs through it, and to the water heater or furnace many attached garages here hold. A door that cannot be closed should at least be brought down manually: pull the emergency release cord to disconnect the trolley, lower the door by hand, and clamp a pair of C-clamps or locking pliers on the vertical track just above the bottom roller so nobody can lift it. That is a holding measure, not a repair, and it is only safe if the springs are intact.

### If the spring is broken, the door is not coming down by hand

A two-car sectional door weighs somewhere between 150 and 200 pounds and the spring is what makes it feel weightless. With a broken spring the door is exactly that heavy, and the release cord removes the only thing holding it up. Do not pull the cord on a door that is stuck open with a snapped spring. Leave it open, unplug the opener, keep the space clear, and wait for winding bars. That is a [spring replacement](../garage-door-spring-repair/), not something to improvise with pliers at ten at night.

### Getting the car out without making it worse

If the door is down and the car is behind it, resist the urge to force the door up. On a door that has come off its rollers or lost a cable, lifting it manually racks the sections and can drop it. Where the door is sound and only the opener has failed — a dead motor, a stripped drive gear, no power — the release cord is the right answer and the door will lift normally. Where the door itself has failed, the honest answer is that the vehicle stays put until the hardware is fixed, and forcing it usually turns a cable job into a [derailed door](../off-track-garage-door-repair/) plus a bent section.

### What after-hours response actually depends on

Two things: whether the parts are on the truck and whether the streets are moving. During a snow event the city plows across more than 500 centerline miles of road, and travel time to the far south side subdivisions off 95th Street runs longer than it does downtown. A realistic arrival window matters more than a promise, and you should be given one along with the price before anyone is dispatched.

### Overnight, if the visit has to wait until morning

Get the door down and clamped if the springs are sound. If the door cannot be closed at all, a sheet of plywood cut to the opening and screwed to the jambs is a legitimate overnight measure, and a technician can remove it in a minute the next day.

## svc_garage_door_hinge_repair_lede

A hinge costs a few dollars and takes minutes to swap. Left cracked, it lets one section articulate independently of the next, and the door quietly ruins rollers, stiles and eventually a whole panel.

## svc_garage_door_hinge_repair_body

### The hinges are numbered for a reason

On a sectional door the hinges along the inside face are stamped 1 through 4 or 5. The number sets the offset between the two leaves, which is what keeps the sections tracking correctly through the curve of the rail. Fitting a number 2 where a number 3 belongs makes the door bind at the radius and wears the track. End hinges are different again: they carry the roller stem as well as the joint, so they take the door's weight and its sideways load together. Hinge and bracket work sits with the other hardware jobs on the [service list](../services/).

### How they fail, and what you hear

Steel hinges crack at the knuckle or tear out their fastener holes, and the noise is a sharp pop at the same height on every cycle, because the crack opens as the joint articulates. On steel doors the more common failure is the stile behind the hinge: the sheet metal tears around the screw holes, the hinge itself stays intact, and the section begins to flex. Left alone, that misalignment lifts a roller out of the rail, which becomes [a derailed door](../off-track-garage-door-repair/), and the popping gets mistaken for a lubrication complaint when it is really [a noise with a structural cause](../noisy-garage-door-repair/).

### Salt, and the fasteners in the older subdivisions

The city keeps up to 18,000 tons of road salt across three domes, and a measurable share of what goes down arrives home on tires and dries on the garage slab. Brine wicks into the screw threads at the bottom two sections, and after a decade the self-tapping screws holding the lowest hinges are seized or have lost their heads. That is the practical difference between a hinge job in a 1990s subdivision and one in Cress Creek or Saybrook, where the land was bought in 1962 and 1963 and much of the door hardware is decades old: the parts cost the same and the labor does not, because seized fasteners have to be drilled out and a torn stile needs a reinforcement plate rather than a longer screw in a ruined hole.

### The bottom bracket is not a hinge

The bottom bracket at each lower corner anchors the lift cable, and it carries the full tension of the springs whenever the door is down. Unbolting one on a closed door releases that tension violently, which makes it the second most dangerous fastener on a garage door after the winding cones. It is also the part that pulls out of a rusted or rotted bottom section, and the symptom — one bottom corner rising before the rest of the door — looks exactly like a cable fault. The correct sequence is to open the door fully, clamp the track, and support the door so the cables go slack before the bracket comes off. Not a homeowner repair.

### Repair, reinforcement, or a new section

Three outcomes. A cracked hinge on sound steel is a direct swap with the same number. A torn stile takes a hinge reinforcement plate that spreads the load into undamaged metal, which is a legitimate permanent fix when the surrounding steel is solid. Rust-through at the bottom section, or a stile torn in more than one place, means the section is finished, and on doors older than about fifteen years a matching section is usually out of production.

### What the visit covers

Every hinge inspected individually with the door partly open and under no load, hinge numbers verified against their positions, fasteners replaced in the correct gauge rather than longer screws in old holes, torn stiles plated, roller stems checked for wear where they pass through the end hinges, and the door balanced and cycled before anyone leaves.

## svc_garage_door_insulation_lede

An insulated door is worth the money on an attached garage and rarely worth the premium on a detached one. What decides the outcome is panel construction, not the R-value printed on the brochure.

## svc_garage_door_insulation_body

### What R-value means on a door, and what it does not

Door R-values are quoted for the panel, at its thickest point, and a door is not only panels. It has stiles and rails, a bottom section, four edges of perimeter and a top section carrying the operator bracket. The honest comparison is construction plus seals: a two-inch three-layer door with a thermal break and tight stop molding beats a thicker door with poor edges. That decision sits next to [full door replacement](../garage-door-replacement/) on the [service list](../services/).

### The three constructions you will be quoted

Single-layer steel: one skin, no insulation, lightest and loudest. Two-layer: a steel skin with polystyrene board glued behind it, typically R-6 to R-9, a real gain in stiffness and quiet. Three-layer sandwich: steel, polyurethane foamed in place, steel back skin, roughly R-12 to R-18 depending on thickness. The polyurethane is bonded to both skins, which is why those doors are so much stiffer — the insulation is structural as much as thermal.

### Why it pays on an attached garage here, and the code that applies

The city reviews plans under the Illinois Energy Conservation Code alongside the 2024 International Residential Code, and the requirement that matters is on the wall and ceiling between the garage and the house, not on the door. A garage is not conditioned space, so no inspector checks your door's R-value. What the door changes is the temperature of the buffer on the other side of that insulated wall. With 56 freeze-thaw cycles in an average year and long stretches below freezing, an uninsulated door lets the buffer sit near outdoor temperature, so the insulated wall works against a far larger difference — and any room over the garage feels it first. In the two-story stock of Ashbury and White Eagle, built from the late 1980s through the 1990s with bedrooms and bonus rooms above attached garages, that is the entire argument. On a detached garage with no plumbing and nothing above it, the premium buys quieter operation and little else.

### The thermal break, and where insulation stops

The steel skins on many two-layer doors wrap the panel edges, connecting inside to outside directly through metal. That bridge is why frost forms on the inside face of an insulated door on a cold morning. A thermal break — a polymer separation between the skins — removes it. If the reason for a new door is partly condensation, the thermal break matters more than two points of R-value. The other leak is the perimeter, which is why an insulation upgrade and [new weather seals](../garage-door-weather-seal-replacement/) belong in the same visit.

### Retrofit kits, and the weight problem

Foam board or reflective batt kits fitted into the panel cavities of an existing steel door do work, up to a point: a modest gain, better sound, no thermal break. The catch is weight. A retrofit adds 15 to 40 pounds to a two-car door, and the springs were wound for the original weight. A door that used to balance now drifts down, the opener carries the difference, and the drive gear pays for it. Adding insulation means re-rating the springs, and that is not optional. The same applies to a heavier replacement door: the old spring is sized for the old door, and reusing it is the most common corner cut on an insulated upgrade.

### What the visit covers

The existing door identified by construction rather than brochure, the case for insulating weighed against whether the garage is attached and whether a room sits above it, spring specification recalculated for the finished weight, and a hand balance test at the end. The price comes before the work.

## svc_garage_door_lock_repair_lede

Two different jobs get called lock repair: making a door secure again, and undoing the damage a lock did when it was left thrown while the opener pulled against it. The second one is more common than the first.

## svc_garage_door_lock_repair_body

### The hardware, and what each piece does

A manual garage door has three possible locks. A slide bolt is a flat steel bar on the inside of the door that shoots into a slot in the vertical track. An inside slide latch is the same idea on both sides, joined by a bar. And a T-handle is the exterior key handle on the outside of the door, with a spindle running through the section to a pair of cables that pull two spring-loaded bolts back out of the track. Lock and handle work is one line on the [service list](../services/) here.

### The lock that broke the opener

This is the call. Somebody throws the slide bolt for a vacation, or a T-handle spring has weakened and a bolt creeps back out on its own. Then the opener gets a signal. The motor pulls against a door that is pinned into its own track, and something has to give: the drive gear strips, the trolley arm bends, the top section pulls away from the operator bracket, or the vertical track deforms around the bolt. On the older chain drives the nylon drive gear is designed to be the weak point, which is a mercy, and it turns a security oversight into an [opener repair](../garage-door-opener-repair/) rather than a new door.

### Cold weather, and the locks on detached garages

A T-handle spindle and a cylinder lock live in the outside face of the door where moisture collects. Water in the cylinder freezes and the key will not turn; worse, ice in the bolt channels stops the bolts retracting fully, so the handle turns but one bolt stays half in the track. In a climate with 56 freeze-thaw cycles in an average year, that is a seasonal event rather than a rarity. Graphite or a dry PTFE lubricant is correct in a cylinder; oil collects grit and water and makes the next freeze worse. This matters most on the detached garages behind the older houses — the local historic district alone contains 258 secondary structures, most of them garages — where a keyed T-handle is often still the only way in and there is no opener as a fallback.

### When a lock is genuinely the right answer

Three cases. A detached garage with no opener, where the T-handle is the door's only security. A garage used for storage where the door is opened rarely and a slide bolt is a real deterrent against someone lifting an unpowered door from outside.

### Emergency release cords and the security question

The release cord is the red handle on the trolley, and it exists so you can raise the door by hand when there is no power. On some doors, a coat hanger fed through the gap at the top of the door can hook that cord from outside, and pulling it disengages the trolley. Two fixes: shorten the cord so it does not hang low enough to catch, or fit a plate or shield that closes the gap at the top section. Neither compromises the release from the inside, which has to stay usable, and a shortened cord is still reachable from a step stool. If the gap at the top of the door is wide enough for that hanger, the [header seal](../garage-door-weather-seal-replacement/) is probably also failing and can be dealt with in the same visit.

### What the visit covers

The lock hardware identified and freed or replaced, spindle and bolt cables adjusted so both bolts retract fully, cylinders cleaned and lubricated with a dry lubricant, any track damaged by a bolt straightened or replaced, and manual locks disabled where an opener is in service. Collateral damage to the opener is diagnosed separately and quoted before work starts.

## svc_garage_door_opener_installation_lede

A new opener is a choice among three drive types, a horsepower class, and a rail length that has to match your door height. The install is straightforward; the specification is where the money goes right or wrong.

## svc_garage_door_opener_installation_body

### Chain, belt or direct drive

A chain drive is the cheapest and the loudest, and it is a reasonable choice on a detached garage where nobody hears it. A belt drive uses a reinforced rubber belt on the same rail and is noticeably quieter, which matters in the two-story houses common in the 1990s subdivisions, where a bedroom sits directly over the garage. A direct-drive or jackshaft unit moves the motor: a wall-mount jackshaft drives the torsion bar from the side and needs no rail at all, which is the answer when there is a car lift, a storage platform or almost no headroom above the door. All three are on the [service list](../services/) here and all three install in the same afternoon.

### Horsepower, and the number that matters more

Residential units are sold as ½, ¾ and 1 horsepower, or in newton ratings. A ½ horsepower unit lifts a standard uninsulated single or a light double. A ¾ horsepower unit is the right default for a wide insulated double door, which can weigh over 200 pounds. What actually determines the load, though, is spring balance: a properly balanced door presents the opener with almost no weight, and an oversized motor on an unbalanced door just breaks other things more slowly. Any quote that offers more horsepower as the fix for a hard-pulling door is skipping the [spring work](../garage-door-spring-repair/) that is the real repair.

### Rail length, headroom and the older garages

Standard rails suit a seven-foot door. An eight-foot door needs an extension kit. Headroom is the other measurement: a rail-drive opener needs roughly two to three inches of clear space above the fully open door plus room for the motor head, and the 1960s garages in the older Moser subdivisions — Maplebrook land bought in 1960, Cress Creek in 1962, Saybrook in 1963 — were framed with far less headroom than a modern garage. That is why a wall-mount unit or a low-headroom track is sometimes the only thing that fits, and why the measurement happens before the unit is ordered rather than on install day.

### The electrical question, and the Naperville permit rule

Naperville requires a permit before any electrical work in the city, and plans submitted since April 1, 2026 are reviewed under the 2023 National Electrical Code. Two exemptions matter here. Connecting a listed cord-and-plug appliance to an existing permanently installed receptacle does not require a permit, so a new opener plugged into a ceiling receptacle that is already there is not a permit job. Low-voltage wiring under 25 volts and 50 watts is also exempt, which covers the photo eyes and the wall console. What is not exempt is adding the receptacle. Older garages here often have a single ceiling light on a pull chain and no outlet at all, and putting one in is electrical work with a permit and an inspection attached. Confirm your own scope with the city before the work starts.

### Install day, and what should be on the invoice

The old unit and its rail come down. The new motor head is hung from solid framing with a mounting angle, not from a single joist and a bent strap. The rail is squared to the door center, the header bracket lands on structure rather than drywall, and the trolley arm attaches to the top section reinforcement. Then travel limits, close force, photo eyes mounted at six inches or lower, and a reversal test with an obstruction. The invoice should name the model, the drive type, the horsepower class, whether battery backup is included, and whether the wall console, photo eyes and remotes are new hardware. If the plan includes phone control, [the smart module](../smart-garage-door-opener/) is a separate decision worth making deliberately.

## svc_garage_door_remote_programming_lede

A remote that stopped working is usually a battery, a lost pairing, or a receiver that no longer hears it from where you park. All three are radio problems, and none of them means the opener is failing.

## svc_garage_door_remote_programming_body

### Battery first, always

A remote transmits for a few milliseconds at a time, so a coin cell can read fine on a meter and still collapse under load. Most visor remotes use a CR2032 or CR2016 at three volts; most wireless keypads use a nine-volt or a pair of AAs. Cold makes it worse, because chemical capacity drops as the temperature does and an outdoor keypad on a north-facing jamb spends January well below freezing. If a keypad works at noon and fails at seven in the morning, replace the battery before anything else. Battery and pairing work is the fastest call on the [list of jobs covered here](../services/).

### Rolling code, and why old remotes cannot simply be copied

Anything built since the late 1990s uses a rolling code: the transmitter and the receiver both advance through a sequence, so the number sent is never reused. That is why a universal remote has to be taught by the opener rather than cloned from a working one. Pairing means pressing the learn button on the motor head, then the remote button within about thirty seconds. The learn button also erases: holding it down for six to ten seconds until the indicator goes out clears every paired device, which is the right move after a house sale, a lost remote, or a contractor who was given a spare.

### Keypads take the weather directly

The outside keypad is the only part of the system mounted in open air. Water gets behind the rubber keys, freezes in the 56 freeze-thaw cycles an average year brings here, and lifts the membrane off the contacts underneath. The usual symptom is one number that has stopped registering. Keypads are not repairable at that point, and the replacement should be paired the same visit. On a keypad that has been in place for a decade, the plastic gasket behind it is normally gone, so a fresh bead of sealant along the top edge is worth the two minutes.

### Range, and the detached garages behind the Historic District houses

Range complaints are almost always about geometry, not power. The city's local historic district covers 322 properties and includes 258 secondary structures, most of them garages, and a great many sit at the back of a deep lot with the house between the driveway and the receiver. Older openers run their receiver off a short antenna wire hanging from the motor head; if that wire is coiled, tucked into the housing or wrapped around the rail, effective range drops sharply. Straightening it downward often restores twenty or thirty feet. LED shop bulbs are the other common cause: the cheap ones broadcast broadband noise across 315 and 390 MHz and can cut a remote's usable distance to a few feet. Swapping to a bulb rated for garage door openers is a genuine fix, not a superstition.

### When the receiver, not the remote, has failed

If a brand-new battery in two different remotes plus the wall button all fail together, the logic board or the receiver has quit and no amount of pairing will help. If the wall button works and nothing wireless does, the receiver alone is at fault, and on many units it is a separate plug-in module rather than the whole board. External receivers exist for openers whose boards are no longer made, which is often cheaper than [a new opener](../garage-door-opener-installation/). If you want phone control while the panel is open anyway, look at [a smart upgrade](../smart-garage-door-opener/) at the same time.

### What the visit covers

Batteries checked under load, every remote and keypad re-paired, old codes cleared, the antenna re-dressed, interference ruled out by substitution, and the receiver tested before anything gets replaced. Parts get quoted before they go on.

## svc_garage_door_safety_inspection_lede

A safety inspection checks one thing above all others: whether the door will stop and reverse when something is under it. Everything else on the list follows from that, and the standard it is measured against is federal and specific.

## svc_garage_door_safety_inspection_body

### The 1993 line, and why the age of your opener decides the visit

The federal safety standard for automatic residential garage door operators, [16 CFR Part 1211](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-16/chapter-II/subchapter-B/part-1211), applies to units manufactured on or after January 1, 1993. It requires primary inherent entrapment protection — the opener senses the obstruction itself — plus one of three additional arrangements, in practice almost always an external secondary device: the photo eyes across the opening, or a monitored edge sensor on the bottom of the door. An opener made before that date has neither, and no adjustment can add them. That is the single clearest reason to replace rather than repair an old unit. Inspections are one item on the [service list](../services/); the pre-1993 outcome usually leads to [a new opener](../garage-door-opener-installation/).

### The two tests that matter, done the way the standard describes

First, the obstruction test. Place a solid object one inch high on the floor in the door's path, in line with the operator, and close the door. Under the standard the operator has to begin reversing within two seconds of contact and then return the door to the fully open position. A door that stops without reversing fails. A door that keeps pushing fails badly. Second, the beam test: with the door closing, break the photo-eye beam with an object — not a hand — and the door must reverse to full open. If either test fails, the door should be left disconnected until it is corrected rather than used carefully.

### What else gets checked, and what a failure looks like

Spring condition and the hand balance test at waist height. Lift cables at the bottom bracket, where fraying begins. The bottom brackets themselves, which are under full spring tension. Roller bearings and end hinges. Track alignment and the lag bolts into the framing. The manual release cord, which must be reachable and functional. Whether any manual lock is still able to engage while the opener is connected. Whether the wall control is mounted at least five feet up and out of a child's reach, and whether it is within sight of the door. And the close-force setting, because a force turned up to overcome a stiff door has defeated the primary protection the standard requires. A pre-1993 opener, a missing safety cable inside an extension spring, a frayed lift cable and a door that will not reverse are the four findings that mean stop using it now.

### Where this comes up most in this city

Three situations. A house being sold or bought, where the buyer wants a written condition report and the garage has original hardware. A rental property, where the landlord is the one responsible for the door working safely. And a household where a child, an older parent or a dog now uses the garage as a walking route to the driveway. Housing age drives the findings: Hobson West went up between the mid 1970s and the 1980s, and the earliest Moser subdivisions are older still, so pre-1993 openers and extension springs without safety cables both turn up regularly there. In the newer subdivisions the common findings are worn cables and a close force that has been raised over the years to compensate for a door nobody ever serviced — the kind of thing [a tune-up](../garage-door-tune-up/) is meant to catch first.

### What you get at the end

A list, item by item, of what passed, what is worn but functional with a rough timeline, and what fails outright with the reason. Nothing on that list should be a general recommendation to replace the door. If the reversal test failed, you should be shown the test being repeated after the correction, not simply told it is fixed.

## svc_garage_door_sensor_repair_lede

Two photo eyes sitting about six inches off the floor have to see each other before the opener will let the door finish closing. When one is blocked, dirty or knocked out of aim, the door travels down a foot and comes straight back up.

## svc_garage_door_sensor_repair_body

### What the two eyes are actually doing

One bracket carries a sending eye, the other a receiving eye, and an infrared beam runs between them across the opening. Break that beam while the door is moving down and the opener reverses to the full open position. That is required equipment rather than a fault: it is the secondary entrapment protection the federal opener standard has demanded on residential units built since January 1, 1993. Photo-eye work is the least expensive item on the [full list of garage door jobs](../services/) on this site, and also the most misread, because a door that reverses looks broken when it is in fact protecting something.

### Reading the indicator lights before touching anything

On nearly every brand the sending eye shows a steady light whenever it has power, and the receiving eye lights only while it can see the beam. Both steady means aligned. Sending lit and receiving dark or flickering means the beam is broken or the pair is no longer pointed at each other. Neither lit means the low-voltage circuit lost power: a staple driven through the wire, a conductor nicked at the bracket, or a terminal screw on the motor head that has backed out. Flexing the wire along its run while watching the light finds a break faster than any other method.

### Why the floor of a Naperville garage is hard on them

The eyes sit at ankle height, which in winter is the worst place in the building. City crews begin a full plowing operation once two inches have fallen and keep working across 1,500 lane miles of street, and what comes off those streets rides home in wheel wells and melts onto the slab. Engineering climate data for the O'Hare reference station lists 56 freeze-thaw cycles in an average year, so that meltwater refreezes overnight around the bottom section and the sensor brackets. Salt film dries to a haze on the lens, ice shifts a bracket a few degrees, and the door stops staying down. Wipe both lenses with a dry cloth first. Solvents fog the plastic and make a marginal signal worse.

### Alignment, and the brackets in the older subdivisions

Loosen the wing nut, sight one eye at the other, and tighten while the receiving light stays steady. If the light dies the moment you let go, the bracket or its mount is the problem rather than the aim. In the 1960s neighborhoods — the land for Cress Creek was bought in 1962 and Saybrook in 1963 — sensors are a retrofit added decades after the garage was framed, screwed to whatever was handy: a jamb, a furring strip, sometimes the vertical track bracket. Those mounts flex on every cycle. A short length of angle lagged into solid framing ends a recurring alignment complaint that re-aiming never will.

### When the eyes are not the cause

Direct sun down a west-facing driveway in late afternoon can saturate the receiving eye and mimic a blocked beam; the tell is a fault that only happens at one time of day. If the lights are steady and the door still reverses, the close-force setting or the door itself is next, which is covered under [a door that will not close](../garage-door-wont-close/). If the motor is also grinding or the light bulb flickers on every cycle, look at the [opener itself](../garage-door-opener-repair/) instead.

### What the visit covers

Both eyes cleaned and re-aimed to a steady light, the low-voltage run checked end to end for staple damage and corroded splices, brackets replaced or re-anchored where they flex, and a reversal test with an object under the door to confirm the door stops and returns to full open.

## svc_garage_door_tune_up_lede

A tune-up is the only garage door visit you schedule instead of react to. It is a balance test, a hardware inspection and a lubrication, and its whole purpose is to catch the parts that fail quietly before the one that fails loudly.

## svc_garage_door_tune_up_body

### The balance test is the whole point

Disconnect the trolley from the opener with the release cord, lift the door to waist height by hand, and let go. A correctly balanced door holds where you put it. Drifting down means the springs have lost tension. Flying up means too much. Either way the opener has been compensating with force, and the force is being absorbed by the drive gear, the rail and the sections. Most openers that die at twelve years old die because they spent three of those years pulling a door that was out of balance. Everything on the [service list](../services/) that involves an opener starts with this test.

### Cycles, not years

A cycle is one open and one close. A standard torsion spring is rated near 10,000 cycles; high-cycle wire runs 25,000 or more. A household that leaves twice a day and comes back twice a day is using four cycles, which is roughly 1,460 a year, so a builder-grade spring reaches its rated life in about seven years. Anything that adds trips — a home office, teenagers, a garage used as the main entrance — cuts that proportionally. Ashbury filled in during the early and middle 1990s, which puts a lot of its original hardware in its fourth decade and its second or third set of springs. Knowing your cycle count tells you whether to plan for a spring this year or in three.

### Timing it to the Illinois calendar

Two windows matter here. Late October, before the cold arrives: the bearing and roller grease in a garage stiffens as the temperature drops, and stiff grease plus a spring that is already weak is what produces the first broken spring of the season on a fifteen-degree morning. Then March or April, after the salt has finished. The city keeps up to 18,000 tons of road salt across three domes, and a share of what goes down on the streets comes home on tires, dries on the slab and sits against the bottom section and the lift cables. Rinsing the bottom section and the cable ends in spring genuinely extends their life. With 56 freeze-thaw cycles in an average year, hardware here works harder than the same hardware in a mild climate.

### What gets checked, in order

Spring tension and balance. Cable condition at the bottom bracket, where fraying starts. Roller bearings for play and flat spots. Hinges for cracks at the stile, and hinge and bracket fasteners brought to torque, since vibration walks them loose over years. End bearing plates and the center bearing. Track alignment and plumb, and the lag bolts holding the track brackets. Chain or belt tension. Travel limits and close force. The photo eyes, aimed and tested. And a reversal test with an obstruction on the floor, which is the one item on the list that is about people rather than the door.

### What a tune-up is not

It is not an excuse to sell a door. A tune-up should end with a list of what was adjusted, what was lubricated, and what is worn but not yet failed, with a rough timeline for each. If a spring is at the end of its cycle life or a cable has visible broken strands, that is a real recommendation and worth acting on; a general suggestion that the whole assembly is tired is not. Where the finding is a specific noise, [noise diagnosis](../noisy-garage-door-repair/) narrows it down; where the finding is a pre-1993 opener with no photo eyes, [a safety inspection](../garage-door-safety-inspection/) is the honest next step.

## svc_garage_door_weather_seal_replacement_lede

Four separate pieces of rubber and vinyl keep water, snow, salt and mice out of a garage: the bottom astragal, the stop molding down both jambs, the same molding across the header, and the threshold on the slab. They fail on their own schedules.

## svc_garage_door_weather_seal_replacement_body

### The bottom seal, and how it is held on

On any door built since roughly the 1970s the bottom section carries an aluminum retainer with one or two T-shaped slots, and the seal is a length of flexible vinyl or EPDM rubber with matching T-beads that slides in from one end. Replacement is a strip of the correct profile, cut to width, fed through with a little soapy water as lubricant. Getting the profile right is the whole job, and it is the least expensive item on the [service list](../services/) here.

### What freezing water does under the astragal

This is the mechanism that ends most bottom seals in this area. Meltwater from a plowed driveway runs under the seal onto the slab, then freezes overnight. Ice under the rubber bonds the seal to the concrete, and the next time the opener lifts, it tears the seal out of its retainer or pulls the astragal loose. With 56 freeze-thaw cycles in an average year that sequence repeats through the whole winter rather than once. The same ice is why a door reverses at the floor: the opener meets resistance and reads it as an obstruction, which is set out under [a door that will not close](../garage-door-wont-close/). Road salt does the rest, hardening the rubber until it takes a permanent set and stops sealing at all.

### Stop molding, header seal and a straight slab

Stop molding is the vinyl-flanged trim nailed to the outside of both jambs and across the header; the flexible flange is what actually touches the door. It goes brittle from ultraviolet exposure on a south or west elevation and cracks off in pieces. Replacement is trim work: pry the old strip, check the jamb behind it for rot in the bottom eight inches where snow piles, and fasten the new strip so the flange has light contact rather than compression. Too tight and the door binds and the opener fights it.

### The threshold, and when it is the right call

A threshold is a bonded rubber ramp fixed to the slab just outside the door line, and it works with the bottom seal rather than replacing it. It earns its keep in two situations. One is a driveway that slopes back toward the garage, common on the older lots in Hobson West, built from the mid 1970s into the 1980s. The other is a slab that has settled or heaved: with a design frost depth of 61 inches in this region, movement at the garage apron over decades is normal, and a slab that has lifted at one corner leaves a gap no flat seal will close. A threshold is set in adhesive on a clean, dry slab above about 50 degrees, which in practice makes it a spring-through-fall job, not a January one.

### Pests, and why the corners matter more than the draft

A quarter-inch gap under a door is enough for a mouse and generous for insects. The corners are the usual entry: the astragal ends where the retainer ends, and if the stop molding does not overlap it, a triangular hole is left at each bottom corner of the opening. Corner pad inserts close that, and they are a two-dollar part for a problem homeowners often treat with traps for years. Weatherstripping is also not insulation — an uninsulated steel door with perfect seals still radiates heat, and [insulation](../garage-door-insulation/) is a separate decision.

### What the visit covers

The retainer profile and door width measured, the correct bottom seal fitted, jamb and header stop molding replaced where it has cracked, corner gaps closed, and the bottom section checked for rust-through where salt collects behind the retainer. If the jamb itself has rotted, you get told that before new trim is fastened to soft wood.

## svc_garage_door_wont_close_lede

A door that opens fine and refuses to close is the most common single call in this trade. It is also the most misdiagnosed, because the same behavior comes from four unrelated places.

## svc_garage_door_wont_close_body

### Start with what the door actually does

There is a real difference between four failures that all get described the same way. The door goes down a few inches and reverses. The door goes almost to the floor, touches, then goes back up. The door does not move at all but the opener light blinks. Or the door closes only if you hold the wall button down. Each points somewhere different, and telling the technician which one you have shortens the visit considerably. Every branch below is on the [main service list](../services/).

### Reversal partway down: the beam

If the door reverses immediately and the opener light flashes ten times, the photo eyes are not seeing each other. Something in the path, a lens filmed over with salt haze, or a bracket nudged out of aim. This is the first thing to rule out and it is covered in detail under [sensor repair](../garage-door-sensor-repair/). Federal rules have required that beam or an equivalent edge sensor on residential openers built since January 1, 1993, so almost every door in the city has one and almost every door in the city can be stopped by one.

### Reversal at the floor: force, or ice under the seal

A door that touches down and comes back up has hit resistance the opener reads as an obstruction. The federal standard requires the operator to begin reversing within two seconds of contacting a one-inch obstruction and then return to the fully open position, so the opener is doing exactly what it should — it is the resistance that is wrong. In February the resistance is often literal. Meltwater from a plowed driveway runs under the bottom seal, freezes to the slab overnight, and welds the astragal down; with 56 freeze-thaw cycles in an average year that happens repeatedly rather than once. Chip the ice out, do not raise the close force to overcome it. Turning force up is how a bent bottom section or a cracked panel begins. A seal that ices repeatedly usually needs [a new bottom seal and threshold](../garage-door-weather-seal-replacement/) instead of more motor.

### Blinking light with no movement, and the lock

If the opener light blinks steadily and the door will not move at all, most units are telling you the vacation or lock mode on the wall console is engaged. Holding the lock button on the console for a few seconds clears it. The other version of this problem is mechanical: a slide bolt or T-handle left thrown into the vertical track stops the door dead, and if the opener keeps pulling it strips a gear or bends the track.

### Constant pressure only, and travel limits

If the door closes only while the button is held, the opener has decided its secondary protection is unreliable and dropped into the constant-pressure mode the standard allows, which also disables the remotes for closing. Faulty sensor wiring is the usual reason. Separately, a down-limit set too far past the floor makes the opener keep driving after the door lands, which reads as an obstruction; set too high, the door stops an inch off the slab and lets weather in. Limits are adjusted at the motor head and take a few minutes.

### What the visit covers

The technician reproduces your symptom, works the branch it belongs to, and tests the result with the one-inch obstruction check before leaving. Where the fault is a stiff door rather than an electronic one — dry rollers, a spring out of balance, a dragging track — you get told that plainly, with the price of fixing the cause instead of the symptom.

## svc_noisy_garage_door_repair_lede

A garage door tells you what is wrong with it through the noise it makes. Grinding, squealing, a single bang, a rhythmic pop at the same point in travel — each of those has a short list of causes.

## svc_noisy_garage_door_repair_body

### Sort the sound before buying parts

Noise is a diagnostic, and the useful question is not how loud but when. A squeal that rises with speed is dry rollers or dry hinge pins. A rhythmic pop at the same height every cycle is a single roller or hinge, and it can be found by chalking the rollers and running the door. Grinding from the motor head with the door moving normally is the drive gear. A deep bang at rest is the torsion spring shifting on the shaft, which is worth treating as urgent rather than annoying. Everything below is part of the routine on the [service list](../services/) here.

### Rollers, and what winter does to the grease in them

A standard nylon roller carries ten to thirteen ball bearings in a steel race packed with grease. The grease is the whole design, and it does not survive an Illinois winter well: at 56 freeze-thaw cycles in a normal year the pack stiffens on cold mornings, water finds the race, and the bearings begin to spall. A dragging roller stops rotating and starts skidding along the track, which is the metallic screech people describe as a train sound. Sealed nylon rollers with a thirteen-ball race are quieter than steel and are the standard replacement.

### Bedrooms over the garage, and why the drive type matters here

White Eagle, built from the late 1980s through the mid 1990s around the golf course on the far south side, is full of two-story houses with a bedroom or bonus room directly above an attached garage. Chain drive noise is structure-borne in that layout: the rail transmits into the joists and the room above hears the whole cycle. Three things help, in order of cost. Rubber isolators between the mounting angle and the framing. A belt drive instead of a chain. And solid hardware — a bent hanger strap or a rail that has been shimmed with washers rings like a tuning fork no matter what pulls it.

### Torsion bar, bearings and the loose bearing plate

The bar above the door turns in two end bearing plates and often a center bearing. When a bearing dries out, the bar starts to squeal on every cycle and then to chirp under load. Ignore it long enough and the race wears into the plate, which lets the bar wander and chew the drum. That is a bolt-on part while it is still a bearing and a much larger job once the plate is scored. A groan followed by a bang is different: that is the spring unwinding a coil against the shaft, and it belongs with [spring work](../garage-door-spring-repair/) rather than lubrication.

### Lubrication done right, and what to keep off it

Lithium or white silicone spray on hinge pins, roller stems, the torsion spring itself, the bearings and the end plates. A rag to wipe the excess. Nothing at all in the track, because the track is a guide rail and not a bearing surface, and oil in there collects grit into a paste that grinds the rollers. Household penetrating oil is not a lubricant; it flushes the old grease out and leaves nothing behind. Twice a year is right in this climate, timed for late fall before the cold sets the grease and again in spring after the salt has come through. If the door is quiet again but rides rough, a [full tune-up](../garage-door-tune-up/) covers balance and hardware torque as well.

### What the visit covers

The door run several times by hand and under power while the technician locates the noise, rollers and hinges checked individually, bearings inspected for play, hardware brought to torque, correct lubricant applied, and the balance verified afterward. Parts and price are agreed before the work.

## svc_smart_garage_door_opener_lede

Phone control can be added to almost any working opener, and there are two routes: a retrofit controller wired to the existing wall-button terminals, or a new opener with the radio built in. The retrofit is usually the better value.

## svc_smart_garage_door_opener_body

### What a retrofit controller actually is

A small box mounted near the motor head, powered by a plug-in adapter, wired across the same two low-voltage terminals the wall button uses, plus a tilt or contact sensor on the top section so the system knows whether the door is open or closed. Pressing the button in the app closes that contact exactly as the wall console does. Nothing about the mechanics changes. Because that wiring runs well under 25 volts and 50 watts, it falls inside the city's low-voltage exemption from the electrical permit requirement, unlike adding a receptacle for the adapter, which is electrical work. The [service list](../services/) covers both paths.

### Compatibility, in the order it actually breaks

Three checks. First, does the opener have accessible wall-button terminals? Almost all do. Second, is the opener a rolling-code unit built after roughly 1993, and does it have a learn button? Retrofit kits that pair as a remote need one. Third, does the opener already contain a proprietary radio that only the manufacturer's own bridge will talk to? Several late-model units do, and on those the correct part is that brand's bridge rather than a generic controller. A unit predating 1993 has no photo eyes and should not be automated at all, for the reason set out under [safety inspection](../garage-door-safety-inspection/).

### The unattended-closing rule nobody mentions

Federal rules allow remote closing from an app only with an audible and visual warning before the door moves — the beeping and flashing that seems like a nuisance is the condition under which unattended operation is permitted at all. This matters more than it sounds on an attached garage: the door can be closed by someone who cannot see what is under it.

### Wi-Fi reach, and the detached garages in the older parts of town

Signal is the most common installation problem in this city, and it is a building-stock problem. The local historic district takes in 322 properties and 258 secondary structures, most of them garages, and many of those garages sit at the back of a deep lot with a house, a masonry wall and a driveway between them and the router. Cress Creek and Saybrook, from the early 1960s, are similar: detached garages set back from the street. A 2.4 GHz radio inside a steel-clad garage at that distance will connect during the install on a warm afternoon and drop out in February. The fix is a mesh node or an access point in the garage on a wired or reliable backhaul, decided before the controller goes in rather than after the complaints start.

### What you get, and what you do not

Useful: an open or closed status you can check from anywhere, a notification when the door has been open longer than a set time, scheduled auto-close in the evening, and a shared credential for a family member instead of a spare remote. Genuinely useful in this climate, since a door left open on a January night lets weather into the wall the house shares with the garage. Not useful: any expectation that the app fixes a mechanical problem. A door that reverses on the photo eyes will keep reversing, and the app will simply report it. Camera integration is a separate product and worth deciding on its own merits; if remotes and keypads are the actual complaint, [remote and keypad work](../garage-door-remote-programming/) is the cheaper answer.

### What the visit covers

Compatibility confirmed on your unit, the controller mounted and wired to the terminals, the door-position sensor set on the top section, the audible and visual warning verified before an unattended close, the network connection tested from outside the garage, and the app handed over paired to your account rather than an installer's.

## svc_torsion_spring_conversion_lede

An extension-spring door has two long springs stretched along the horizontal tracks. A torsion system has one or two springs wound on a steel bar above the opening. Converting from the first to the second is worth doing on an older door, and it is the only spring job that changes how the door works rather than restoring it.

## svc_torsion_spring_conversion_body

### The two systems, and why the older one persists

Extension springs stretch; torsion springs twist. An extension pair hangs alongside each horizontal track, connected through pulleys and cables to the bottom brackets, and each spring works independently. A torsion setup mounts on a bar above the door with drums at both ends, so a single balanced assembly lifts both sides together. Extension springs were standard on residential doors into the late 1970s, and they persist for one practical reason: they need almost no headroom. That is exactly the situation in the earliest Naperville subdivisions, where the land for Maplebrook was bought in 1960, Cress Creek in 1962 and Saybrook in 1963, and where garages were framed with very little space between the top of the opening and the ceiling joists. Conversion sits alongside ordinary [spring replacement](../garage-door-spring-repair/) on the [service list](../services/).

### What conversion actually buys you

Balance, first. A torsion bar applies lift through both drums simultaneously, so the door rises level and stops racking in the opening; an extension pair that has aged unevenly pulls one side harder every cycle, which is what wears rollers and tears end hinges on old doors. Second, containment. A stretched extension spring under tension is a projectile when it snaps, which is why safety cables run through the middle of each spring on any correctly installed system — and why a door without them is a genuine hazard. A broken torsion spring stays on the bar.

### Headroom is the first measurement, and sometimes it is the answer

A standard torsion assembly needs roughly 12 inches of clear space above the opening for the bar, the drums, the springs and the bearing plates. Many of the 1960s garages here do not have it. Low-headroom conversions exist: a double-track low-headroom kit runs the top rollers on a separate rail, and a bar mounted at the rear of the horizontal tracks with a cable drum arrangement will work in as little as three or four inches. Those kits cost more and take longer, and if the ceiling is finished the framing has to be checked before anything is ordered.

### The door has to be worth the conversion

Conversion re-uses the door, so the door needs to be sound. Three checks. Is it sectional, or is it a one-piece jamb-hardware door? A one-piece door cannot be converted; it needs a sectional replacement, at which point the springs come with the new door. Are the sections solid, without rust-through at the bottom or torn stiles? A door that needs a new section and a new spring system is usually a door to replace, which is covered under [full replacement](../garage-door-replacement/).

### Sizing the springs to the actual door

The torsion spring is sized from the door's real weight, its height, the drum diameter and the required cycle life. The correct method is a scale: disconnect the opener, lift the door on a bathroom scale under the bottom section, and read the weight rather than estimating it from panel count. Wire diameter, inside diameter and overall spring length come out of that number. After winding, the balance test is the proof: at waist height, disconnected, the door holds still.

### What the visit covers

Headroom, backroom and header framing measured, the door weighed, the appropriate standard or low-headroom assembly specified, old extension springs and pulleys removed with the cables released safely, the bar, drums, bearing plates and springs installed and wound to the calculated turns, cables set to equal tension, and the door hand-balanced and cycled. You get the specification and the price in writing before anything is ordered.
