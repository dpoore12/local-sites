### Reading a Collin County estimate

A written estimate shows the diagnostic charge as its own line and states whether it is credited. ### The oversell, said plainly

In this market the pressure point is the compressor conversation. A failed compressor on a fourteen year old condenser is genuinely a replacement discussion. The problem is the reverse move: a capacitor, contactor or fan motor failure presented as a system on its last legs, when those parts fail on healthy equipment routinely. Ask for the measurement behind the diagnosis, the microfarad reading on a capacitor, the amp draw on a motor, superheat and subcooling on a refrigerant claim.

The second pattern is refrigerant sold repeatedly. A sealed system does not consume refrigerant. Two summers of top offs means a leak, and paying a third time without a leak search is filling a bucket with a hole in it.

## svc_air_conditioner_repair_body

### The gap between two grilles

Begin with a number instead of an impression. Hold a thermometer in the return grille, take a second reading at a supply register near the air handler, and subtract one from the other. A system in reasonable order takes a solid double-digit bite out of the air passing through it, and something around twenty degrees is ordinary on a hot afternoon. Six or seven degrees means the machine has stopped carrying its share. The opposite result is just as informative: a healthy gap in a house that keeps climbing anyway usually points at heat arriving faster than the equipment was sized for, or at supply duct dumping cold air into the attic before it ever reaches a back bedroom.

The Dallas Fort Worth station averages 20.2 days a year at 100 degrees or hotter, on a normal annual cooling load of 2,825 degree days. Averages are the wrong way to plan around it. The record for the station shows 55 triple-digit days in 2023 and only 7 in 2025, which is why a condenser that coasted through last summer can be genuinely out of margin by the third week of the next hot one. Weak parts do not announce themselves in April here. They wait for the load.

The city's own 2045 comprehensive plan puts roughly a third of the housing stock in the years 2000 to 2009, records a citywide median home age of 23 years from 2022 Census data, and notes that 46 percent of existing housing had passed 25 years old by 2025. Census tract medians inside the city range from 12 years to 46. Glendover Park homes date to roughly 1998 through 2003 and Waterford Parks to 2002 through 2010, so those streets are now on the second condenser and the first evaporator coil. Star Creek spans 2005 to 2020 and holds the newest controls in town. Lost Creek Ranch has better than three quarters of its homes built after 1999. Almost all of it puts the air handler, the coil and the primary drain in the attic, over finished ceilings.

### Water is a drainage fault until the drain is proven clear

Cooling pulls moisture out of the air, and that moisture has to run somewhere. A plugged primary line, a trap full of slime, a cracked pan or a condensate pump that stopped will send it into the secondary pan and then through the ceiling. Ice on the evaporator does the same thing in one rush when it melts. The useful question is not how to dry the floor. It is which failure sent the water out of its path, and how far it traveled through the framing before it showed up as a stain.

### Two things to leave alone

Do not keep resetting a breaker to see what happens, and do not go up the attic ladder to investigate wet insulation. Attic temperatures in an Allen August sit far above anything comfortable, the platform around an air handler is often nothing more than a couple of boards, and wet drywall will not hold a foot. Switching cooling off at the thermostat is the whole of the useful homeowner response. It stops the coil making more water and it protects a compressor that may be short cycling.

### The order the visit runs in

Thermostat call first, then power: line voltage at the disconnect, 24 volts at the board. Filter pulled and looked at, static pressure across the air handler read, blower current compared with the nameplate. Outside, the fan and compressor are watched through a start attempt while capacitance and contactor condition are checked. Coil faces get inspected front and back. Pan, trap, float switch and drain line get tested rather than glanced at. Only after all of that do gauges go on, because pressures read through a starved coil describe the filter, not the charge.

### What moves the figure

A capacitor, a contactor, a float switch or a cleared drain sit at the tight end: small part, one trip, nothing filed with the city. Anything in the attic costs labor before it costs parts, because a coil change over a bedroom is slow, two-person work in brutal heat. A leak search is its own line. And once the outdoor unit or the coil is being replaced rather than repaired, the job becomes permitted work in Allen and the paperwork belongs on the estimate.

## svc_ac_compressor_repair_lede

A compressor is a pump. It is also the most expensive thing in the cabinet, which is why it gets blamed for failures that belong to a twelve-dollar part sitting eight inches away from it. Proving which is which takes a meter.

## svc_ac_compressor_repair_body

### A dead outdoor unit is not a dead compressor

Four descriptions justify looking hard at the pump, and none of them is a verdict on its own. A deep hum for a second or two, then a click, then silence, repeating every few minutes as the internal overload opens and resets. A breaker that will not stay set. Cooling that behaves in the morning, gives up in the late afternoon and comes back overnight. And a condenser fan spinning cheerfully over a cabinet that is moving no heat at all. Every one of those has a cheap explanation and an expensive one.

### The hour when everything in Collin County calls at once

Grid load is a useful way to picture the stress. ERCOT logged its all-time peak demand of 91,089 megawatts on July 22, 2026, which is the afternoon shape of every thermostat in the region asking for the same thing simultaneously. A start component that is fading will still turn the motor over on a mild morning and fail on that afternoon, when the head pressure is highest and the compressor needs the most help getting going. Failures that look intermittent are usually not intermittent at all. They are load dependent.

### Stop resetting it

A compressor that cannot turn draws several times its running current until something opens the circuit. Each reset sends that current back through the windings and adds heat to insulation that is already cooked. If the breaker trips the instant you set it, leave it off and say so when you book. Turn down anything sold out of a can as a fix, too. Sealants migrate into the metering device and into the next technician's recovery equipment, and they convert a repairable circuit into a replacement.

### What gets measured before the word compressor is used

Voltage at the disconnect and across the contactor with the system calling. Capacitance read on a meter and compared against the rating printed on the case, since a part reading well under its tolerance band will start a motor at 80 degrees and refuse at 102. Contactor faces checked for pitting and welding. Compressor current against rated load amperage, then winding resistance and a test for a winding grounded to the shell. Suction and liquid pressures, with the outdoor temperature written beside them, because that pair is what shows whether the pump develops a pressure difference at all. Ask for those numbers out loud. A reading you can hear is the difference between a diagnosis and a hunch.

### The mistake that wastes a compressor twice

Compressors rarely die of old age alone. They die of conditions: fins matted flat so heat cannot leave the coil, a lazy condenser fan motor, a cabinet crowded by a fence or shrubs, a charge that leaked away months ago and left the pump running hot. Fitting a new compressor into the same conditions buys a repeat performance. Anyone proposing that repair should also say what the surrounding fault was and how it is being corrected in the same visit.

### Repair or replace, and what changed in 2025

Equipment age decides this, and refrigerant rules now sit inside the arithmetic. Under EPA's technology transitions restrictions, residential air conditioning and heat pump systems have faced a [700 global warming potential limit since January 1, 2025](https://www.epa.gov/hfcs/technology-transitions-hfc-restrictions-sector). That ceiling is what moved new manufacturing off R-410A and onto mildly flammable A2L refrigerants: EPA lists R-454B, at a global warming potential of 470, as [acceptable with use conditions for residential air conditioning](https://www.epa.gov/snap/substitutes-residential-and-light-commercial-air-conditioning-and-heat-pumps). Two consequences matter to an Allen homeowner deciding what to do about a failed pump. Parts used to repair an existing system are not caught by the installation restrictions, so fixing what you own is entirely legitimate. But an A2L is not a drop-in for an older system, and matching a new condenser to an existing R-410A coil gets harder as inventory runs down. A large sealed-system repair on a fifteen-year-old outdoor unit is worth pricing against a full changeout rather than authorized on the spot.

### If it turns into a changeout here

Replacing a condenser is not the same scope as fixing one. Allen prices a complete HVAC system permit at $175 in its June 2025 fee schedule, requires the contractor pulling it to hold a $50 annual city registration, and prints the required inspections on the final pages of the permit itself. The city builds to the 2021 International Mechanical and Residential Codes. An estimate for a changeout should name the outdoor unit and the indoor coil by model, the tonnage, the efficiency rating, the refrigerant, whether the lineset and disconnect are new, and that permit as its own line.

## svc_ac_refrigerant_leak_repair_lede

Refrigerant is not fuel and a cooling circuit is not a tank. If the charge is down, it went out through a defect, and locating that defect is the job. Everything else is a recharge with a shelf life.

## svc_ac_refrigerant_leak_repair_body

### How a slow loss announces itself

The pattern is usually recognizable from the hallway before anyone puts gauges on it. Plenty of air at the registers, none of it properly cold, while the outdoor unit runs on. Frost forming at the metering device and creeping out along the fat insulated line. An oily film on copper or a dark stain on the pad, because oil rides out with escaping refrigerant. Above all, a system that got a little worse each of the last three summers rather than failing on one specific afternoon. Sudden total loss happens, but the gradual version is far more common.

### The five places these systems give up

Start at the evaporator coil, which in most Allen houses sits in the attic. Copper tubing that spends five months a year wet develops pinhole corrosion, and a coil is both the most likely leak site and the most expensive one to reach. Second, the brazed joints at the outdoor unit, worked by thermal cycling through a season that runs 2,825 cooling degree days at the DFW station. Third, the lineset where it penetrates brick veneer or runs behind landscaping nobody has moved in a decade. Fourth, the Schrader cores under the service caps, a genuine leak in a part that costs almost nothing. Fifth, the indoor coil's own factory fittings on equipment that has already been swapped once, which describes a lot of streets built between 1998 and 2010.

### Adding gas without a search is a subscription

A pound weighed into a leaking coil in May will be back out of it by September, and the second visit costs what the first one did. There is also a rule attached. Federal certification is required to open a refrigerant circuit, and venting during service or disposal is prohibited. In Texas the contracting side of this work sits under the state licensing program: contractor licenses carry a TACL number with a class and an endorsement, and technicians must be registered or certified and work under a licensed contractor. A company with no licensed contractor at the location cannot enforce the contract it signed. The number is on the truck and on the license search. Ask for it, and ask for the leak search to appear as its own line before anything is weighed in.

### What a real search looks like

Standing pressure gets read and recorded first, because a circuit sitting flat and a circuit slightly low call for different methods. On a flat system, dry nitrogen goes in and the halves are isolated to find which side lost it. On a slow loss, an electronic detector rated for the refrigerant in that system works the coil, the joints and the lineset with the fan off, and anything suspicious gets confirmed with bubbles rather than declared. Then the repair itself, then evacuation down to a micron target that holds with the pump valved off, because moisture left behind turns acidic and eats the compressor from the inside. Charge goes in on a scale, matched to the plate on the cabinet, and the result is checked at a register before anyone leaves.

### The corners that get cut

Dye squirted in with no return visit ever booked to look for it. A detector waved past a coil for twenty seconds. Soap solution used alone on a system that only leaks under running pressure. A vacuum pulled for ten minutes with no micron gauge on the manifold. And charge trimmed by pressure alone rather than weighed, which on a fixed metering device is guesswork with an instrument attached to it.

### Why 2025 changed the repair-or-replace math

EPA's technology transitions rule put a [700 global warming potential ceiling on new residential air conditioning systems from January 1, 2025](https://www.epa.gov/hfcs/technology-transitions-hfc-restrictions-sector), which pushed new equipment onto A2L refrigerants such as R-454B. None of that forces anyone to replace a working R-410A system, and components used to repair an existing system are outside those installation restrictions. What it does change is the arithmetic on a big leak in old equipment. Repairing an accessible joint on a ten-year-old system is easy money well spent. A corroded attic coil under an outdoor unit of the same vintage is the case for pricing both paths side by side, since anything installed new now uses a different refrigerant and different tooling.

### The shape of the cost

Where the leak hides sets almost everything. A valve core or an exposed joint is a short visit and a trivial part. A coil in an attic air handler, or a lineset buried behind masonry, is hours of labor before a single pound of refrigerant is weighed in, and refrigerant itself is billed by the pound. If the coil or the outdoor unit ends up replaced instead of repaired, the Allen permit and its inspections join the estimate.

## svc_ac_tune_up_lede

Maintenance earns its keep only when it produces readings you can compare next spring. A visit that ends in a page of check marks and no numbers on it has told you nothing about whether your system will survive August.

## svc_ac_tune_up_body

### What should be written on the invoice

Ten figures, legible, in your hands when the technician leaves. Return and supply temperatures with the difference between them. Static pressure across the air handler. Blower and condenser fan current against their nameplates. Compressor current against rated load. Capacitance for each run capacitor against the rating on its case. Suction and liquid pressures with the outdoor temperature noted beside them. Superheat or subcooling, whichever the metering device calls for. Those numbers are the entire value of the visit, because a reading taken in March is what tells you in July that something has drifted.

### Book it against the Allen calendar

The window is narrower than most people think. Normal for the DFW station is 20.2 days a year at 100 degrees or above, but the station's own record shows 55 of them in 2023 against 7 in 2025. A mild summer hides weak parts rather than repairing them, so the system that got through last year without complaint is exactly the one worth reading before the next hot run. March and April are the useful months. By the time the first sustained triple-digit week arrives, every truck in Collin County is already committed.

### Fins have to be able to pass air

Heat picked up indoors leaves through the outdoor coil, and it can only do that if air moves through the fins. Grass clippings from a mower pointed the wrong way, cottonwood seed, dust and leaf litter pack that surface until head pressure climbs, capacity falls and current draw rises in the weeks the equipment can least afford it. Cleaning it properly often recovers most of a missing temperature difference with no parts at all. Homeowner limits are worth knowing: power off at the disconnect and a gentle garden hose rinsing outward from inside the coil is reasonable. A pressure washer folds the fins flat and permanently reduces airflow. Prying at fins with a screwdriver does the same thing faster.

### The switch that protects your ceiling

Attic equipment makes condensate directly above finished rooms, so the drainage side of a tune-up matters more here than the coil does. Pan, trap and drain line get flushed. The float switch gets lifted by hand to confirm it genuinely shuts the system down, since that one small part is all that stands between a plugged line and a ruined ceiling in a house where the air handler sits over a bedroom. A corroded secondary pan gets flagged before it fails rather than after.

### A part gets replaced because a meter said so

Contactors and capacitors work on every start, and both wear in ways you can measure. A capacitor drifts down from its rating gradually. Contactor faces pit, then weld. Fan motor bearings tighten and pull more current. All of that shows up on an instrument, which is the correct basis for swapping a part. A line item labeled preventive replacement with no reading beside it is a sale, not maintenance. The reverse is also true: a new buzz, a repeated breaker trip, a burnt smell or a fan that has stopped is a repair to be scoped and quoted, not something to fold quietly into a maintenance ticket.

### What Allen conditions do to wiring and controls

Low-voltage wiring, connections and control boards in this city spend five months a year in an attic that is far hotter than the equipment cabinet outside. Insulation stiffens, connections loosen through thermal cycling, and a marginal joint that worked all winter starts dropping the call for cooling intermittently in July. A thorough visit tightens and inspects those connections, checks thermostat calibration and staging, and confirms that the outdoor unit actually responds to a call rather than assuming it does.

### What maintenance cannot do

A tune-up finds conditions. It does not cure a leaking coil, a failed compressor, burnt wiring or a duct system that was never big enough for the house. It also cannot promise a summer without a breakdown, and any pitch that implies otherwise should be treated with suspicion. Maintenance is flat-rate work, priced in the market as a fixed visit rather than by the machine, and the range moves with the season rather than with the equipment. Refrigerant by the pound and any part replacement fall outside it. Each of those is a separate scope, quoted on its own, and yours to approve or turn down.
