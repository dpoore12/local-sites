## symptom_1_title

The system runs, but the rooms stay warm

## symptom_1

Warm supply air does not point to one part. The thermostat may be calling for cooling while the outdoor unit never starts. The outdoor unit may run while a weak capacitor, failed contactor, compressor problem or control issue keeps it from doing its full job. A dirty filter, blower fault, restricted return, closed register or a coil that cannot move heat well can also leave rooms warm even though you hear equipment operating. The first job is to separate an airflow problem from a refrigeration or electrical problem rather than naming a part by sound alone.

At the visit, the technician checks what the thermostat is requesting and whether the indoor and outdoor pieces are responding. Air movement, temperature change across the indoor coil, electrical readings and the condition of accessible components help narrow the cause. If cooling capacity is low, the technician should look for why before adding refrigerant; refrigerant is not a fuel that simply gets used up. A leak, an airflow condition or another equipment fault can be behind a low charge reading.

In Allen, a system that is merely limping can become much more noticeable during a long hot run. Close off unused rooms only if the system and duct layout are designed for it; shutting several supplies can create a different airflow problem. Until help arrives, use a clean filter of the correct size, keep returns unblocked and set the thermostat to a realistic temperature. If the house remains hot, schedule repair rather than running a struggling unit around the clock. The proposed fix should identify the problem found, the work planned and the price before any repair begins.

## symptom_2_title

Cold air was there, then it changed to warm air

## symptom_2

An abrupt change from cold to warm can be electrical, mechanical or control-related. A condenser fan that has stopped, a contactor that is not closing, a failed capacitor or a tripped safety can keep the outdoor side from rejecting heat. The indoor blower may still push air through the vents, which makes the failure feel confusing: there is airflow, just no cooling. Other times the unit cycles briefly and quits, or the thermostat display looks normal while a component outside is silent.

Start with safe observations. Check that the thermostat is set to cool and that its temperature setting is below the room temperature. Look for a tripped breaker once, but do not keep resetting it. Repeated trips are a reason to leave the system off and let a technician trace the cause. Keep weeds, storage and furniture away from the outdoor equipment so it has breathing room, but do not reach through a guard or pull a disconnect. Electrical parts can hold a charge after power is removed.

Diagnosis at the unit matters because several faults create the same warm-air result. A technician can verify voltage where it is needed, inspect a contactor and capacitor, observe the fan and compressor sequence, and check whether a safety device is stopping operation. Those steps distinguish a small failed component from a condition that needs a broader repair. In a post-1990 Allen house, the original system may have been changed once already, so model information and prior work can be useful but should not become an assumption. The repair estimate comes after the fault is identified. If the equipment needs to remain off to prevent damage or an electrical risk, the technician should say so plainly.

## symptom_3_title

Water is collecting near the indoor unit

## symptom_3

Water around an air handler, furnace closet or ceiling opening deserves attention even when the system still cools. During normal cooling, the indoor coil removes moisture and sends it to a condensate drain. A blocked drain line, a full secondary pan, a failed condensate pump or a drain connection problem can let that water back up. Ice on the evaporator coil can also produce a noticeable spill when it melts, and ice itself may be tied to low airflow, a dirty filter, a blower problem or a refrigeration fault.

Do not treat every puddle as ordinary condensation. First, protect nearby flooring and belongings without opening the cabinet. If water is near wiring, a breaker panel, an electrical outlet or exposed electrical equipment, turn the cooling system off at the thermostat and keep the area clear. A ceiling stain or sagging drywall below attic equipment is another reason to stop the system and arrange prompt service. Running it longer can turn a drainage repair into a ceiling or wall cleanup.

The technician should identify the source before clearing or replacing anything. That may include checking the primary and secondary drains, pan condition, pump operation, float safety, filter and coil access. The goal is not simply to make the water disappear; it is to restore the drain path and correct the condition that made it overflow. Ask what caused the backup and where the water traveled. For Allen homes with equipment in attics or upstairs closets, that answer matters because the first visible drip may be some distance from the actual blockage. The repair proposal should make clear whether the work addresses the drain, the pump, an airflow condition or a separate cooling fault.

## symptom_4_title

The air conditioner will not start at all

## symptom_4

A no-start can begin at the thermostat, but it should not end with one. A blank thermostat display may be a battery, power or control issue. A lit display with no indoor blower or outdoor response can involve a breaker, disconnect, float safety, transformer, relay, contactor or another electrical component. If the indoor blower starts but the outside unit does not, the diagnosis moves in a different direction than a system where nothing responds. The sequence is important, and it is something a technician can observe and test at the equipment.

Before scheduling service, check the simple, safe items once: the thermostat is in cooling mode, the set temperature is below the room temperature, and any accessible breaker is not obviously tripped. If a breaker is tripped, reset it only once. A breaker that trips again, a burning smell, buzzing from electrical equipment or a hot disconnect is a stop sign, not a troubleshooting project. Leave the system off and keep people away from the equipment until it can be examined.

At the house, a technician can check low-voltage signals, line power, protective safeties and component response without treating a new thermostat as the default answer. This prevents a replacement part from being installed before the actual interruption is known. Because City of Allen lists HVAC/mechanical among contractor classifications and routes permits through its building process, any replacement that changes the scope should be handled with the applicable paperwork rather than treated as an invisible swap. For a straightforward repair, the useful outcome is simpler: identify why the start sequence stopped, show what needs to be repaired, provide the price before work and verify operation afterward.

## qa_1_question

Why does my Allen AC run but not lower the temperature?

## qa_1_answer

The sound of a running system only confirms that something is on. The indoor blower can move air when the outdoor equipment is not cooling, and low airflow or a coil problem can reduce cooling even when both sections operate. A technician needs to check the thermostat call, airflow, electrical response and cooling performance at the equipment to tell which situation applies.

## qa_2_question

Should I keep an AC running if it is leaking water?

## qa_2_answer

Not automatically. A drain backup can worsen while the system keeps removing humidity, and water near wiring, outlets, an electrical panel or a sagging ceiling is a reason to turn cooling off at the thermostat and arrange service. The source may be a drain, pump, pan, ice-related melt or another condition, so an inspection should find the source rather than just dry the floor.

## qa_3_question

What should be checked before refrigerant is added?

## qa_3_answer

The system should be tested to understand why the cooling performance is low. Refrigerant does not normally disappear through ordinary operation, so a technician may need to inspect for a leak, confirm airflow and look for component or coil conditions that affect readings. Adding refrigerant without finding the cause can leave the original issue in place and delay the repair that actually restores operation.

## pricing_body

### The diagnostic fee decides how the rest of the bill reads

Start with the smallest number in the table, because it shifts everything above it. Collin County shops publish diagnostic and service call fees across a wide spread and handle that fee three ways: waived when the repair is authorized, credited against the total, or charged separately and never applied to the work. At least one local price list states in writing that its diagnostic is not credited. That is a business model rather than a scam, but it means two identical capacitor repairs at the same part price can differ by most of a hundred dollars.

So the first thing worth doing on a Collin County service call is asking whether the fee applies to the repair, and whether the quoted repair figure already contains it. A published part price of $135 alongside a separate $95 diagnostic is a $230 visit, and the table above reflects totals rather than headline part prices.

### Why a young suburb still generates big repair bills

Allen is not an old housing market. Much of it went up during and after the 1990s growth wave, so most systems here are on their second or third condenser and the equipment is generally R-410A rather than phased out R-22. That should make repairs cheaper, and for small parts it does. What it does not solve is where the equipment lives. Builders here put the air handler and evaporator coil in an attic that runs past 130 degrees on an August afternoon, and a coil or blower change up there is slow two person work.

The labor market sets the floor. Across the Dallas, Fort Worth and Arlington metro area, the May 2025 median wage for heating and air conditioning mechanics and installers was $28.63 an hour across 10,910 workers, a large trade pool that gives Collin County real price competition on small repairs. August demand still hits every shop at once, and priority scheduling is priced accordingly.

### What the city charges once equipment changes

Repair work is not permitted. Replacement is. The City of Allen fee schedule, June 2025 prices a complete HVAC system permit at $175, over the counter trade permits at $75, and requires every contractor pulling a permit to hold a $50 annual city registration. That registration is the useful part: an unregistered shop cannot legally permit the changeout it is selling.

Inspections have their own arithmetic. Allen prices an inspection outside normal business hours at $75 an hour with a two hour minimum, and reinspections escalate at $75, then $100, then $125 for each additional trip. A changeout that fails inspection over the disconnect, the condensate line or the equipment pad costs both money and days without cooling.

### Reading a Collin County estimate

A written estimate shows the diagnostic charge as its own line and states whether it is credited. On a component repair it names the part, states whether it is under manufacturer warranty, and separates part from labor. Refrigerant is priced per pound with the pounds stated, not described as a top off, and a leak search is a separate line, because finding a leak and fixing one are different jobs.

On a changeout, an estimate should list the outdoor unit and indoor coil by model number, the tonnage, the efficiency rating, whether the lineset is reused or replaced, whether the disconnect and pad are new, the thermostat, any duct modifications, and the Allen permit as its own line item. Anything missing is a change order waiting to happen.

### The oversell, said plainly

In this market the pressure point is the compressor conversation. A failed compressor on a fourteen year old condenser is genuinely a replacement discussion. The problem is the reverse move: a capacitor, contactor or fan motor failure presented as a system on its last legs. Those parts fail on healthy equipment routinely. Ask for the measurement behind the diagnosis, the microfarad reading on a capacitor, the amp draw on a motor, superheat and subcooling on a refrigerant claim.

The second pattern is refrigerant sold repeatedly. A sealed system does not consume refrigerant. Two summers of top offs means a leak, and paying a third time without a leak search is filling a bucket with a hole in it.

### What the online average leaves out

National cost pages price a condenser and a coil in a driveway. They do not price the Allen permit, a reinspection, the attic access that makes an indoor coil change hard here, a lineset carrying mineral oil from an R-22 system, a panel that cannot support a larger unit, or the duct static problem that made the old system struggle. That last item decides whether a new system cools the back bedrooms.
