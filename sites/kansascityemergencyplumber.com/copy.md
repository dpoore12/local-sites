## symptom_1_title

Water is spraying, dripping fast, or appearing after a freeze

## symptom_1

An active supply leak is a containment problem first. If the leak is at a sink, toilet, washer, water heater, or an exposed pipe, use the nearest fixture stop valve only if it turns normally and you can reach it without standing in water near electricity. Otherwise, use the building’s main water shutoff. In many houses it is near the meter, where the service enters the basement, or in a mechanical room. Leave the power alone if water has reached an outlet, appliance, panel, or extension cord; move people clear and use the main shutoff rather than touching wet equipment.

Frozen-pipe calls have a particular Kansas City pattern. A pipe may stop delivering water during the cold spell, then split or pull open at a weak spot as it begins to thaw. The burst can be behind a finished basement wall, beneath a cabinet on an exterior wall, or above a garage ceiling, so the first visible sign may be a stain, a wet floor, or the sound of water rather than the pipe itself. Do not use an open flame, torch, or unattended heater to thaw a line. That can turn a plumbing problem into a fire or damage concealed wiring.

Once the water is off, open a faucet on the lowest level to relieve pressure and place towels or a pan beneath the accessible leak. Photograph damage from a safe distance for your records, but do not delay shutting water off to do it. The visit should identify whether the failed part is a supply tube, valve, fitting, appliance connection, or a split pipe. A small exposed repair can be direct. A leak inside a wall or ceiling may require opening access, checking the surrounding material, and making a plan for drying after the water is controlled.

## symptom_2_title

The toilet is rising, or a sink and tub are both backing up

## symptom_2

A single toilet that keeps rising after a flush can be a local clog at that fixture. Lift the tank lid and push the flapper closed if water is still feeding the bowl, then turn the small stop valve behind the toilet clockwise only if it moves easily. Do not keep flushing to see whether it clears. Each test adds more water to a bowl that may already be near the rim. A plunger is reasonable only when the water level is stable and the problem is plainly one toilet; stop if water appears at another drain or on the floor.

When a tub, shower, sink, or floor drain reacts while a toilet is used, treat it differently. Multiple fixtures moving together usually point farther downstream than one fixture trap. Keep water out of that side of the house: no laundry, dishwasher, showers, or extra flushes. Wastewater can emerge at the lowest open drain, which is often a basement floor drain or shower. Keep children and pets away, protect yourself from contact, and avoid putting chemical drain cleaner into a system that is already backing up. Those products do not remove a main-line obstruction reliably and create a hazardous liquid for anyone opening the line.

The on-site work begins with identifying the affected fixtures and the lowest point where water is appearing. Clearing the immediate obstruction may be enough, but a repeated multi-fixture backup needs a reason, not only a temporary improvement. The technician may recommend a camera after flow is restored to look for a collapsed section, roots, a displaced connection, or buildup. In a Kansas City house built in the period when cast-iron drain piping was common, the material and condition of the buried or exposed drain matter to what happens next. The emergency is stopped first; the evidence is then used to decide whether cleaning, repair, or a broader plan makes sense.

## symptom_3_title

There is a sewer odor, slow drains everywhere, or sewage at a floor drain

## symptom_3

Sewage at a floor drain, black water in a tub, or a persistent sewage odor is not a normal slow-drain call. Do not run water to “push it through.” Stop using fixtures that feed the affected line, keep the area ventilated if that can be done without spreading contamination through the house, and keep people and pets out of the wet area. If sewage has reached electrical equipment, do not enter the space or touch switches. A drain blockage is unpleasant; contaminated water across a finished basement adds health and material-damage concerns that need to be contained promptly.

Recurrent slow drainage, repeated backups, rust at exposed joints, or wet ground along a line’s route are reasons to investigate after the active backup is stopped.

The immediate plumbing task is to find where flow stops and clear it safely. The next decision is whether the obstruction was a one-time accumulation or a sign of a pipe condition that will repeat. A camera inspection can show a rough interior, offset joint, root intrusion, separated section, or other condition that a cable alone cannot explain. ## symptom_4_title

There is little or no water at the fixtures

## symptom_4

No water at one sink is usually different from no water in the whole house. Start with a simple check: try a cold-water fixture and a hot-water fixture in another room. If only one fixture has weak flow, its stop valve, aerator, supply tube, or a local branch may be involved. If both hot and cold are absent throughout the building, do not dismantle pipes in search of an answer. First check whether the main shutoff was closed for recent work and whether neighbors appear to have water. A utility outage, a closed valve, a failed pressure component, and a plumbing leak being isolated can look similar from the kitchen faucet.

If there is no water after a freeze, do not assume the line is safely thawed just because flow comes back. Look and listen around exposed piping, under sinks, near the water heater, in the basement, and where pipe passes through an exterior wall. A split can release water only after ice melts. If water is visible or you hear it running where no fixture is on, close the main shutoff and keep electricity away from the wet area. Never try to thaw an inaccessible pipe with a flame or by turning the furnace up and leaving the property.
