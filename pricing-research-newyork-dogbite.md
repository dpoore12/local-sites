## What actually governs a dog bite fee in Manhattan

New York County sits in the **First Judicial Department**. The Appellate
Division, First Department rule on contingent fees in personal injury and
wrongful death claims is **22 NYCRR 603.25(e)**.

Source: **22 NYCRR 603.25, Appellate Division First Department**, New York State
Unified Court System —
https://www.nycourts.gov/LegacyPDFS/rules/jointappellate/1st%20Dept.%20-%20603.25.pdf
(retrieved 2026-08-23).

Verified text and figures:

- 603.25(e)(1): in **any claim or action for personal injury or wrongful death,
  other than one alleging medical, dental or podiatric malpractice**, whether
  resolved by judgment or settlement, where the attorney's compensation is
  contingent, compensation equal to or less than the department's scheduled fee
  is **deemed fair and reasonable**. Compensation in excess of the schedule
  "shall constitute the exaction of unreasonable and unconscionable
  compensation" in violation of the Rules of Professional Conduct (Part 1200),
  unless authorized by written court order.
- 603.25(e)(2) **Schedule A** (graduated):
  - **50 percent** on the first **$1,000** recovered
  - **40 percent** on the next **$2,000**
  - **35 percent** on the next **$22,000**
  - **25 percent** on any amount **over $25,000**
- 603.25(e)(2) **Schedule B**: "A percentage not exceeding 33⅓ percent of the sum
  recovered," available **if the initial contractual arrangement so provides**,
  in which event the procedure for applying for additional compensation because
  of extraordinary circumstances **does not apply**.
- 603.25(e)(3): the percentage is computed by one of two methods **selected by
  the client in the retainer agreement or letter of engagement** — (i) on the
  **net** sum after deducting expenses and disbursements for expert testimony and
  investigative or other services properly chargeable to the claim, or (ii) where
  the attorney agrees to pay costs and expenses under Judiciary Law 488(2)(d), on
  the **gross** sum before deducting expenses. The agreement must describe both
  methods, explain the financial consequences of each, and clearly show the
  client's selection. Costs as taxed, including interest on a judgment, count as
  part of the amount recovered. **No deduction** is made in computing the
  percentage for liens, assignments or claims in favor of hospitals, for medical
  care and treatment by doctors and nurses, or of self-insurers or insurance
  carriers.
- 603.25(e)(4): where counsel believes in good faith that **Schedule A** gives
  inadequate compensation because of extraordinary circumstances, an application
  for greater compensation may be made on affidavit, with written notice and an
  opportunity to be heard to the client and to lienholders, to the designated
  justice.

Because a dog bite claim is a personal injury claim and is **not** medical,
dental or podiatric malpractice, the 603.25(e) schedule is the operative ceiling
and Judiciary Law 474-a is not.

## Judiciary Law 474-a — the malpractice sliding scale that does not apply here

Source: **Judiciary Law § 474-a**, New York State Senate —
https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/JUD/474-A (retrieved 2026-08-23; the
30/25/20/15/10 schedule was read there verbatim). Subdivisions (3) through (5)
were additionally read at
https://law.justia.com/codes/new-york/jud/article-15/474-a/ on the same date.

474-a(2): notwithstanding any inconsistent judicial rule, a contingent fee in a
**medical, dental or podiatric malpractice** action shall not exceed:

| Tier | Maximum |
| --- | --- |
| First $250,000 recovered | **30%** |
| Next $250,000 | **25%** |
| Next $500,000 | **20%** |
| Next $250,000 | **15%** |
| Any amount over $1,250,000 | **10%** |

474-a(3): computed on the **net** sum after deducting expert, investigative and
other service expenses; no deduction for hospital, medical, dental or podiatric
liens or for carrier/self-insurer claims. 474-a(4): a court may allow more on a
showing of extraordinary circumstances, capped at the contractual fee. 474-a(5):
an infant's malpractice claim remains subject to Judiciary Law 474.

Recorded here so the page can state plainly what does **not** govern a dog bite
claim. The 30/25/20/15/10 scale is malpractice-only.

## Judiciary Law 474 — an infant's claim

Source: https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/JUD/474 (retrieved 2026-08-23).

Compensation is governed by agreement "which is not restrained by law," **except**
that no agreement between an attorney and the **guardian of an infant** for
compensation dependent on the success of the infant's claim, or for a percentage
of any recovery or award for the infant, "shall be valid or enforceable unless
made as hereinafter provided." An attorney may contract with the guardian subject
to the **power of the court to fix the amount**. On recovery, award, compromise or
settlement, the attorney applies on notice to the guardian, and the court
"shall proceed summarily to determine the value of the services," taking proof by
affidavit, reference or examination of witnesses, and then makes an order
determining suitable compensation.

## 22 NYCRR 1215.1 — the engagement letter

Source: https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/new-york/22-NYCRR-1215.1
(retrieved 2026-08-23; text of the court rule). An attorney who undertakes
representation and charges or collects a fee must provide a **written letter of
engagement** before commencing the representation, or within a reasonable time
after if otherwise impracticable or the scope cannot yet be determined. Required
contents: an explanation of the **scope** of services; an explanation of the
**fees, expenses and billing practices**; and where applicable a provision that
the client may have a right to **fee-dispute arbitration under Part 137**. A
signed written retainer agreement covering those matters satisfies the rule. An
updated letter is required on a significant change in scope or fee.

## CPLR 8018 — what the county clerk charges to open the case

Source: **CPLR § 8018**, New York State Senate —
https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/CVP/8018 (retrieved 2026-08-23).

- 8018(a)(1)(i): index number fee **one hundred ninety dollars ($190)**, payable
  in advance. An additional $190 applies only in an RPAPL article 13 foreclosure.
- 8018(a)(3): an **additional five dollars ($5)** (Local Government Records
  Management Improvement Fund) and an **additional fifteen dollars ($15)**
  (cultural education account) — **$210 total** to assign an index number.
- 8018(d): once the index number is assigned, the clerk charges no further fee for
  filing, entering, indexing or docketing papers in that action.

Also verified: **Note of Issue $125** where no Request for Judicial Intervention
is required, Appellate Division record on appeal $315, appellate motion $45
— New York State Unified Court System filing fees page,
https://www.nycourts.gov/forms/filingfees.shtml (retrieved 2026-08-23).

## NYC dog license — Health Code Article 161

Source: **NYC Department of Health, Dog Licenses** —
https://www.nyc.gov/site/doh/services/dog-licenses.page (retrieved 2026-08-23),
implementing NYC Health Code 161.04.

- Spayed or neutered dog: **$8.50** per year.
- Dog not spayed or neutered, older than four months: **$34**.
- Dog not spayed or neutered, under four months: **$8.50**.
- Late renewal fine: **$2 for each year** the license was not renewed.
- Replacement tag **$1**; registering a trained guard dog **$10**.
