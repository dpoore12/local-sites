## EPA — remediation method by affected area
Source: https://www.epa.gov/mold/mold-remediation-schools-and-commercial-buildings-guide-chapter-3
- "Limited containment is generally recommended for areas involving between 10 and 100 square feet (ft^2) of mold contamination."
- Limited containment: "single layer of 6-mil, fire-retardant polyethylene sheeting"; "slit entry and covering flap on the outside"; seal "all supply and air vents", "doors", "chases", "risers"; "must be maintained under negative pressure relative to surrounding areas" using "a HEPA-filtered fan unit exhausted outside of the building."
- "Full containment is recommended for the cleanup of mold-contaminated surface areas greater than 100 ft^2..." Double poly layers; "A decontamination chamber or airlock should be constructed."
- "In general, the size of the area helps determine the level of containment." "However, a heavy growth of mold in a relatively small area could release more spores than a lighter growth of mold in a relatively large area." "Choice of containment should be based on professional judgment."
- "In most cases, if visible mold growth is present, sampling is unnecessary."
- "Since no EPA or other Federal threshold limits have been set for mold or mold spores, sampling cannot be used to check a building's compliance with Federal mold standards."
- Minimum PPE "is an N-95 respirator, gloves and eye protection."
- "The remediation plan should include steps to fix the water or moisture problem, or the problem may reoccur."

Source: https://www.epa.gov/mold/mold-remediation-schools-and-commercial-buildings-guide-chapter-2
- "Maintain low indoor humidity, below 60% relative humidity (RH), ideally 30-50%, if possible."
- "Clean and dry wet or damp spots within 48 hours."

## EPA — homeowner guidance
Source: https://www.epa.gov/mold/brief-guide-mold-moisture-and-your-home
- "If the moldy area is less than about 10 square feet (less than roughly a 3 ft. by 3 ft. patch), in most cases, you can handle the job yourself."
- "The use of a chemical or biocide that kills organisms such as mold (chlorine bleach, for example) is not recommended as a routine practice during mold cleanup."
- "Dead mold may still cause allergic reactions in some people, so it is not enough to simply kill the mold, it must also be removed."
- "Absorbent or porous materials, such as ceiling tiles and carpet, may have to be thrown away if they become moldy."
- "Do not paint or caulk moldy surfaces. Clean up the mold and dry the surfaces before painting. Paint applied over moldy surfaces is likely to peel."
- "EPA does not regulate mold or mold spores in indoor air."
- "It is important to dry water-damaged areas and items within 24-48 hours to prevent mold growth."

Source: https://www.epa.gov/mold/ten-things-you-should-know-about-mold
- "There is no practical way to eliminate all mold and mold spores in the indoor environment; the way to control indoor mold growth is to control moisture."
- "Reduce indoor humidity (to 30-60%)."
- "Potential health effects and symptoms associated with mold exposures include allergic reactions, asthma and other respiratory complaints."

Source: https://www.epa.gov/mold/mold-testing-or-sampling
- "Standards or Threshold Limit Values (TLVs) for airborne concentrations of mold, or mold spores, have not been set."
- "Currently, there are no EPA regulations or standards for airborne mold contaminants."

Source: https://www.epa.gov/mold/mold-course-chapter-3
- "Most mold is found by simply looking." "In general, the most important equipment is your own eyes and nose, although a good flashlight may help."
- "Many investigators use moisture meters to find wet areas where mold may be growing." Usable on "Carpet, Wallboard, Wood, Brick, Concrete."
- "A borescope is an optical probe, inserted through a small hole drilled into a wall..."
- "humidity gauges may be useful for checking or monitoring humidity throughout the building."
- Hidden mold locations: "The backside of drywall", "the top side of ceiling tiles", "the underside of carpets and pads", "damp areas behind walls and in crawlspaces", "On roof materials above ceiling tiles."
- "Investigating hidden mold requires caution since disturbing moldy areas may spread mold throughout the building."

## CDC
Source: https://www.cdc.gov/mold-health/about/index.html
- "CDC does not recommend mold testing."
- "there are no set standards for what is and what is not an acceptable quantity of different kinds of mold in a home."
- "No matter what type of mold is present, you need to remove it."
- Bleach: "no more than 1 cup of household laundry bleach in 1 gallon of water"; "Never mix bleach with ammonia or other cleaners."
- Health: "For some people, mold can cause a stuffy nose, sore throat, coughing or wheezing, burning eyes, or skin rash." "People with asthma or who are allergic to mold may have severe reactions." "Immune-compromised people and people with chronic lung disease may get infections in their lungs from mold." IOM 2004 findings cited.

## Colorado: no mold license, no state regulator
- Division of Professions and Occupations regulates "more than 610,000 licensees within more than 60 professions, occupations and businesses." Listed occupational programs include Electrical, Plumbing and **Radon Professionals**. Mold remediation/assessment is NOT listed. https://dpo.colorado.gov/
- "CDPHE does not regulate mold. Mold issues fall under two state laws, including House Bill 18-1397: Landlord Tenant Warranty Of Habitability and Senate Bill 24-094: Safe Housing for Residential Tenants." https://cdphe.colorado.gov/about-cdphe/contact-us and https://cdphe.colorado.gov/apcd/submitcomplaint

## Colorado landlord habitability statute — mold is named
Source: https://law.justia.com/codes/colorado/title-38/tenants-and-landlords/article-12/part-5/section-38-12-505/
- 38-12-505(1)(a): premises uninhabitable if "There is mold that is associated with dampness, or there is any other condition causing the residential premises to be damp, which condition, if not remedied, would materially interfere with the health or safety of the tenant, excluding the presence of mold that is minor and found on surfaces that can accumulate moisture as part of their proper functioning and intended use."
- 38-12-505(3)(c),(3)(d): on and after January 1, 2025 every rental agreement must carry a 12-point bold-faced habitability statement, and an address plus e-mail or portal for written notice of an uninhabitable condition, in English and Spanish.

Source: https://law.justia.com/codes/colorado/title-38/tenants-and-landlords/article-12/part-5/section-38-12-503/
- 38-12-503(2)(b)(I): landlord must commence remedial action within **24 hours** where the condition materially interferes with life, health or safety, or **72 hours** where the premises are uninhabitable as described in 38-12-505.
- 38-12-503(6)(a)(I): landlord must contact the tenant **not more than 24 hours** after receiving notice, with an estimate of when remedial action starts and finishes.
- 38-12-503(12)(a),(b): on notice of "Mold associated with dampness in a dwelling unit," the landlord must, **within 72 hours**, install containment, stop active sources of water contributing to the mold, and install a HEPA filtration device; then maintain containment, eliminate or limit moisture sources and dry impacted materials, decontaminate or remove damaged materials, evaluate success "including post-remediation testing for the existence of mold," and reassemble to control moisture.
- 38-12-503(12)(c): comparable dwelling unit or hotel room at tenant's request where the condition interferes with life, health or safety.
- 38-12-503(3)(e): notice exists where there is "any writing that provides a basis for the landlord to substantially know that the condition exists."
- 38-12-503(6)(a)(III): 24 hours advance written notice of entry, with exceptions for imminent threats.

## ANSI/IICRC S520
Source: https://iicrc.org/s520/ — "ANSI/IICRC S520: 2024 Fourth Edition"; "Standard for Professional Mold Remediation"; describes "the procedures to be followed and the precautions to be taken when performing mold remediation."
Source: https://blog.ansi.org/ansi/ansi-iicrc-s520-2024-professional-mold-remediation/
- Condition 1 (normal fungal ecology), Condition 2, Condition 3 (actual mold growth in an indoor environment).
- Section 2 "places more emphasis on discouraging remediators from spraying products on mold without first physically removing the mold and cleaning the surfaces."
- "the use of stain removers is cosmetic and can be used at the remediator's discretion only after complete source removal."
- Section 9.1.7 ozone gas and other antimicrobial devices: "less emphasis placed on biocide use."
- PRE by remediator; PRV is "an inspection and assessment performed by an independent third-party IEP after a remediation project."
- Standard "assumes that determining and correcting the underlying cause of mold contamination is the responsibility of a property owner, landlord, or their agent(s)."

## Local water sources
- Evaporative coolers: "Evaporative coolers continually use water"; "If windows are not open far enough, humidity will build up in the home"; "they add humidity to the air in your home." https://www.energy.gov/energysaver/evaporative-coolers
- Colorado State University Extension: coolers "work best in dry climates"; at 100 F outdoors and 10% RH they can deliver 73 F indoors. https://livesmartcolorado.colostate.edu/keeping-your-cool-in-colorado/
- Spring snow, NWS Pueblo record snowfall for Colorado Springs: heaviest month on record is **April 1957, 42.7 inches**; April 1900, 34.5 inches; March 1909, 30.2 inches; greatest 24-hour total **25.6 inches on March 11, 1909**. https://www.weather.gov/pub/climateCosSnowfallRecords
- Seasonal snowfall: "The 30 Year Average for seasonal snowfall = 33.6"; all-time average 39.0. https://www.weather.gov/pub/climateCosSeasonalSnowfall
- Severe hail definition, NWS Pueblo county warning area (southeast and south central Colorado): "Hail = 1 inch in diameter or LARGER"; "The size of the largest hailstone determines if the storm is considered severe"; hail depth on the ground does not. https://www.weather.gov/pub/severeWeatherClimatology
- Radon, El Paso County Public Health: "Over 40 percent of all homes tested between 2005-2023 in El Paso County had high levels of radon"; county and state in EPA "Zone 1"; test kits sold at the county public health laboratory on West Garden of the Gods Road. https://www.elpasocountyhealth.org/licenses-permits-inspections-water-testing/radon/
- Expansive soils, Colorado Geological Survey: "Colorado's most significant geologic hazard is expansive or swelling soil"; clays "can expand up to 20% by volume when exposed to water and exert a force of up to 30,000 pounds-per-square-foot"; "permitting the ponding of water near a foundation during and after construction" and "planting vegetation next to the foundation so irrigation water enters the soil" are named problems; "arid or semi-arid regions such a Colorado with seasonal changes of soil moisture experience a much higher frequency of expansion problems." https://coloradogeologicalsurvey.org/hazards/expansive-soil-rock/
