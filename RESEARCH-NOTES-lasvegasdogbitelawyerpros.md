All facts below were fetched during this session. URLs are the pages actually read.

## 1. No civil dog-bite statute in Nevada
- NRS Chapter 202 (crimes against public health and safety) contains the only dedicated dog provision, NRS 202.500. Full text fetched: https://www.leg.state.nv.us/nrs/nrs-202.html
- NRS 41.130 (general personal-injury liability): "whenever any person shall suffer personal injury by wrongful act, neglect or default of another, the person causing the injury is liable to the person injured for damages." Chapter 41 contains no dog-bite liability section (NRS 41.740 covers damages for killing/injuring another person's pet). https://www.leg.state.nv.us/nrs/nrs-041.html
- Nevada Supreme Court authority applying ordinary tort principles to a dog attack: Wright v. Schum, 781 P.2d 1142 (Nev. 1989). "Schum cannot be held liable by reason of his status as a landlord nor by reason of his failure to evict a tenant with a dog known by him to be dangerous. This does not end the matter, however." Court applied Turpel: no reason to except landlords or property owners "from the general application of the basic principles of tort law"; defendant "as other persons must exercise reasonable care not to subject others to an unreasonable risk of harm." Liability theory = Restatement (Second) of Torts § 324A(b) (negligent undertaking of another's duty). Two criteria courts elsewhere use, quoted by the court: (1) landlord's actual knowledge of the animal's vicious propensities, (2) landlord's retention of substantial control over the premises. https://law.justia.com/cases/nevada/supreme-court/1989/18282-1.html
- CORRECTION TO COMMON ADVERTISING CLAIM: many Nevada firm blogs say Glass v. Eighth Judicial District Court, 486 P.2d 1180 (1971) "abolished the one-bite rule." Fetched the opinion: it is an OBSCENITY case about an ex parte search warrant for two films. The "a dog is entitled to one free bite" line is a rhetorical analogy in dictum, not a dog-bite holding. NOT cited on the site. https://law.justia.com/cases/nevada/supreme-court/1971/6303-1.html

## 2. NRS 202.500 (dangerous / vicious dogs) — verbatim elements
- "Dangerous": declared so under subsection 2, OR without provocation, on two separate occasions within 18 months, it behaved menacingly to a degree that would lead a reasonable person to defend against substantial bodily harm, while (I) off the premises of its owner or keeper or (II) not confined in a cage, pen or vehicle.
- "Provoked" = tormented or subjected to pain.
- "Vicious": without provocation killed or inflicted substantial bodily harm on a human being; OR continued the dangerous behavior after a law enforcement agency notified the owner/keeper that the dog is dangerous.
- Subsection 2: a law enforcement agency may declare a dog dangerous if used in the commission of a crime by its owner/keeper.
- Subsection 3: cannot be found dangerous or vicious based solely on breed, or for a defensive act against a person committing/attempting a crime or who provoked the dog.
- Subsection 4: knowingly owning/keeping a vicious dog more than 7 days after actual notice, or transferring ownership after actual notice = misdemeanor.
- Subsection 5: if substantial bodily harm results from an attack by a dog known to be vicious, owner/keeper is guilty of a category D felony (NRS 193.130); judge may order the dog humanely destroyed.
- Subsection 6: local authority may not adopt or enforce a breed-based dangerous/vicious ordinance. Subsection 7: does not apply to law enforcement dogs on duty.
- Source: https://www.leg.state.nv.us/nrs/nrs-202.html
- Category D felony punishment, NRS 193.130(2)(d): minimum 1 year, maximum 4 years in state prison, plus fine of not more than $5,000. https://www.leg.state.nv.us/nrs/nrs-193.html

## 3. Negligence per se from a local ordinance
- Vega v. Eastern Courtyard Associates, 24 P.3d 219 (Nev. 2001): "the violation of a building code provision adopted by a county ordinance is negligence per se if the plaintiff belongs to the class of persons the building code provision is intended to protect, and the injury the plaintiff suffered is of the type the provision was intended to prevent." Both protection questions are questions of law for the court; violation and proximate cause are for the jury. Clark County-adopted code. https://law.justia.com/cases/nevada/supreme-court/2001/33932-1.html

## 4. Local leash / licensing / at-large rules
- Clark County Code 10.04.100: "At large" = animal not secured and restrained by a leash or lead no longer than six feet and under adequate control of the handler, or not confined within the owner's real property limits. Expressly includes: voice-command-only restraint; tethering in a public area without attendance; and being unrestrained "within private areas accessible by the general public or a considerable number of persons, regardless of enclosure, such as common areas of common interest communities." Excludes enclosed dog runs in parks with warning signage. https://www.clarkcountynv.gov/adobe/assets/urn:aaid:aem:f1f58666-7941-4544-91ea-dd69326071b7/original/as/titles-1-and-10-final-combined-ordinance-jan2025.pdf
- LVMC 7.36.030(A): unlawful for an owner or person with possession, custody or control to allow an animal to be at large (definition in 7.04.090). (B) exempts cats unless obviously injured or ill. http://lasvegas-nv.elaws.us/code/coor_title7_ch7.36_sec7.36.030
- City brochure: leash rule per 7.36.030; city pet license required for dogs/cats/ferrets over four months per 7.08.010; tethering unlawful longer than 10 hours in 24 hours or at any time during a National Weather Service heat advisory per 7.32.160; APS dispatch 702.229.6444 option 2. https://files.lasvegasnevada.gov/public-safety/Animal_Control/Animal_Protection_Services-brochure-2026.pdf

## 5. Quarantine and rabies control
- NAC 441A.425: the rabies control authority "shall cause a dog, cat or ferret, regardless of current vaccination against rabies, which has bitten a person, to be quarantined and, for 10 days following the bite, to be observed under the supervision of a licensed veterinarian" or designee; confinement must prevent direct contact. Waiver possible for law-enforcement canine or service dog meeting three conditions. https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/nevada/NAC-441A-425
- Clark County Code 10.28.030 (home quarantine): allowed only if no prior bite history, the animal will not potentially be deemed dangerous or vicious, the property is adequately secured, the owner is willing to comply, and no other extenuating circumstances; owner must confine the animal separately and securely for ten days and post a quarantine notice.
- Clark County Code 10.28.040 (offsite quarantine): if home quarantine is not authorized, the animal is confined at the owner's expense at a veterinarian's premises or the animal shelter for ten days of observation; no release until vaccination requirements are met and impound, board and other fees are paid. Same county PDF as above.
- Southern Nevada Health District reportable conditions list: "Animal bite from a rabies-susceptible species" must be reported by the next SNHD business day; Animal Bites — Clark County (702) 455-7710, x1. https://media.southernnevadahealthdistrict.org/download/epi/charts-forms/20250911-reportable-diseases-conditions.pdf

## 6. Dangerous/vicious declaration procedure, Clark County
- Code 10.16.050: animal control officer declares; notice by personal service, posting, or mail. A vicious declaration means immediate impound whenever possible and the owner may not keep the animal during the hearing process. A declared dangerous animal may stay with the owner but may not leave the private property unless muzzled, leashed and under the effective control of an adult. Hearing request within 72 hours of service (3 days added for mail). Hearing set no later than 10 business days after the written request; at least 3 days' notice with witness names and documents. Owner bears burden of persuasion by a preponderance after CCAPS meets its prima facie burden. Hearing audio recorded; hearing officer defers to CCAPS factual determinations absent abuse of discretion. Same county PDF.

## 8. Deadlines and fault
- NRS 11.190(4)(e): 2 years for "an action to recover damages for injuries to a person or for the death of a person caused by the wrongful act or neglect of another." https://www.leg.state.nv.us/nrs/nrs-011.html
- NRS 11.250: if the person entitled to bring the action was "within the age of 18 years" when the cause of action accrued, "the time of such disability shall not be a part of the time limited for the commencement of the action." Same page.
- NRS 41.141(1): comparative negligence of the plaintiff "does not bar a recovery if that negligence was not greater than the negligence or gross negligence of the parties to the action against whom recovery is sought." (2)(a): jury instructed the plaintiff may not recover if the plaintiff's comparative negligence is greater than the defendant's or the combined negligence of multiple defendants. (2)(b): general verdict on total damages plus special verdict on percentages. https://www.leg.state.nv.us/nrs/nrs-041.html

## 9. Courts
- NRS 4.370(1)(b): justice court civil jurisdiction in actions for damages for injury to the person where the damage claimed does not exceed $15,000. https://www.leg.state.nv.us/nrs/nrs-004.html
- Las Vegas Justice Court Rule 5.3(b): causes of action needing evidentiary support by an expert witness or expert report are not authorized in small claims. Rule 5.3(a) also excludes NRS 41A professional negligence, HOA actions, punitive damages claims and others. https://www.leg.state.nv.us/courtrules/jcr_lv.html
- Eighth Judicial District Court Clerk filing fee list (already cited in site.json pricing): $270 general civil complaint, $30 each additional plaintiff, $223 defense first appearance, $135 third party complaint, $200 summary judgment motion, $520 complex/construction defect. https://www.clarkcountycourts.us/res/clerk/civil-criminal-library/legal-forms/Filing-Fee-List.pdf

## 10. Fees
- Nev. R. Prof. Conduct 1.5(a): no unreasonable fee or unreasonable expenses; eight listed factors including the fee customarily charged in the locality and whether the fee is fixed or contingent. 1.5(b): scope and basis communicated, preferably in writing. 1.5(c): contingent fee agreement in writing, signed by the client, stating in boldface type at least as large as the largest type used: method of calculation including percentages on settlement, trial or appeal; whether expenses come out of the recovery and before or after the fee; whether the client owes expenses regardless of outcome; that on a loss the client may owe the opposing party's attorney fees and will owe its costs as required by law; and that a suit brought solely to harass or coerce settlement may create malicious prosecution or abuse of process liability. Written outcome statement at the end. 1.5(d): no contingency in a divorce-related matter or for a criminal defendant. https://www.leg.state.nv.us/courtrules/rpc.html
- NRS 7.095: 35 percent net-recovery ceiling applies only to a professional negligence action against a provider of health care. https://www.leg.state.nv.us/nrs/nrs-007.html
