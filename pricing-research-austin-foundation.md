# Pricing research — foundationrepairaustinco.com (Austin, TX / foundation repair)

All figures retrieved 2026-08-23. Every URL below was fetched in this session.

## Anchors

1. & 2. **Texas Section ASCE, Guidelines for the Evaluation and Repair of Residential Foundations, Version 2** (adopted May 1, 2009) — <https://www.texasce.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/05/Guidelines-for-the-Evaluation-and-Repair-of-Residential-Foundations-V2-2008.pdf>
   - Verbatim: "The evaluation and repair design shall be performed by a professional engineer licensed in the State of Texas." The engineer who seals the documents is the engineer of record with approval authority over changes.
   - Deflection: the guidelines adopt the residential code limit **L/360** as the analogous limit for judging a foundation profile, and state that "A floor slope greater than 1 percent is usually noticeable."
   Two anchors are drawn from this one document (different figures, same source URL), which the build permits.
3. **USDA official soil series description, Austin series** — <https://soilseries.sc.egov.usda.gov/OSD_Docs/A/AUSTIN.html>
   Clay content **35 to 55 percent** in the particle-size control section; mapped in the Blackland Prairies (MLRA 86). Deliberately a different source from the Travis County AgriLife page referenced in prose on the Austin **roof** site, so the two Austin sites do not share a soil citation.
4. **City of Austin, types of permits** — <https://www.austintexas.gov/development-services/types-permits>
   Foundation repair appears among residential **Minor Repairs** handled through a Residential Express Building Permit. Deliberately a *different* Austin permit page from the roof site, which cites the **work exempt from building permits** list.

### Divergence discipline (both Austin sites)

- Roof site: TREC inspector standards + Austin's permit-**exemption** list + shingle/flashing/chimney framing + Travis AgriLife soil prose.
- Foundation site: ASCE engineer-of-record and L/360 + Austin's **Minor Repairs / Residential Express** permit + USDA Austin series clay fraction + Balcones escarpment east/west contrast.
No permit sentence, authority, soil source, or local-condition paragraph is shared.

### Arithmetic and basis honesty

- **Mid-size slab row.** Its basis was changed from "8 to 12 piers" to "flat, mid-size slab scope" because the three companies post **totals** for slab repair without stating a pier count; only Austin Expert mentions "sometimes 8–20 units." Cross-check on per-pier math: 8 piers × $900 = $7,200 and 12 × $1,800 = $21,600, i.e. the posted totals ($3,500–$18,000) are not a clean multiple of any single pier count, so no synthetic multiplication was asserted.
- **Full perimeter row.** Level Your House explicitly ties its $20,000–$40,000+ figure to "20 or more piers," which is the stated quantity in the basis. Sanity check: 20 × $900 = $18,000 (row low, matching Austin Expert's posted $18,000+ high range) and 20 × $2,000 helical = $40,000 (row high). The bounds are posted totals; the multiplication is a consistency check only.
- **No fee stacking.** Tri-County Foundation Repair posts a **$1,100** structural engineering inspection that "is credited toward your repair costs" (<https://tcfoundationrepair.com/foundation-inspection-cost/>). Because it is credited, it was **not** stacked on any repair row — and because Tri-County serves Tarrant/Wise/Parker counties, not Austin, it was not used as a source at all. The credited-fee concept appears only as body prose about how to read a proposal. Same page: independent engineer site visit $800–$1,200, independent elevation survey $600–$900 in North Texas — geography mismatch, not used.
- Free inspections (Level Best, DuraPier, Foundation Repair Plus network) were not used to set the evaluation row's low; Round Rock's posted $0–$300 range supplies the $300 low as a paid figure.
