## Strongest verified local facts (all fetched this session)

1. **The gap a left-turning driver actually gets on US 101 south of Salinas.** TAMC measured the gap a
   peak-period left turn across the highway requires at about 7.5 seconds, against an actual average gap in
   northbound traffic of roughly 2.9 seconds, and estimated that closing the 11 median openings between
   Chualar and Salinas would eliminate about 75 percent of serious injury collisions (average 13 injuries per
   year) and 100 percent of fatal collisions (average 1 death per year), an annual safety benefit near
   $16 million — [TAMC board presentation, Feb 26 2025](https://www.tamcmonterey.org/files/ba8558188/SOS+Safety+Proj+Update_TAMC+BOARD+presentation_02262025.pdf).
   Corroborated by Caltrans, which says collisions at some of these at-grade intersections occur at a rate
   higher than the statewide average and that District 5 Traffic Safety initiated the project as a
   cross-median collision countermeasure — [Caltrans 05-1S590 fact sheet](https://dot.ca.gov/-/media/dot-media/district-5/documents/05-1s590/05-1s590_factsheet_a11y.pdf)
   and [corridor engagement page](https://engage.dot.ca.gov/05-0h330) (about 8 miles, 11 at-grade
   intersections, 7 at-grade rail crossings of which 4 unsignalized).

2. **Route 68 is a documented rear-end corridor.** Caltrans states that rear-end collisions are the most
   common type occurring within the project area, that several segments carry collision rates above the
   statewide average for similar facilities, and that daily delays are predicted to nearly triple by 2045;
   the preferred alternative converts nine intersections (post mile 4.8 to 13.7) to roundabouts at
   $189,200,000, construction March 2028 to November 2030 —
   [Caltrans Scenic Route 68 Corridor Improvements](https://dot.ca.gov/caltrans-near-me/district-5/district-5-current-projects/d5-scenic-route-68-improvements).

3. **The rumble-strip numbers Caltrans publishes for a road with no median barrier.** Shoulder rumble strips
   reduce run-off-the-road crashes by 30 to 40 percent; centerline rumble strips reduce cross-over crashes by
   40 to 60 percent. Required on all freeway and expressway shoulders, on all rural facilities with at least
   four feet of clearance from the outside edge of the strip to the outer edge of the paved shoulder where the
   posted speed exceeds 35 mph, and at locations identified by the annual Cross Over Collision Monitoring
   Program or Run off Road Collision Monitoring Program —
   [Caltrans Traffic Safety Bulletin 20-07, Jan 14 2021](https://dot.ca.gov/-/media/dot-media/programs/safety-programs/documents/policy/20-07-rumble-strips-guidelines-and-memo-011421-a11y.pdf).

## Where the brief could not be satisfied

1. **"Any documented emergency-response travel distance for rural collisions."** Not verifiable. Monterey
   County EMS response-time standards were not obtainable from a primary source in this session, so no
   distance or minute figure appears anywhere on the site. Written around rather than invented.
2. **California's uninsured rate specifically.** The IRC's own release gives national figures and names
   high/low states, but the California-specific 20.4 percent figure appears only on the Insurance Information
   Institute's page citing IRC — a secondary source. The Department of Insurance's own California study is
   from 1998 (22.6 percent of vehicles, data as of June 1, 1997) and its county table does not include
   Monterey County. The site therefore uses the national IRC numbers and does not state a California or
   Monterey County uninsured rate.
3. **A documented fatal-collision history on US 101 south of Salinas** beyond TAMC's "average of one death
   per year." CHP's Monterey Area page returned a client error and no per-corridor fatality table was
   reachable, so the site cites the TAMC average only.
4. **Salinas Vision Zero collision counts.** The city Vision Zero page and the TAMC Regional Vision Zero
   Action Plan PDF contain no collision figures, so no Vision Zero number is used.
5. **Civil Code 3045.4** (the hospital-lien 50 percent cap) could not be verified from a fetched page with
   operative text. The pricing page therefore says only that hospital liens run under separate provisions,
   with no percentage attached.
6. **Brief vs site.json:** no conflict found. site.json's city, county, four slugs, neighborhoods and three
   local facts were used exactly as given, including the slug spellings.
