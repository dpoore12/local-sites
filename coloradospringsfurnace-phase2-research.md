# Colorado Springs furnace repair — phase 2 research (verified 2026-08-25)

All facts below were fetched in this session. Any number used in copy.md appears here with its URL.

## Altitude and derating
- Colorado Springs elevation: **6,035 feet**, stated in the City of Colorado Springs budget "Springs at a Glance" profile (also 2024 population estimate 497,331). https://coloradosprings.gov/system/files/2024-03/2024fbudget-00-04-glanceprofile.pdf
- Code rule (NFPA 54 11.1.2 as reproduced in code text): gas input ratings of appliances are used up to 2,000 feet; above 2,000 feet input must be reduced by one of: 4 percent per 1,000 feet above sea level before selecting appliances; as permitted by the AHJ; or per the manufacturer's installation instructions. Related sections: high-altitude vent/chimney sizing uses sea-level input for capacity but derated actual input (803.1.4, IRC G2428.2.5 / 504.2.5), water heaters 510.1.4. https://up.codes/s/high-altitude
- Manufacturer method (Johnson Controls / AJAX high altitude conversion kit manual): above 2,000 feet air density is reduced, derate 4% for each 1,000 feet **above 2,000 feet**; derate by reducing burner orifice size, NOT by lowering manifold pressure, because low air density plus low manifold pressure stops the orifice aspirating primary air. Normal manifold pressure 3.5 in. w.c. (adjust range 3.2–3.8); inlet not over 10.5 in. w.c. Orifice 2.15 mm at 0–2,000 ft → 2.05 mm (part OBN-205H) at 2,000–4,500 ft, one orifice per 20,000 Btu/hr input, tightened to 50 inch-pounds. https://docs.johnsoncontrols.com/ductedsystems/api/khub/documents/VunWlFwosRrP2twOx5drnA/content
- Rheem high altitude kit instructions: elevations above 2,000 ft require 4% derate per thousand feet; "for every thousand feet of elevation the furnace loses 4% of its heating capacity"; **90-plus furnaces installed above 5,000 ft require the pressure switch be exchanged** for the kit switch (high-fire, and low-fire on some models); orifice change may or may not be needed depending on elevation and gas heating value; factory orifices sized on 1,075 Btu/ft3 sea-level heating value (#50 natural gas). Example table: at 1,050 Btu/ft3, #50 at sea level → #51 at 5,000 ft → #52 at 8,000 ft. http://pts.myrheem.com/docstore/webdocs/ServiceDocs/histlib/pdfs/Accessories/RXGY/92-24096-06-04_RXGY-F18-F42_High_Alt_Kit.pdf
- Goodman HASFK-2 high altitude kit: above 7,000 ft a derate must be followed because induced-draft CFM stays nearly constant while pounds of oxygen fall; if input is not reduced, combustion can be inefficient or incomplete and can "cause premature heat exchanger failure due to excessive temperature rise"; do not derate by lowering manifold pressure below nameplate; natural gas orifice #45 (0–7,000 ft) → #49 above 7,000 ft, with a -.45/-.85 pressure switch. https://hvacdirect.com/media/pdf/Goodman_HASFK_2_Manual.pdf
- Derived arithmetic (my own, from the cited rules): 4%/1,000 ft from sea level at 6,035 ft ≈ 24% reduction, so an 80,000 Btu/h nameplate ≈ 61,000 Btu/h. The manufacturer form (4% per 1,000 ft above 2,000 ft) gives ≈ 16%, about 67,000 Btu/h. Same furnace, two legitimate methods, different answers.

## Code and permits (Pikes Peak Regional Building Department)
- Current code: **2023 Pikes Peak Regional Building Code, 2nd Printing, effective June 30, 2026**, adopting by reference 2021 IBC, **2021 IRC, 2021 IMC, 2021 IFGC**, 2021 IPC, **2023 NEC**, 2021 IECC, Colorado Model Electric Ready and Solar Ready Code, 2021 IEBC, 2019 ASME A17.1, 2021 ISPSC. City of Colorado Springs adopted it by **Ordinance 23-16** (council March 28, 2023; effective April 10, 2023; implementation June 30, 2023); El Paso County by Resolution 23-158 (May 2, 2023). https://www.pprbd.org/Information/Codechanges
- PPRBD serves: unincorporated El Paso County, Colorado Springs, Fountain, Manitou Springs, Green Mountain Falls, Monument, Palmer Lake, and Woodland Park in Teller County. It does not serve Ramah, Calhan, or other parts of Teller County. **Furnace** is on the list of projects whose construction, installation and replacement require a permit (with water heater, boiler, fireplace or stove, air conditioning system, basement finish, etc.). Homeowner permits only for a residence the homeowner owns AND lives in — not a rental, not when a contractor does the work; the homeowner is then responsible for knowing the codes and passing inspections. A hired contractor must be licensed and registered with PPRBD and in good standing. https://www.pprbd.org/Information/HomeownerPermit
- PPRBD "Top 5 Most Common Residential Mechanical Mistakes": (1) replacement water heater vent left as cell-core PVC instead of solid-core; (2) **mechanical room lacks combustion air** — can lead to venting and carbon monoxide issues, multiple ways to bring it in and it is the contractor's choice; (3) **no manufacturer installation instructions left on site** for inspectors; (4) no contrasting-color primer at PVC vent joints; (5) **missing CO alarms — any new or replacement mechanical installation requires a working CO detector on any level with a bedroom**. https://www.pprbd.org/File/Resources/Downloads/ResidentialHandout/Residential%20Mechanical%20Mistakes.pdf
- PPRBD Equipment Change Form (Residential), 2021 IRC / 2023 PPRBC: used when installed heating or cooling equipment differs from the approved duct design or equipment certificate; requires make, model and efficiency for the furnace, and certification against 2021 IRC R303.10 (required heating) and 2021 IECC R403.7 (equipment sizing and efficiency rating). The "more efficient HVAC equipment" energy option requires >= 95 AFUE gas furnace with 16 SEER AC, or >= 10 HSPF/16 SEER air-source heat pump, or >= 3.5 COP ground source (2021 IECC R408.2.2). https://www.pprbd.org/File/Resources/Downloads/ResidentialHandout/Equipment%20Change%20Form%202021%20IRC%20fillable.pdf
- PPRBD Basic Design Information: ground snow load 43 psf below 7,000 feet and 57 psf at or above 7,000 feet (flat roof 30/40 psf); basic wind speed 130 mph Vult for Risk Category I/II, 135 mph III, 140 mph IV; **Exposure C required**; Ss 18.5%, S1 5.9%. https://www.pprbd.org/File/ByAlias/DesignCriteria
- Fee schedule (adopted under RBC108.2 of the 2023 PPRBC): furnace or boiler replacement $50; water heater, furnace or boiler replacement including vent $75; furnace/boiler over 400,000 Btu or chiller $100; trade permit attached to a one/two-family alteration $50 per trade. https://www.pprbd.org/Information/FeeSchedule

## Combustion air, tight houses, depressurization
- DOE "Combustion Safety in the Codes": indoor combustion air where infiltration rate is unknown = **50 cubic feet of indoor volume per 1,000 Btu/h of appliance input** (historical basis 0.5 ACH); where indoor air is insufficient the code requires outdoor air, with openings sized on appliance input, number of openings, and whether they are direct or ducted; mechanical combustion air must supply at least **0.35 cfm per 1,000 Btu/h** above all ventilation air and be interlocked; placing equipment in operation includes "adjust input (on rate and derate for high altitude)", adjust primary air, check safety shutoffs, ignition devices, limit controls and draft. Worst-case test setup closes doors and windows and runs dryer and exhaust fans at maximum. https://www.energy.gov/sites/prod/files/2013/12/f6/combustion_safety_codes.pdf
- PNNL Building America Solution Center, rooms containing fuel-burning appliances: code air-leakage limits are 3 ACH50 in climate zones 3–8; a 2,000 sq ft home with 9-foot ceilings (18,000 cubic feet) tested at 900 cfm with a single **300 cfm exhaust fan** can be depressurized to the point that no natural-draft gas appliance could safely draft; combustion-air ducts require R-8; appliance must be outside the thermal envelope or in a sealed, insulated room with a weather-stripped door that stays closed; Appendix RA adds an optional Combustion Appliance Zone (CAZ) test. https://basc.pnnl.gov/code-compliance/rooms-containing-fuel-burning-appliances-code-compliance-brief
- El Paso County is IECC/Building America climate zone **5B, "Cold"**. https://www.energy.gov/sites/prod/files/2015/10/f27/ba_climate_region_guide_7.3.pdf

## Radon
- EPA Map of Radon Zones, Colorado: **El Paso County is Zone 1**, the highest potential, meaning a predicted average indoor screening level greater than 4 pCi/L; zone 2 is 2–4 pCi/L; EPA says fix at 4 pCi/L or higher and consider fixing between 2 and 4; map built in 1993 from indoor measurements, geology, aerial radioactivity, soil parameters and foundation types, and is not a substitute for testing a house. https://www.epa.gov/sites/default/files/2014-08/documents/colorado.pdf
- CDPHE: about half of Colorado homes exceed 4 pCi/L; radon comes from uranium breakdown in soil; a house is usually warmer and at lower pressure than the surrounding soil, so soil gas moves in through the space between basement wall and slab, foundation cracks, sump and drain openings, construction joints, plumbing penetrations and crawl spaces; age and type of home do not determine radon levels. https://cdphe.colorado.gov/hm/understanding-radon

## Utility
- Colorado Springs Utilities is a community-owned four-service municipal utility: electricity, natural gas, water, wastewater. https://www.csu.org/about/
- Gas leak guidance: leave immediately, do not stop to open a window, call 911 or (719) 448-4800, do not create a spark (light switches, garage door openers, flashlights, vehicles), stay off carpet to avoid static, a meter still moving with all appliances off indicates a leak; the utility responds to indoor and outdoor gas odor complaints at no charge. https://www.csu.org/safety/natural-gas
- CO guidance: a yellow flame means the appliance is not working correctly and could be producing CO; a healthy flame should be about **90% blue**; leave and call 911 or (719) 448-4800; alarms on every floor, especially near sleeping areas, meeting UL 2034; annual professional inspection of vents for corrosion and blockage; **after hailstorms have a contractor check the vent cap** because a damaged cap can make appliances vent improperly and raise indoor CO. https://www.csu.org/safety/carbon-monoxide
