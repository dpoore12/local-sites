## Bottom line on California

**California caps no contingency percentage in ordinary injury work.** Rule 1.5(a) of
the Rules of Professional Conduct bars an *unconscionable or illegal* fee and rule
1.5(b) lists thirteen factors judged on the facts existing when the agreement was
made. Business & Professions Code § 6147(a)(4) goes further and requires the contract
itself to state that the fee **"is not set by law but is negotiable."** The 25%/33%
schedule in § 6146 applies **only to claims against a health care provider** for
professional negligence.

## Caps found that do NOT apply to a personal injury fee

- **Bus. & Prof. Code § 6146(a), 25%/33%** — medical negligence against a health care
  provider only. Labeled that way in the fee table and in the body.
- **Rule 1.5(c)(2)** — the contingent-fee prohibition for a criminal defendant is
  subdivision **(c)(2)**, not (d). Confirmed on the State Bar's own rule page.
- **Lab. Code § 4906(b)** — appeals-board approval belongs to workers' compensation;
  § 6147(c) expressly excludes comp contracts from the contingency-contract statute.
- **Civ. Code § 3045.4, 50%** — a ceiling on what a hospital lien can pull out of the
  injured person's money, not a limit on a lawyer's fee.
