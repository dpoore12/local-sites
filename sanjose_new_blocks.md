## services_summary

Four jobs hide inside the phrase San Jose DUI lawyer, and each keeps its own clock. A first misdemeanor charge is filed by the county prosecutor's misdemeanor unit and answered at the Hall of Justice on West Hedding Street. The license case belongs to the Department of Motor Vehicles, argued through its Driver Safety Office on Great Oaks Boulevard, on a window measured in days. A breath or blood reading is really a records fight governed by state testing rules. Injury or a qualifying prior moves the same night onto the felony calendar, with a heavier bail listing. Confusing one track for another is how people lose driving privileges they could have kept.

## svc_first_offense_dui_defense_lede

Two buildings on West Hedding Street shape a first San Jose DUI: the prosecutor's office at number 70, and the Hall of Justice at number 190 where the charge is answered. A third deadline is already running at the Department of Motor Vehicles.

## svc_first_offense_dui_defense_body

### Two accusations that share one name

Vehicle Code section 23152 holds two counts rather than one. Subdivision (a) makes it unlawful for a person under the influence of any alcoholic beverage to drive a vehicle, and subdivision (b) turns instead on a measured concentration of 0.08 percent or more by weight. The statute defines percent by weight as grams of alcohol per 100 milliliters of blood, or per 210 liters of breath, and it hands the prosecution a rebuttable presumption when a chemical test taken within three hours after driving reaches that figure. Two counts mean two proof problems. A file with no number still has the impairment theory behind it, and a file with a number still depends on whether the reading survives the rules that let it in.

### The office that files it, the building that hears it

Non-felony charges out of this city are handled by the District Attorney's central misdemeanor unit at 70 West Hedding Street. The office describes that unit as covering the San Jose facility of the Superior Court for San Jose, Santa Clara, Campbell, Milpitas and Los Gatos, with driving under the influence named among its routine offenses. The answer happens a block away. The court's own records page assigns the Hall of Justice at 190 West Hedding Street the criminal matters arising in San Jose, Milpitas, Los Gatos, Saratoga, Monte Sereno and Santa Clara. Morgan Hill and Palo Alto take the south county and the northwest cities. A Downtown or Japantown arrest lands downtown; that is worth confirming against your own notice before anyone drives anywhere.

### The hour on the calendar can turn on your last name

The criminal division has published arraignment times that split the out-of-custody misdemeanor calendar alphabetically, with last names A through M at 9:00 a.m. and N through Z at 1:30 p.m. on weekdays, and out-of-custody felony arraignments at 1:35 p.m. Those postings get revised, so the date and hour printed on your release paperwork is the one that controls. What does not change is that the slot comes from the court's calendar rather than from anyone's convenience. Missing it produces a bench warrant, and the court's warrant policy now lets any person with an active bench warrant put the case back on calendar and appear out of custody, with the warrant staying active until that appearance happens.

### Filing is a judgment call, not a rubber stamp

A county management audit of the District Attorney's Office lists what a deputy weighs before issuing a case. The questions are whether a public offense was committed, whether the person who committed it is identified, whether it can be proved beyond a reasonable doubt, what equitable considerations apply, and whether prosecution is warranted. The same audit reports that in calendar year 2022 the office reviewed 18,161 cases, issued 9,304, and rejected 51.2 percent of them. Volume is real as well. The office states it filed 3,770 DUI cases in 2023. Anything a defense wants that decision-maker to see has to arrive while the questions are still open.

### The program clock can start before sentencing

The court-published county DUI program brochure warns that failing to enroll within 30 days of your court appearance, and failing to complete the program, may bring a warrant, added penalties and consequences for driving privileges. First-offender enrollment comes in three shapes: a three-month program of 30 hours, a six-month program of 44 hours, or a nine-month program of 60 hours, each mixing education hours with group counseling. A reckless driving conviction involving a measurable amount of alcohol carries a 12-hour class instead. Providers notify the court and the department within 10 days of completion, and the brochure states that no one is denied services for a documented inability to pay.

### The mistake that costs the most driving time

Treating the number as the verdict and the pink form as a reminder. The department is explicit that reducing a DUI charge to reckless driving in criminal court does not affect the suspension of the driving privilege, because the administrative action stands on its own record. Meanwhile the criminal side has deadlines that favor a prepared defense: the court's misdemeanor page cites a right to trial within 30 days of arraignment or plea for someone in custody, and 45 days for someone out of custody. Both tracks reward early document collection, and neither waits for the other.

### How a defense fee has to be built here

California puts the rule in rule 1.5 of the Rules of Professional Conduct, and subdivision (c)(2) removes any contingent arrangement when the client is a defendant in a criminal matter. What remains is a flat sum tied to described work, or an hourly rate drawn against an advance, in writing. Read the scope rather than the total. Ask whether the administrative hearing is inside it, who covers records subpoenas and any independent analysis, and where the engagement stops if the case is set for trial.

## svc_dui_license_suspension_lede

The pink slip in your pocket is not a court reminder. It is the opening page of a separate licensing case with its own hearing office in south San Jose, its own request deadline, and its own way of giving driving back afterward.

## svc_dui_license_suspension_body

### What the administrative case is actually about

The Department of Motor Vehicles describes its post-arrest action as an immediate administrative step against the driving privilege alone, imposed independently of any penalty the criminal court later orders. That means the question at a hearing is the driving privilege and the circumstances of the arrest, not whether the state can prove a crime. The consequences of ignoring it are ordinary and permanent: a suspension runs, and the reasons it might not have been supported never get examined. The department also gives a firm window, stating that a hearing must be requested within 10 days of receiving the order of suspension or revocation.

### The office that hears it sits at the far south end of the city

Hearings for this area run through the department's San Jose Driver Safety Office at 90 Great Oaks Blvd., Suite 104. The department's directions describe the middle building of a three-building complex, with the parking entrance on the south side of Great Oaks Boulevard between San Ignacio Avenue and Via Del Oro. Published hours are 8:00 a.m. to 5:00 p.m. on Monday, Tuesday, Thursday and Friday, with a 9:00 a.m. opening on Wednesday, and the office can be reached at (833) 543-7703. Two practical notes follow. Driver Safety Offices do not handle license or registration transactions, so the counter errand belongs somewhere else. The department also offers online case management for receiving notices, scheduling a hearing and uploading documents, which matters when the drive from Berryessa or Alum Rock to Great Oaks Boulevard crosses the whole city.

### Refusal carries its own ladder of consequences

Vehicle Code section 23612 treats a driver lawfully arrested for driving under the influence as having consented to chemical testing of blood or breath, and requires the officer to warn what refusing does. The statute's admonition describes a fine and mandatory imprisonment on conviction, plus an administrative suspension of one year. Revocation lengthens to two years where the refusal follows a separate qualifying conviction within ten years, including a violation of Section 23103 as specified in Section 23103.5, the reduced reckless driving charge. Two or more of those priors puts the revocation at three years. A recorded refusal is a factual allegation about what was said and offered, and it deserves the same record work as a reading.

### What the criminal case does and does not fix

Very little of what happens in court reaches back into the licensing file. The department states that a charge reduced to reckless driving leaves the suspension in place, while an acquittal on the DUI charge can reverse the administrative action once the department determines the court decision amounts to one. Sequence therefore matters more than optimism. The hearing request goes in on its own schedule, and any hope that a negotiated plea will quietly clear the record belongs at the bottom of the plan rather than the top.

### Getting back on the road usually starts with enrollment

The county program brochure notes that first offenders may bring the administrative per se form, generally the pink temporary license handed over at arrest, and enroll before their court appearance. That single step often unlocks the paperwork the department wants. For a first offense the department describes several routes: an interlock restriction for up to six months, or a restriction permitting travel to, from and during employment and to and from the program for one year, alongside whatever the court orders on its own.

### Then the interlock decides how long the limits last

The department administers the statewide ignition interlock program under Vehicle Code section 23575.3 for violations from the first day of 2019 through the end of 2032. Its published terms scale with history. The listed periods run one year where a first offense involved injury, one or two years with a single prior, two or three years with two priors, three years at three or more, and three or four years where a prior felony conviction is involved. A court may order a device for up to six months on a first offense without injury, and a minimum of three months on a wet reckless. Participants may install and drive rather than sit out the suspension, once other suspensions are cleared and the installation verification, program certificate, insurance proof and applicable fees are on file.

### Fees on the licensing side

A criminal defense engagement cannot be built on a share of any outcome, because rule 1.5(c)(2) of California's Rules of Professional Conduct forbids it for a defendant in a criminal case. The useful questions are about coverage. Ask whether the hearing, a subpoena for the arresting officer, a continuance request and a later restriction application are all inside the described work, or priced separately.

## svc_dui_breath_test_defense_lede

A reading arrives looking like a fact and behaves like a conclusion built from paperwork: a fifteen-minute watch, two samples that have to agree, dated accuracy checks, and a sample kept for a year. This page is about that paperwork.

## svc_dui_breath_test_defense_body

### The consent the statute treats you as having given

Vehicle Code section 23612 deems a person who drives a motor vehicle to have consented to chemical testing of blood or breath, once lawfully arrested for an offense allegedly committed while driving under the influence. Two limits sit inside that language. The testing must be incidental to a lawful arrest and administered at the direction of a peace officer with reasonable cause, so the stop and the arrest remain live issues even where a sample exists. The driver also chooses between blood and breath, must be advised of the choice, and takes the other test if incapable of completing the chosen one. Where drugs are suspected, the statute allows a breath test followed by a blood test, with the officer stating the reasons in the report.

### What Title 17 requires before a number counts

California's regulations govern collection, and they are specific enough to check. Title 17 of the Code of Regulations, section 1221.1, permits a breath sample only after fifteen continuous minutes during which the subject has not ingested alcoholic beverages or other fluids, regurgitated, vomited, eaten or smoked. Section 1221.2 requires two separate samples whose results do not differ by more than 0.02 grams per 210 liters. It also requires accuracy checks agreeing within plus or minus 0.010 grams percent of a reference sample, applied across the range from 0.08 to 0.30 grams percent. The required periodic interval is defined as not exceeding 10 days or 150 subjects, whichever comes sooner. Records are kept for each instrument, and a precautionary checklist is to be available at every instrument location. Each of those items is a document that either exists or does not.

### The county laboratory behind the report

The District Attorney's crime laboratory describes itself as an internationally accredited forensic laboratory serving all criminal justice agencies in the county, which means most local blood analysis passes through one facility rather than several. That facility is also being rebuilt around newer instruments. The office announced a grant of $410,000 from the California Office of Traffic Safety, funded through the National Highway Traffic Safety Administration, to improve forensic blood alcohol testing, with the program period running to the end of September 2025. Instrument changes, method validations and training records are all discoverable, and they are the sort of material a defense asks for by name.

### Blood leaves a different trail than breath

Section 1219.1 of the same regulations directs that blood be collected by venipuncture as soon as feasible, that no alcohol or other volatile organic disinfectant touch the skin, and that the sample be mixed with an anticoagulant and a preservative. It also requires that whatever remains of the sample be kept for one year after collection, and that a portion be provided to the defendant on request along with the identifying information. That retention rule is the practical difference between arguing about a number and testing it. Urine, where it is taken, is collected no sooner than twenty minutes after first voiding under section 1219.2 and is likewise retained for a year.

### The three-hour presumption cuts in both directions

The presumption in section 23152 attaches to a chemical test taken within three hours after driving, and the statute calls it rebuttable. Time is therefore part of the evidence rather than a footnote to it. The interval between driving and testing, what was consumed and when, and the sequence of a preliminary screening device followed by an evidential instrument all change what a single figure can honestly say about the moment the car was moving.

### What to write down while it is still exact

Note the agency, the report or case number, the instrument or laboratory identifier, and every printed time on the paperwork. Then record the ordinary details of the evening: the last food and drink, any dental appliance or mouth injury, any medical condition or medication, and whether anyone was present before or after the stop. Do not annotate the officer's forms, and do not post an account online. Preserve the documents and let counsel decide which details carry weight.

### Where the money goes in a records case

Rule 1.5(c)(2) of the Rules of Professional Conduct keeps criminal defense fees off any contingent basis, so this work is quoted as described tasks or hourly effort. What varies most is the record work. Ask what the engagement includes for subpoenas to the arresting agency and the laboratory, for maintenance and accuracy-check logs, and for independent analysis of a retained sample if the timeline makes that worthwhile.

## svc_felony_dui_defense_lede

Injury, a third qualifying prior inside ten years, or an earlier felony conviction moves a San Jose DUI to the felony calendar. The bail listing multiplies, the deadlines shorten, and the evidence to be collected stops being a single printout.

## svc_felony_dui_defense_body

### What lifts a DUI out of the misdemeanor column

Three fact patterns do most of the work. Causing injury to another person while driving under the influence is charged under Vehicle Code section 23153 and may be filed as a felony on its own. Enough qualifying prior convictions inside a ten-year window can elevate a plain DUI count, and the county's bail schedule lists that combination with section 23550. A prior felony conviction of the same kind supports the enhancement in section 23550.5. None of those depends on how the night felt, and all three depend on records rather than recollection, which is why verified documents matter before anyone talks about a resolution.

### The bail schedule changes shape immediately

Santa Clara County's criminal bail schedule prices the difference plainly. A DUI with no separate convictions is listed at $5,000 where the alleged concentration is under 0.15, and $10,000 at 0.15 or greater. An injury count under section 23153 with no priors is listed at $25,000, rising to $50,000 with one qualifying prior. The felony combinations built on section 23550 or section 23550.5 are listed at $100,000. The schedule also carries a direction worth knowing about: in any DUI case, including those alleging prior convictions, defendants are to be eligible for supervised own recognizance release, with pretrial services advised to consider terms such as alcohol monitoring or testing. Custody status is therefore arguable rather than fixed by the listed figure.

### A felony calendar runs on shorter deadlines

The court's felony page sets out the path: arraignment, then a preliminary hearing where a judge decides whether the evidence supports holding the person to answer, then trial. An information must be filed within 15 days of that holding order, and the statutory clock allows 60 days from arraignment on the information to trial. Out-of-custody felony arraignments have been calendared at 1:35 p.m. at the Hall of Justice. Where charges are filed and a warrant issues, the court's protocol lets counsel schedule an out-of-custody arraignment after an informal booking, set for the second or fourth Monday of a month in the felony arraignment department at 190 W. Hedding Street at 1:46 p.m., with all charges eligible.

### Priors have to be proved from paper

An alleged prior is an element to be established, not a note in a file. The relevant material includes certified court records of the earlier disposition, the terms actually imposed, and the department's driver history, because a date that falls outside the window or a disposition that is not what everyone assumed changes the exposure. The refusal provisions in section 23612 show how wide the net is drawn: a violation of Section 23103 as specified in Section 23103.5, the reduced reckless charge, counts as a qualifying prior for the longer revocation. An old plea that felt minor at the time can be doing real work in the current case.

### A collision expands the record enormously

When a crash is involved, the evidence stops being one instrument printout. San Jose police assign fatal and serious collisions to a traffic investigations unit, and its accounts of felony impaired-driving arrests describe suspects booked into the county main jail. What follows is scene work: dispatch audio, body-worn video, vehicle inspections, medical records, roadway measurements and witness accounts. State traffic-safety figures show why this city generates many such files. The Office of Traffic Safety recorded 3,294 people killed or injured in San Jose crashes in 2023, with 377 of those victims in alcohol-involved crashes, 406 fatal and injury crashes in the 9:00 p.m. to 2:59 a.m. window, and 679 DUI arrests, third among the fifteen California cities of comparable size.

### What the first week should accomplish

Follow every written release condition to the letter, including any monitoring or testing term ordered as part of release. Leave the vehicle untouched and available for inspection, keep medical treatment documented, and say nothing about the incident to anyone but counsel. Preservation requests to the arresting agency and to any nearby business with cameras are time-sensitive, and video from a corner on Alum Rock Avenue or Monterey Road is often gone within weeks. Nothing in the county's collaborative court and diversion programs is promised by a charge label, so eligibility questions belong in front of the court on the actual filing rather than in a hopeful assumption.

### Fees when the exposure is a felony

Contingent arrangements are unavailable in criminal defense under rule 1.5(c)(2) of California's Rules of Professional Conduct, and a felony DUI is priced by the work it takes. That work usually includes a preliminary hearing, records and expert review, and possibly a motion practice phase. Ask where the described engagement ends, whether the preliminary hearing and any prior-conviction challenge are inside it, and how an expert would be retained if reconstruction or laboratory review becomes necessary.
