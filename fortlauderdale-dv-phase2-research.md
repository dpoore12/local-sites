## Florida statutes (flsenate.gov, 2025 Florida Statutes)
- **741.28** definitions. "Domestic violence" = assault, aggravated assault, battery, aggravated battery, sexual assault, sexual battery, stalking, aggravated stalking, kidnapping, false imprisonment, or any criminal offense resulting in physical injury or death of one family or household member by another. "Family or household member" = spouses, former spouses, persons related by blood or marriage, persons presently residing together as if a family or who have resided together in the past as if a family, and parents of a child in common regardless of marriage. **Except for parents of a child in common, the members must be currently residing or have in the past resided together in the same single dwelling unit.** https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2025/741.28
- **741.2901(2)** each state attorney adopts a pro-prosecution policy; "the filing, nonfiling, or diversion of criminal charges … shall be determined by these specialized prosecutors over the objection of the victim, if necessary." **741.2901(3)** SAO must investigate defendant history before first appearance; "When a defendant is arrested for an act of domestic violence, the defendant shall be held in custody until brought before the court for admittance to bail in accordance with chapter 903"; court shall consider victim safety in determining bail. https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2025/741.2901
- **741.2902(1)** legislative intent: at first appearance the court shall consider victim safety and exercise caution in releasing defendants. (2)(b) parties cannot amend an injunction verbally, in writing, or by invitation to the residence. https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2025/741.2902
- **741.281** finding of guilt, withheld adjudication, or nolo plea to a crime of domestic violence → minimum 1 year probation + court shall order attendance and completion of a batterers' intervention program as a condition; court may decline the program condition only by stating on the record why it might be inappropriate, or if the person does not qualify under 741.325. https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2025/741.281
- **741.283** if **adjudicated guilty** of a crime of domestic violence **and** the person intentionally caused bodily harm: minimum 10 days county jail first offense, 15 second, 20 third or subsequent; 15/20/30 if it took place in the presence of a child under 16 who is a family or household member. https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2025/741.283
- **741.325** batterers' intervention program: **at least 29 weeks, including 24 weekly sessions plus intake, assessment and orientation**; funded by user fees paid by the batterers who attend. (BRIEF SAID 26 WEEKS — WRONG.) https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2025/741.325
- **741.30(2)(a)** assessment of a filing fee for a petition for protection against domestic violence is prohibited; clerk reimbursed $40 per petition issued by the Justice Administrative Commission; serving agency fee may not exceed $20. **(1)(f)** cause of action does not require either party to be represented by an attorney. **(3)(a)** verified petition must allege specific facts. **(5)(a)** ex parte temporary injunction on immediate and present danger; **(5)(c)** effective for a fixed period not to exceed 15 days, full hearing set no later than the date the temporary injunction ceases; continuance for good cause including to obtain service, injunction extended through continuance. **(6)(a)** relief: no acts of violence; exclusive use and possession of shared dwelling; temporary parenting plan up to 100 percent of time-sharing; neutral safe exchange location; temporary support; **treatment, intervention or counseling paid for by the respondent**; referral of petitioner to certified DV center; exclusive control of an animal; other relief. **(6)(c)** terms remain in effect until modified or dissolved; either party may move at any time. **(6)(g)** final judgment must state on its face that it is a violation of 790.233 and a first-degree misdemeanor to have any firearm or ammunition in care, custody, possession or control. **(6)(h)** all proceedings under the subsection are recorded. **(7)** advocate may be present with petitioner or respondent on request. **(8)(a)1** within 24 hours after issuance the clerk electronically transmits the papers to the sheriff for service on the respondent, any day, any hour. **(9)(a)** violation enforceable by civil or criminal contempt or prosecuted by the state attorney under 741.31. https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2025/741.30
- **741.31(4)(a)** willful violation of a 741.30 injunction: refusing to vacate the shared dwelling; going to or being within 500 feet of the petitioner's residence, school, place of employment or a place frequented regularly; committing an act of domestic violence; other violation by intentional unlawful threat, word or act; telephoning, contacting or communicating directly or indirectly unless indirect contact through a third party is specifically allowed; knowingly and intentionally coming within 100 feet of the petitioner's motor vehicle, occupied or not; defacing or destroying personal property including the vehicle; refusing to surrender firearms or ammunition if ordered — first-degree misdemeanor. **(4)(b)1** possession of a firearm or ammunition in violation of a final injunction is a 790.233 violation, first-degree misdemeanor. **(4)(c)** two or more prior convictions for violating an injunction + a later violation against the same victim = third-degree felony; "conviction" includes a plea regardless of withheld adjudication. **(1)-(2)** clerk assists with affidavit or directs petitioner to circuit central intake; law enforcement completes investigation within 20 days; **state attorney determines within 30 working days** whether to file charges, seek an order to show cause for criminal contempt, both, or note continued investigation. https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2025/741.31
- **790.233** a person under a final injunction against acts of domestic violence may not have any firearm or ammunition in care, custody, possession or control; first-degree misdemeanor. https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2025/790.233
- **903.011(5)-(6)** Supreme Court adopts an annual uniform statewide bond schedule for offenses **not** in (6); (6) "A person may not be released before his or her first appearance hearing or bail determination" for listed criteria, including (c) arrest for violating a protective injunction and (g)3 **domestic violence as defined in 741.28**, felony battery, domestic battery by strangulation, stalking; (f) three or more arrests in the preceding 6 months. Schedule does not bind the judge at first appearance. https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2025/903.011
- **903.047(1)(b)** if the court issues an order of no contact, it is effective immediately, enforceable for the duration of pretrial release until modified, and the defendant must be informed in writing before release. "No contact" includes: communicating orally or in any written form, in person, telephonically, electronically or otherwise, directly or indirectly through a third person; physical or violent contact with the person or the property; being within 500 feet of the residence **even if the defendant and the victim share the residence**; being within 500 feet of the vehicle, place of employment or a specified place frequented regularly. Court may designate a third person to facilitate contact with children in common at the defendant's request. (2) modification on motion for good cause; victim permitted to be heard. https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2025/903.047
- **938.08** surcharge of $201 on an offense of domestic violence described in 741.28; $85 to the Domestic Violence Trust Fund; $1 retained by the clerk; payment is a condition of probation, community control or other court-ordered supervision. https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2025/938.08
- **948.09** misdemeanor probation through a county court: contribution of **not less than $40 per month** to the entity providing supervision, amount set by the sentencing court; electronic monitoring billed separately; nonpayment can be grounds for revocation. https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2025/948.09
- **943.0584(2)(f)** a **conviction** for assault or battery of one family or household member by another as defined in 741.28(3) is ineligible for a certificate of eligibility or court-ordered sealing (943.059) or expunction (943.0585). "Conviction" there = determination of guilt at trial or a plea of guilty or nolo contendere **regardless of whether adjudication is withheld**. https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2025/943.0584
- **943.0585(1)** expunction available where no charging document was filed, or where the case was dismissed or nolle prossed by the state attorney, dismissed by the court, or ended in acquittal or a not-guilty verdict; certificate of eligibility from FDLE required, **$75 processing fee**, valid 12 months; the 10-year prior-sealing requirement does not apply where no plea was entered or all charges were dismissed before trial. https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2025/943.0585
- **943.059** sealing: certificate of eligibility, $75 FDLE processing fee, never adjudicated guilty of a criminal offense in Florida, no adjudication for the acts in the petition, no longer under court supervision, no prior seal/expunction; sealing may be denied at the court's sole discretion. https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2025/943.059
- **741.29** law enforcement notice of rights and remedies to victims; police report copy to the nearest certified DV center within 24 hours. https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2025/741.29

## Brief corrections
1. Brief says the batterers' intervention program is **26 weeks**. Florida Statutes 741.325 requires **at least 29 weeks, including 24 weekly sessions** plus intake, assessment and orientation. Wrote 29 weeks.
2. Brief says "adjudication may not be withheld for a person who is adjudicated guilty" under 741.281/741.283. No such provision was found. What the statutes actually say: 741.281 applies equally to a finding of guilt, a withheld adjudication, and a nolo plea; 741.283's mandatory jail minimums apply only where the person **is adjudicated guilty** and intentionally caused bodily harm. Wrote it that way.
3. Brief attributes the no-bond hold to 741.2901 "and the Rules of Criminal Procedure." 741.2901(3) does require custody until the person is brought before the court for admittance to bail, but the operative no-bond rule is **903.011(6)(g)3** plus the circuit's uniform bond schedule order (AO 2025-50-Crim), with Rule 3.130(a) supplying only the 24-hour deadline. Wrote it that way.
4. Brief calls sealing the question for a dismissed charge. For a dismissal or nolle prosequi the route is **expunction under 943.0585**; sealing under 943.059 is the withheld-adjudication route, and 943.0584(2)(f) bars both once there is a conviction, including a plea with adjudication withheld.
