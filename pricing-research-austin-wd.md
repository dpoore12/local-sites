Mode: `fees` / `fee_kind: contingency`. All sources retrieved **2026-08-23**.

## Bottom line on caps

**Texas sets no percentage cap on a contingency fee in a wrongful death case.** Neither the Texas Disciplinary Rules
of Professional Conduct nor Civil Practice and Remedies Code chapter 71 states a maximum. The page says so plainly.
Texas numbers its fee rule **1.04**, not 1.5 — verified directly against the rule text.

## Rules cited on the page

Source for all Rule 1.04 text: https://www.legalethicstexas.com/resources/rules/texas-disciplinary-rules-of-professional-conduct/fees/

| Subdivision | What it says |
|---|---|
| 1.04(a) | No illegal fee and no unconscionable fee. A fee is unconscionable if a competent lawyer could not form a reasonable belief that it is reasonable. **No number appears in the rule.** |
| 1.04(b) | Eight factors: time and labor / novelty and difficulty / skill required; likelihood the engagement precludes other employment; fee customarily charged in the locality for similar legal services; amount involved and results obtained; time limitations imposed by the client or the circumstances; nature and length of the professional relationship; experience, reputation and ability of the lawyer; whether the fee is fixed or contingent. |
| 1.04(c) | Basis or rate of the fee communicated to the client, preferably in writing, before or within a reasonable time after commencing representation. |
| 1.04(d) | Contingent fee must be in writing and signed by the client; must state the method of determination including **separate percentages for settlement, trial and appeal**; must state the litigation and other expenses deducted from the recovery and **whether they are deducted before or after the contingent percentage is calculated**; at conclusion, a written statement of outcome plus the remittance and how it was determined. |
| 1.04(e) | No contingent fee for defending a criminal case. (Researched; trimmed from the final page for word count.) |
| 1.04(f) | Division with a lawyer outside the firm only in proportion to services performed or where each assumes joint responsibility; client consent in writing to the identity of every lawyer/firm, the basis of the division, and the share each receives; total fee may not be increased. |

## A minor's share — Property Code chapter 142

Source: https://statutes.capitol.texas.gov/Docs/PR/htm/PR.142.htm

- **142.001** — next friend or guardian ad litem; court may, on application and after a hearing, decree how the funds
  are invested.
- **142.002** — bonded manager may be appointed.
- **142.004** — permitted investments: qualified tuition programs (529 / prepaid tuition), ABLE accounts, and
  federally insured interest-bearing time deposits; the clerk on written order may also use treasury bills, an
  interlocal investment pool, or a money market fund with a stable $1 NAV and a weighted average maturity of 90 days
  or less.
- **142.005** — court-created trust; (b)(1)-(7) mandatory terms, including that a minor's trust terminates at the
  minor's death, at a stated age, or at the **25th birthday**, whichever occurs first. **(l) caps the petitioner's
  reasonable attorney's fees for creating the trust at $1,000.** (m) applies where principal is $50,000 or less;
  (n) where it exceeds $50,000 (trustee eligibility).
- Estates Code 1301.052-.054 (management trusts): https://statutes.capitol.texas.gov/Docs/ES/htm/ES.1301.htm

## Divergence from Dallas

Austin and Dallas were built from different structures. Austin runs rule-first (1.04(a) → (b) eight factors → (c)/(d)
paper requirements → 71.004 standing and the three-month executor deadline → 71.010 share division → chapter 142
minor's trust → Travis County clerks). Dallas runs courthouse-first (two clerks → 1.04(a)/(b) → (c)/(d) disclosure →
1.04(f) fee division between firms → 71.004/71.010/71.021/71.009 two-track claims → Dallas probate → sheriff/constable
costs). The 1.04(a) unconscionability sentence is worded differently on each. Verified: zero shared 15-word runs across
all 83 sites.
