
## services_summary

One phrase covers four separate claims in Craighead County, and each runs on rules of its own. A fall inside a Jonesboro business is a premises question about control, notice and how long the hazard sat there. Harm that came out of treatment is a medical injury claim on a two-year statute that starts at the act itself. An injury from a machine or a store-bought good is a product claim against whoever supplied it. A permanent injury is mostly a damages question, because the state constitution forbids the legislature from putting a ceiling on it. Filing the wrong claim under the wrong rule is how a deadline slips past unnoticed.

## svc_slip_and_fall_lawyer_lede

A fall in Craighead County turns on three facts nobody writes down at the time: who controlled that surface, how long the hazard sat there, and whether it was hidden or plainly in view. The cleanup happens the same shift.

## svc_slip_and_fall_lawyer_body

### Whether the fall is a premises claim at all

Going down on somebody's floor does not create a claim by itself. Arkansas courts start narrower. A property owner owes an invitee ordinary care to keep the premises in a reasonably safe condition, the owner is not an insurer of anyone's safety, and liability has to rest on negligence. The duty to warn is narrower still: it reaches defects in the nature of hidden dangers, traps, snares and pitfalls, meaning conditions known to the owner that an invitee would not spot while paying ordinary attention. Where the danger is known or obvious, no warning is owed at all.

Proof runs down one of two roads. Either the substance was on the floor because of the owner's own negligence, or it sat there long enough that the owner knew or reasonably should have known about it and did not use ordinary care to clear it. That second road is why the length of time matters more than the severity of the bruise. A mop schedule, a work order, a prior complaint or a camera clock can supply it; a description written from memory six weeks later cannot.

### The three-year clock, and the shorter one hiding behind it

Negligence in this state runs on the three-year list in Arkansas Code § 16-56-105, which covers actions founded on a liability expressed or implied. Three years is generous compared with most of the South, and it lulls people. If any part of the story involves treatment rather than the floor, § 16-114-203 cuts the period to two years and starts it at the wrongful act. Where a fall ends in death, § 16-62-102 gives three years from the date of death and one year from any nonsuit.

### If the property belongs to the state, the case leaves the courthouse

Article 5, § 20 of the Arkansas Constitution is one sentence long: the State of Arkansas shall never be made defendant in any of her courts. In 2018 the Supreme Court applied that literally in Board of Trustees of the University of Arkansas v. Andrews, holding a legislative waiver of immunity repugnant to the clause. So a fall on a state campus, and Arkansas State University is a state institution, does not begin with a complaint at all.

It begins at the Arkansas State Claims Commission, which § 19-10-204 gives exclusive jurisdiction over claims against the state and its agencies and institutions. The commission charges no filing fee and requires no attorney, the agency answers within thirty days of receipt, and a contested award is held forty days for reconsideration or appeal. Two numbers matter most. Under § 19-10-209 the claim must be filed inside the same period a court action would have, and under § 19-10-215 the commission may not pay more than $15,000 without referring the claim to the General Assembly for approval and an appropriation. Counties, cities and school districts sit outside that system entirely, so a municipal sidewalk stays in circuit court.

### Which Craighead County courthouse takes the filing

The county has two county seats, Jonesboro and Lake City, an arrangement shared by only a handful of Arkansas counties. The Second Judicial Circuit's administrative plan describes six counties and nine courthouses, and it splits the work here by district. In the Eastern District at Lake City every civil and criminal case goes to the judge in Division 10, a consequence of the smaller caseload there. In the Western District at Jonesboro civil filings are divided equally among Divisions 1, 2 and 9. Opening the case costs $167.50 by the Circuit Clerk's published list, while the county's own fee page prints $165.00 for the identical line.

### What the other side does with the file

Expect a request for a recorded statement early, and a medical authorization written broadly enough to reach back years. Expect fault to be argued hard, because § 16-64-122 compares a claimant's fault with the fault of the parties sued, reduces damages in proportion when the claimant's share is lower, and bars recovery outright when it is equal to or greater. Expect the past medical figure to shrink too. Act 28 of 2025 amended § 16-64-120 so that recovery for past necessary medical care includes only what was actually paid, or what remains unpaid and legally owed.

### Nothing in Arkansas law caps what the fall was worth

Article 5, § 32 permits the General Assembly to set compensation amounts for injured workers and then closes the subject: otherwise no law shall be enacted limiting the amount to be recovered for injuries resulting in death or for injuries to persons or property. That clause has teeth. The punitive damages limit written into the 2003 tort act was held unconstitutional in Bayer CropScience LP v. Schafer, and the 2018 ballot measure that would have imposed a $500,000 non-economic ceiling never got counted, because the Supreme Court found it violated the separate-vote rule in article 19, § 22.

### The first week, and how a fee gets measured here

Photograph the approach before the defect, so the sight lines are in the frame. Keep the footwear unwashed. Ask for video in writing and name the hours you want held. Then read Rule 1.5 of the Arkansas Rules of Professional Conduct, which prints no percentage anywhere: a fee shall be reasonable, tested against eight factors including the charge customary in this locality. Any engagement likely to run past $1,000.00 must be confirmed in writing, and where payment depends on the outcome the writing states the percentages for settlement, for trial and on appeal, plus which expenses come out of the recovery and when.

## svc_medical_malpractice_lawyer_lede

Jonesboro is where a large stretch of northeast Arkansas comes for care, so a treatment injury here often crosses several buildings and several record custodians. The statute gives two years, and it starts on the date of the act.

## svc_medical_malpractice_lawyer_body

### What this state counts as a medical injury

The definition in § 16-114-201 is broader than most people assume. A medical injury is any adverse consequence arising out of professional services rendered to a patient or resident, whether from negligence, error or omission, from services given without informed consent, from failure to diagnose, from premature abandonment of a patient or a course of treatment, or from failure to keep necessary equipment in working order. The same section defines a medical care provider by list, and the list runs well past physicians: nurses, anesthetists, physician assistants, pharmacists, chiropractors, physical therapists, optometrists, dentists, psychologists, clinics, hospitals and nursing homes all appear in it.

That breadth cuts both ways. A pharmacy error and a nursing-home pressure injury are governed by the malpractice chapter, which means they inherit its short clock instead of the ordinary negligence period.

### Two years, running from the act rather than the discovery

Section 16-114-203(a) requires an action for medical injury to be commenced within two years after the cause of action accrues. Subsection (b) then removes the escape hatch found in many states: the date of accrual is the date of the wrongful act complained of and no other time. A patient who learns in year three that a study was misread is generally out of time, not at the start of a discovery period.

Two carve-outs exist in the text. A foreign object left in the body, not discoverable inside the two years, may be sued on within one year of discovery or of when it reasonably should have been discovered, whichever comes first. For a child nine or younger at the time of the act, the period runs to the later of the eleventh birthday or two years from the act, with an outer limit tied to the nineteenth birthday when nothing was discoverable earlier. Where a patient dies, the medical injury period rather than the general wrongful-death period governs the timing.

### The expert requirement, and the words the court struck out of it

Section 16-114-206(a) sets the burden when the alleged negligence lies outside a jury's common knowledge. Expert testimony must establish the degree of skill and learning ordinarily used by members of the profession in good standing in the same type of practice, in this locality or a similar one, that the provider departed from it, and that the injury would not otherwise have occurred. In 2003 the General Assembly inserted a requirement that the expert be a provider of the same specialty as the defendant. The Supreme Court removed that phrase in Broussard v. St. Edward Mercy Health System, holding that setting the qualifications a witness must possess is procedure, which Amendment 80 reserves to the judiciary. The rest of the section survived because the added language was severable.

One more limit sits in § 16-114-207. No witness whose compensation for services is in any way dependent on the outcome may give expert testimony, so the reviewing expert is paid for the review either way.

### Where the record actually lives in northeast Arkansas

The Arkansas Department of Health designates both Jonesboro hospitals, St. Bernards Medical Center and NEA Baptist Memorial Hospital, as Level III trauma centers. No Level I or Level II center is designated anywhere in this corner of the state; the nearest Level I facilities are Regional One Health in Memphis and UAMS in Little Rock. Paragould's hospital is Level IV. That geography produces transfers, and transfers produce split charts.

A complete request therefore reaches further than one release. Ask each facility separately, ask for imaging on disc as well as the radiologist's report, and ask for the itemized bill, which frequently records what was actually done when the narrative note is thin. Save portal messages before an account closes, and write a dated account of symptoms and conversations that keeps description apart from conclusion.

### The first weeks after something goes wrong

Continue the care you need, and never annotate an original document. Put every request in writing so the date is fixed. List the buildings, departments and outside laboratories involved, including the clinic that referred and the pharmacy that filled. Bring the reviewer a chronology, not an argument, because the sequence of orders, timing of tests and reading of images is what a qualified reviewer can actually evaluate.

### What the institution's side argues

Hospital counsel is usually involved before the patient holds the chart. The recurring defenses are that the outcome was a recognized complication, that consent covered the risk, that a delay or a missed appointment on the patient's side contributed, and that causation cannot be pinned to any one decision. Shared fault is governed by § 16-64-122, which bars recovery when the claimant's fault is equal to or greater than the defendant's. On the money side, Act 28 of 2025 limits past medical damages to amounts actually paid or still legally owed, a change to the collateral source picture drawn in Montgomery Ward & Co. v. Anderson, where a negotiated hospital discount was excluded from the jury's view.

### No ceiling, and how the fee is measured

Article 5, § 32 of the constitution forbids any law limiting the amount recoverable for injuries to persons or for injuries resulting in death, and Arkansas accordingly has no cap on non-economic damages in a malpractice case. The 2018 attempt to write a $500,000 cap and a 33 1/3 percent fee limit into the constitution was struck from the ballot for failing article 19, § 22. What governs the fee instead is Rule 1.5, which requires only that it be reasonable and lists eight factors for testing it, including the time and labor involved, the difficulty of the questions and the result obtained. Agreements likely to exceed $1,000.00 go in writing, and an outcome-based fee must state its percentages and its expense treatment on paper.

## svc_product_liability_lawyer_lede

Craighead County keeps 283,467 acres in farms, so the machine that hurt someone here is as likely to be a header, an auger or a hitch as a household appliance. Arkansas measures the deadline from the day of the injury.

## svc_product_liability_lawyer_body

### What makes it a product claim instead of an accident

Section 16-116-101 states the elements plainly. A supplier is liable in damages when it is engaged in the business of manufacturing, assembling, selling, leasing or otherwise distributing the product, the product was supplied in a defective condition that rendered it unreasonably dangerous, and that condition was a proximate cause of the harm. The statute then adds the part that surprises people: it applies although the claiming party never bought from the supplier or entered any contract with it. A borrowed tool and a machine bought used both fit.

Real estate and improvements on it are generally not products under the section. A tangible good that is affixed to, installed on or incorporated into real estate is, which is how a water heater, a lift gate or a grain-bin fan stays inside the chapter after installation.

### Three years, measured from the injury and not from the purchase

The limitation is short and written without qualifications: all product liability actions shall be commenced within three years after the date on which the death, injury or damage complained of occurs. Section 16-116-203 ties the period to the event rather than to a delivery date, which is a real difference from states that also run a repose clock from first sale. Where the theory is ordinary negligence rather than product defect, § 16-56-105 supplies the same three years.

### The object is the evidence, and a warranty return destroys it

Set the item aside and photograph the data plate, serial number, lot code and every legible warning before anything is cleaned. Keep the fragments that look like debris, since a failure surface tells a metallurgist more than the whole assembly does. Do not accept a refund, an exchange or a factory repair, because each of those routes ends with the proof sitting in the possession of the company on the other side. If a landlord, employer, dealer or insurer is holding the machine, send a written preservation request naming the item and its location. Capture the listing page and its safety language with the date visible, because product pages change quietly.

### Delta equipment, and the compensation gap behind it

The 2022 Census of Agriculture counts 393 farms in the county, with 91,851 acres in soybeans, 79,950 in cotton, 38,632 in rice and 33,955 in corn for grain. Machinery follows that calendar, and so does the traffic. Highway 63 through town was converted to interstate standards over decades of federal investment exceeding $300 million, with the last 4.8-mile segment between Southwest Drive and Dan Avenue folded into Interstate 555 in November 2021, linking the region to Interstate 55 and Memphis freight.

Behind the machinery sits a gap most people discover after the fact. Arkansas workers' compensation defines covered employment in § 11-9-102 and then excludes agricultural farm labor from it. A hired hand injured by a defective auger may therefore have no compensation claim standing between the injury and the wage loss, which puts the entire weight of the matter on the negligence and product routes.

### Who can be named, and why the jury will not be asked to blame a ghost

The 2003 tort act tried to let a fact finder assign fault to people who were never sued, including nonparties identified by a filed pleading. In Johnson v. Rockwell Automation the Supreme Court held § 16-55-202 unconstitutional, reasoning that a provision creating a procedure for litigating nonparty fault invades the rulemaking authority Amendment 80 gives the judiciary. The practical consequence in a defective-equipment case is large. Fault is compared under § 16-64-122 among the claimant and the parties actually sued, so identifying every supplier in the chain early, including the dealer, the installer and whoever last serviced the unit, changes the arithmetic rather than just the caption.

The same opinion struck § 16-55-212(b), which had limited medical-cost evidence to amounts actually paid, because admissibility is a matter for the court's own rules. That is worth knowing, because the General Assembly returned to the subject in 2025 and put a similar limit in § 16-64-120 as a rule about recovery instead of a rule about evidence. Whether that redraft survives the same objection has not been settled.

### What the manufacturer's side does

The first move is usually a polite request to inspect or take custody of the item. Expect a defense built on alteration, a removed guard, deferred maintenance, use outside the manual, and the argument that the hazard was obvious to anyone operating the machine. Expect an inspection protocol to be negotiated, and expect the defense expert to want the failed part in hand before any testing is agreed.

### After contact, and what the fee agreement has to say

Early work is inventory work: what the item is, who made and sold it, who has it now, what maintenance records exist and how the three-year period runs against the injury date. Filing a civil case with the Craighead County Circuit Clerk costs $167.50 on the clerk's published list, with the county's page showing $165.00, and the sheriff charges $30.00 to serve a summons on each person named unless several are served in one place. Rule 1.5 governs the fee, requires that it be reasonable under eight named factors, forces a writing above $1,000.00, and where payment turns on the outcome requires the writing to give the percentages and identify the expenses charged against any recovery.

## svc_catastrophic_injury_lawyer_lede

A catastrophic file in Arkansas is unusual in one respect: no statute tells the jury where to stop. What actually limits the outcome is the coverage stacked behind the collision and the quality of the proof about what changed.

## svc_catastrophic_injury_lawyer_body

### The clause that keeps the legislature out of the number

Most states answer a severe injury with a cap. Arkansas cannot. Article 5, § 32 of its constitution lets the General Assembly set compensation for injured workers and then forbids the rest: otherwise no law shall be enacted limiting the amount to be recovered for injuries resulting in death or for injuries to persons or property. Amendment 80, § 3 draws the other half of the line, giving the Supreme Court the rules of pleading, practice and procedure while barring rules that abridge, enlarge or modify a substantive right.

Those two clauses have decided real cases. The punitive damages limit in the 2003 tort act fell in Bayer CropScience LP v. Schafer. The nonparty fault provision and the medical-cost evidence provision from the same act fell in Johnson v. Rockwell Automation, on separation of powers and Amendment 80 grounds. The same-specialty expert requirement fell in Broussard v. St. Edward Mercy Health System. When supporters tried to write caps into the constitution itself in 2018, proposing a $500,000 non-economic ceiling and a 33 1/3 percent limit on an outcome-based fee, the court ordered that no votes on the measure be counted, because bundling those subjects violated the separate-vote requirement of article 19, § 22.

### Coverage arithmetic on a northeast Arkansas highway

An uncapped claim still collides with a policy limit. The state minimum under § 27-19-605 is $25,000 for bodily injury to one person, $50,000 for two or more people in one accident, and $25,000 for property damage. Intensive care can pass the first figure inside a week.

That makes the injured person's own declarations page part of the investigation. Uninsured motorist coverage at those limits must be provided under § 23-89-403 unless a named insured rejected it in writing, and where higher liability limits were purchased the insurer must have available and the agent must offer matching uninsured limits. Underinsured coverage under § 23-89-209 works differently: it must be offered, may be rejected in writing, has to be at least equal to the bodily injury limits in § 27-19-605, and cannot be issued at all unless uninsured coverage was elected alongside it. The statute also sets a thirty-day window for the underinsured carrier to respond after written notice of a tentative settlement at the tortfeasor's limits.

Truck exposure concentrates the problem. Highway 63 through the city was rebuilt to interstate standards over more than $300 million in investment, and a 4.8-mile stretch between Southwest Drive and Dan Avenue joined Interstate 555 in November 2021, tying local traffic into the Interstate 55 corridor toward Memphis.

### Where the patient goes, and why the file follows

St. Bernards Medical Center and NEA Baptist Memorial Hospital are both designated Level III trauma centers by the Arkansas Department of Health, and no Level I or Level II center is designated in this part of the state. Serious cases move on, most often to Regional One Health in Memphis or to UAMS in Little Rock. A single patient can generate records in three cities and two states inside a week, along with an air ambulance bill and a rehabilitation admission somewhere else again.

### Documenting a changed life instead of a stack of bills

The first invoice says nothing useful about a spinal cord injury, an amputation, a severe burn or a brain injury. What describes those is duller: written restrictions and their revisions, therapy attendance, equipment and its replacement cycle, home modification quotes, medication lists, transportation needs, and the specific hours a spouse or an adult child now spends on care that used to take nobody's time. Payroll records, tax returns and a plain description of the former job duties carry the wage side. A dated calendar kept by somebody in the household is often the most persuasive document in the file, precisely because it was not written for a lawsuit.

### When the injury kills

The wrongful death statute, § 16-62-102, is brought by the personal representative, or by the heirs at law when there is no representative. The period is three years from the death, with one year from the date of any nonsuit. Beneficiaries named in the statute are the surviving spouse, children, parents and siblings, along with anyone standing in loco parentis to the deceased and anyone to whom the deceased stood in that role. Damages may include a spouse's loss of services and companionship, and the statute is explicit that mental anguish includes the grief normally associated with losing a loved one. No part of the recovery is subject to the decedent's debts or becomes an asset of the estate. Where the death arose from medical care, the two-year medical injury period controls the filing instead.

### What gets repaid before the family sees anything

Health plans, Medicaid and Medicare all look to an injury recovery, and in a severe case those claims can rival the available coverage. Arkansas long treated benefits from a collateral source as no concern of the wrongdoer, and Montgomery Ward & Co. v. Anderson extended that shelter to gratuitous and discounted medical services, while recognizing four narrow situations in which the evidence comes in anyway. Act 28 of 2025 then moved the line, amending § 16-64-120 so that recovery for past necessary care includes only what was actually paid or remains legally owed. The headline number and the amount a household keeps have never been the same figure, and that gap deserves an explicit answer before anything is signed.

### Fault is fought hardest where the damages are largest

Section 16-64-122 does not soften for severity. A claimant whose fault is lesser recovers damages reduced in proportion to it, and a claimant whose fault is equal to or greater recovers nothing. A ten-point move in that percentage is worth more in one catastrophic file than in a hundred small ones, which is why scene measurements, vehicle data and the earliest recorded statements draw such attention. Suit belongs in the Craighead County Circuit Court, where the Eastern District at Lake City sends every civil case to Division 10 and the Western District at Jonesboro divides civil filings among Divisions 1, 2 and 9. The fee is governed by Rule 1.5, which sets reasonableness and eight factors rather than a percentage, requires a writing above $1,000.00, and requires an outcome-based agreement to state its percentages, the expenses deducted and whether they come off before or after the fee is calculated.
