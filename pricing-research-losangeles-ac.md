### 1. Mechanical permit for a residential changeout — "$90 minimum"
- Source: LADBS mechanical and HVAC permit fee schedule (PDF) — https://ladbs.org/docs/default-source/forms/plan-check-2014/mechanical-hvac-permit-fee-schedule.pdf?sfvrsn=b26dea53_22 (retrieved 2026-08-23)
- Figures stated: AC/compressor ≤25 HP **$24.00**; 26–49 HP $59.00; ≥50 HP $116.00; Air Handling Unit **$24.00**; add/alter/repair $24.00; evaporative cooler $24.00; air inlet/outlet $5.00; **minimum permit fee $90.00** where a permit covers two or more items (or one item over $55); $55.00 minimum for a single item under $55; permit issuing fee $24.00 when Sub-total 1 exceeds $90; **Development Services Centers surcharge 3%** of Sub-total 2 ($1 min); **Systems Development surcharge 6%** ($1 min); witnessing test of heating/cooling equipment $108.00.
- Arithmetic used in the anchor detail: condenser $24.00 + air handler $24.00 = $48.00 → raised to the **$90.00 two-item minimum** → 3% + 6% surcharges = $8.10 → **≈ $98** total. Corroborated independently by Around the Clock Heating and Air Conditioning, which posts "approximately $98" for a basic HVAC permit (https://aroundclock.com/blog/cost-to-replace-central-air-conditioner-in-los-angeles-2026-pricing-guide/).
- Permit requirement itself: https://dbs.lacity.gov/services/plan-review-permitting/mechanical-hvac-permits — mechanical permit required for any installation or modification of heating/cooling systems.
- Note: the alternate path `ladbs.org/docs/default-source/forms/plan-review-permitting/...` returns 404; the fee-schedule index at https://www.ladbs.org/faq/fee-schedules is HTML only.

### 2. Duct leakage limit on an altered system — "10 percent"
- Source: California Energy Commission prescriptive alterations form **CF1R-ALT-02-E** (2022 Energy Code, HVAC alterations) — https://www.energy.ca.gov/filebrowser/download/6349 (retrieved 2026-08-23)
- Stated: under §150.2(b)1E and F, a duct leakage test (MCH-20-H) is required when heating or cooling components are installed in a ducted system or more than 25 ft of duct is replaced; compliance is **≤10% total leakage**, **≤7% leakage to outside**, or sealing of all accessible leaks. **Refrigerant charge verification (MCH-25-H) is required in Climate Zones 2 and 8–15** when refrigerant-containing components are installed or altered; airflow ≥**300 CFM/ton** (MCH-23) applies when refrigerant charge verification is required. Exceptions: previously sealed and registered ducts; heating-only / air-handler-or-furnace-only changes exempt from MCH-23/MCH-25; asbestos ducts exempt. A completely new duct system must test ≤5% leakage with fan efficacy ≤0.58 W/cfm and airflow ≥350 cfm/ton.
- Supporting (not cited as anchor):
  - CEC HERS program page — https://www.energy.ca.gov/programs-and-topics/programs/home-energy-rating-system-hers-program — "properly permitted work will trigger any necessary HERS testing"; rater fees vary; HERS documentation is still required to close a permit even where no test is performed; **effective January 1, 2026 the HERS Program no longer supports an Energy Code compliance demonstration program**, with field verification/diagnostic testing provisions moved into Title 24 Part 6 (Title 20 §§1670–1675 amended).
  - ERE Inspections (Los Angeles-area rater) — https://ereinspections.com/services/duct-leakage-testing — replacing an outdoor condenser, air handler, coil or furnace heat exchanger is an "altered space-conditioning system" under §150.2(b)1E and triggers third-party duct leakage testing (25 Pa, RA3.1.4.3.1; 10% total / 7% to outside; 6% for altered garage ducts).
  - Venta HVAC — https://ventahvac.com/blog/california-hers-testing/ — as of Jan 1 2026 the rules are administered under the Energy Code Compliance (ECC) Program and the CF-3R is now an ECC certificate.
- **Answer to the brief's question:** yes — third-party Title 24 field verification is required in substance for an LA changeout. Duct leakage verification applies whenever cooling or heating equipment is replaced on a ducted system; refrigerant charge verification plus the airflow check apply in Climate Zones 2 and 8–15 (inland/Valley LA is CZ 9; coastal LA near the airport is CZ 6, so it escapes the refrigerant-charge trigger); documentation is required to close the permit even where a test is exempted; and since Jan 1 2026 this runs through the Energy Code Compliance program rather than the HERS program.

### 3. Municipal utility heat pump incentive — "up to $2,500 per ton"
- Source: LADWP Consumer Rebate Program — https://www.ladwp.com/residential-services/assistance-programs/consumer-rebate-program (retrieved 2026-08-23)
- Stated: central/split HVAC **$100 per ton at 15.2 SEER2**, **$120 per ton at 16.0 SEER2**; heat pump HVAC **up to $2,500 per ton** (minimum ≥7.7 HSPF2 / ≥15.2 SEER2), ducted tiers $1,000/ton rising to $1,250/ton (>19.6 SEER2 / >8.9 HSPF2), ductless/mini-split tiers $1,500/ton to $2,500/ton (>20.5 SEER2 / >9.1 HSPF2); heat pump water heater up to $2,500/unit; new construction and ADUs not eligible; requires active LADWP electric service, application after installation, paid itemized invoice, matching AHRI certificate, and final approved LADBS permit; LADWP may inspect; funding limited.

### 4. Refrigerant limit on new residential systems — "700 GWP"
- Source: EPA technology transitions HFC restrictions by sector — https://www.epa.gov/hfcs/technology-transitions-hfc-restrictions-sector (retrieved 2026-08-23)
- Stated: residential and light-commercial AC and heat pump **products and systems** (including mini-splits and unitary equipment) limited to **GWP < 700**; manufacture/import compliance date **Jan 1, 2025**; installation compliance date **Jan 1, 2025**; exception where all components were manufactured/imported before Jan 1, 2025; components used to repair existing systems are not restricted.
- Caveat: the EPA page does not name R-454B specifically, so the site copy describes the GWP limit rather than naming a replacement refrigerant.
