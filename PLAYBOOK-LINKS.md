# Links and Listings — The Other 40%

The site is done. The site is not the business. Kyle splits ranking effort roughly
40% content, 40% links, 20% technical basics, and says so in one line:
"The 80% is content and backlinks. Volume of content, quality of content." We have
built the content half and zero of the link half.

This document is the link half. It is the same procedure for every one of the 83
sites, which is the only reason it can be run at that scale.

---

## The target: 10 to 30 links per site

Kyle's number, stated directly: "Get 10–30 relevant links pointing at your site.
Local directories, guest posts, related-niche mentions."

That is the whole bar. Not 500 links. Not a link-building agency. Ten to thirty,
weighted toward local relevance, built over the first eight weeks and then left
alone. His month-one checkpoint is deliberately lower than that: listed in at
least 5 directories, at least 1 external link. The rest accumulates in months two
and three, which is also when he expects the site to reach page one.

Why so few works: we are not competing against national SEO. Kyle is blunt about
the competition — "I'm competing against the keyword junk removal Austin Texas,
and the other sites there are run by Joe, Bill, and Steve." His own competitor
profile is a site built between 2005 and 2018 with one or two pages, few or no
backlinks, and domain authority under 20. Fifteen decent links beats that.

## Tier 1 — The free listings, every site, no exceptions

These are citations, not really links. Several are nofollow and pass little
authority directly. They matter because they are the trust and consistency signal,
and because a few of them send actual calls. Kyle's list, verbatim from his
citations step: "Google Business Profile, Yelp, Yellow Pages, BBB, HomeAdvisor,
Angi. Consistent NAP (Name, Address, Phone) across all."

| # | Listing | Cost | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Google Search Console | free | Do this first. Submit the sitemap. This is how we learn whether the page is indexed and what it is getting impressions for. Not a link. |
| 2 | Bing Webmaster Tools | free | Import from Search Console in one click. Bing indexes small local sites faster than Google. |
| 3 | Yelp | free | Real call volume in home services. Claim, do not solicit reviews. |
| 4 | Yellow Pages | free tier | Low value, cheap to do, everyone in Kyle's list has it. |
| 5 | Better Business Bureau | free listing | The paid accreditation is not needed. |
| 6 | Angi | free | Home services only. Expect sales calls. |
| 7 | Thumbtack | free to list | Home services only. |
| 8 | Nextdoor Business | free | Genuinely local. Useful in the neighborhoods we already name on the page. |
| 9 | Apple Business Connect | free | Feeds Apple Maps and Siri. Almost nobody in these niches has done it. |
| 10 | Local chamber of commerce | $200–400/yr | **Real followed link from a genuinely local domain.** The single best-value item on this list. Only worth buying once a site has a paying tenant. |

Everything in Tier 1 except the chamber is free and takes under fifteen minutes
per listing. Ten listings per site is a bit over two hours of clicking. Across 83
sites that is a real number of hours, and it is the most mechanical work in the
whole business — it is the first thing to hand to UG or to hire out.

### The NAP problem, stated honestly

Kyle's instruction is consistent name, address, phone across every listing, using
"a mail forwarding service or your business address if you have one."

The phone is easy. Dan already owns roughly 50 Telnyx numbers, each gets a local
area code for its city, and that number is the one thing that must be identical
everywhere.

The address is not easy, and this is where Kyle's playbook stops scaling.

**Google Business Profile does not scale to 83 cities and we should not pretend
it does.** GBP requires a real address where the business operates, and a
service-area business still has to verify a real location. Registering 83 mail
drops in 83 cities and putting them on GBP is the pattern Google's own guidelines
are written to catch, and the outcome is suspension — usually of the whole
account, not one listing. Kyle runs a few dozen sites and can absorb the manual
work per city. At 83 it becomes a footprint.

So: **build Tier 1 without GBP.** Everything else on the list either accepts a
service-area listing with a city and phone and no street address, or accepts our
single real business address. We lose the map pack.

That is a loss we already decided to accept. Dan's position from the start: "if we
are number 4-7 thats find Map Pack does nto scare me." Organic position four to
seven, with a call bar pinned to the top of the page, is the plan. Nothing here
changes it.

Where a site earns a paying tenant, the tenant already has their own verified GBP
at their own real address. At that point the right move is forwarding calls into
their existing listing, not creating a new one. That is cleaner, it is legitimate,
and it costs nothing.

## Tier 2 — Niche directories, 3 to 6 per site

These are followed links from sites that are topically relevant, which is what
gives them weight. They differ by trade, so they are picked per niche, not per
city.

**Home services** (garage door, plumbing, HVAC, roofing, restoration — 34 of our 83)
- Houzz — Kyle names it explicitly. Free professional profile, followed link.
- Porch, HomeAdvisor, Networx, Modernize — contractor directories, free listings
- BuildZoom — free claim
- Trade associations where a tenant is a member. For garage door specifically, the
  International Door Association. Association member directories are high-trust
  local links and almost nobody in these niches claims them.

**Legal** (49 of our 83 — car accident, personal injury, dog bite, DUI, family)
Legal is a different world and better supplied with free directories:
- Justia — free lawyer profile, followed link, strong domain
- Avvo — free claim
- FindLaw, Lawyers.com, Nolo — free basic tiers
- HG.org, Super Lawyers (if the tenant qualifies)
- State and county bar association directories — the best local legal links there are

Legal note: these profiles describe an attorney, so they can only be filled in
honestly once a tenant firm is signed. **Do not create attorney profiles for a
site with no tenant.** For an unrented legal site, stop at Tier 1 plus Tier 3 and
add Tier 2 after the firm signs. The site can rank on content and Tier 1 while it
waits.

## Tier 3 — Genuinely local links, 3 to 8 per site

This is where the actual ranking difference lives, and it is the only part that
cannot be templated. Kyle's version: "Research three to five websites that might
link to your site: local community blogs, home improvement directories,
neighborhood Facebook group resource pages, or related-niche websites that accept
guest contributions. Write one short guest post (400–600 words) for the most
relevant site you can get a guest post on."

What actually works, roughly in order of effort-to-value:

1. **Local news and neighborhood blogs.** Patch has a page for most of our cities.
   Local outlets run seasonal service stories constantly. A 500-word piece on what
   the August 11 derecho did to garage door tracks across DuPage County is a real
   story with a real local hook, and we already researched it for the site.
2. **Community and HOA resource pages.** The subdivisions we name on the page —
   Cress Creek, Ashbury, White Eagle — many have websites with a vendors or
   resources page. Direct ask, high acceptance, extremely local.
3. **Adjacent-trade cross-links.** A garage door site and a roofing site in the
   same metro can each carry a genuinely useful referral link. **Only across
   different metros and different niches**, never in a ring inside our own
   portfolio — a link network among 83 sites we own is exactly the pattern that
   gets the whole portfolio devalued at once.
4. **Guest posts.** 400 to 600 words, per Kyle. One per site, on the most relevant
   local site that will take it.
5. **Sponsorships.** A youth sports team or a local 5K, $100 to $500, produces a
   followed link from a local domain and a legitimate community tie. Worth it on
   the sites priced at $1,000/month and up.

Realistic yield: 3 to 8 links per site from this tier, which is what gets us into
Kyle's 10 to 30 range when stacked on Tier 1 and 2.

## The eight-week schedule, per site

Kyle's own timeline runs on a 30-day sprint and expects page one in one to three
months. Compressed to what we actually do:

| Week | Work |
|---|---|
| 1 | Domain live. Search Console + Bing, sitemap submitted. |
| 2 | Tier 1 free listings, all of them except GBP. Identical NAP. |
| 3 | Tier 2 niche directories for that trade. |
| 4 | Build the Tier 3 prospect list — 10 to 15 local targets. Send the first outreach. |
| 5–6 | Write and place one guest post. Chase the community and HOA pages. |
| 7–8 | Follow up on everything unanswered. Add sponsorship on the high-price sites. |
| 8+ | Stop. |

That last line is Kyle's actual position and it is the reason this model works at
83 sites: once a site ranks in a weak local niche, it mostly stays. His auto glass
site in New Orleans has been paying $600 a month for five years with roughly five
conversations total and no ongoing SEO. His reasoning is that the competitors are
not investing, not monitoring, and do not know they were displaced.

## What we deliberately are not doing

- **Buying links.** PBNs, link packages, fiverr gigs. The competition is Joe, Bill
  and Steve with two pages and no links. We do not need to take that risk.
- **Google Business Profile at scale.** Covered above.
- **Schema markup.** Kyle: "That mostly applies to the major sites... We're not
  doing any of that." We emit one FAQ block, which is more than he does, and we
  emit no LocalBusiness schema until a tenant is actually signed — the build fails
  if anyone tries.
- **Reciprocal links inside the portfolio.** The single fastest way to turn 83
  independent sites into one detectable network.
- **Review solicitation** on any listing where we are not the service provider.

## Cost, per site and total

| Item | Per site | 83 sites |
|---|---|---|
| Tier 1 and 2 listings | $0 | $0 |
| Guest post placement | $0 (we write it) | $0 |
| Chamber of commerce | $0 until a tenant signs, then $200–400/yr | apply to the ~30 that rent first |
| Sponsorship | $0, or $100–500 on high-price sites | optional |
| **Cash floor** | **$0** | **$0** |

The entire link half of this business has a cash cost of zero. What it costs is
hours, and the hours are mechanical and delegable. That is the whole reason it
fits the constraint of not selling on the phone: nothing in this document is a
sales call. It is form-filling and a handful of emails.
