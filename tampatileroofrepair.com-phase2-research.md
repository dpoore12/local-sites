## Code: the 25 percent rule and the 2022 change
- [USED] FBC Existing Building (2023, 8th ed.) §706.1.1: "Not more than 25 percent of the total roof area or roof section of any existing building or structure shall be repaired, replaced or recovered in any 12-month period unless the roof covering on the entire existing roof system or roof section is replaced or recovered to conform to the requirements of this code." Exception mirrors FS 553.844(5) and bars local amendment. https://up.codes/viewer/florida/fl-existing-building-code-2023/chapter/7/alterations-level-1
- [USED] SB 4-D (2022 Special Session D) enrolled text added FS 553.844(5): if the existing roofing system or roof section was built, repaired or replaced in compliance with the 2007 Florida Building Code or any subsequent edition, and 25 percent or more is repaired/replaced/recovered, only the repaired portion must meet the code in effect; a local government may not adopt an amendment to the exception. "This act shall take effect upon becoming a law" (signed May 26, 2022). https://www.flsenate.gov/Session/Bill/2022D/4D/BillText/er/HTML
- [USED] FS 553.844 (2025) current text of (5), plus (2)(b)2 secondary water barriers, (2)(b)3 roof-to-wall connections capped so cost "does not exceed approximately 15 percent of the cost of reroofing", (3)(a) roof replacement must incorporate (2)(b)2 and 4, (3)(b) roof-to-wall retrofit required in wind-borne debris region where insured value/just value is $300,000 or more. https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2025/553.844

## Code: mitigation retrofit and secondary water barrier
- [USED] FBC EB §706.7 Mitigation: when a roof covering on an existing structure with sawn lumber, wood plank or wood structural panel deck is removed and replaced, roof-decking attachment (706.7.1) and a secondary water barrier (706.7.2) apply. Exception: "Structures permitted subject to the Florida Building Code are not required to comply with this section." https://up.codes/viewer/florida/fl-existing-building-code-2023/chapter/7/alterations-level-1
- [USED] Florida Building Commission hurricane-mitigation reroof options document: secondary water barrier defined as a water-resistant underlayment layer/product that resists water intrusion if the primary covering is damaged, removed or penetrated by wind-driven rain in hurricane-force winds. Methods: (a) cover all deck joints with min 4-inch self-adhering polymer-modified bitumen tape, then an approved underlayment; (b) cover the entire deck with an approved self-adhering polymer-modified bitumen cap sheet. https://floridabuilding.org/fbc/thecode/Secondary_water_barrier_revised.pdf

## Code: tile fasteners, deck and underlayment
- [USED] FBC Building §1507.3.6: tile fasteners shall be corrosion resistant, not less than 11-gage [0.120 in], 5/16-inch head, of sufficient length to penetrate the deck not less than 3/4 inch or through the deck; attaching wire not smaller than 0.083 in; "Perimeter fastening areas include three tile courses but not less than 36 inches from either side of hips or ridges and edges of eaves and gable rakes." §1507.3.2: tile on slopes 2.5:12 or greater; from 2.5:12 to 4:12 double underlayment per §1507.1.1. §1507.3.3 underlayment ASTM D226 Type II / D2626 / D6380 Class M. https://up.codes/s/clay-and-concrete-tile

- [USED] Hillsborough County publishes windborne debris maps at 130 MPH, 140 MPH and 150 MPH. https://hcfl.gov/businesses/permits-and-records/permitting-tools/data-maps-and-reports

## Insurance (the local angle the brief asked for)
- [USED] FS 627.7011(5)(b): an insurer may not refuse to issue or renew a homeowner's policy on a residential structure with a roof less than 15 years old solely because of roof age. (5)(c): for a roof at least 15 years old the insurer must allow an inspection by an authorized inspector at the homeowner's expense before requiring replacement as a condition of issuing/renewing, and may not refuse solely on roof age if the inspection shows 5 or more years of useful life remaining. (5)(d): roof age is calculated from the last date 100 percent of the roof's surface area was built or replaced. (5)(e): applies to policies issued or renewed on or after July 1, 2022. (3)(a): insurer must initially pay at least actual cash value, then remaining amounts as work is performed; roof-deductible holdback rules. https://www.leg.state.fl.us/statutes/index.cfm?App_mode=Display_Statute&URL=0600-0699/0627/Sections/0627.7011.html
- [USED] FS 627.701(10) (created by SB 2-D, 2022): a separate roof deductible may not exceed the lesser of 2 percent of the Coverage A limit or 50 percent of the cost to replace the roof; it does not apply to a total loss under the valued policy law, a roof loss from a hurricane, a roof loss from a tree fall or other hazard that punctures the roof deck, or a roof loss requiring repair of less than 50 percent of the roof; if applied, no other deductible applies to that loss; opt-out form approved by OIR; 18-point boldface warning page. https://www.leg.state.fl.us/statutes/index.cfm?App_mode=Display_Statute&URL=0600-0699/0627/Sections/0627.701.html
- [USED] Citizens Property Insurance roof age requirements: documentation of full roof replacement required for tile, slate, clay, concrete or metal roofs more than 50 years old, and shingle/other roofs more than 25 years old; alternative is documentation verifying at least five years of remaining useful life, reviewed by underwriters. https://securesupport.citizensfla.com/app/answers/detail/a_id/2513/~/what-are-the-roof-age-requirements-for-personal-residential-policies%3F
- [USED] Citizens roof rule changes to comply with SB 2-D, effective May 1, 2023 (new business) and September 1, 2023 (renewals): minimum eligible remaining useful life raised from three years to five; if the entire roof was not replaced at once, roof age is based on the oldest part; remaining useful life entered from the 4-Point Inspection Form or Roof Inspection Form, maximum five years; the excessively-patched condition rule was revised to remove the 25% reference. https://www.citizensfla.com/-/20230331-roof-rule-changes-1
- [USED] Four-point inspection forms: Citizens roof and 4-point forms must be completed and signed by a verifiable Florida-licensed professional; a trade-specific professional may sign only their trade component (a roofing inspector signs only the roofing portion). https://www.nachi.org/4point.htm

## Salt air and fasteners
- [USED] Florida Building Commission study of roofing fastener corrosion: 79.5% of surveyed roofing professionals (306 of 385) had observed corrosion in the exposed portion of metal fasteners; corrosion observed most frequently in coastal regions; for buildings within about 10 miles of a Florida coast 57.8% said corrosion is "usually" or "always" present (112 usually + 65 always of 306) versus 17% inland. http://www.floridabuilding.org/fbc/commission/FBC_0614B/roofing/FBC_project_2_roof_fastener_corrosion_final_report_DRAFT.pdf

## Tile life versus underlayment life
- [USED] Eagle Roofing Products: concrete roof tile is intended to last the life of the structure; cites a 2007 National Association of Home Builders study naming concrete tile, clay tile and slate as roofing materials that last a lifetime. https://eagleroofing.com/specifiers/why-tile/life-expectancy/ ; warranty FAQ cites the NAHB average life expectancy of concrete roof tile at 75 years. https://eagleroofing.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/EAGLE-2025-Warranty-FAQ-Brochure-8_5x11-8pg-digital.pdf
- [USED] Florida roofing contractor guidance: tiles last 50-100 years but underlayment lasts only 20-25 years in Florida, and most tile roofs are replaced because of underlayment failure. https://integrityroofingofflorida.com/resources/tile-roof-repair-replacement-florida/
