## How the employment site was handled (per instruction)

1. **Contingency is treated as permitted and ordinary**, not as a prohibited arrangement. The page opens by stating that wrongful termination, retaliation, discrimination and unpaid wage claims are routinely taken on a contingent fee in California. Nothing on the page resembles the criminal-defense treatment used on the defense sites.
2. **No percentage ceiling is asserted, because California sets none for employment work.** The page says this plainly: no provision of the Labor Code, Government Code or Business and Professions Code fixes a rate, and the only substantive limit is rule 1.5(a)'s bar on an unconscionable or illegal fee, tested under rule 1.5(b) on the facts as they stood at signing.
3. **What the law does set is published instead**: B&P 6147's written-contract requirements including the "not set by law but is negotiable" term, B&P 6148(a)'s $1,000 written-agreement threshold for non-contingency work, the San Diego Superior Court filing figures, and the fee-shifting statutes.
4. **Fee shifting is kept separate from any notion of a cap.** The page states in terms that a fee-shifting statute is not a percentage limit and should not be described as one, and adds that where a statutory fee award and a contingency can both arrive in one case, the written agreement should say how they interact.
5. **B&P 6146 is labeled medical-negligence-only** and is used solely to explain where the 25%/33% figures people find online come from — expressly not an employment rule.
