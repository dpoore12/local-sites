## services_summary

Four different jobs hide behind one sentence: something is wrong with the plumbing. Emergency plumbing repair is triage work, where the water gets stopped and the failed system gets named. Burst-pipe repair is pressure-side work on the piping that carries water toward a fixture. Drain cleaning removes an obstruction that a cable, a jetter, or an auger can actually reach. Sewer-line repair begins only after a camera shows that the buried lateral is broken, sagging, displaced, or full of roots.

Housing age makes that sorting more useful here than a symptom list. Grant Park sits on more than 430 acres, and the National Register nomination for the district describes its street network and residential pattern taking shape from the late 1880s into the early 1900s. Inman Park was laid out between 1889 and 1891 as a streetcar suburb, and the federal nomination for it counts roughly 135 acres. Neighborhoods that old carry cast iron drainage, clay laterals, and several generations of remodel piping in the same crawlspace. A build era never proves what is in the ground, but it tells a plumber what to look for before opening a wall.

The city layer matters too. Watershed Management draws the ownership line at the meter, so the piping running from there to the house belongs to the property owner while the run from the main to the meter belongs to the city. Atlanta also still operates a portion of its collection system as combined sewer, which is why some backups arrive with a rainstorm rather than with a clog. Getting the category right decides whether the answer tonight is a cable, a camera, an excavation, or a phone call to 311.

## svc_emergency_plumbing_repair_lede

Emergency plumbing repair is the first hour: stop the water, work out which system failed, and decide what genuinely has to be corrected tonight. A Grant Park bungalow, a Kirkwood crawlspace, and a slab addition in West End each go wrong in their own way.

## svc_emergency_plumbing_repair_body

### Close the water before anything else gets touched

Pressurized water that keeps arriving makes every other decision worse. If the leak is clearly at one toilet, sink, or appliance connection, close the fixture stop. If water is running behind a wall, through a ceiling, or from a pipe nobody can isolate, close the house main and then open a low cold-water tap to bleed the remaining pressure. Where waste water is the problem, the instruction reverses: stop using water anywhere in the building, because every gallon sent down a working drain reappears at the lowest one. Keep clear of wet ceilings, submerged outlets, and any appliance the water has reached.

### Sort the supply side from the drain side

Water that keeps coming with every fixture idle points at the pressure side, a water heater, or an appliance connection. Water that appears only while a shower runs or a washer empties points at drainage. One sluggish lavatory is almost always a branch problem. A tub and a toilet reacting to each other is not, and neither is a floor drain that fills when nothing upstairs is running. ATL311 is explicit that some of this is not municipal work at all: its published list of private-property matters includes a burst water pipe on private property, noting the resident may have an internal issue requiring the expertise of a licensed plumber.

### Learn where the city's responsibility stops

Watershed Management publishes the boundary plainly. Repairs to distribution piping running from the main to the meter are the city's responsibility, and repairs to the piping from the meter to the house are the homeowner's. Reports of a suspected main break or a public leak go to 404-546-0311, and the department's service description says a specialist investigates within 24 hours, while full resolution including street or property restoration can take 30 to 45 days. That timeline is worth knowing before a household decides to wait on a public repair. Water rising inside a house rarely waits for anybody.

### Water heaters fail in a few recognizable ways

A tank that weeps from the shell has usually lost its lining, and no fitting replacement fixes that. A drip at the temperature and pressure relief connection can mean thermal expansion, a failing valve, or a control problem, and it should never be capped. Wet connections at the top of the tank are often corroded nipples or dielectric fittings rather than the tank itself. Sediment noise, lukewarm output, and a burner or element that short cycles all belong in the same conversation. The replacement decision should account for the failure mode, the discharge routing, and the shutoff condition together.

### Georgia licenses the plumber, not the truck

Plumbing licensure in Georgia is a state matter, handled through the Secretary of State's division for master and journeyman plumbers. State law says no person may engage in the business of plumbing as a master plumber without a valid license from that division, and a company cannot run plumbing work unless a full-time licensed master plumber is regularly connected with it. The state rules split the credential in two: a statewide Class I master license is limited to single-family dwellings, one-level dwellings for not more than two families, and commercial buildings no larger than 10,000 square feet, while a statewide Class II master license is unrestricted. Master applicants must show five years in the trade, at least two of them holding a journeyman license. The state also publishes a license search, and renewals fall in September through November of even-numbered years with four hours of continuing education a year.

### A sudden bill can be part of the same emergency

A hidden leak often shows up as consumption before it shows up as a stain. Watershed Management publishes what that costs in water: a leaky toilet flapper at 0.25 gallons per minute wastes roughly 10,800 gallons a month, a running toilet at 2.5 gallons per minute reaches about 108,000 gallons, and a leaking pipe at three gallons per minute passes 129,600 gallons. The department's account review process allows adjustments for certain leaks under the city code, but the written request must arrive within 12 months of the payment due date and include satisfactory proof that the leak existed and was repaired. Keep the invoice, keep photographs of the failed section, and note the date the water stopped.

### What the visit should have settled before it ends

Three things. The flow is under control by shutoff, repair, cap, or clearing. The system has been tested rather than assumed, with pressure checked after supply work and controlled fixture use after drain work. The remaining scope is stated as separate choices, so a temporary stabilization is never sold as a completed repair. The figure for the work is explained after inspection and before anyone starts cutting.

## svc_burst_pipe_repair_lede

Burst-pipe repair means stopping pressure, then proving what actually failed: a corroded threaded run, a pinholed copper section, a brittle plastic fitting, a heater connector, or the service line crossing the yard. Cold snaps and old material both produce the same wet ceiling.

## svc_burst_pipe_repair_body

### Kill the pressure, then look

The house main comes first, and the second choice is the valve at the meter if the interior shutoff is seized or unreachable. Old gate valves in this housing stock fail closed and fail open, so force applied to a corroded stem can turn one repair into two. After the water is off, open a bathtub cold tap at the lowest level to drop residual pressure and let the split stop spraying. If the water has reached a panel, a ceiling fixture, or an appliance feed, treat the room as an electrical problem as well and shut that circuit off from a dry position.

### A hard freeze reaches houses built for heat

Southern construction assumes mild winters, and the National Weather Service office serving north Georgia defines a hard freeze as 28 degrees or lower. Its records put the average first freeze for Atlanta on November 13, with the earliest on October 11, 1906 and the latest on December 18, 1998. Cold snaps are not theoretical here: the January 2025 climate summary for the area recorded a low of 14 degrees on the 22nd, and Watershed Management reported responding to several isolated main breaks across the city during sustained freezing temperatures in mid-December of that winter. A house that never freezes for years can still lose a pipe in one night.

### The places that freeze first around here

Vented crawlspaces are the classic weak point, especially where insulation has fallen away from a supply run near a foundation vent. Hose bibs left connected to a filled garden hose are next, because the trapped water freezes back into the wall. Uninsulated laundry lines in an unheated garage, kitchen piping in an exterior wall, and supply runs added into an attic during a remodel all sit outside the heated envelope. Pipes rarely split while frozen. They split when the ice plug thaws, which is why damage often appears the afternoon after a cold night rather than during it.

### Material clues in intown housing stock

Neighborhoods that formed in the streetcar era carry layers. Grant Park's street pattern and housing developed from the late 1880s into the early 1900s, and Inman Park was laid out between 1889 and 1891 across about 135 acres. Nothing about a neighborhood name proves pipe type, but houses of that age have typically been repiped at least once. Threaded galvanized supply corrodes from the inside and fails at the joint. Copper develops pinholes and splits along a run. Older plastic supply becomes brittle at the fitting. Where two materials meet without a proper transition, the joint is the suspect. Enough sound pipe has to be exposed on both sides of the failure before a fitting goes on.

### Yard leaks and the line the city draws

A wet spot in the yard, a meter dial that will not settle, or a warm patch on a slab suggests the leak sits between the meter and the house, and Watershed Management assigns that section to the property owner. The city's own service catalog reinforces the split: the resident who needs an emergency shutoff because of a burst pipe on private property is directed to a licensed plumber rather than to a crew. When the wet ground is on the street side of the meter, the call to 404-546-0311 is the right one instead.

### Document the loss while the evidence exists

Water lost through a split pipe leaves the property but stays on the bill. The city's published leak table puts a leaking pipe at three gallons per minute near 129,600 gallons in a month, which is enough to turn an ordinary account into a shock. Photograph the failed section before it is cut out, keep the removed piece if it is small, keep the paid invoice, and note the shutoff date and the repair date. The account review request for a leak adjustment must be written, filed within 12 months of the payment due date, and supported by proof that the leak existed and was repaired.

### What a finished repair looks like

The replacement section should be the correct material for what it joins, supported so it cannot rub or sag, and pressure tested with the system refilled slowly to purge air. Frozen-related work should end with the cause addressed rather than only the symptom: insulation restored, a foundation vent closed for winter, an exposed hose bib fitted properly, or a vulnerable run rerouted inside the heated space. Drywall repair, floor drying, and any larger repipe belong in a separate, clearly priced conversation.

## svc_drain_cleaning_lede

Drain cleaning is for an obstruction that can be reached and removed. The useful question is not which chemical to buy, but how far down the problem sits: a trap, a branch line, the building drain, or the lateral heading for the street.

## svc_drain_cleaning_body

### One fixture or a whole side of the house

A single slow lavatory, tub, or kitchen sink usually holds a local restriction: hair and soap at a trap, grease in a short branch, or a fouled tailpiece. The signal changes when two fixtures talk to each other. A toilet that gurgles while a shower drains, a tub that fills as the washer empties, or a basement floor drain that surfaces when nothing near it is running all place the restriction downstream of both fixtures. That is a building-drain or lateral question, and running more water to test the theory only moves waste onto a floor.

### What older drainage here is made of

Cast iron interior drainage was standard for decades and does not fail cleanly. It scales inward, catches paper, and can develop a rough channel that reopens with a cable and closes again within months. Galvanized branch lines behave the same way at a smaller diameter. Vitrified clay was the common material for the buried run, joined in short sections that can shift as soil moves. The city's own repair work illustrates how long clay survives underground: Watershed Management tied a January 2026 sinkhole investigation on Georgia Avenue SE to a 24-inch clay combination sewer pipe roughly 18 feet down. Private laterals of similar vintage sit under yards all over the east side.

### Roots go where the water is

Mature canopy is part of what makes Virginia-Highland, Candler Park, Kirkwood, and Grant Park pleasant, and it is also why laterals close. Fine roots follow moisture and vapor to a hairline crack or an open joint, then thicken inside the pipe until paper and grease complete the plug. Cutting them restores flow without repairing the opening that let them in, so the interval between backups is the real diagnostic. Yearly is a maintenance conversation. Quarterly is a pipe conversation. Excavation near protected trees also carries its own planning steps in the city, and from late June of 2025 the Office of Buildings has required an arborist meeting before permit applications that affect trees.

### Cable, jetter, auger: matching the tool to the line

A hand auger or a small drum machine belongs on a trap and branch work. A sectional or drum cable with the correct head reaches a building drain and a lateral, and head selection matters more than motor size. Hydro jetting scours grease and soft buildup from the pipe wall rather than punching a hole through it, which suits a kitchen line or a scaled main, but pressure applied to a cracked clay joint or a corroded cast iron section can do harm. Caustic drain openers deserve a plain warning: they seldom clear a real obstruction, they attack older metal, and they leave a hazardous pool for whoever opens the line next.

### Camera and locate work before anyone digs

After the line is open, a camera answers what a cable cannot. It shows the material, the joints, standing water in a belly, root mass, scale, offsets, and any missing section. A locator paired with the camera head marks the depth and position on the surface, which turns a vague estimate into a marked point in the yard. That distinction decides everything downstream: an obstruction that clears cleanly and shows sound pipe is maintenance, while a displaced joint or a collapsed section is a repair with excavation or a lining decision behind it. Utility locating is a separate step required before digging.

### Rain-driven backups are their own category

Not every backup involves a clog. Part of Atlanta's collection system still carries sanitary flow and storm water in the same pipe, so a heavy downpour can be the trigger rather than grease. If a fixture backs up while it rains and behaves normally in dry weather, that pattern belongs in the sewer-line discussion instead of the drain-cleaning one. ATL311 handles sewer backup reports at 404-546-0311 around the clock and states that a first responder investigates a suspected backup within eight hours, with a confirmed backup taking four to eight hours to contain.

### Keeping the line open afterward

An accessible cleanout is worth more than any product on a shelf. Without one, a technician works through a pulled toilet or a roof vent, which slows every future visit. Grease belongs in a container, not down a kitchen drain with hot water behind it. Wipes labeled flushable still bind to scale and roots. Where a house has repeatedly backed up, a scheduled clearing interval based on the camera evidence is cheaper than emergency work, and the interval itself tells the owner when the pipe has stopped being a maintenance item.

## svc_sewer_line_repair_lede

Sewer-line repair starts where clearing stops. Once a camera shows a broken joint, a sagging section, or roots entering the pipe, the questions become depth, length, access, permits, and whether the failure sits on the private lateral or in the public main.

## svc_sewer_line_repair_body

### Two federal decrees rebuilt the public side, not yours

Atlanta has spent decades under court supervision of its sewers. A 1998 decree addressed combined sewer overflows and a 1999 decree addressed sanitary sewer overflows and treatment, with the required improvements estimated by the city at 4 billion dollars. Watershed Management reports repairing or replacing more than 387 miles of aged sewer line and adding over 57 miles of capacity relief, and the city's 2024 briefing material describes 2.3 billion dollars of financed sewer improvements and an evaluation covering 1,574 miles of sewer main. That work continues, with the sanitary program running through 2027 under a court-granted extension. None of it repairs the pipe between a house and the street.

### What a combined pipe means on a rainy night

In a combined area, roof water, street runoff, and household waste share one pipe. The city's consent decree materials explain that overflow structures exist so the treatment plants are not flooded when rain volume exceeds what the combined pipes can carry, and that captured flow is sent for treatment once the rain stops. Combined pipe was reported as roughly 15 percent of the system, with about 85 percent of the city already separated and the program aiming near 90 percent. Downtown separation under the first decree covered 11 miles of combined line, and 18 miles of deep tunnel, reaching depths up to 270 feet, now store excess wet-weather flow. For a homeowner, the practical reading is narrow but important: in these areas, a hard rain can pressurize the public system at the same moment a private lateral is already restricted, and the lowest opening in the building is where that shows.

### Where private laterals actually fail

Clay sections separate at joints as clay soils shrink and swell. Cast iron thins and flakes until a hole opens along the bottom. A section can settle into a belly that holds water and solids without ever cracking. Roots enter through any of those defects. Grease hardens on a rough wall and narrows a pipe that has otherwise held up. The city's January 2026 work on Georgia Avenue SE, where a sinkhole investigation led to a 24-inch clay combination sewer about 18 feet below the surface, is a public example of the same material and the same age band that sits under private yards nearby.

### Proving the problem before the shovel

Repair decisions rest on three pieces of evidence: what the camera sees, where the locator puts it, and how the line behaves in dry versus wet weather. A defect that appears every time, regardless of rain, is a pipe defect. A backup that arrives only with storms suggests a wet-weather capacity path, and the city's own overflow record separates the two the same way, with regulators reporting an average of roughly 14 rain-induced and 242 dry-weather overflows a year across a recent multi-year window on the public system. A report should state depth, distance from the cleanout, pipe material, and the specific defect, because those four facts set the method and the cost.

### Backflow protection and its limits

A backwater valve is a one-way device installed in the building drain or lateral to stop waste from returning into the lowest fixtures. It is worth discussing with a plumber for a basement bathroom, a floor drain below street grade, or a house that has flooded before. Two cautions belong with it. A valve needs an accessible cover and periodic cleaning, because debris on the flapper defeats it silently. And a valve controls the direction of flow without correcting a broken pipe, so it should never be presented as an alternative to repairing a failed lateral.

### Repair methods and the paperwork behind them

A single defect near a cleanout at moderate depth can often be handled as a spot repair. Multiple offsets, a long root-infested run, or a flattened section point toward full replacement, or toward a trenchless option where cured-in-place lining or pipe bursting can renew a run with fewer excavations. Depth, soil, driveways, existing utilities, and mature trees decide which of those is realistic. Sewer work is permitted and inspected work in the city, and where digging affects trees the arborist meeting requirement now precedes the permit application. A plan that skips the permit conversation is not a cheaper plan.

### Renters, landlords, and the night it happens

Georgia law puts repair duty on the landlord, stating that the landlord shall keep the premises in repair, and a 2024 amendment means residential leases entered into or renewed from the first of July that year include a provision that the premises is fit for human habitation. A tenant facing waste on a floor should notify the landlord or manager in writing, keep dated photographs, and record every call. Whoever owns the pipe, the immediate steps are the same: stop all water use in the building, keep people and pets out of the affected rooms, and report the backup to 404-546-0311 so the city can determine whether the main is involved.
