## Compliance rule that governs the page

- **A contingency fee is prohibited in a criminal defense matter in Florida.**
  Rule 4-1.5(f)(3) of the Rules Regulating The Florida Bar: "A lawyer must not enter
  into an arrangement for, charge, or collect: (A) any fee in a domestic relations
  matter, the payment or amount of which is contingent on the securing of a divorce
  or on the amount of alimony or support, or property settlement in lieu thereof; or
  **(B) a contingent fee for representing a defendant in a criminal case.**"
  Source (official Florida Bar Chapter 4 text, RRTFB June 30, 2026):
  https://www-media.floridabar.org/uploads/2026/06/2026_12-JUNE-Chapter-4-RRTFB-1.pdf
  Mirrors ABA Model Rule 1.5(d)(2). Confirmed independently in Fla. Bar Ethics Op. 90-8
  ("a fee in a criminal case cannot be contingent. See Rule 4-1.5(F)(3)(b)"):
  https://www-media.floridabar.org/uploads/2017/04/FL-Bar-Ethics-Op-90-8.pdf
- **Fee must be communicated, and any nonrefundable part must be in writing.**
  Rule 4-1.5(e)(1): "the basis or rate of the fee and costs must be communicated to
  the client, preferably in writing, before or within a reasonable time after
  commencing the representation. A fee for legal services that is nonrefundable in
  any part must be confirmed in writing…" Same PDF as above.
- Reasonableness factors for fees and for costs: Rule 4-1.5(b)(1) and 4-1.5(b)(2),
  same PDF.
- **No dollar range for what a defense lawyer charges appears anywhere on the page.**
  Every number on the page is an amount Florida law or a Hillsborough County office
  publishes.

Exact statutory wording captured:

- 27.52(1)(b): "An applicant shall pay a $50 application fee to the clerk for each
  application for court-appointed counsel filed. The applicant shall pay the fee
  within 7 days after submitting the application." If unpaid before disposition, the
  court assesses it as part of the sentence, as a probation condition, or under
  s. 938.29.
- 27.52(1)(c): the clerk assigns "the first $50 of any fees or costs paid by an
  indigent person as payment of the application fee"; a person found indigent "may
  not be refused counsel … for failure to pay the fee."
- 27.52(2)(a): indigent if income "is equal to or below 200 percent of the
  then-current federal poverty guidelines prescribed for the size of the household
  … by the United States Department of Health and Human Services."
- 27.52(2)(a)1.: presumption of non-indigency at net equity of "$2,500 or more,"
  excluding homestead and one vehicle with net value not exceeding "$5,000."
- 27.52(4)(a)1.: bail "in an amount of $5,000 or more" is a factor.
- 938.05(1)(a)-(c): "Felonies: $225 … Misdemeanors: $60 … Criminal traffic
  offenses: $60."

Verification detail on the fine tiers — the statute's own wording, which differs from
the way this is usually summarised online: 775.083(1)(a) is **$15,000 for a life
felony**, and (b) is **$10,000 for a felony of the first *or* second degree**. A
first-degree felony is therefore $10,000, not $15,000. (c) $5,000 third-degree
felony, (d) $1,000 first-degree misdemeanor, (e) $500 second-degree misdemeanor or
noncriminal violation, (f) any higher amount equal to double the pecuniary gain to
the offender or double the pecuniary loss to the victim. 775.083 also defines
"conviction" to include a plea of guilty or nolo contendere "regardless of whether
adjudication is withheld."

938.29(1)(a) exact text: "Attorney's fees and costs shall be set in all cases at no
less than $50 per case when a misdemeanor or criminal traffic offense is charged and
no less than $100 per case when a felony offense is charged…" 938.29(1)(b): the fee
judgment is imposed "notwithstanding the defendant's present ability to pay."
