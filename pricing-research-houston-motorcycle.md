## Rules of professional conduct cited

Texas uses **Rule 1.04 of the Texas Disciplinary Rules of Professional Conduct** (not ABA-numbered 1.5).
Source: State Bar of Texas ethics site, "Fees" — https://legalethicstexas.com/resources/rules/texas-disciplinary-rules-of-professional-conduct/fees/ (retrieved 2026-08-23)

- **1.04(a)** — no illegal or unconscionable fee; "a fee is unconscionable if a competent lawyer could not form a reasonable belief that the fee is reasonable." No percentage stated anywhere in the rule.
- **1.04(b)** — eight reasonableness factors (time/labor/difficulty; preclusion of other work; customary local fee; amount involved and results; time limits; length of relationship; experience/reputation/ability; fixed vs. contingent).
- **1.04(d)** — contingent fee must be in writing; must state the method of determination; must state separate percentages for settlement, trial and appeal; must state which litigation/other expenses are deducted from the recovery and **whether they come out before or after the fee is calculated**; requires a written closing statement showing the outcome and, where money is collected, the remittance and its computation.
- **1.04(e)** — contingent fee prohibited for representing a defendant in a criminal case (cited on the page only to show why an injury claim is different).
- **1.04(f)(2)** — division of fees requires the client's written consent to the terms, identity of every lawyer, whether the division is by services or responsibility, and each participant's share.

Page language paraphrases the rule rather than quoting it verbatim, to clear the 15-word cross-site guard against the other Texas legal sites (austinwrongfuldeath…, dallaswrongfuldeath…).
