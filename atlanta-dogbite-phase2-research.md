## Core doctrine — O.C.G.A. § 51-2-7

Source: https://law.justia.com/codes/georgia/title-51/chapter-2/section-51-2-7/

Exact text: "A person who owns or keeps a vicious or dangerous animal of any kind and who, by
careless management or by allowing the animal to go at liberty, causes injury to another person
who does not provoke the injury by his own act may be liable in damages to the person so injured.
In proving vicious propensity, it shall be sufficient to show that the animal was required to be
at heel or on a leash by an ordinance of a city, county, or consolidated government, and the said
animal was at the time of the occurrence not at heel or on a leash."

Carve-outs: domesticated fowl including roosters with spurs; domesticated livestock.

## Steagald v. Eason, 300 Ga. 717, 797 S.E.2d 838 (Mar. 6, 2017), Docket S16G0293

Sources: https://www.animallaw.info/case/steagald-v-eason
and https://law.justia.com/cases/georgia/supreme-court/2017/s16g0293.html

- Snapping can reasonably be read as an attempted bite; owner knowledge is a jury question.
- The "first bite rule" label is "more than a bit misleading"; the rule "does not literally
  require a first bite" (Kringle v. Elliott).
- Test: at least one incident that would cause a prudent person to anticipate the actual
  incident that caused the injury.
- Disapproved Hamilton v. Walker, 235 Ga. App. 635.
- Barking and growling alone are insufficient (Huff v. Dyer; Custer v. Coward; Wade v. American
  Nat. Ins.; Durham v. Mooney). Lunging/jumping can create a jury issue (Green v. Wilson;
  Marshall v. Person).
- Footnote 2: the 1985 amendment changed "shall be liable" to "may be liable" to clarify the
  standard is negligence, not strict liability (see Eshleman v. Key, 297 Ga. 364, n.3). The same
  1985 amendment added the leash sentence.

## Local restraint ordinances

Fulton County Code § 34-205 — http://fultoncounty-ga.elaws.us/code/coor_ch34_artvi_div1_sec34-205
- Unlawful for an owner, custodian or harborer to allow a dog to leave the premises "unless such
  dog is securely under leash; said leash being not more than six feet long, and under the
  control of a competent person."
- On-premises confinement by fence, wall, enclosure or leash.
- Applies in unincorporated Fulton and in any municipality with a county animal-control agreement.
- Electronic confinement systems acceptable, but not for classified or guard dogs; tethering
  unlawful except while attended.

Fulton County Code § 34-281 — http://fultoncounty-ga.elaws.us/code/coor_ch34_artvi_div3_sec34-281
- Classified dog: leash no more than six feet in a street or public place, muzzled where the
  animal control officer determines, handled by a competent person; locked pen not sharing common
  fencing with a perimeter fence; attended, collared and leashed outside the enclosure on the
  owner's property; "beware of dog" sign conspicuously posted.

Atlanta Code § 18-152 (quarantine, Fulton portion) —
http://atlanta.elaws.us/code/coor_ptii_ch18_artiv_div3_sec18-152
- A dog or cat that bit a human "shall be immediately confined at the animal control shelter,
  veterinary hospital, or other such premises deemed acceptable by the health department" for
  **ten days from the date of the bite**; owner pays boarding.
- Unlawful to fail to surrender the animal "upon the sworn statement of the person bitten."
- Applies regardless of current rabies vaccination or tag. Other warm-blooded animals follow the
  Rabies Control Manual compiled by the Georgia Department of Public Health.

Atlanta Code § 18-11 (nuisance animals) —
http://atlanta.elaws.us/code/coor_ptii_ch18_arti_sec18-11
- Barking/howling 20 consecutive minutes; citation requires sworn affidavits from two unrelated
  adult witnesses at different addresses, or one adult witness with recorded video; affiants
  within a 1,000-foot radius; fines $150 / $250 / $350 / $500 / $1,000.

Atlanta Code § 18-61 (permits and impoundment) —
https://library.municode.com/ga/atlanta/codes/code_of_ordinances/177780?nodeId=PTIICOORENOR_CH18AN_ARTIIIDO_S18-62ADRACOREHECOFUCO
- $3.00 annual owner's permit per dog six months or older, $10.00 family cap.
- Impoundment $7.50 plus $3.00 per day boarding; seven days from mailed notice to reclaim.

## Responsible Dog Ownership Law (O.C.G.A. Title 4, Ch. 8)

- § 4-8-1 — https://law.justia.com/codes/georgia/title-4/chapter-8/article-1/section-4-8-1/ —
  state law sets minimum standards; local governments may adopt more restrictive control.
- § 4-8-21 — https://law.justia.com/codes/georgia/title-4/chapter-8/article-2/section-4-8-21/ —
  dangerous dog: substantial puncture of the skin by teeth without serious injury (a nip, scratch
  or abrasion is insufficient); or an aggressive attack causing a reasonable belief of imminent
  serious injury (barking, growling, showing teeth insufficient); or, off the owner's property,
  kills a pet animal. Vicious dog: inflicts serious injury. Serious injury: substantial risk of
  death; death, broken or dislocated bones, lacerations requiring multiple sutures, disfiguring
  avulsions; plastic surgery or hospital admission; protracted impairment including transmission
  of infection. Subsection (b): no classification where the injured person was trespassing,
  abusing the dog, or committing an offense under Chapter 5 of Title 16.
- § 4-8-22 — county jurisdiction is the unincorporated area, municipality within corporate limits;
  dog control officers designated.
- § 4-8-23 — https://law.justia.com/codes/georgia/title-4/chapter-8/article-2/section-4-8-23/ —
  officer mails dated notice within 72 hours; owner has 7 days to request a hearing (authority or
  probate court); hearing scheduled within 30 days; at least 10 days' written notice of date, time
  and place; determination mailed within 10 days; judicial review under 15-9-30.9 or 5-3-4.
- § 4-8-24 — immediate impoundment where the officer believes the dog poses a threat to public safety.
- § 4-8-26 — euthanasia required where a dog is found, after notice and hearing, to have seriously
  injured a human more than once (no injury before July 1, 2012 counts).
- § 4-8-27 — https://law.justia.com/codes/georgia/title-4/chapter-8/article-2/section-4-8-27/ —
  vicious dog registration requires a secure enclosure, clearly visible warning signs at all
  entrances, a microchip injected between the shoulder blades, and proof of liability insurance of
  at least $50,000.00 from an insurer authorized in the state; one certificate per domicile; no
  person may own more than one vicious dog; annual renewal; failure to renew within 10 days is a
  violation.
- § 4-8-28 — owner of a classified dog must notify the dog control officer within 24 hours if the
  dog is loose or has attacked a human.
- § 4-8-30 — confiscation on any violation; owner recovers on paying reasonable confiscation and
  housing costs and proving compliance; if not compliant within 14 days, the dog is released to a
  shelter or euthanized.
- § 4-8-31 — no local government or its employees liable for failure to enforce the article.
- § 4-8-32 — violations are misdemeanors.

## Deadlines and fault

- § 9-3-33 — https://law.justia.com/codes/georgia/title-9/chapter-3/article-2/section-9-3-33/ —
  injuries to the person 2 years; reputation 1 year; loss of consortium 4 years.
- § 9-3-32 — https://law.justia.com/codes/georgia/title-9/chapter-3/article-2/section-9-3-32/ —
  personal property recovery, conversion or destruction 4 years.
- § 9-3-90(b) — https://law.justia.com/codes/georgia/title-9/chapter-3/article-5/section-9-3-90/ —
  a person under 18 when the cause accrues gets the same period after turning 18.
- § 51-12-33 — https://law.justia.com/codes/georgia/title-51/chapter-12/article-2/section-51-12-33/
  — (a) award reduced in proportion to the plaintiff's fault; (b) apportioned severally, not
  jointly, no contribution; (d)(1) nonparty fault considered on notice filed not later than 120
  days before trial; (g) no damages where the plaintiff is 50 percent or more responsible.

## Landlord and premises

- § 44-7-14 — https://law.justia.com/codes/georgia/title-44/chapter-7/article-1/section-44-7-14/ —
  "Having fully parted with possession and the right of possession, the landlord is not
  responsible to third persons for damages resulting from the negligence or illegal use of the
  premises by the tenant; provided, however, the landlord is responsible for damages arising from
  defective construction or for damages arising from the failure to keep the premises in repair."
- Tyner v. Matta-Troncoso, S18G0364 (Ga. 2019) —
  https://law.justia.com/cases/georgia/supreme-court/2019/s18g0364.html — broken gate latch at a
  rental; tenants' dogs escaped and attacked a woman walking her dogs roughly two blocks away.
  The Supreme Court reversed the Court of Appeals, holding there was no genuine issue of material
  fact on whether the failure to repair the latch caused the injuries. Causation, not notice,
  decided it.
- § 51-3-1 — https://law.justia.com/codes/georgia/title-51/chapter-3/article-1/section-51-3-1/ —
  owner/occupier liable to invitees for failure to exercise ordinary care in keeping the premises
  and approaches safe.
- § 51-3-3 — https://law.justia.com/codes/georgia/title-51/chapter-3/article-1/section-51-3-3/ —
  a lawful possessor owes a trespasser no duty except to refrain from willful or wanton injury.

## Rabies control

- § 31-19-1 — https://law.justia.com/codes/georgia/title-31/chapter-19/section-31-19-1/ — county
  boards of health have primary responsibility for rabies control.
- § 31-19-4 — https://law.justia.com/codes/georgia/title-31/chapter-19/section-31-19-4/ — a person
  bitten by an animal reasonably suspected of being rabid must immediately notify the county board
  of health; the owner or custodian must notify and confine.
- § 31-19-5 — https://law.justia.com/codes/georgia/title-31/chapter-19/section-31-19-5/ — county
  boards must adopt inoculation rules.

## Fees — Georgia Rule of Professional Conduct 1.5

https://www.gabar.org/handbook?rule=rule55 (full working notes in
`/home/user/workspace/local-sites/pricing-research-atlanta-dogbite.md`)
- 1.5(a): no unreasonable fee; eight enumerated reasonableness factors.
- 1.5(b): communication of the basis or rate of the fee.
- 1.5(c)(1): a contingent fee agreement must be in writing and must state the method by which the
  fee is determined, the percentages accruing on settlement, trial and appeal, the litigation and
  other expenses deducted from the recovery, and whether those expenses are deducted before or
  after the percentage is calculated.
- 1.5(c)(2): written closing statement at the conclusion of a contingent matter.
- 1.5(d): no contingency in domestic relations tied to divorce, alimony, support or a property
  settlement in lieu of it; none for a criminal defendant.
- Maximum penalty for a Rule 1.5 violation is a public reprimand. No Georgia percentage cap exists.
