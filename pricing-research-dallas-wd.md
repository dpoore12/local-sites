Mode: `fees` / `fee_kind: contingency`. All sources retrieved **2026-08-23**.

## Bottom line on caps

**Texas has no percentage cap on a contingency fee in a wrongful death case.** The governing rule is **Rule 1.04** of
the Texas Disciplinary Rules of Professional Conduct — Texas does **not** use 1.5. Nothing in Rule 1.04 or in Civil
Practice and Remedies Code chapter 71 states a maximum percentage. The page says so in those terms.

## Rules cited on the page

Source: https://www.legalethicstexas.com/resources/rules/texas-disciplinary-rules-of-professional-conduct/fees/

| Subdivision | Use on this page |
|---|---|
| 1.04(a) | Prohibits an illegal or unconscionable fee; unconscionable defined as a fee no competent lawyer could form a reasonable belief was reasonable. No number in the rule. |
| 1.04(b) | Eight factors. The page names three of them: amount involved and results obtained; fee customarily charged in the locality for similar legal services; time limitations imposed by the circumstances. |
| 1.04(c) | Basis or rate communicated, preferably in writing, before or within a reasonable time after representation begins. |
| 1.04(d) | Written and client-signed; method of determination; **separate percentages for settlement, trial and appeal**; expenses deducted from the recovery and whether **before or after** the percentage; end-of-matter written statement of outcome plus remittance and how it was determined. |
| 1.04(f) | **Fee division between firms** — proportional to services performed or joint responsibility; client's **written consent** to the identity of every lawyer/firm, the basis of the division, and each share; total fee may not be increased because more than one lawyer is involved. |

## Divergence from Austin

Different build: Dallas opens on the two-clerk problem (district court + probate court), then Rule 1.04, then 1.04(f)
fee division between referring firms, then the wrongful-death/survival two-track structure, then Dallas County probate
and sheriff costs. Austin opens rule-first and ends on Travis County. All fee figures are Dallas County's own. The
1.04(a) unconscionability sentence is phrased differently from Austin's. Verified: zero shared 15-word runs across all
83 sites.
