## Kyle's business in one paragraph

Kyle builds a plain website for one service in one city, for example auto glass repair in
New Orleans. He gets it onto page one of Google and puts a tracking number on it. Until he
has a client, the calls go to voicemail. Once the site is getting calls, he phones the local
businesses that do that work and offers a free week of calls with no contract and no card.
If the owner makes money, they agree a flat monthly fee, about $600 a site on average, and
never sign anything. He says the company does about $100K a month with partners, he keeps
about $35K a month, and overhead is about $25 a site a month. All of this is self-reported,
and the interviews lead to his $2,980 course.

## His method, step by step

1. **Pick a market where the competition is weak.** He sizes up the top three sites for the
   search (their links, authority, content, photos, headings) and picks the easiest. He says
   to avoid "personal injury lawyer Los Angeles" markets and to go after small businesses whose
   websites haven't been touched since 2005. His niches: junk removal, auto glass, epoxy floors,
   spray foam, gutters, towing. In the Doser interview he adds: cities under about 100,000
   people, and no plumbing, HVAC or roofing in big metros.
2. **Build a plain site that tells Google exactly what and where.** About 10 pages, ugly is
   fine. "New Orleans auto glass repair" everywhere, written for Google, not for readers.
3. **Rank it: 80% is content plus backlinks.** Citations and headings are the other 20%.
   He says he still does what he did in 2019 and ignores Google updates and schema.
4. **Keep adding content until it reaches page one.** He doesn't build a fixed site and wait.
5. **Get links two ways:**
   - **Link swaps in his paid community.** Students put links to each other's sites.
     rentlocalsites.com: "we help each other get backlinks and citations in the group."
   - **The copycat method** (the one he says he uses most): look up a competitor's backlinks
     in Ahrefs or Mangools, in any city, and take the free ones (directories, blog comments).
     "You just need some activity."
6. **Measure calls, not rankings.** A site in position 3 to 5 still gets 50 or more calls.
7. **Sell from proof.** Call the local businesses: "I've got a site getting 50 calls a month
   and I'm not a junk removal company. Can I send them to you for a week?" It takes him 1 to 3
   free trials to land a client. Clients who stay past 6 months tend to stay for years.

Timeline he gives: his first win came at 3 months (and that was a friend who paid up front).
He reached $10K a month by the end of year 1 and $100K a month around year 4 or 5. Students
are told to expect 3 to 6 months to the first client. His partner Shiv says ranking takes 2
to 9 weeks after the build.

What he does **not** say in the episode: anything about Google Business Profiles. His sales
page promises page one "plus showing up in the Maps section."

## Why we're failing, in plain words

1. **Most of our sites are in the fights Kyle says never to pick.** He picks markets where
   the competition is "Joe, Bill and Steve" with old websites. Ours are mostly lawyers and
   big-city plumbing, AC and garage doors, where the winners have hundreds of pages and real
   marketing budgets. A 9-page site can't climb past them, and that's why the 83 have sat at
   page six for two months.
2. **We skipped half of his "80%".** He says rankings come from content *and* links. We
   built the content and have essentially no links. We never joined a link-swap group, never
   copied a competitor's free links, and never filed one directory listing.
3. **We stopped adding.** His rule is to keep adding until the site reaches page one. Our
   live sites haven't changed since 8 Sep, and the extra pages written on 14–15 Sep were
   never published.
4. **No Maps presence.** Where we already reach page one ("allen ac repair" at 4.2,
   "salinas car accident lawyers" at 2.5) we still got zero clicks, because the map pack
   sits above us. Kyle's sales page counts Maps as part of ranking.
5. **We only had one site in a "Kyle market" until September.** The four test sites
   (Kalamazoo and Evansville towing, Savannah and Wilmington mobile mechanic) were the only
   real copies of his method. Evansville's number turned out to be recycled.

## Our own data agrees with him

- The four Kyle-style test sites reached an average position of **31** in six weeks. The 83
  are at **58** after two months. That's twice as high in less time, with no links.
- Those 4 sites got **4 clicks**. The 83 sites got **5**. Per site, that's about 17 times more.
- The **only real customer** in 272 calls (16 Sep) called
  **wilmingtonmobilemechanicexperts.com**, one of the four Kyle-style sites. None of the 83
  produced one.

## About his community

The link-swap group is real: it's the Facebook group inside his $2,980 course
(rentlocalsites.com, payment plans of $1,750 × 2 or $1,000 × 4). It's one of his two link
sources; the copycat method costs nothing but time. Swapped links and blog comments are on
Google's link-spam list. They seem to work in weak markets because nobody there is competing
hard. They won't lift a Cincinnati injury-lawyer site past firms spending $50K a month.
