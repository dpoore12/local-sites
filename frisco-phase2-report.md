## What I found wrong in the brief

1. **"Newer Texas legislation restricting HOA authority over certain exterior items" does not cover garage doors, and the statute cited around the web for it does not exist.** There is no §209.0151 in Property Code chapter 209. Secondary blogs attributing an aesthetics limitation to HB 5225 (89R) are wrong: [HB 5225 as introduced](https://capitol.texas.gov/tlodocs/89R/billtext/html/HB05225I.htm) amends §209.0061(b) on fine policy, adds §209.0066 on assessments and a §209.0094(h) lien limit — nothing about exterior appearance. Chapter 202's carve-outs cover wind/hail-resistant and solar shingles (§202.011), solar devices, flags, religious items, rain barrels, xeriscape, firearms storage and pool enclosures (§202.022) — [ch. 202 text](https://statutes.capitol.texas.gov/Docs/PR/pdf/PR.202.pdf). **Garage doors are not among them.** So the honest finding, and what the page now says, is that a garage door color, material, window or profile change remains subject to architectural review; the owner's remedy is the §209.00505 written-denial/appeal/hearing track plus the §202.004(a) standard, under which a discretionary association decision is presumed reasonable unless a court finds by a preponderance of the evidence that it was arbitrary, capricious or discriminatory (with civil damages up to $200 per day of violation under §202.004(c)). The real recent change is [SB 711 (2025)](https://capitol.texas.gov/tlodocs/89R/billtext/pdf/SB00711F.pdf), which amended the architectural-review section and added online dedicatory-instrument requirements — procedural, not a garage door exemption.
2. **The pricing page was not over its ceiling at the start.** It measured 1746 words on the phase-1 check. It went to 1815 only after the phase 2 navigation added service links to every page, so the trim was still needed — just for a different reason than the brief predicted.
3. **No conflict between site.json and the brief.** Both give Frisco, TX, Collin and Denton counties and garage door repair; the four slugs in site.json were used exactly as written. site.json's neighborhoods (Phillips Creek Ranch, Stonebriar, Panther Creek Estates, Christie Ranch, The Trails, Grayhawk) were left to the existing phase-1 blocks — I could not verify plat years or county placement for individual subdivisions from primary sources, so I did not add any new per-neighborhood claims rather than invent them.
4. **Frisco's "builder registration requirement" exists but is broader than the brief implies.** It is a contractor registration with Building Inspections covering general, electrical, mechanical, plumbing, pool, fence, sign, backflow and irrigation contractors plus third-party raters and the residential engineer of record, and subcontractors are validated before a permit is issued.
