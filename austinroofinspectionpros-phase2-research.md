## Roofing contractor regulation in Texas
- **Texas does NOT license or register roofing contractors at the state level.** RCAT: "The Texas Department of Licensing & Regulation (TDLR) does NOT currently administer licensure for roofing contractors. Anyone can call themselves a roofer... not required to be knowledgeable, insured, licensed, or registered with the state." Voluntary RCAT license: principal of a Texas-domiciled roofing company for 2+ continuous years, fixed physical address, exams at 70%+, $500,000 combined single limit GL (commercial) / $300,000 (residential). https://www.rcat.net/licensing.html
- HB 3344, 89(R), **introduced only** — would create Occupations Code ch. 1306 "Roofing Contractor Consumer Protection Act", §1306.151 license requirement, Class C misdemeanor, license requirement effective Sept 1, 2026. Passage not verified (capitol bill-history page robots-blocked), so not asserted on the site. https://capitol.texas.gov/tlodocs/89R/billtext/html/HB03344I.htm

## Adjuster / claim-handling limits
- Ins. Code §4101.251(b): "A roofing contractor may not act as an adjuster or advertise to adjust claims for any property for which the contractor is providing or may provide roofing services, regardless of whether the contractor holds a license under this chapter." https://codes.findlaw.com/tx/insurance-code/ins-sect-4101-251/
- TDI, "Roofing and insurance: Know the law" (updated 3/24/2025): roofer may not act as public adjuster or advertise to; advertisement includes a website (28 TAC §21.102(1)); illegal to offer to waive/rebate/absorb a deductible; AG complaint line 800-621-0508. https://www.tdi.texas.gov/consumer/storms/roofing-and-insurance-know-the-law.html
- TDI unlicensed-adjusting FAQ (May 2014): roofer MAY discuss amount of damage, appropriate replacement, reasonable cost, its own estimate/supplements; may NOT advocate for the consumer or discuss policy coverages and exclusions. https://www.tdi.texas.gov/bulletins/2014/documents/unlicensedfaq.pdf
- TDI Bulletin B-0006-21: "Contractors are not prohibited from providing estimates or discussing those estimates and other technical information with an insurer or its adjuster." https://www.tdi.texas.gov/bulletins/2021/B-0006-21.html

## Deductible statute
- Ins. Code §707.002: insured "shall pay any deductible applicable to a first-party claim." https://texas.public.law/statutes/tex._ins._code_section_707.002
- Ins. Code §707.004: insurer may withhold recoverable depreciation/holdback until reasonable proof of deductible payment (canceled check, money order receipt, credit card statement, executed installment/financing contract). https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/insurance-code/title-5/subtitle-f/chapter-707/section-707-004/

## Disaster remediation contracts / door-to-door

- Texas AG: door-to-door sales over $25 — 3 business days to cancel (B&C ch. 601). https://www.texasattorneygeneral.gov/consumer-protection/home-real-estate-and-travel/door-door-sales-3-day-right-rescission

## Inspection standards
- TREC 22 TAC §535.228(c) roof covering: inspect from roof surface, report vantage point, water penetration, previous repairs; deficiencies in fasteners, adhesion, covering, flashing, skylights, penetrations. NOT required to: inspect from roof level if unsafe, determine remaining life expectancy or number of layers, **identify latent hail damage**, exhaustively examine fasteners. §535.228(d): report average attic insulation depth, attic ventilation not performing, deflections related to framing/decking, deficiencies in framing and decking; not required to enter openings under 22 by 30 inches or headroom under 30 inches. https://www.trec.texas.gov/online-sops
- HUD Handbook 4000.1 (II.D.3.h Roof Covering): appraiser must report if roof has less than two years remaining life and make the appraisal subject to inspection by a professional roofer. https://www.hud.gov/sites/dfiles/OCHCO/documents/4000.1hsgh.pdf
- TDI "Repairing my roof after a storm": select shingles tested to ASTM D3161 Class F or ASTM D7158 Class H; have the deck inspected and replace deteriorated decking before installing a new covering; min No. 15 felt, two layers 2:12–4:12, one layer 4:12+; nails galvanized/stainless 12 gauge (0.105") shank, 3/8" head; "The code does not permit staples!"; shingles only on slopes 2:12 or greater. https://www.tdi.texas.gov/wind/documents/RepairingRoofAfterStorm.pdf

## Hail damage forensics (NRCA)
- "Asphalt shingles... must be punctured or fractured by hail before they are considered functionally damaged." Fractures start on the underside (max tensile strain) and propagate upward, so an impact fracture is always detectable on the underside. Soft spots felt with fingertips are "bruises." Granule loss alone is not functional damage; it begins at manufacture and is normal weathering. A roof is not functionally damaged "unless the roof's water-shedding capabilities are diminished or the roof's expected service life is reduced." Footfall splits on wood do not create surface indentations; near-linear fractures on slate and tile usually mean foot traffic. Threshold hail sizes: three-tab lightweight 1 in.; laminated heavyweight 1.25 in.; wood shingles 1.25 in.; medium wood shakes 0.5 in.; clay tile 1.5 in.; concrete tile 1.75 in. https://www.nrca.net/roofingguidelines/pdf?id=158908&k=1789444
- NRCA hailstorm consumer sheet: "Most hail-related damage is cosmetic and will not affect the life of roof shingles." Blister = shingle expansion caused by trapped gases such as air or water vapor; puncturing a blister or spreading mastic destroys evidence. https://nrcawebstorage.blob.core.windows.net/files/nrca_website/documents/technical/Roof-Repair-After-a-Hailstorm.pdf

## Code and permits (Austin)
- Austin adopted the 2021 IRC, effective September 1, 2021. https://publicinput.com/2021TechnicalCodeChanges
- Austin Table R301.2 as adopted: ultimate design wind speed **105 mph**; ice barrier underlayment required **NO**; weathering negligible; frost line 12 in. https://up.codes/viewer/austin/irc-2021/chapter/3/building-planning
- R905.1.2: ice barrier required only where Table R301.2 shows a history of ice damming; R905.2.4.1 shingle wind-classification table begins at 110 mph. https://up.codes/viewer/austin/irc-2021/chapter/9/roof-assemblies
- Attic ventilation: minimum net free ventilating area 1/150 of the vented space; exception 1/300 where 40–50% of the vent area is in the upper portion (within 3 ft of the ridge) and, in Climate Zones 6–8 only, a Class I or II vapor retarder is installed. https://up.codes/s/minimum-vent-area ; DOE Building America: same 1/300 and 1/150 rules, upper vents at least 3 ft above the eave vents. https://basc.pnnl.gov/information/calculating-attic-passive-ventilation
- Roof recover not permitted where the existing roof is water soaked or deteriorated, where the covering is slate/clay/cement/asbestos-cement tile, or where there are two or more applications of any covering (IRC R908.3.1.1). https://up.codes/s/roof-recover-not-allowed
- Austin work exempt from building permits: asphalt shingles replacing existing asphalt shingles, and replacement of any roof covering that does not adversely affect the roof structure, unless the property is in the Wildland-Urban Interface area and 50% or more of the roofing is replaced; roof decking not exceeding 128 square feet; exempt work must still comply with the codes. https://www.austintexas.gov/development-services/work-exempt-building-permits
- Express permit triggers (site.json fact): decking replacement over 128 sq ft or >50% roofing replacement in the WUI area. https://www.austintexas.gov/development-services/express-permits

## Neighborhood-specific roof rules
- Hyde Park Design Standards 3.5 Roofs: "Retain the original roof pitches and profiles on the building." In replacing roof materials, consider the original material first, then a product resembling it such as a fiberglass shingle; metal roofs also acceptable; do not use shaped, scalloped or diamond shingles unless original; preserve original gable/attic vents and roof brackets. https://services.austintexas.gov/edims/document.cfm?id=407691
- Mueller design guidelines: sloping roofs with gables, hips and dormers; principal building slopes generally 6:12 to 9:12, up to 12:12 where living space is in the roof; south-facing slopes for photovoltaics subject to NCC approval; rooftop equipment integrated and not visible from streets. https://www.austintexas.gov/sites/default/files/files/Redevelopment/Redevelopment_Projects/Mueller/Chapter_2_opt.pdf
- Certificate of Appropriateness required for all non-routine exterior work in historic districts. https://services.austintexas.gov/edims/document.cfm?id=287786
- Austin tree canopy 41% citywide, 2022 (site.json fact). https://www.austintexas.gov/climate/austin-nature-city/austin-closer-its-canopy-goal-50-2050
