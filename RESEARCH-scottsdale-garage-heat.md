## NWS Phoenix heat records (https://www.weather.gov/psr/ExtremeTemps)
- Phoenix period of record 1896–2025.
- Days >= 100F: average 94 (POR), 111 (1991–2020 normals); fewest 48 (1913); greatest 145 (2020).
- Days >= 110F: average 13 (POR), 21 (1991–2020); fewest none (1911); greatest 70 (2024).
- Longest >=100F streak: 113 consecutive days, 27 May 2024 – 16 Sep 2024.
- Longest >=110F streak: 31 consecutive days, 30 Jun 2023 – 30 Jul 2023.
- All-time hottest: 122F on 26 Jun 1990.

## NWS threshold dates (https://www.weather.gov/psr/FirstandLastTemperatures)
- Phoenix average first 110F day Jun 11, average last Aug 26 (1991–2020).
- Earliest 110F May 8, 1989; latest 110F Oct 7, 2024.
- 100F: average first May 2, average last Oct 5; earliest Mar 26, 1988; latest Oct 27, 2016.

## NWS 2023 monsoon review (https://www.weather.gov/psr/2023MonsoonSeasonReview)
- Maricopa County had 42 Excessive Heat Warning days in 2023, second to 49 in 2020.
- 29 of those 42 ran consecutively July 1–July 29, a record.
- Phoenix had 30 consecutive days of 110+ highs and 17 days with lows of 90+, both records.
- Aug 31, 2023: outflow-driven dust storm covered most of the Phoenix metro, visibility a
  quarter mile or less, winds in excess of 55 mph, downed trees and power lines.
- NOTE / DISCREPANCY: site.json local_fact says "30 consecutive days" in July 2023. The NWS
  extremes table lists the record streak as 31 days spanning 30 Jun – 30 Jul 2023. Both are
  NWS pages; the monsoon review counts the July-only portion. Copy uses the extremes table
  figure with its dates and does not contradict site.json.

## NWS Western Region Tech Memo 290, "Blowing Dust and Dust Storms" (July 2016)
https://www.weather.gov/media/wrh/online_publications/TMs/TM-290.pdf
- Haboob = wall of dust moving with a thunderstorm gust front, several hundred to a couple
  thousand meters deep; can span up to 160 km horizontally and 2.4 km vertically (Idso 1972).
- 1955–2013 statistics rank blowing dust the 3rd deadliest weather phenomenon in Arizona
  after flooding and extreme heat/cold. 1,521 events 1955–2011, 157 fatalities, 1,324 injuries.
- June–August accounts for 34 percent of events; 36 percent occur 4 PM to 6 PM MST.

## 16 CFR Part 1211 (https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CFR-2015-title16-vol2/pdf/CFR-2015-title16-vol2-part1211.pdf)
- 1211.1: applies to residential garage door operators manufactured on or after Jan 1, 1993.
- 1211.5(a)(3): Composite Operational and Cycling Test run 14 days at temperature extremes of
  -35C (-31F) and 70C (158F).
- 1211.5(a)(8): Endurance test conditioning 14 days at ambient air temperature of 60C (140F),
  or 10C (18F) above the operating temperature of the control.
- 1211.4(c): solid-state circuits performing entrapment protection must meet UL 991 including
  environmental and stress tests.
- 1211.7(a)(1): past the first 1 foot of downward travel, operator must initiate reversal within
  2 seconds of contact with the obstruction, then return the door to and stop at full upmost.
- 1211.7(b)(1): floor test object is 1 inch high.
- 1211.7(f)(1): position-monitoring systems must monitor door position at increments not
  greater than 1 inch.
- 1211.7(g)(1): non-position-monitoring operators must reverse and return to full up when the
  lower limit device is not actuated within 30 seconds of the start of the close cycle.
- 1211.10(c): a mechanical switch or relay in an entrapment protection circuit must withstand
  100,000 cycles of operation and still function.
- 1211.10(d): if such a switch or relay fails and entrapment protection is lost, the operator
  must become inoperative and hold the door within 1 foot of the uppermost position.

## DASMA
- TDS 190, Factors Affecting Spring Cycle Life
  https://www.dasma.com/wp-content/uploads/pubs/TechDataSheets/CommercialResidential/TDS190.pdf
  - Doors left open for extended periods regularly expose springs to increased temperature,
    moisture and air-quality variation; doors in climates subject to extreme heat should be
    kept closed where possible.
  - Painting a door or adding components can push door weight past manufacturer specification;
    a technician can weigh the door with the counterbalance disabled.
  - Regular lubrication per the manufacturer's instructions maximizes spring cycle life.
  - Spring cycle life is not the same as door cycle life; ANSI/DASMA 109 is the cycle-life test.
- TDS 185, Thermal Bowing of Garage Doors with Bonded Core Sections
  https://www.dasma.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/12/TDS185.pdf
  - Bowing is inherent, not a defect. Larger temperature differential = more bow. Darker doors
    facing the sun bow more than white doors. Without reinforcement, thermal bow on a 20-foot
    wide door could exceed several inches. Reported field issues include the top section
    rubbing the header, or a gap at the header. Horizontal struts are the usual remedy.
- TDS 364, Installation Location of Photoelectric Sensors on Residential Garage Doors
  https://www.dasma.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/12/TDS364.pdf
  - Lowest eye lens must emit a beam no higher than 6 inches above the garage floor.
  - Sensors usually located up to 20 feet apart, within 6 inches of the path of the door.
  - Monthly testing of the inherent reversing mechanism and of the photo eyes is called for.
- ANSI/DASMA 102 https://www.dasma.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/01/ANSIDASMA102.pdf
  - 2.2 one cycle = closed to fully open and back to closed.
  - 9.2.1 sections must not deflect more than 1/120 of door width in the horizontal position;
    9.2.2 horizontal track assembly not more than 1/240 of door height, measured after 24 hours.

## City of Scottsdale
- Minimum permit list (https://eservices.scottsdaleaz.gov/bldgresources/minimumpermit):
  "Window/Door Replacement — Replacement of an existing window and or door at a single-family
  residence." Conditions: installed in an existing opening, no structural changes; SHGC 0.25
  max and U-factor 0.40 max; leave energy stickers on until the inspector approves.
- Home improvement page (https://www.scottsdaleaz.gov/planning-development/home-improvement):
  no-permit list covers replacing non-structural items such as glass in doors or windows,
  interior doors and hardware, "not affecting a pool enclosure or garage." Minimum permits
  "require the applicant to schedule a progress and final inspection." Exterior painting in
  Environmentally Sensitive Lands and Historic Property zoning districts needs no permit but
  does need planning department approval.
- Code adoption history
  (https://www.scottsdaleaz.gov/docs/default-source/scottsdaleaz/codes---ordinances/building-codes/code-adoption-history.pdf?sfvrsn=12050a91_2):
  2021 IBC and IRC and 2021 IEBC adopted by Ordinance 4550 on 9/20/2022; 2020 NEC same
  ordinance; 2021 IECC and IgCC by Ordinance 4576 on 12/6/2022.
- Development Review Board "Reviews architectural and urban designs for all development types
  (except single family homes)"
  (https://www.scottsdaleaz.gov/planning-development/about-planning-and-development).
- Historic Property Ordinance requires the Historic Preservation Commission to review and
  approve exterior alterations and demolition for buildings on the Scottsdale Historic
  Register; certificates issued; denial appealable to City Council; process starts with a
  Pre-Application Request (https://www.scottsdaleaz.gov/historic-preservation-program).
- ESL Overlay covers 134 square miles of desert and mountain areas located north and east of
  the Central Arizona Project canal (https://www.scottsdaleaz.gov/codes-and-ordinances/eslo).
- Permit fee floor of $121 per single-discipline permit, and same amount for a reinspection,
  per site.json pricing anchor (Resolution 13391 fee schedule, effective July 1, 2025).

## Licensing
- Arizona ROC classifications (https://roc.az.gov/license-classifications): there is no
  garage-door-specific residential classification. R-7 Carpentry (and commercial C-7, dual
  CR-7) covers rough and finish carpentry, hardware, millwork, metal studs, metal doors or
  door frames, and windows. R-60/C-60 Finish Carpentry covers metal doors and automatic door
  closers. R-61 Carpentry, Remodeling and Repairs covers general remodeling for projects of
  $50,000 or less including labor and materials.
- ARS 32-1121 handyman exemption threshold of $1,000 aggregate contract price with no permit
  required, and the bar on splitting a project to get under it (https://www.azleg.gov/ars/32/01121.htm),
  as cited in site.json.
