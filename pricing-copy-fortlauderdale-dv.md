## pricing_lede

A domestic violence accusation in Broward County usually runs on two tracks at once: a criminal case the State controls, and a civil injunction the other person files. Defense work on the criminal side cannot be billed as a share of the outcome, the injunction petition cannot be charged a filing fee at all, and the amounts Florida attaches to a conviction are fixed by statute.

## pricing_body

### A defense fee cannot ride on the verdict

Rule 4-1.5(f)(3)(B) of the Rules Regulating The Florida Bar is unqualified: where the client is the defendant in a criminal case, a Florida lawyer may not arrange for, charge or collect a fee contingent on the outcome. The companion subparagraph, (f)(3)(A), covers domestic relations matters where payment turns on securing a divorce or on the amount of alimony, support or property settlement. A battery charge sits under (B): the client is a defendant in a prosecution, and a percentage of a dropped charge or an acquittal is not an arrangement a Florida lawyer may make.

What is left is an hourly rate against a deposit, or a fixed sum for a stated scope. Scope is where two quoted numbers stop being comparable: a fixed sum that ends when the State announces its filing decision is a different agreement from one that carries through a motion hearing, a deposition of the complaining witness, and a trial.

### The injunction side costs the petitioner nothing to start

Florida is explicit here. Section 741.30(2)(a) provides that notwithstanding any other law, the assessment of a filing fee for a petition for protection against domestic violence is prohibited. The clerk is instead reimbursed by the Justice Administrative Commission at $40 per petition issued, and out of that reimbursement the clerk pays any law enforcement agency serving the injunction a fee that may not exceed $20. Section 741.30(2)(b) adds that no bond is required for entry of an injunction, and the statute states that the cause of action does not require either party to be represented by an attorney.

The Broward County Clerk of Courts says the same thing in plainer words on its domestic violence page: there is no fee associated with filing a restraining order. Petitions are taken at the Central Courthouse in Fort Lauderdale and at the West Regional Courthouse in Plantation, and the clerk directs anyone seeking an injunction outside posted hours to the Women In Distress crisis line.

### What a conviction costs, apart from any fee

Battery under section 784.03 is a first-degree misdemeanor unless a prior battery conviction applies, in which case it becomes a third-degree felony. Section 775.082(4)(a) caps a first-degree misdemeanor at a definite term not exceeding one year, and (4)(b) caps a second-degree misdemeanor at sixty days.

Then the domestic violence statutes add their own layer. Section 938.08 requires the court to impose a surcharge of $201 for an offense of domestic violence as described in section 741.28, with $85 going to the Domestic Violence Trust Fund, $1 retained by the clerk, and the remainder to the county to defray the cost of incarcerating people sentenced under section 741.283 and to train law enforcement. Payment is a condition of probation or other court-ordered supervision.

Section 741.281 requires the court to order a minimum term of one year's probation on a finding of guilt, a withheld adjudication, or a plea of nolo contendere to a crime of domestic violence, and to order attendance and completion of a batterers' intervention program as a condition of that probation. Section 741.283 sets minimum jail: where the person intentionally caused bodily harm, ten days for a first offense, fifteen for a second, and twenty for a third or subsequent one, rising to fifteen, twenty and thirty days when the offense happened in the presence of a family or household member under sixteen.

### The trap is the quick plea

People try to make a domestic violence case cheap by resolving it fast. Read the paragraph above again before doing that. A plea that keeps the case short still triggers the one-year probation term, the intervention program, the $201 surcharge, and the statutory minimum jail where bodily harm was found. A withheld adjudication does not remove the probation-and-program requirement, because section 741.281 is written to include it. On the injunction side, a final judgment must state on its face that it is a first-degree misdemeanor under section 790.233 for the respondent to have any firearm or ammunition in his or her care, custody, possession or control.

One more line is worth knowing. Under section 741.30(6)(a)6., where a court orders a respondent into treatment, intervention or counseling services, those services are paid for by the respondent. That is a recurring cost that shows up after the hearing, and it never appears in any number quoted at the front end.

### Court-appointed counsel and the paperwork after

Broward's Clerk publishes the figures for the appointed-counsel route: an applicant pays a $50 application fee for each application for court-appointed counsel filed, payable online, in person or by money order. Later cleanup has its own price list — the clerk's sealing and expungement packet costs sixty cents, the initial charge from the Florida Department of Law Enforcement is $75, and the clerk adds a $42 statutory processing fee on top of copies and certification.

### What to ask before signing

Ask which proceedings the written agreement covers, because the criminal case and the injunction hearing are separate matters that often need separate work. Ask what happens if the State files a charge different from the arrest paperwork, or adds a violation of an existing injunction. Ask who pays for a records subpoena, a deposition transcript, or a phone extraction if messages are in dispute.
