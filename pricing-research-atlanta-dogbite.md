## Georgia fee rule — no percentage cap, a reasonableness test

Source: State Bar of Georgia, **Georgia Rules of Professional Conduct, Rule 1.5
Fees** — https://www.gabar.org/handbook?rule=rule55 (retrieved 2026-08-23;
content is delivered inside the page markup of the Bar's own handbook).

Verified text:

- Rule 1.5(a): "A lawyer shall not make an agreement for, charge, or collect an
  unreasonable fee or an unreasonable amount for expenses." **Eight** enumerated
  factors follow: (1) time and labor, novelty and difficulty, skill; (2)
  likelihood the employment precludes other employment; (3) the fee customarily
  charged in the locality for similar services; (4) amount involved and results
  obtained; (5) time limitations; (6) nature and length of the professional
  relationship; (7) experience, reputation and ability; (8) whether the fee is
  fixed or contingent.
- Rule 1.5(b): scope and the basis or rate of fee and expenses must be
  communicated, preferably in writing, before or within a reasonable time after
  the representation starts. A fee-arbitration agreement needs informed consent
  in writing, in a separate paragraph, in type at least as large as the rest of
  the contract, separately initialed.
- Rule 1.5(c)(1): a contingent fee agreement **shall be in writing** and shall
  state the method by which the fee is determined, including the percentages
  that accrue on settlement, trial or appeal, the litigation and other expenses
  to be deducted from the recovery, and **whether expenses are deducted before
  or after the contingent fee is calculated**.
- Rule 1.5(c)(2): at the conclusion of a contingent fee matter the lawyer must
  give the client a **written statement** of the outcome and, if there is a
  recovery, the remittance to the client, the method of its determination, the
  amount of the attorney fee, and any split with an outside lawyer.
- Rule 1.5(d): no contingent fee in a domestic relations matter tied to securing
  a divorce or to alimony/support, and no contingent fee for representing a
  defendant in a criminal case.
- Stated maximum penalty for a violation of Rule 1.5: **public reprimand**.

There is **no** Georgia statute or bar rule fixing a percentage ceiling on a
contingent fee in an injury case. That is the material difference from New York.

## City of Atlanta animal ordinance — dog amounts

Source: **Atlanta Code of Ordinances, Ch. 18, Art. III (Dogs), Sec. 18-61** —
https://library.municode.com/ga/atlanta/codes/code_of_ordinances/177780?nodeId=PTIICOORENOR_CH18AN_ARTIIIDO_S18-62ADRACOREHECOFUCO
(retrieved 2026-08-23).

- Annual owner's permit fee: **$3.00 per dog**, maximum **$10.00 per family**
  regardless of the number of dogs six months or older — Sec. 18-61(a)(4).
- Reclaiming an impounded dog: **$7.50** impoundment fee plus **$3.00 per day**
  boarding, plus the unpaid annual permit fee and the cost of rabies inoculation
  if the dog wore no current tag — Sec. 18-61(a)(3)(a).
- Seven days to reclaim after the notice is mailed; after that the dog may be
  offered for adoption on payment of the license fee, rabies cost and the $7.50
  impoundment fee — Sec. 18-61(a)(3)(b).
