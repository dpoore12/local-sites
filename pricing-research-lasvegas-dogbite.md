## Nevada Rules of Professional Conduct 1.5 — reasonableness, no cap

Source: **Nevada Rules of Professional Conduct, Rule 1.5 Fees**, Nevada
Legislature court rules — https://www.leg.state.nv.us/courtrules/rpc.html
(retrieved 2026-08-23).

Verified text:

- RPC 1.5(a): a lawyer shall not make an agreement for, charge, or collect an
  unreasonable fee or an unreasonable amount for expenses. **Eight** factors:
  time and labor / novelty / skill; whether the work precludes other employment
  if apparent to the client; the fee customarily charged in the locality; the
  amount involved and results obtained; time limitations; nature and length of
  the relationship; experience, reputation and ability; whether the fee is fixed
  or contingent.
- RPC 1.5(b): scope and the basis or rate of fee and expenses must be
  communicated, preferably in writing, before or within a reasonable time after
  the representation begins; changes must also be communicated.
- RPC 1.5(c): a contingent fee agreement must be **in writing, signed by the
  client**, and must state **in boldface type at least as large as the largest
  type used in the agreement**: (1) the method of determining the fee, including
  the percentages on settlement, trial or appeal; (2) whether litigation and
  other expenses are deducted from the recovery and whether before or after the
  fee is calculated; (3) whether the client is liable for expenses regardless of
  outcome; (4) that on a loss the client **may be liable for the opposing
  party's attorney fees and will be liable for the opposing party's costs as
  required by law**; and (5) that a suit brought solely to harass or coerce a
  settlement may create liability for malicious prosecution or abuse of process.
  At the conclusion the lawyer must give a written statement of the outcome and,
  if there is a recovery, the remittance and the method of its determination.
- RPC 1.5(d): no contingent fee tied to securing a divorce or to the amount of
  alimony, support or property settlement in lieu of it; no contingent fee for
  representing a defendant in a criminal case.

**No** Nevada rule or statute caps a contingent fee in an ordinary injury or
animal-attack claim.

## NRS 7.095 — the one Nevada contingent-fee cap, and it is not dog bite

Source: **NRS Chapter 7**, Nevada Legislature —
https://www.leg.state.nv.us/nrs/nrs-007.html (retrieved 2026-08-23).

- NRS 7.095(1): an attorney "shall not contract for or collect a fee contingent
  on the amount of recovery for representing a person seeking damages in
  connection with an action for injury or death against a provider of health
  care based upon professional negligence in excess of **35 percent of the
  amount recovered**."
- NRS 7.095(2): the limit applies to settlement, arbitration and judgment alike.
- NRS 7.095(3): "recovered" is the **net** sum after deducting disbursements or
  costs of prosecuting or settling the claim.

A dog bite claim against an owner or keeper is not an action against a health
care provider based on professional negligence, so the 35 percent ceiling does
not reach it.

## NRS 18.005 — what counts as "costs"

Source: **NRS Chapter 18, NRS 18.005** —
https://www.leg.state.nv.us/nrs/nrs-018.html (retrieved 2026-08-23).

Seventeen enumerated categories, including clerks' fees, reporters' fees for
depositions, jurors' fees, witness fees, interpreters, process service,
photocopies, postage, travel and lodging for depositions, fees charged under
NRS 19.0335, and reasonable and necessary expenses including computerized legal
research. Key figure: **reasonable fees of not more than five expert witnesses
in an amount of not more than $15,000 for each expert witness**, unless the
court allows more (NRS 18.005(5)).

## Clark County Code Title 10 — animal permit amounts

Source: **Clark County, NV, Titles 1 and 10 combined ordinance (Jan 2025)** —
https://www.clarkcountynv.gov/adobe/assets/urn:aaid:aem:f1f58666-7941-4544-91ea-dd69326071b7/original/as/titles-1-and-10-final-combined-ordinance-jan2025.pdf
(retrieved 2026-08-23).

- CCC 10.08.135(b): breeder/show permit fee **$800**, including the initial site
  inspection by Clark County Animal Protection Services; **$800** annual
  renewal, reduced to **$400** where each animal has participated in a show once
  a year as defined in 10.04.275.
- CCC 10.08.135(c): **$100** reinspection fee where the owner fails the site
  inspection or is found in violation of Chapter 10.

## NRS 202.500 — dangerous and vicious dogs (context, not a fee)

Source: https://www.leg.state.nv.us/nrs/nrs-202.html (retrieved 2026-08-23).
Knowingly keeping a vicious dog more than 7 days after actual notice, or
transferring it, is a misdemeanor; substantial bodily harm from an attack by a
dog known to be vicious is a **category D felony** under NRS 193.130. Breed
alone cannot make a dog dangerous or vicious, and local authorities may not
adopt breed-based ordinances.
