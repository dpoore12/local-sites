# How We Move Up — Measured, Then Planned

**August 29, 2026.** Everything in Part 1 was measured today. Nothing is quoted from memory.

---

## Part 1 — What the page-one winners actually have

### How the measurement was done

I pulled the live top 20 for all 83 of our markets — the exact query a customer types, `service + city`, run from inside that city. All 83 came back clean. Then I threw out the directories (Yelp, Reddit, Justia, Thumbtack, Angi) and the national chains (Roto-Rooter, Overhead Door, Ben Crump), kept the 282 real local businesses sitting in the top five, downloaded each one's ranking page to count its words, and read each one's sitemap to count how many pages the whole site has. 266 of 282 pages downloaded. 239 of 282 had a readable sitemap. Then I pulled link data on a 111-site sample.

### The headline

| | **Our sites** | **The winners** |
|---|---|---|
| Pages on the whole site | **9** | **286** (middle value) |
| Words on the page that ranks | **1,353** | **1,472** (middle value) |
| Referring domains | **0** | **488** (middle value, 111 sampled) |
| Domain rating | **0** | **13** (middle value) |

**Our individual pages are the right length. Our sites are 32 times too small.**

That is the whole finding. We wrote good pages. We built tiny websites.

### How small is small

Of the 239 winners whose sitemap I could read:

- **5 have fewer than 10 pages.** That's 2%. We are in that bucket.
- **14 have 25 pages or fewer.** 6%.
- **181 have 100 pages or more.** 76%.

Page count by niche: home services winners run **204 pages** (middle value), legal winners run **351**.

Word count per ranking page holds steady at every position — position 1 is 1,482 words, position 5 is 1,444. **Longer pages do not buy you a higher spot.** More pages do.

### The link side, honestly

The 111-site sample: middle value **488 referring domains**, and **not one single winner had fewer than 200**. But domain rating tells a different story — middle value **13**, with **65% of winners under DR 20** and **29% under DR 5**.

Read those two numbers together: winners carry hundreds of *junk* links. Scrapers, spam blasts, syndication. That matches what I found when I mined the competitor link data earlier this month — one $100 press release purchase showed up as 200 separate links. Nobody in these markets earned 488 real links. They accumulated 488 pieces of internet debris, and it correlates with a domain rating of 13.

This is good news and bad news. Bad: we have zero of anything, and zero is measurably outside the range. Good: the bar is low quality, not high quality.

### Who we are actually fighting

830 top-ten slots measured across 83 markets:

- **75% real local businesses**
- **21% directories** (Yelp, Reddit, Justia, Superlawyers, Thumbtack)
- **4% national chains**

Three quarters of every page-one is a real local business. Those seats are takeable. In eight of our markets — all the dog-bite and truck-accident legal ones — the top 10 is **ten out of ten real local firms, zero directories.** Cleanest field we have.

The most crowded-out markets: moving services Orlando (4 local, 4 directories, 2 national), garage door Fort Worth, emergency plumbing Tucson, garage door Boca Raton, garage door Ann Arbor.

**We appear nowhere in the top 20 of any of the 83.** Six days live. Every operator's stated lag is 3 to 12 weeks minimum. This is expected, not failure.

## Part 2 — What proven operators actually do

Full sourced research: `RESEARCH-HOW-OPERATORS-RANK.md`. The short version.

### Kyle (rentlocalsites.com)

- **About 10 pages per site.** Service pages plus location pages for adjacent suburbs. He also says "I have sites that are one page and ranking number one," and his sales page markets a 1–5 page build.
- His answer to a site that won't rank is not a page target, it's **"I just keep adding content to it until it gets to page one."**
- **10 to 30 links.** He refuses cold outreach. Two methods only: swap links with other members of his paid group, and the **copycat method** — pull a competitor's link profile in Ahrefs and take the free ones.
- **"You just need some activity"** — quality bar deliberately low.
- **Citations are 5–10% of the work.** He says the 80% is content and backlinks.
- He does not track rankings. He tracks calls. Position 4–5 still produces 50–80 calls a month, and rank 3–4 still supports $500–800/month rent.
- **The part that matters most to us: Kyle deliberately avoids plumbing, HVAC, and roofing in big metros, and avoids personal injury law.** He targets obscure niches — epoxy floors, popcorn ceiling removal, junk removal, septic, towing, auto glass — in cities under 100,000 people.

**Our portfolio is built almost entirely out of the categories Kyle refuses to enter.** Garage door repair, AC repair, emergency plumbing in Phoenix, Atlanta, San Jose, Los Angeles, and personal injury law in San Diego and Houston. That's not a small mismatch. It means Kyle's 10-page build was never going to work here, and the 286-page median I measured is the reason why.

### Ippei Kanehara (ippei.com) — the one that actually fits us

He is the only operator who documents ranking **without** Google Business Profiles, which is our exact constraint. His launch spec is the most specific number in the whole research:

- **"I would start with like 15 service pages because most your competition only has like five. And then maybe 10, 15, 20 sub city pages. That's about 30 pages."**
- **"The average compact keywords landing page is only 415 words."** Short pages, lots of them. The only per-page word count any operator states.
- Internal links: everything flat off the root, homepage is the hub, every subpage links **up** to the homepage in its first sentence, **no footer link blocks**.
- A sub-city page = main keyword + a neighboring town. He'll build them for towns of 2,000 people.
- **He has largely stopped building external links.** "Screw PBNs, it's too much effort." Guest posts, "became really difficult to tell if these links were actually moving the needle." He focuses on topical authority instead.
- **No GBP costs about 50% of your leads** — his number — and he compensates by owning more sites. Only about 1/4 of his sites have a profile. He names a student at $15,000/month with none.
- **He built and ranked 170 sites in six months** targeting the suburbs around Los Angeles.
- Timeline: 3–4 months to first leads, 6 weeks to 6 months to rank.

### His anti-duplicate procedure — copy this literally

We run 83 near-identical templates. This is the fix he uses:

1. Shift the heading structure per page instead of reusing one template.
2. Feed **the target city's Wikipedia page** into the writer to pull real local landmarks, entities, and weather for that specific place.
3. Write per-location case studies that name local schools and buildings.
4. Strip the AI padding.
5. Put unique stats and process content in the lower third of the page.

### Luke Van Der Veer

231 sites over 10 years. "One of my masonry sites took 4 hours to build and 12 weeks to rank. It now pays $1,500 every month." His link method is the same copycat play: "Start by analyzing the links of your top 10 competitors. Identify the highest quality links from each competitor and build them for your own website."

### The 2026 evidence

Whitespark's 2026 ranking-factor survey, organic side, scored:

| Rank | Factor | Score |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Dedicated page for each service** | 210 |
| 2 | **City / neighborhood keyword relevance of content** | 190 |
| 3 | Quality of inbound links to domain | 187 |
| 6 | Internal linking across the site | 174 |
| 14 | Volume of content on the whole site | 161 |
| **80** | **Quantity of directory listings** | **79** |

Directory listings rank 80th out of 100+. A page per service ranks 1st. That is the mathematical version of what I told you this morning, and it's the reason stopping the directory work was right.

Two more findings from the same survey worth acting on: thin blog posts for "topical authority" **"don't seem to be helping AT ALL anymore,"** and long-form ultimate guides **"does not work anymore — Google and AI search prefers shorter, scannable, readable pages."**

### The risk I'm not going to hide from you

Google's spam policy names, word for word, **"having multiple websites with slight variations to the URL and home page"** and **"creating multiple sites with the intent of hiding the scaled nature of the content."** That describes 83 near-identical sites.

There's one documented mass enforcement: after Google's October 2022 spam update, a named practitioner observed duplicate-content location pages **deindexed across 200+ service-area sites**. The pages that survived had **unique content**.

Two things partly protect us: each site serves a different real business, so we're not funneling everyone to one place; and enforcement has historically been deindexing, not a ranking penalty. But the failure mode to watch for is **pages dropping out of the index, not pages ranking low.** That's the first thing I check.

## Part 3 — The plan

### Step 0 — Check we're indexed, before anything else (this week)

If pages aren't in the index, page count is irrelevant. All 83 are already in Search Console. I pull the index-coverage status for every page on every site and flag anything excluded as duplicate or crawled-not-indexed. **This is a stop-or-go gate.** If we're being filtered as duplicates, uniqueness comes before volume.

### Step 1 — Rebuild each site from 9 pages to about 35

Target shape, per site, matching Ippei's spec:

| Page type | Count | Words each |
|---|---|---|
| Home | 1 | keep as is |
| Individual service pages | **12–15** | 400–600 |
| Adjacent suburb pages | **10–20** | 400–600 |
| Pricing / cost | 1 | keep |
| About, contact | 2 | keep |

That's roughly **2,200 new pages across the portfolio.** It runs through the existing `template/build.py` — same pipeline that built the 83, same locked navy-and-gold design. Not a rewrite.

**Two hard rules, because of the deindexing risk:**

1. **Every page gets real, city-specific facts** pulled from that city's Wikipedia page — landmarks, neighborhoods, climate, housing age — plus a case study naming an actual local school or building.
2. **Heading structure varies per site.** No two sites share an H2 sequence.

Do NOT add blog posts. The evidence says they don't help and Sterling Sky reports declines for sites that published lots of articles.

### Step 2 — Fix internal linking in one pass

Flat URLs off the root. Homepage is the hub. Every subpage links up to the homepage in its first sentence. **Kill the footer link blocks.** Internal linking scores 174 — rank 6 — and this is a one-day template change across all 83.

### Step 3 — 10 to 30 links per site, dripped not blasted

Not market-rate buying. Average paid guest post runs $459 and a link insertion $225 — across 83 sites that's six figures, and paid links are a named spam violation. Operators at this level budget "$200+ per site" total.

What we do instead, in priority order:

1. **The copycat method, automated.** I already have 111 winners' link profiles pulled and the full mined set. Filter to the genuinely local, genuinely obtainable ones. Both Kyle and Luke run exactly this play.
2. **Chambers of commerce.** $200–400/year, real followed links from genuinely local domains. I have five already verified in our cities: Austin (DR 70), Colorado Springs (DR 62), Boca Raton (DR 53), Tucson (DR 51), McKinney (DR 50). Buy these for sites once they have a paying tenant.
3. **Local news and city publications.** I have 27 verified genuinely-local targets. 19 are attached to the frozen legal markets, so about 8 are live today.
4. **Drip them.** "Sustained influx of links over time rather than bursts" is a scored factor.
