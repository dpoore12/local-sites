## Which Florida subdivision applies

A dissolution / family matter is a **domestic relations matter**, so the governing prohibition is **Rule 4-1.5(f)(3)(A)** of the Rules Regulating The Florida Bar — not (f)(3)(B), which is the criminal subparagraph used by the Fort Lauderdale site in this batch.

Verbatim (Bar Chapter 4 PDF, retrieved 2026-08-23, https://www-media.floridabar.org/uploads/2026/06/2026_12-JUNE-Chapter-4-RRTFB-1.pdf):
> "(A) any fee in a domestic relations matter, the payment or amount of which is contingent on the securing of a divorce or on the amount of alimony or support, or property settlement in lieu thereof"

## How the narrowness was handled

The page states the prohibition as **two hooks** and nothing wider: payment contingent on the divorce being **secured**, and payment sized by the **amount** of alimony, support or property settlement in lieu of those. It then adds the rule's own extension, from the comment: in domestic relations cases, fees containing a **bonus provision or an additional fee to be determined later based on results obtained** have been treated as impermissible contingency fees, subject to restitution and discipline. It does **not** claim that every family fee must be paid in advance or that outcome may never be mentioned in an agreement.

Carve-out, given its own fee_row and body paragraph: the provision does not preclude a contingent fee contract for legal representation in connection with recovering **post-judgment balances due under support, alimony or other financial orders**. The page states this in paraphrase (not verbatim) because the verbatim carve-out sentence collided with denverdivorcelawyerpros.com under the 15-word guard. Where such a contract is used, **Rule 4-1.5(f)(2)** requires a written contract signed by the client and by a lawyer for the firm, with a copy furnished to the client.

### Note on the 44.108 amounts
The flsenate.gov page for 44.108 rendered without the dollar amounts in the initially fetched text (only the exclusions were visible: no mediation fee in a residential eviction, against an indigent party, or in small claims; clerk may deduct $1 per assessment). The **$120 / $60** amounts were then confirmed verbatim in **44.108(2)(a)-(b)** from the Florida House statutes copy of chapter 44 — https://www.flhouse.gov/Statutes/2025/Chapter0044/All/ (retrieved 2026-08-23) — and independently corroborated by the Hillsborough County Clerk fee list, which prints the same two figures with the same income brackets. Both figures are therefore double-sourced; the on-page source link points to the statute.
