## Florida Wrongful Death Act
- 768.19 right of action (death caused by wrongful act, negligence, default, or breach of contract or warranty): https://www.leg.state.fl.us/Statutes/index.cfm?App_mode=Display_Statute&URL=0700-0799/0768/Sections/0768.19.html
- 768.20 personal representative brings the action for survivors and the estate (site.json fact): https://www.leg.state.fl.us/Statutes/index.cfm?App_mode=Display_Statute&URL=0700-0799/0768/Sections/0768.20.html
- 768.18 survivors = spouse, children, parents, and dependent blood relatives / adopted siblings; "minor children" = under 25: https://www.leg.state.fl.us/Statutes/index.cfm?App_mode=Display_Statute&URL=0700-0799/0768/Sections/0768.18.html
- 768.21 damages. Preamble: ALL potential beneficiaries including the estate must be identified in the complaint and relationships alleged.
  - (1) each survivor: lost support and services, past and future; factors = relationship, decedent's probable net income available for distribution to that survivor, replacement value of services; joint life expectancies and period of minority.
  - (2) surviving spouse: companionship and protection, mental pain and suffering from date of injury.
  - (3) minor children, and ALL children if no surviving spouse: lost parental companionship, instruction and guidance, plus mental pain and suffering. Both-spouses-within-30-days provision.
  - (4) each parent of a deceased minor child; each parent of an adult child only if there are no other survivors.
  - (5) medical or funeral expenses recoverable by the survivor who paid them.
  - (6)(a) estate: lost earnings injury-to-death less lost support excluding contributions in kind, with interest; prospective net accumulations if survivors include spouse or lineal descendants, OR decedent not a minor child, no lost support recoverable under (1), and a surviving parent. (6)(b) expenses charged against the estate. Evidence of remarriage of the spouse admissible.
  - (7) estate awards subject to creditor claims complying with probate law.
  - (8) THE EXCLUSION: damages in (3) not recoverable by adult children and damages in (4) not recoverable by parents of an adult child, for claims for medical negligence as defined by 766.106(1).
  - URL: https://www.leg.state.fl.us/Statutes/index.cfm?App_mode=Display_Statute&URL=0700-0799/0768/Sections/0768.21.html (displayed as "The 2026 Florida Statutes" — subsection (8) still present)
- 768.25 court approval of settlement; 768.26 fees and expenses paid by PR, deducted pro rata from survivors' and estate's awards: https://www.leg.state.fl.us/Statutes/index.cfm?App_mode=Display_Statute&Search_String=&URL=0700-0799/0768/Sections/0768.26.html

## Comparative fault
- 768.81(2) proportional reduction, subject to (6); (3)(a) defendant must plead and then prove nonparty fault to put it on the verdict form; (6) a party more than 50 percent at fault for their own harm recovers nothing, and (6) expressly does NOT apply to personal injury or wrongful death arising out of medical negligence under chapter 766: https://www.leg.state.fl.us/Statutes/index.cfm?App_mode=Display_Statute&URL=0700-0799/0768/Sections/0768.81.html

## Medical negligence presuit
- 766.203(2) claimant investigation + verified written medical expert opinion submitted when the notice of intent is mailed; 766.203 applies to 768.19 rights of action and to 768.28 claims: https://www.leg.state.fl.us/Statutes/index.cfm?App_mode=Display_Statute&URL=0700-0799/0766/Sections/0766.203.html
- 766.106(2) notice of intent by certified mail / tracked mail / commercial carrier / process server, with provider lists and the s. 766.1065 authorization; (3)(a) no suit for 90 days; (3)(b) response rejecting, offering settlement, or offering arbitration with liability admitted; (3)(c) no reply within 90 days = final rejection; (4) limitations tolled on mailing and during the 90 days, extension by stipulation tolls, 60 days or remainder after termination of negotiations: https://www.leg.state.fl.us/Statutes/index.cfm?App_mode=Display_Statute&URL=0700-0799/0766/Sections/0766.106.html
- Fla. Const. Art. I, s. 26 (adopted 2004): claimant entitled to no less than 70 percent of the first $250,000 of damages and 90 percent above that, exclusive of costs, in a medical liability claim involving a contingency fee; self-executing: https://www.flsenate.gov/laws/constitution#A1S26

## Government defendants
- 768.28(5)(a) caps: $200,000 per person, $300,000 per incident; excess may be reported to the Legislature and paid only by further act (claim bill). (6)(a) written claim to the agency within 3 years and, except for claims against a municipality, county or the Florida Space Authority, also to the Department of Financial Services; (6)(a)2 wrongful death claim must be presented to DFS within 2 years. (6)(d) failure to make final disposition within 6 months = denial, but in medical malpractice and wrongful death actions 90 days = denial, and limitations are tolled while the claim is pending. (14) 4-year outer limit for other claims: https://www.leg.state.fl.us/Statutes/index.cfm?App_mode=Display_Statute&URL=0700-0799/0768/Sections/0768.28.html

## Work deaths
- 440.11(1) employer liability exclusive; (1)(a) if the employer failed to secure payment of compensation the legal representative may elect an action at law; (1)(b) intentional tort only on clear and convincing proof of deliberate intent, or conduct the employer knew from prior similar accidents or explicit warnings was virtually certain to cause death, with concealment: https://www.leg.state.fl.us/Statutes/index.cfm?App_mode=Display_Statute&URL=0400-0499/0440/Sections/0440.11.html
- 440.16: death must result within 1 year of the accident, or follow continuous disability and result within 5 years; funeral expenses up to $7,500 payable within 14 days after receiving the bill; total not to exceed $150,000; spouse with no child 50 percent of average weekly wage, plus 16 2/3 percent for a child or children; each child 33 1/3 percent where there is no spouse; each parent 25 percent during dependency; each brother, sister or grandchild 15 percent; remarriage lump sum of 26 weeks at 50 percent: https://www.leg.state.fl.us/Statutes/index.cfm?App_mode=Display_Statute&URL=0400-0499/0440/Sections/0440.16.html
- Florida has no OSHA-approved state plan (not in either list of 22 + 7 plans): https://www.osha.gov/stateplans
- A work-related fatality must be reported to OSHA within 8 hours: https://www.osha.gov/report
