## Brief accuracy notes
- site.json and the brief agreed on city, state, county and the four slugs; nothing needed overriding.
- The brief asked for "the statutory percentage limit on what a hospital lien may take." The 50 percent limit in Civil Code 3045.4 is not a cap on the hospital's charge; it is a limit on the payer's exposure, expressed as the amount satisfiable out of 50 percent of the money due after prior liens. The copy states it that way.
- The brief described the Medi-Cal formula as deducting attorney fees and costs before the state's share. Welfare and Institutions Code 14124.72(d) actually reduces the lien by a flat 25 percent as the director's share of fees, plus a pro-rata share of litigation expenses; 14124.78 is the separate cap ensuring the director never takes more than the beneficiary keeps after fees and costs, and 14124.785 applies whichever of the formulas yields less.
- ERISA reimbursement is not simply "treated differently" by statute: the split comes from the deemer clause as read in FMC Corp. v. Holliday, and Civil Code 3040(h) expressly declines to make a preempted plan lien enforceable.
- "Letters of protection" have no California statutory source that could be verified. The copy instead describes provider liens by agreement and the factoring of medical accounts, which are documented in Gilman v. Dalby and Dodd v. Cruz.
- No route-level AADT figure for I-10 through the city was retrievable (Caltrans publishes those in bulk files), so no such number appears.
