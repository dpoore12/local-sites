## Contributory negligence (the differentiator)
- NC bars recovery where the injured person's negligence joined with the defendant's negligence in producing the injury, however small the contribution. Site.json cited case: https://law.justia.com/cases/north-carolina/supreme-court/1968/278-0.html
- Burden: "A party asserting the defense of contributory negligence has the burden of proof of such defense." G.S. 1-139 — https://www.ncleg.net/enactedlegislation/statutes/html/bysection/chapter_1/gs_1-139.html
- Last clear chance, four elements, plaintiff's burden, greater weight of the evidence: N.C.P.I. Motor Vehicle 105.15 (May 2020) — https://www.sog.unc.edu/sites/default/files/pji-master/mv/mv105.15%20Last%20Clear%20Chance--Burden%20of%20Proof;%20Definition;%20Final%20Mandate.%20%5B5-2020%5D.pdf
  1) plaintiff negligently placed self in position of peril from which he could not escape by reasonable care; 2) defendant knew or should have discovered the peril and inability to escape; 3) defendant had the time and means to avoid injury and failed to use reasonable care; 4) that failure proximately caused the injury.
- Watson v. White, 308 S.E.2d 268 (N.C. 1983) (quoting Clodfelter v. Carroll; Exum v. Boyles, 272 N.C. 567) — held plaintiff failed on the third element — https://law.justia.com/cases/north-carolina/supreme-court/1983/53a83-0.html

## Compulsory limits, UM and UIM (Financial Responsibility Act)
- G.S. 20-279.21(b)(2): $50,000 one person / $100,000 two or more / $50,000 property damage. https://www.ncleg.net/enactedlegislation/statutes/html/bysection/chapter_20/gs_20-279.21.html
- NCDOI: before July 1, 2025 the minimums were 30/60/25; for all new or renewed policies on or after July 1, 2025 they are $50,000/$100,000 bodily injury and $50,000 property damage. UIM is included in ALL new or renewed policies beginning July 1, 2025. https://www.ncdoi.gov/changes-rating-automobile-insurance-policies-effective-july-1-2025
- (b)(3): UM coverage required for damages from owners/operators of uninsured motor vehicles AND hit-and-run motor vehicles; UM BI limits equal highest BI liability limit for any one vehicle on the policy, capped at $1,000,000 per person / $1,000,000 per accident; insured may buy greater or lesser but not below the (b)(2) minimums. UM property damage limits equal highest PD limit, subject to an exclusion of the first $100 of damages. A written statement from the other vehicle's liability insurer that the vehicle was uninsured is prima facie presumption of uninsured status.
- (b)(4): UIM required in addition; UIM limits equal UM limits, $1,000,000 cap. "Underinsured highway vehicle" = sum of liability limits applicable at the time of the accident is less than the TOTAL DAMAGES sustained by the individual seeking UIM benefits. UIM not reduced by setoff or credit against any coverage except workers' compensation under subsection (e). If the claimant is insured under UIM on separate or additional policies, total UIM = sum of the highest limit available under each policy (nonfleet private passenger vehicles only).
- No intra-policy stacking: "The underinsured motorist limits applicable to any one motor vehicle under a policy shall not be combined with or added to the limits applicable to any other motor vehicle under that policy."
- Consent/subrogation: no insurer may exercise subrogation or a right to approve settlement with the underinsured motorist where it received written notice before a settlement between its insured and the underinsured motorist and failed to advance a payment equal to the tentative settlement within 30 days of receipt of that notice.
- UM suit: no action may be initiated by the insured until 60 days after notice to the insurer that the prospective defendants are believed uninsured; failure is not grounds for dismissal but extends the answer time to 60 days. Service outside the limitations period is valid if the summons was properly issued, preserved and served under Rule 4.
- Commercial-only or fleet-only motor vehicle liability policies are not required to provide UM or UIM coverage.
- (e): UM/UIM insures the portion of loss uncompensated by workers' compensation plus the employer's lien amount under G.S. 97-10.2(h)/(j).

## Deadlines
- G.S. 1-52(16): three years; for personal injury the cause of action does not accrue until bodily harm becomes apparent or ought reasonably to have become apparent; no cause of action accrues more than 10 years from the last act or omission. https://www.ncleg.gov/EnactedLegislation/Statutes/HTML/BySection/Chapter_1/GS_1-52.html
- G.S. 1-53(4): two years for death caused by wrongful act, neglect or fault under G.S. 28A-18-2; the cause of action does not accrue until the date of death. https://www.ncleg.gov/EnactedLegislation/Statutes/HTML/BySection/Chapter_1/GS_1-53.html

## Crash reports
- Reportable crash = death or injury of a human being, or total property damage of $1,000 or more (or damage of any amount to a vehicle seized under 20-28.3). G.S. 20-4.01(33b) — https://www.ncleg.gov/EnactedLegislation/Statutes/HTML/BySection/Chapter_20/GS_20-4.01.html
- G.S. 20-166.1: driver must immediately notify the police department of the city where it happened; investigating officer must make a written report within 24 hours; a local agency that receives it must forward it to the Division within 10 days; a report made by a person other than a law enforcement officer is without prejudice and may not be used as evidence, while officers' reports are public records and may be used as the rules of evidence permit; supplemental report required if an injured person dies within 12 months. https://www.ncleg.net/EnactedLegislation/Statutes/HTML/BySection/Chapter_20/GS_20-166.1.html
- NCDMV crash reports: individuals may order only a redacted copy, by mail or in person, on form TR-67A; forms are not fillable; allow 10 business days by mail; in-person counters listed are Raleigh Central Services and the Huntersville license plate agency; five records or fewer in person can be received immediately; DPPA 18 USC 2721-2725 governs unredacted copies. https://www.ncdot.gov/dmv/offices-services/records-reports/Pages/crash-reports.aspx
- NCDOT Traffic Safety takes public requests for crash data on an intersection or road section, and warns many crashes go unreported; HSIP letters/reports are not discoverable or admissible under 23 USC 409. https://www.ncdot.gov/initiatives-policies/safety/traffic-safety/Pages/crash-data.aspx
- Concord Police P2C accident report search fields: first name, last name, driver's license, DL state, date occurred, accident number, insurance policy number. https://www.concordp2c.com/AccidentDetail.aspx
- Concord PD customer service page: traffic accidents and hit-and-run may NOT be reported online or by the Telephone Reporting Unit; everything else must be dispatched through the Communications Center (911, non-emergency 704-786-9155); headquarters 41 Cabarrus Ave. West; police records line listed. https://concordnc.gov/Departments/Police/Customer-Service

## Rating, points, and the uninsured driver's exposure
- SDIP thresholds, G.S. 58-36-75(a): "major accident" = at-fault crash causing bodily injury or death, or property damage of $3,850 or more; "intermediate" = property damage over $2,300 but less than $3,850; "minor" = $2,300 or less. (f) speeding 10 mph or less and PJC lookback periods are five years. (f1) only the higher surcharge between the accident and the conviction is assessed. (h) one point for failing to yield to a pedestrian under 20-158(b)(2)b. http://www.ncleg.net/EnactedLegislation/Statutes/HTML/BySection/Chapter_58/GS_58-36-75.html
- NCDOI: from July 1, 2025 a conviction carrying four or more SDIP points (other than the listed high-speed speeding offenses) is surcharged for 5 years instead of 3; the inexperienced operator surcharge extends from 3 to 8 years for drivers first licensed on or after that date; speeding-10-or-less and PJC lookbacks move from three to five years. Authority: S.L. 2023-133 as amended by S.L. 2024-29. https://www.ncdoi.gov/changes-rating-automobile-insurance-policies-effective-july-1-2025
- G.S. 20-311: DMV notice of lapse, owner must respond within 10 days; penalty $50 with no prior lapse in three years, $100 with one, $150 with two or more; $50 restoration fee. https://www.ncleg.gov/EnactedLegislation/Statutes/HTML/BySection/Chapter_20/GS_20-311.html
- G.S. 20-313: operating or permitting operation without the required financial responsibility is a Class 3 misdemeanor. https://www.ncleg.gov/EnactedLegislation/Statutes/HTML/BySection/Chapter_20/GS_20-313.html
- G.S. 20-152(a): a driver shall not follow another vehicle more closely than is reasonable and prudent, having due regard for the speed of the vehicles and the traffic upon and condition of the highway. https://www.ncleg.gov/EnactedLegislation/Statutes/HTML/BySection/Chapter_20/GS_20-152.html

## Commercial vehicles
- G.S. 20-309(a1): an owner of a commercial motor vehicle must have financial responsibility in an amount equal to that required for for-hire carriers transporting nonhazardous property in interstate or foreign commerce under 49 C.F.R. 387.9. https://www.ncleg.net/EnactedLegislation/Statutes/HTML/BySection/Chapter_20/GS_20-309.html
- 49 C.F.R. 387.9 Table 1: for-hire, nonhazardous property, GVWR 10,001 lb or more, interstate/foreign: $750,000. Oil and most listed hazardous materials: $1,000,000. Bulk hazardous substances and listed bulk divisions: $5,000,000. https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CFR-2024-title49-vol5/xml/CFR-2024-title49-vol5-sec387-9.xml
- 49 C.F.R. 395.8: "A motor carrier shall retain records of duty status and supporting documents required under this part for each of its drivers for a period of not less than 6 months from the date of receipt." https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CFR-2024-title49-vol5/xml/CFR-2024-title49-vol5-sec395-8.xml
- 49 C.F.R. 391.51: the driver qualification file is retained as long as the driver is employed and for three years after; contents include the employment application, the motor vehicle record from each licensing authority, the road test certificate or accepted equivalent, the annual inquiry record, the annual review note, and the medical examiner's certificate. https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CFR-2024-title49-vol5/xml/CFR-2024-title49-vol5-sec391-51.xml
