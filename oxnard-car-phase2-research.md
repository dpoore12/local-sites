# Oxnard Car Accident Lawyer — phase 2 research notes (verified 2026-08-25)

All facts below were fetched during this session. Differentiator: California farm labor
vehicle (FLV) rules, agricultural transport, worker-vs-public claim split, ag equipment
on public roads, Cal/OSHA, plus Oxnard rail crossing and Highway 101 texture.

## Farm labor vehicles

- VC 322 defines an FLV as a motor vehicle designed, used or maintained to transport
  nine or more farm workers in addition to the driver, to or from a place of employment
  or employment-related activities; a farm worker renders personal services for hire in
  connection with producing or harvesting farm products. Excludes immediate-family-only
  vehicles and PUC/transit-authorized operations. For-hire transport must be registered
  commercially. https://www.dmv.ca.gov/portal/handbook/vehicle-industry-registration-procedures-manual-2/commercial-vehicles/farm-labor-vehicles/
- CHP program page: FLVs must be inspected and certified annually; registration will not
  be issued or renewed without inspection certification; every FLV must have seatbelts
  for each passenger and CHP will not certify without approved seatbelts; each passenger
  must wear one on a highway; violations reportable to 1-800-TELL-CHP (1-800-835-5247);
  AB 1165 added FLV laws after a fatal FLV crash.
  https://www.chp.ca.gov/programs-services/programs/commercial-vehicle-section/farm-labor-vehicle-program
- VC 31401(b): CHP inspects every FLV at least once annually; no person shall drive an
  FLV unless a certificate is displayed showing inspection within 13 months of the date
  of operation. 31401(d)-(e): the owner or the farm labor contractor (Labor Code 1682)
  who rents or uses an FLV is responsible for the inspection and may not operate without
  a current certificate.
- VC 31401.5: display sticker listing certification date and the 800 reporting number;
  renewal inspection must be requested no later than four weeks before expiration;
  initial inspection request no later than three business days before the requested date.
- VC 31402(b)(1): operating an FLV after notice, while in a condition presenting an
  immediate safety hazard, is a misdemeanor with a fine of not less than $1,000 and not
  more than $5,000, up to six months county jail, no part of the fine suspendable.
  31402(c): CHP may impound under 34506.4.
- VC 31404: knowing/willful violation adds a fine of not less than $1,000 per violation
  plus $500 per passenger present, capped at $5,000 per violation, none suspendable.
- VC 31405: Type 1 or Type 2 seatbelt assembly at each passenger position conforming to
  49 CFR 571.209 with anchorages per 571.210; no initial certificate without them; owner
  must keep them in working order; no operation on a highway unless driver and all
  passengers are restrained.
- VC 31406: all passenger seating positions must comply with 49 CFR 571.207.
- VC 31407: cutting/sharp tools in latched containers firmly attached; other tools
  secured; nothing may obstruct an aisle or emergency exit.
- VC 31408: both headlamps lighted regardless of time of day.
  All of 31400-31409: https://law.justia.com/codes/california/2010/veh/31400-31409.html
- CHP HPM 82.6 ch.14: CHP 292 Inspection Approval Certificate posted in the vehicle;
  serialized CHP 408 stickers front and rear; CHP 408C notice posted at eye height
  inside with the hotline; CHP 350 records certification/decertification; officers may
  stop a vehicle displaying expired FLV stickers; VC 23116(d) lets a farmer or rancher
  carry people in a truck bed without belts on their own land and up to one road mile of
  incidental highway travel between parts of the same operation; passenger-capacity
  weight figured at 150 pounds per person (13 CCR 1270.3).
  https://www.chp.ca.gov/siteassets/policy/hpm/hpm-82.6/hpm-82.6-ch-14.pdf
- VC 12519: FLV driver needs a license of the appropriate class endorsed for passenger
  transportation plus, when carrying one or more farmworker passengers, a DMV-issued
  certificate; applicant must complete the Department of Education driver training course
  (Education Code 40081) approved by DMV and CHP; examinations by CHP with a $12 fee.
  https://casetext.com/statute/california-codes/california-vehicle-code/division-6-drivers-licenses/chapter-1-issuance-of-licenses-expiration-and-renewal/article-1-persons-required-to-be-licensed-exemptions-and-age-limits/section-12519-requirements-to-operate-farm-labor-vehicle
  CHP manual adds VC 12804.9(b)(2)(D): FLV operator needs a Class A or B CDL, and
  15275/15278 passenger endorsement.

## Agricultural equipment on public roads

- VC 24615: unlawful to operate on a public highway a vehicle or combination designed to
  be and operated at 25 mph or less unless the rearmost vehicle displays a slow-moving
  vehicle emblem; truncated equilateral triangle, minimum height 14 inches, red
  reflective border not less than 1 3/4 inches, fluorescent orange center; mounted base
  down, 3 to 5 feet from ground to base. https://codes.findlaw.com/ca/vehicle-code/veh-sect-24615/
- VC 36500: implements of husbandry are exempt from many lighting articles but remain
  subject to 24254, 24615, 25803 and 25950. VC 36508: new implements designed for 25 mph
  or less sold after July 1, 1970 must carry the emblem. VC 36509: flashing amber lamps
  or turn signals permitted, and required (or daylight red flags not less than 16 inches
  square at the outer extremities) when a load exceeds 120 inches wide. VC 36510: if it
  cannot stop within 32 feet from 15 mph it may not be driven faster than that.
  https://law.justia.com/codes/california/2007/veh/36500-36520.html
- VC 25803: vehicles not otherwise required to have lamps must show, during darkness, a
  red light visible 500 feet to the rear and a white light 500 feet to the front, plus an
  amber reflector front near left and red reflector rear near left mounted 16 to 60
  inches above ground; over 100 inches wide adds a left-extremity amber light at night or
  an 18-inch red or fluorescent orange flag by day.
  https://codes.findlaw.com/ca/vehicle-code/veh-sect-25803/

## Cal/OSHA

- 8 CCR 3441(a)(2)(B): permit no riders on agricultural equipment other than persons
  required for instruction or assistance in machine operation; exception only for
  tractor-mounted personnel transport carriers under subsection (i). (i)(1) PTC use only
  for employees installing, removing or maintaining irrigation pipe for low-lying row
  crops; (i)(2) only in furrowed areas; (i)(3) field slope not over 5 percent; (i)(4)(C)
  seat belts at all passenger positions; (i)(5)(C) speed not over 5 mph; (i)(5)(E) no
  passengers when the tractor is within 10 feet of the paved edge of a public road;
  (i)(6)(A) daily pre-use inspection by a qualified person; (i)(7)(B) annual refresher
  training documented, in languages employees understand. Operative January 1, 2016.
  https://www.dir.ca.gov/title8/3441.html
- Labor Code 6409.1: for a serious injury, illness or death the employer must report
  immediately to the Division of Occupational Safety and Health by telephone or the
  division's online mechanism; civil penalty of not less than $5,000 for a violation;
  the routine subdivision (a) report is filed within five days of knowledge.
  https://law.justia.com/codes/california/code-lab/division-5/part-1/chapter-3/section-6409-1/

## Workers' compensation vs civil claim

- Labor Code 3600(a): compensation liability exists "in lieu of any other liability
  whatsoever" except as provided in 3602, 3706 and 4558, without regard to negligence,
  for injury arising out of and in the course of employment, where listed conditions
  concur, including that the employee is performing service growing out of and incidental
  to the employment and acting within the course of employment.
  https://codes.findlaw.com/ca/labor-code/lab-sect-3600/
- Labor Code 3852: a compensation claim does not affect the employee's claim or right of
  action for all damages proximately resulting from injury or death against any person
  other than the employer; an employer that pays compensation may also sue the third
  person and recover in the same suit.
  https://codes.findlaw.com/ca/labor-code/lab-sect-3852/
- Hinojosa v. Workmen's Comp. Appeals Bd. (1972) 8 Cal.3d 150: farm laborer for an
  operator of "seven or eight non-contiguous ranches," paid $1.75 per hour, nine to nine
  and one-half hours a day six days a week; had no car and paid a coworker $3 per week to
  ride; injured June 8, 1970 riding home after work; two other farmworkers in the same
  car were killed. Court annulled the board's denial. Key language: the going and coming
  rule is "not a legislatively enacted limitation, but wholly a judicially created one";
  it "composes no formula of automatic application"; the rule excludes "the ordinary,
  local commute that marks the daily transit of the mass of workers"; coverage extends to
  transits that "vary from the norm because the employer requires a special, different
  transit, means of transit, or use of a car, for some particular reason of his own."
  Listed exception triggers include where the employer provides the transportation or
  expressly or impliedly requires the employee to furnish it. Holding: because the job was
  structured around travel between scattered fields, the car was "an essential requirement
  of the job," and the passenger could not be treated differently from the owner-driver.
  https://law.justia.com/cases/california/supreme-court/3d/8/150.html

## Ordinary California layer

- CCP 335.1: two years for an action for injury to or death of an individual caused by
  the wrongful act or neglect of another.
  https://law.justia.com/codes/california/code-ccp/part-2/title-2/chapter-3/section-335-1/
- Government Code 911.2(a): a claim for death or injury to person or personal property or
  growing crops must be presented not later than six months after accrual; other causes
  of action, one year. https://codes.findlaw.com/ca/government-code/gov-sect-911-2/
- Civil Code 3333.4 (Proposition 213): no noneconomic loss recovery in an action arising
  out of the operation or use of a motor vehicle where (a)(1) the injured person was
  convicted of a 23152 or 23153 offense committed while driving, (a)(2) the injured person
  owned an uninsured vehicle involved in the accident, or (a)(3) the injured operator
  cannot establish financial responsibility; (b) insurers may not indemnify such
  noneconomic loss; (c) an uninsured owner injured by a driver convicted of drunk driving
  in the same crash is not barred.
  https://codes.findlaw.com/ca/civil-code/civ-sect-3333-4/
- SB 1107 raised the VC 16056 minimums for policies issued or renewed on or after
  January 1, 2025 to $30,000 per injured person, $60,000 per crash and $15,000 property
  damage, with the DMV cash deposit alternative rising to $75,000; Insurance Code
  11580.1(b) ties every auto policy to the 16056 figures; UM offering limits are $30,000
  and $60,000 under Insurance Code 11580.2(m). A further step to $50,000/$100,000/$25,000
  arrives January 1, 2035.
  https://www.insurance.ca.gov/0250-insurers/0300-insurers/0200-bulletins/bulletin-notices-commiss-opinion/upload/bulletin-2023-1-re-sb-1107-final-003.pdf
  https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/billTextClient.xhtml?bill_id=202120220SB1107

## Local texture

- Ventura County Superior Court: general civil filing, records and the law library sit in
  the Hall of Justice at 800 South Victoria Avenue, Ventura; records in Room 218; clerk
  hours 8:00 a.m. to 4:00 p.m.; e-filing mandatory for attorneys in limited and unlimited
  civil under CRC 2.253(b); the juvenile courthouse is at 4353 Vineyard Avenue in Oxnard.
  https://ventura.courts.ca.gov/divisions/civil  https://ventura.courts.ca.gov/divisions/records
- FRA accident report HQ-2015-1016, Rice Avenue crossing (DOT 745855H, UP Santa Barbara
  Subdivision MP 406.23): February 24, 2015, 5:42 a.m., Metrolink Train 102 southbound,
  48 passengers, struck a 2005 Ford F-450 with a Wells Cargo trailer belonging to Harvest
  Management LLC of Somerton, Arizona; the driver had been on duty and driving nearly 24
  consecutive hours per the NTSB investigation, turned onto the tracks 68 feet short of
  Fifth Street and abandoned the vehicle 85 feet up the track. Impact speed 57 mph;
  recorded crossing warning time 28 seconds; horn 10 seconds before impact; emergency
  brake 5 seconds before. 26 passengers and all 3 crew injured; the locomotive engineer
  died one week later. Equipment damage $14,000,000; track, signal and structures
  $154,754. Crossing has 20 twelve-inch flashers, gate arms with three lights each, and
  traffic signal preemption at Rice and Fifth.
  https://railroads.dot.gov/sites/fra.dot.gov/files/fra_net/16960/HQ-2015-1016%20Final_Oxnard,%20CA.pdf
- City of Oxnard: construction began March 2025 on a six-lane Rice Avenue bridge that
  grade-separates the Fifth Street/SR-34 intersection and the Union Pacific tracks; CPUC
  Rail Division ranked the crossing 4th in the state for needed safety improvements;
  environmental phase 2014-2018, design 2018-2024 with 75 utility facilities owned by
  more than 10 providers, right of way 2020-2024 with 17 parcels; six funding programs.
  https://www.oxnard.gov/project/rice-avenue-fifth-street-state-route-34-grade-separation
- Oxnard Local Road Safety Plan: 15 emphasis areas, 5 hot spot corridors (Ventura Road
  5th to Wooley, Gonzales Road Victoria to Rose, Wooley Road Factory to Pacific, Saviers
  Road Laurel to Pleasant Valley, Bard Road Saviers to Pleasant Valley) and 10 collision
  profiles including broadside collisions at signals without fully protected left turns
  and unsafe speed on 4-5 lane roadways.
  https://www.oxnard.gov/public-works/traffic-engineering/local-roadway-safety-plan
- Oxnard Safe Travel Plan framework report: most injury collisions 2016-2020 fell on
  Oxnard Boulevard, Rose Avenue, C Street, Ventura Road, Victoria Avenue, Gonzales Road,
  Wooley Road and Channel Islands Boulevard.
  https://www.oxnard.gov/wp-content/uploads/Oxnard-STP-Framework-Report.pdf
- OTS 2023 rankings, Oxnard (group B, average population 198,384, DVMT 1,360,230):
  1,312 victims killed and injured, composite ranking 1 of 62; 119 hit-and-run fatal and
  injury collisions (9/62); 178 speed-related (10/62); 143 nighttime (4/62); 78 bicyclist
  victims (8/62); 187 alcohol-involved victims (5/62).
  https://www.ots.ca.gov/rankings/oxnard-2023/
- Ventura County EMS policy 0400 lists the county's Level II trauma centers as Ventura
  County Medical Center and Los Robles Regional Medical Center. There is no designated
  trauma center inside Oxnard.
  https://hca.venturacounty.gov/public-health/ems/documents/policies/0400-ventura-county-emergency-departments-oct-2024/?layout=file
- Ventura County agriculture: 2023 gross value $2,170,243,000, up 2 percent, with
  strawberries first at $733,257,000.
  https://news.venturacounty.gov/en/20240724-crop-and-livestock-report-released/
