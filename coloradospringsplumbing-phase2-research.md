# Colorado Springs emergency plumbing — phase 2 research (verified 2026-08-25)

Differentiator: WATER PRESSURE AND THE PRESSURE ZONE PROBLEM.

## Colorado Springs Utilities water system and pressure

- "Our system is divided into 52 pressure zones (primarily gravity-driven)"; "260 system pressure
  regulating valves"; "25 water pump stations"; "38 water storage tanks"; project goal is
  "Consistent water pressure to standard of 60 pounds per square inch for customers."
  https://www.csu.org/hubfs/Document-Library/Water-Pressure-Zone-Presentation.pdf?hsLang=en
- "Colorado Springs' water distribution is generally a gravity system, however, pumped service is
  provided to some areas." "Ground elevations range from about 7,800 feet to 5,750 feet throughout
  the service area." "There are five major service areas: Briargate, Templeton, Northfield,
  Highline and Lowline." "Each lower service level can be fed through regulators from the higher
  levels and from its own distribution storage tanks." Distribution system: "more than 2,500 miles
  of mains and 40 treated water storage tanks and reservoirs."
  https://www.csu.org/hubfs/Document%20Library/UtilityWisePacket.pdf?hsLang=en
- West-side pressure zone project (near Garden of the Gods Park): Phase 1, Sept 2025–June 2026,
  "we assessed pressure reducing valves in about 675 homes or commercial buildings and replaced them
  as needed to prevent damage to plumbing and appliances." Phase 2 June–Sept 2026 in and around the
  Kissing Camels neighborhood. Phase 3 Sept–Nov 2026 re-assesses PRVs "after water pressure has
  increased." https://www.csu.org/current-projects/west-side-pressure-zone
- Neither CSU page states who owns a house-side PRV. WROTE AROUND IT: used the code language
  (PRV installed on the domestic branch main at the water service connection = private plumbing)
  plus the city ordinance duty to keep pipes and fixtures in good repair.

## Code (Colorado adopted 2021 IPC + 2021 IRC plumbing chapters)

- 3 CCR 720-1, State Plumbing Board: adopts "Chapters 2-13, 15; and Appendices B, C, D, and E of
  the IPC, 2021 edition" and "Chapters 25-32, of the IRC, 2021 edition."
  Colorado amendment adds IPC 504.6.1: "A means shall be provided to capture the discharge from a
  relief valve and convey it to the sanitary drainage system or exterior of the structure either by
  gravity or a pumped discharge," with an exception for "Replacements for existing water heaters."
  Colorado amendment replaces IPC 312.10.2: listed backflow assemblies "shall be tested at the time
  of installation, immediately after repairs or relocation and at least annually by a certified
  cross connection control technician."
  https://www.coloradosos.gov/CCR/GenerateRulePdf.do?ruleVersionId=11192&fileName=3%20CCR%20720-1
- P2903.3.2 Maximum Pressure: "The static water pressure shall be not greater than 80 psi (551 kPa).
  Where the main pressure exceeds 80 psi (551 kPa), an approved pressure-reducing valve conforming to
  ASSE 1003 or CSA B356 shall be installed on the domestic water branch main or riser at the
  connection to the water service pipe."
- P2903.4 Thermal Expansion Control; P2903.4.1 (device required where thermal expansion pushes
  pressure downstream of a PRV above the valve setting, service sizes up to and including 2 inches);
  P2903.4.2 (device required where a backflow prevention device, check valve or other device is
  installed on a system using storage water heating equipment).
- P2903.5 Water Hammer: "A water-hammer arrestor shall be installed where quick-closing valves are
  utilized... shall conform to ASSE 1010."
  All three: https://up.codes/viewer/colorado/irc-2021/chapter/29/water-supply-and-distribution
- Pikes Peak Regional Building Department water heater handout: "An expansion tank or expansion
  device is required when a PRV, check valve, or backflow preventer is installed in the water supply
  system." "Temperature and pressure relief valve (maximum 150 psi) shall terminate not more than
  6-inches above and not less than two times the discharge pipe diameter above the floor or flood rim
  of the waste receptor."
  https://www.pprbd.org/File/Resources/Downloads/ResidentialHandout/Water%20heater%20installation%20ACCESS.pdf

## Ownership / responsibility

- City Code 12.4.410 (water): Utilities bears maintenance and replacement "in public rights-of-way,
  generally between the connection to the distribution main and the property line or the curb stop if
  the curb stop is on or near the property line"; owner bears it "from the property line or curb stop";
  "The owner shall keep the service line and all pipes and fixtures on the owner's premises in good
  repair so as to prevent waste of water."
  https://codelibrary.amlegal.com/codes/coloradospringsco/latest/coloradosprings_co/0-0-0-19877
- City Code 12.5.410 (wastewater): owner responsible "from and including the connection to the
  collection line to the premises served"; must replace disintegrated portions at own expense;
  "Prior to repair or alteration of the service line, a permit must be obtained from Utilities";
  owner "responsible for returning the public right-of-way and the street to City standards."
  https://codelibrary.amlegal.com/codes/coloradospringsco/latest/coloradosprings_co/0-0-0-20573
- CSU wastewater backup policy: "Customers are responsible for maintaining the four-inch service line
  that runs between our main line and their home." "You should have your service line professionally
  cleaned every two years." "More frequent cleaning may be required for those living in older
  neighborhoods with large trees." Voluntary assistance up to $12,500 for residential homeowners on
  denied claims. https://www.csu.org/policies/wastewater-backup-policy
- Lead & copper program: "you are responsible for maintaining the water service line within your
  property, which typically lies between the curb stop and the structure receiving water service";
  no evidence of lead pipe in service area, all lines classed non-lead; pre-1970 era: 3% galvanized;
  goosenecks/pigtails "typically less than 24 inches" used with galvanized services before 1970; if a
  homeowner replaces a galvanized line on their property the utility replaces the curb-stop-to-main
  section. https://www.csu.org/current-projects/lead-and-copper-program
- Service line responsibility page: "From there, it is your responsibility to maintain and repair your
  private service lines." https://www.csu.org/safety/service-line-responsibility
- Emergency reporting: "24 hours per day: (719) 448-4800 or (888) 278-6847."
  https://www.csu.org/contact-us
- Water outages: "Most water main breaks take between 6 and 8 hours to repair"; "The most common
  reason for water main breaks in Colorado Springs is our rapid freeze/thaw cycle, which causes soil
  to shift and puts increased pressure on underground pipes."
  https://www.csu.org/safety/water-outages
- Backflow: City Code 12.4.1209 — tests at user's expense on installation, repair or replacement and
  at least annually; irrigation assemblies by July 1 each year; non-irrigation by October 1;
  non-testable methods inspected by Utilities by October 1.
  https://codelibrary.amlegal.com/codes/coloradospringsco/latest/coloradosprings_co/0-0-0-20182
  CSU program page: requirement falls on businesses and multi-family dwellings; noncompliance brings
  "Fines of $405 per backflow assembly" and suspension of water service.
  https://www.csu.org/safety/backflow-prevention

## Climate and housing

- NWS Pueblo 1991–2020 normals, Colorado Springs: January high 45.0F / low 18.5F; December
  44.6F / 18.7F. https://www.weather.gov/pub/climateCosDailyNormalsRecords
- Old North End Historic District (NPS nomination, History Colorado): period of significance
  1885–1965; 1,001 buildings exclusive of outbuildings, 903 contributing, 98 noncontributing;
  about 392 acres; bounded by Uintah Street, Monument Valley Park, the alley between Nevada Avenue
  and Weber Street, and Lilac Street; over four hundred new homes around the turn of the twentieth
  century; over 130 more in the 1920s and 1930s; very limited new construction after 1965. El Paso
  Canal used 1872–1956; city moved to a municipal water system in 1954.
  https://www.historycolorado.org/sites/default/files/media/documents/2018/5ep333.pdf
- 2025 CSU drinking water quality report: water is "blended from multiple surface water sources";
  "Almost 75% of our water originates from mountain streams"; collection systems include Homestake,
  Fryingpan-Arkansas, Twin Lakes and Blue River; local storage at Rampart Reservoir and North Slope
  of Pikes Peak reservoirs. https://www.csu.org/hubfs/Document-Library/2025-Water-Quality-Report.pdf?hsLang=en
