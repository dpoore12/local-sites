## I-15 freight corridor and Cajon Pass

- Caltrans 2024 Annual Average Daily Truck Traffic, route 015, San Bernardino County, postmile 43.488 ahead leg, "VICTORVILLE, JCT. RTE. 18 SOUTHEAST": total vehicle AADT 62,000; total truck AADT 14,669; trucks 23.66% of traffic; 9,919 of those trucks are five-axle. Same file: Nevada state line (PM 186.238) 45,000 total / 8,082 trucks / 17.96%; Yermo inspection station 25.43% trucks; Barstow Lenwood Road 23.91%; I-15 at Route 10 junction 211,000 total / 21,839 trucks. Source: https://dot.ca.gov/-/media/dot-media/programs/traffic-operations/documents/census/2024/2024-truck-aadt-a11y.xlsx (linked from https://dot.ca.gov/programs/traffic-operations/census)
- SBCTA I-15 Comprehensive Corridor Study: Cajon Pass grades approach 6% for about five miles; southbound truck speeds restricted below 45 mph on the Cajon Summit descent while auto speeds exceed 65 mph; crash rates through the pass roughly 58% higher than similar facilities; the segment between SR-138 and US-395 has the highest number of collisions; rear-end is the predominant collision type; speeding is the primary contributing factor and contributes to up to 50% of crashes on some segments; Friday daily traffic at the Cajon Junction truck scales exceeds 130,000 vehicles; holiday-Friday traffic at Cleghorn Road approaches 150,000; Sunday southbound peak runs 1:00 PM to 7:00 PM; autos are over 90% of weekend daily traffic. Source: https://www.gosbcta.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/10/I-Comprehensive-Corridor-Study.pdf
- SBCTA I-15 Northbound Truck Climbing Lane Extension fact sheet: extends the northbound truck climbing lane 3.4 miles from north of Kenwood Avenue to SR-138, removes a lane drop, adds signals and queue management at SR-138, improves operations at the northbound CHP truck scales; estimated construction cost about $102 million. Source: https://www.gosbcta.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/I-15-NB-TCL-Ext-fact-sheet_013026.pdf
- Caltrans Traffic Bulletin No. 24, Design Guide for Truck Escape Ramps: brake temperatures above 500 degrees lose the ability to slow a truck; descending in too high a gear increases braking effort; scale facilities, inspection stations, signals and construction can make already overheated brakes fail; the sequence before building an escape ramp is signing and posted downhill truck speeds, then a roadside brake inspection area at the top of the grade, then the ramp. Source: https://dot.ca.gov/-/media/dot-media/programs/safety-programs/documents/safety-devices/19861001-tsb-24-design-guide-for-truck-escape-ramps-a11y-v3.pdf
- California Vehicle Code 22406: 55 mph maximum for a motortruck or truck tractor with three or more axles, and for any truck tractor drawing another vehicle. Source: https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?sectionNum=22406.&lawCode=VEH
- City of Victorville: the city sits between Los Angeles and Las Vegas, 40 miles from I-10 and I-40, less than 100 miles from the Ports of Los Angeles and Long Beach, with freeway access to I-15, US-395 and SR-18. Source: https://www.victorvilleca.gov/business/why-victorville/market-access

## Weather (NOTE: brief said NWS Las Vegas; that is wrong for Victorville)

- api.weather.gov reports Victorville (34.5362, -117.2928) in forecast zone CAZ060, "Apple and Lucerne Valleys", with NWS San Diego (SGX) as the responsible office, not NWS Las Vegas. Sources: https://api.weather.gov/points/34.5362,-117.2928 and https://api.weather.gov/zones/forecast/CAZ060
- NWS San Diego all-hazard criteria: in the deserts a wind advisory needs sustained winds of at least 30 mph or gusts of at least 45 mph, and a high wind warning needs sustained 45 mph or gusts of 58 mph. For ridges, passes and canyons the advisory threshold is sustained 35 mph or gusts of 50 mph. A blowing dust advisory covers visibility of one quarter mile to one mile; a dust storm warning is visibility under one quarter mile. Source: https://www.weather.gov/media/sgx/documents/WWA_Criteria.pdf

## California law

- Two years for personal injury, three years for property damage, per the Judicial Council self-help guide citing Code of Civil Procedure 335.1 and 338. Source: https://selfhelp.courts.ca.gov/civil-lawsuit/statute-limitations
- Minimum liability limits: $30,000 for injury or death of one person, $60,000 for more than one person, $15,000 property damage, per Insurance Code 11580.1b. Source: https://www.dmv.ca.gov/portal/vehicle-registration/insurance-requirements/
- Insurance Code 11580.2: unknown-driver claims require physical contact; a report within 24 hours to the city police, or to the county sheriff or local CHP headquarters if the crash happened in unincorporated territory; a sworn statement to the insurer within 30 days; coverage does not apply where the insured settles with or sues a possibly liable person without the insurer's written consent; no cause of action accrues unless within two years suit is filed, the amount is agreed, or arbitration is instituted by certified mail. Source: https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?sectionNum=11580.2.&lawCode=INS
- Civil Code 3333.4 (Proposition 213): no non-economic damages where the injured person was the uninsured owner of a vehicle in the crash, or an operator who cannot establish financial responsibility, or a convicted drunk driver; subdivision (c) restores non-economic damages to an uninsured owner injured by a driver convicted under Vehicle Code 23152 or 23153. Source: https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?sectionNum=3333.4.&lawCode=CIV
- Comparative fault: CACI 405 requires the defendant to prove the plaintiff was negligent and that the negligence was a substantial factor, after which damages are reduced by the percentage of the plaintiff's responsibility; sources note Li v. Yellow Cab Co. (1975) 13 Cal.3d 804 abandoned all-or-nothing contributory negligence. Source: https://courts.ca.gov/system/files/itc/caci09-02.pdf
- Rules of Professional Conduct 1.5: (a) bars an unconscionable or illegal fee; (b) lists thirteen factors; (c)(2) bars a contingent fee for representing a defendant in a criminal case. Source: https://www.calbar.ca.gov/legal-professionals/rules/rules-professional-conduct/current-rules-professional-conduct/chapter-1-lawyer-client-relationship
- FMCSA guidance: motor carriers must retain records of duty status and supporting documents for six months from receipt, under 49 CFR 395.8(k)(1). Source: https://www.fmcsa.dot.gov/hours-service/elds/how-long-must-motor-carriers-retain-records-duty-status-rods-and-supporting
