# West Covina car accident — phase 2 research (medical lien / reimbursement angle)

All facts below were fetched during this session. URLs are the pages actually read.

## California Hospital Lien Act
- Civ. Code 3045.1: licensed hospital furnishing "emergency and ongoing medical or other services" to a person injured by accident or wrongful act has a lien on damages recovered, to the extent of "reasonable and necessary charges." https://law.justia.com/codes/california/code-civ/division-3/part-4/title-14/chapter-4/section-3045-1/
- Civ. Code 3045.3: lien not effective unless written notice (injured person's name/address, date of accident, hospital name/location, amount claimed, each party alleged liable) delivered or mailed registered mail, return receipt requested, to each allegedly liable party before money is paid; copy also to that party's known insurer. https://law.justia.com/codes/california/code-civ/division-3/part-4/title-14/chapter-4/section-3045-3/
- Civ. Code 3045.4: payer that pays the injured person after notice without paying the lien is liable for the lien amount, "or so much thereof as can be satisfied out of 50 percent of the moneys due under any final judgment, compromise, or settlement agreement after paying any prior liens." https://law.justia.com/codes/california/code-civ/division-3/part-4/title-14/chapter-4/section-3045-4/
- Civ. Code 3045.5: hospital may sue to enforce within one year after the date of payment to the injured person. https://law.justia.com/codes/california/code-civ/division-3/part-4/title-14/chapter-4/section-3045-5/
- Civ. Code 3045.6: chapter does not apply to claims against a common carrier subject to PUC or ICC jurisdiction. https://law.justia.com/codes/california/code-civ/division-3/part-4/title-14/chapter-4/section-3045-6/
- Long Beach Memorial Medical Center v. Allstate Ins. Co. (2023) 95 Cal.App.5th 710: a two-payee check delivered to the patient's attorney and never cashed is not "payment" satisfying the lien; insurer remained liable. Purpose of Act: secure part of recovery for the hospital bill "while ensuring that the patient retained sufficient funds to address other losses." https://caselaw.findlaw.com/court/ca-court-of-appeal/115099230.html

## Health plan / HMO liens — Civil Code 3040
- Cap on Knox-Keene plan or disability insurer liens: amount actually paid to providers (or 80% of usual and customary for capitated services), plus reasonable perfection costs.
- With an attorney: lien may not exceed one-third of the moneys due the enrollee. Without an attorney: not more than one-half.
- Lien reduced by the same comparative fault percentage by which recovery was reduced; subject to pro rata reduction for attorney fees and costs "in accordance with the common fund doctrine."
- Does not apply to workers' comp liens, Medi-Cal liens, or Hospital Lien Act liens; does not make an ERISA-preempted plan lien enforceable.
  https://law.justia.com/codes/california/code-civ/division-3/part-4/title-14/chapter-3-5/section-3040/

## Medi-Cal (DHCS Personal Injury Program)
- W&I Code 14124.72(d): director's lien reimbursement reduced by 25 percent as the director's share of attorney fees where the beneficiary alone brought the claim and bears fees/costs, plus a pro-rata share of litigation expenses (actual expenses times ratio of amount reimbursed to full settlement). https://law.justia.com/codes/california/code-wic/division-9/part-3/chapter-7/article-3-5/section-14124-72/
- W&I 14124.78: director may in no event recover more than the beneficiary recovers after deducting attorney's fees and litigation costs; if this section controls, 14124.72(d) reductions do not apply. https://law.justia.com/codes/california/code-wic/division-9/part-3/chapter-7/article-3-5/section-14124-78/
- W&I 14124.76: no settlement final without notice and a reasonable chance for the director to perfect and satisfy the lien; recovery limited to the portion representing payment for medical expenses, guided by Arkansas Dept. of Health & Human Servs. v. Ahlborn (2006) 547 U.S. 268; disputes by motion; 30 days to reject a tentative determination. https://law.justia.com/codes/california/code-wic/division-9/part-3/chapter-7/article-3-5/section-14124-76/
- W&I 14124.785: recovery limited to the lesser result of 14124.72, 14124.76 or 14124.78; limitations tolled until the director is notified all claims are resolved. https://law.justia.com/codes/california/code-wic/division-9/part-3/chapter-7/article-3-5/section-14124-785/
- DHCS lien process page: member must notify DHCS within 30 days of filing an action or claim; DHCS confirms within 30 days; eligibility verification 30 days; DHCS waits 120 days from settlement or final treatment before ordering payment data because providers have up to one year from date of service to bill Medi-Cal (W&I 14115); plans usually respond within 30 days; review takes 30 to 60 days; Medi-Cal lien must be paid before funding a special needs trust (Prob. Code 3604(d)). https://www.dhcs.ca.gov/services/the-personal-injury-lien-process/

## Medicare secondary payer
- 42 CFR 411.37: procurement-cost reduction. Where Medicare payments are less than the settlement: ratio of procurement costs to total settlement, applied to the Medicare payment, gives Medicare's share of procurement costs, subtracted from Medicare payments. Where payments equal or exceed the settlement: recovery is total settlement minus total procurement costs. https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/42/411.37
- CMS recovery process: BCRC sends Conditional Payment Letter and Payment Summary Form within 65 days of the Rights and Responsibilities letter; a Conditional Payment Notice must be answered within 30 calendar days or "a demand letter will automatically be issued without any reduction for fees or costs"; disputes take 45 calendar days; interest runs from the demand letter and is assessed each 30-day period; Intent to Refer letter at day 90; referral to Treasury within 60 days of that letter (150 days from the demand). https://www.cms.gov/medicare/coordination-benefits-recovery/beneficiary-services/recovery-process

## ERISA plans
- US Airways, Inc. v. McCutchen, 569 U.S. 88 (2013): in a 502(a)(3) equitable-lien-by-agreement action the plan's terms govern; neither the double-recovery nor the common-fund rule can override the contract. But where the plan is silent on allocating attorney's fees, "the common-fund doctrine provides the appropriate default rule to fill that gap." Facts: plan paid $66,866; recovery $110,000 ($10,000 tortfeasor + $100,000 own insurer); 40% fee = $44,000. https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/569/88/
- FMC Corp. v. Holliday, 498 U.S. 52 (1990): deemer clause; ERISA preempted Pennsylvania's antisubrogation statute as applied to a self-funded plan. Insured plans can be reached through state insurance regulation; self-funded ones cannot. https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/498/52/

## Med-pay and the common fund
- 21st Century Ins. Co. v. Superior Court (2009) S154790: made-whole rule applies to med-pay reimbursement, but attorney fees are not part of the made-whole calculation; fees are apportioned pro rata, analogous to the common fund doctrine. Example in the opinion: $1,000 med-pay, $6,000 settlement, ~$2,106.50 fees and costs; $400 attributable to the med-pay recovery, so $600 reimbursement discharged the obligation. Med-pay insurers must proceed by contractual reimbursement against the insured, not subrogation against the tortfeasor. https://law.justia.com/cases/california/supreme-court/2009/s154790/
- Quinn v. State of California (1975) 15 Cal.3d 162: a passive beneficiary of a recovery must contribute to the cost of creating the fund in proportion to its benefit; court first fixes a reasonable fee, then apportions. Workers' comp benefits were $13,942; judgment $91,314.99. https://law.justia.com/cases/california/supreme-court/3d/15/162.html

## Provider liens by agreement, factoring, and the value of care
- Gilman v. Dalby (2009): an attorney lien for fees and costs has priority over a medical lien regardless of which came first; recovery goes first to the attorney lien. Facts describe a factor buying medical accounts at a discount and taking assignment of the lien for the full amount. https://caselaw.findlaw.com/court/ca-court-of-appeal/1343433.html
- Dodd v. Cruz (2014) B247493: quoting Howell v. Hamilton Meats & Provisions, Inc. (2011) 52 Cal.4th 541, 555 and Corenbaum v. Lampkin (2013), past medical damages are limited to the lesser of the amount paid or incurred and the reasonable value of the services; the amount billed is not relevant to economic damages. Discovery into a factor's purchase of the lien was allowed. https://law.justia.com/cases/california/court-of-appeal/2014/b247493.html

## Litigation basics
- CCP 335.1: two years for injury or death caused by the wrongful act or neglect of another. https://law.justia.com/codes/california/code-ccp/part-2/title-2/chapter-3/section-335-1/
- Gov. Code 911.2(a): claim for death or injury to person or personal property presented not later than six months after accrual; other causes of action one year. Also: $25 filing fee for a claim presented to the Department of General Services. https://law.justia.com/codes/california/code-gov/title-1/division-3-6/part-3/chapter-2/article-1/section-911-2/
- Li v. Yellow Cab Co. (1975) 13 Cal.3d 804: pure comparative negligence; liability assigned in direct proportion to each party's negligence, damages diminished in proportion to the injured person's negligence; last clear chance abolished. https://law.justia.com/cases/california/supreme-court/3d/13/804.html
- Civ. Code 3333.4 (Proposition 213, 1996 General Election, per Hodges v. Superior Court (1999) 21 Cal.4th 109): no recovery of noneconomic losses for pain, suffering, inconvenience, physical impairment, disfigurement and other nonpecuniary damages where the injured person was convicted of DUI in the crash, owned an uninsured vehicle involved, or was an operator who cannot establish financial responsibility. Exception in (c) for an uninsured owner injured by a convicted drunk driver. Insurers may not indemnify such noneconomic losses. Hodges holds a products claim against a manufacturer falls outside the section. https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?sectionNum=3333.4.&lawCode=CIV and https://law.justia.com/cases/california/supreme-court/4th/21/109.html
- DMV: minimum liability limits per Ins. Code 11580.1b are $30,000 one person, $60,000 more than one person, $15,000 property damage. https://www.dmv.ca.gov/portal/vehicle-registration/insurance-requirements/
