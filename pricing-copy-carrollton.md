## pricing_lede

What does a garage door repair cost in Carrollton? Below are the working market ranges for this part of the Metroplex, the city's published permit and inspection charges, and the specific conditions that decide whether a job sits at the bottom or the top of its band.

## pricing_body

### Start with the permit question, because it is unusually clear here

Carrollton draws a bright line that saves homeowners money. Replacing a door or window with one of the same size does not require a permit; changing the size of the opening does. So a like-for-like sectional door swap on a Rosemeade or Josey Ranch house is a straightforward transaction between owner and installer, while widening a single opening into a double brings in structural work, a header, and the city.

When a permit does apply, the published figures are modest and predictable. The city's building inspection fee page lists a residential remodel permit at $125 and a nonrefundable application processing fee of $125, noting that where a permit would cost less than $125 the permit fee is charged as that application fee. Single-trade permits, the category that covers electrical work such as a new circuit for an opener, run $4 per $1,000 of value with a $75 minimum. A reinspection costs $50. Miscellaneous construction, which includes residential accessory structures, is listed at $50.

### Why the ranges above are ranges

Housing age sets the baseline. The 2020 to 2024 American Community Survey estimate puts the median year built in Carrollton at 1988, which means the typical door in this city is either an original builder-grade unit at the far end of its service life or the second unit installed on that opening. Two very different jobs share one description. On an original door, the springs are undersized by modern standards, the hinges are worn oval, and the track has been shimmed at least once. On a replacement door, the hardware is often sound and the fault is genuinely a single part.

Storm history sets the second variable. The city reported straight-line winds of 80 to 95 mph across all 37.1 square miles on May 28, 2024. Wind of that kind does not always break a door outright. It racks it. A racked door still opens, but it wears cables unevenly, drags one side of the track, and eventually pulls a top-section bracket loose. When a technician finds that pattern, the honest scope is wider than the symptom the caller described, and the total sits higher in the range.

Third comes access and heat. An attached garage on a slab with clear side room is the low end of every row. A door with a low-headroom track kit, an opener mounted with the trolley crowded against a stored roof rack, or an afternoon in a closed garage in July all add labor time to identical parts.

### What a fair quote itemizes

The spring line should carry four numbers: wire diameter, inside diameter, length and wind direction. Anything less means nobody has measured the door, and a spring chosen by eye is a spring that fails early or burns out an opener. The quote should state the door's approximate weight or the drum series it is set up for, list rollers by stem length and whether they are steel or nylon with sealed bearings, and count hinges rather than describing them.

Labor should appear as its own line, with the trip or diagnostic charge shown separately and a plain statement of whether it is credited toward the repair. On a full replacement, expect the model and gauge, the insulation value if the garage is conditioned, whether the existing opener and rail are being reused, the track configuration, new bottom weatherseal, and haul-away of the old sections. Where the opening size is not changing, the quote should say so, because that sentence is what confirms no permit is needed.

### The upsell to push back on

The pattern here is the opener sale. A door that reverses on its way down, hesitates, or runs rough is presented as a control board or motor problem, and a new opener with a bright feature list is proposed. On a 1980s and 1990s housing stock, the far more common causes are a spring that has lost tension and left the opener lifting weight it was never meant to lift, a photo eye knocked out of alignment by a bicycle, dry rollers, or a track pinched where a car door has kissed it. Replacing the opener over any of those puts a new motor to work against the same resistance.

The tell is simple and worth asking for. Disconnect the opener, lift the door by hand to waist height, and let go. A correctly balanced door stays roughly where it is. A door that slams down or shoots up is a spring problem wearing an opener costume, and the balance test costs nothing to perform.

Also worth resisting: a proposal to replace all hardware because one part broke. Selective replacement is legitimate when a technician can point to measured wear. Wholesale replacement without that explanation is a way of turning a repair into a project.

### What the number online does not cover

Search results for this trade quote a national parts-plus-labor figure. They do not carry the reinspection charge when a permitted electrical rough fails a first visit, the single-trade minimum on a new opener circuit, the low-headroom conversion on a garage that was framed tight, the reinforcement strut a heavier opener requires across the top section, the cost of matching one panel on a door profile that has been out of production for a decade, or a second trip when a homeowner association in Castle Hills or Country Place has to sign off on the appearance of a street-facing door. On the repair side they leave out the part that comes off with the broken one, which on a door approaching forty years old is the normal case rather than the exception.
