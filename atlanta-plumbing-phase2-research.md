## City of Atlanta Department of Watershed Management (DWM)

- "Atlanta's water and sewer infrastructure is old." Leak categories with ownership split:
  "In distribution lines from the meter to the house (repairs are the homeowner's responsibility)";
  "In distribution lines from the main to the meter (repairs are the City's responsibility)";
  transmission lines generally City. Report a water system leak: 404-546-0311.
  https://atlantawatershed.org/report-a-problem/
- Main break reporting: 404-546-0311, 24/7. "A specialist will be at the location to investigate the
  problem within 24 hours." Can take 30-45 days to fully resolve including repairs and restoring the
  property and/or street. Prioritized by critical facilities, critical infrastructure, public safety,
  severity of water loss. https://www.atl311.com/en-us/knowledgearticle/?code=KB0012772
- Sewer backup: call 404-546-0311; "A first responder will be at the location to investigate the
  possible backup within 8 hours"; if confirmed "it can take 4-8 hours to contain"; 30-45 days to fully
  resolve. Definition of sewage overflow includes discharges on private property.
  https://www.atl311.com/en-us/knowledgearticle/?code=KB0011833
- Private property matters the City will NOT handle: "Homeowner/resident requests an emergency water
  turn-on/off due to a burst water pipe located on private property. You may have an internal issue
  that requires the expertise of a licensed plumber."; same for inspecting a water event isolated to
  a place in the home; also "Leaking basement and wet crawl space caused by property slope."
  https://www.atl311.com/en-us/knowledgearticle/?code=KB0012672

## Consent decrees / combined sewers

- MOST page: CSO consent decree 1998 and SSO consent decree 1999, "require investing in water and sewer
  improvements estimated at $4 billion." City has "Repaired and replaced over 387 miles of aged sewer
  lines"; spills down 62% in number and 97% in volume since 2004; "over 57 miles of sewer capacity
  relief." https://atlantawatershed.org/most/
- 2024 MOST briefing booklet: "$2.3 billion in sewer system improvements"; separated 11 miles of combined
  sewers in downtown Atlanta under the first decree; 90% separation achieved; 18 miles of sewer tunnels at
  Nancy Creek and the West Area CSO facility to manage excess flow in major rain events; evaluated 1,574
  miles of sewer mains; Peachtree Creek trunk project rehabbed ~11,000 linear feet of 90-inch, 80-year-old
  sewer pipe; Old Fourth Ward Park 17-acre greenspace with 9-million-gallon stormwater pond.
  https://www.atlantawatershed.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/01/2024-MOST-Briefing-Booklet-New-Date-1-23-24-Rev1.pdf
- Clean Water Atlanta consent decree FAQ: "A CSO... occurs in a combined sewer/storm water system, where
  both storm water and sanitary sewage flow in the same pipe"; overflows designed to prevent flooding at
  the plants "when the volume of rain exceeds the capacity of the combined pipes"; combined system was
  15% of the city with 85% already separated; tunnel system serves downtown, Midtown near Piedmont Park,
  Georgia Tech area, parts of east Atlanta near Grant Park; Intrenchment Creek Tunnel about 1.76 miles
  long and 26 feet in diameter; Three Rivers Tunnel about 7 miles long; depths up to 270 feet below
  ground surface. https://cleanwateratlanta.h2o4atl.com/index.php/frequently-asked-questions/
- EPA OIG 18-P-0206: 1998 decree levied $2.5 million in civil penalties; Atlanta agreed to no more than
  four CSO overflows per year; 2002 CSO Remedial Measures Plan said facilities recorded CSO events about
  50 to 70 times per year; 2009-2017 total of 10 CSO events; 2012 amendment extended construction to 2027,
  supported in part by "a 252-percent increase in water and sewer rates and a 1 percent municipal sales
  tax"; SSOs 2013-2017 sent between 1.3 and 9.5 million gallons per year; average of 14 rain-induced and
  242 dry-weather overflows 2013-2016.
  https://www.epa.gov/sites/default/files/2018-05/documents/_epaoig_20180530-18-p-0206.pdf
- EPA green/gray page: Atlanta separated 33 miles of combined sewers, built 18 miles of storage; since
  2001 CSO number down 62 percent and untreated volume down 97 percent.
  https://www.epa.gov/npdes/combined-sewer-overflow-greengray-infrastructure

## Rates, bills, leak adjustments

- DWM: rate structure held July 1, 2024 through June 30, 2028; "Atlanta's water and sewer rates have not
  increased since 2012." https://atlantawatershed.org/waterrates/
- Base charge $6.56 for water inside and outside the city and for sewer; tiers 1-3 CCF, 4-6 CCF, 7+ CCF;
  one CCF = 748 gallons. Adjustments allowed under City Code Chapter 154 Article II for billing errors,
  fees and penalties, leaks (including some customer-side leaks and vandalism), meter leaks, department
  error. Request must arrive within 12 months of the "Payment Due By" date, include satisfactory proof the
  leak existed and was repaired, and the customer must pay the Temporary Payment Amount within 20 days of
  the acknowledgement letter. Appeal to the Water and Sewer Appeals Board within 14 days. Published leak
  rates: running toilet 2.5 gallons per minute = 108,000 gallons per month; leaking pipe 3 gpm = 129,600
  gallons per month; leaky toilet flapper 0.25 gpm = 10,800 gallons per month.
  https://atlantawatershed.org/account-review-request/
- S&P raised DWM senior-lien revenue bond rating to AA from AA-; DWM "provides drinking water and
  wastewater services to more than 1.2 million people across the region."
  https://www.atlantaga.gov/Home/Components/News/News/15684/1338

## Georgia state layer

- Ga. Comp. R. & Regs. 121-3-.02: "Statewide Class I Master Plumber licenses are limited to plumbing
  involving single-family dwellings and one-level dwellings designed for not more than two families and
  commercial structures not to exceed 10,000 square feet in area. Statewide Class II Master Plumber
  licenses are unrestricted." Master applicants: 5 years experience, at least 2 licensed as a journeyman;
  exam score minimum 70. https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/georgia/Ga-Comp-R-Regs-R-121-3-.02
- O.C.G.A. 43-14-8: "No person shall engage in the business of plumbing as a master plumber unless such
  person has a valid license from the Division of Master Plumbers and Journeyman Plumbers." A company must
  have a full-time licensed master plumber connected with it.
  https://law.justia.com/codes/georgia/title-43/chapter-14/section-43-14-8/
- Secretary of State FAQ: no reciprocity with other states; "Only licensed Master Plumbers may have a
  business (not Journeyman)"; renewal period September through November of even years; 4 CE hours annually
  (8 per renewal); License Search available. https://sos.ga.gov/page/division-master-and-journeyman-plumbers-faq
- DCA current codes: "International Plumbing Code, 2024 Edition, with Georgia Amendments - 2026."
  https://dca.georgia.gov/community-assistance/construction-codes/current-state-minimum-codes-construction
- O.C.G.A. 44-7-13: "The landlord shall keep the premises in repair"; leases include a provision that the
  premises is fit for human habitation, per 2024 Ga. Laws 392, effective 7/1/2024 for leases entered into
  or renewed on or after that date. https://law.justia.com/codes/georgia/title-44/chapter-7/article-1/section-44-7-13/

## Neighborhood era

- Inman Park: "approximately 135-acre residential neighborhood laid out from 1889 to 1891 by developer
  Joel Hurt"; featured one of the nation's first electric streetcar lines along Edgewood Avenue; period of
  significance 1889-1945. https://npgallery.nps.gov/GetAsset/23f221d5-096e-4454-b6a3-4815012cfec2
- Grant Park: city historic district designated April 11, 2000; neighborhood occupies over 430 acres;
  streetcar service along what is now Park Avenue from 1883, line extended into the park 1888; "by 1893
  maps show that almost all of the present day roads were designed, if not built"; Craftsman bungalow the
  prevalent early 20th century style. https://www.atlantaga.gov/?navid=470
- Grant Park NR nomination: district listed July 20, 1979; street network and residential patterns
  developed "from the late 1880's into the early 1900's."
  https://npgallery.nps.gov/GetAsset/35056631-2b9c-4e54-873a-2bf8a98fed54
