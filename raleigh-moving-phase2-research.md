## 1. State authority — NC General Statutes Chapter 62, Article 12
Source: https://ncleg.gov/EnactedLegislation/Statutes/HTML/ByArticle/Chapter_62/Article_12.html

- **G.S. 62-262(a)** — "no person shall engage in the transportation of passengers or household
  goods in intrastate commerce unless such person shall have applied to and obtained from the
  Commission a certificate authorizing such operations."
- **G.S. 62-262(e)** — applicant must show public convenience and necessity, fitness, and solvency.
- **G.S. 62-261(8)** — Commission issues (and may revoke) certificates of exemption for intrastate
  household goods; no denial or revocation without opportunity for hearing.
- **G.S. 62-271** — no common carrier of household goods may relinquish possession at destination
  until all tariff rates and charges have been paid.
- **G.S. 62-272** — the Commission "may … determine what is a reasonable charge as the maximum."
- **G.S. 62-273** — C.O.D. funds are "held in trust"; failure to remit within **15 days** after
  collection is prima facie evidence of wilful conversion; **Class H felony**.
- **G.S. 62-273.1(b)** — "The Commission shall conduct a criminal history record check of applicants
  and current holders of a certificate to transport household goods."
- **G.S. 62-280** — vehicle marking violation (G.S. 20-398): civil penalty up to **$5,000**.
- **G.S. 62-280.1** — falsely representing that you hold a household-goods certificate is a
  **Class 3 misdemeanor**, fine up to **$500** first offense, up to **$2,000** for subsequent;
  civil penalty up to **$5,000**.
- **G.S. 62-268** — security/insurance filed before a certificate is in force; $50,000 bodily
  injury one person, $100,000 two or more, $50,000 property of others.

## 2. NCUC oversight and how to verify a mover
Sources: https://www.ncuc.gov/Industries/transportation/transportation.html ,
https://www.ncuc.gov/industries/transportation/movingguide.html ,
https://www.ncuc.gov/industries/documents/moving.pdf (Moving 101, 2026 edition),
carrier list: https://www.ncuc.gov/industries/documents/carriers.pdf

- Commission regulates intrastate household-goods transportation; mover needs a Commission
  certificate, referred to as a **"C" number**.
- Certificated movers must carry a Commission-required minimum of **$50,000 cargo insurance** and
  **$50,000 general liability** (Moving 101 PDF).
- Public list: "LIST OF CARRIERS ISSUED A CERTIFICATE OF EXEMPTION," updated **monthly**;
  cancelled/suspended/new certificates do not appear until the next update, so current status must
  be confirmed by phone.
- Complaint / assistance contacts in the 2026 Moving 101 PDF: Public Staff **Transportation Rates
  Division, 919-880-1837** (unresolved claims; reporting an uncertificated mover); Commission
  **919-733-4036** for tariff/carrier-list access.
- The older HTML version of Moving 101 (movingguide.html) still carries **superseded figures**
  ($0.75 per $100 FVP, $4.00/lb minimum, 500-lb and 35/36-mile thresholds, 919-733-7766).
  The 2026 PDF and Tariff No. 2 govern. **Do not use the HTML page's numbers.**
- Interstate, international, government/military, commercial office, and new-furniture retail
  deliveries are outside Commission jurisdiction.

## 3. Maximum Rate Tariff No. 2 (NCUC HHG NO. 2)
Issued **December 30, 2025**; effective **January 1, 2026**. Cited from the local text extract.

- **Rule 2** — the published rates are *maximum* rates; a carrier may not exceed any individual
  component. Customers and carriers may negotiate lower.
- **Rule 12** — regular hours 8:00 a.m.–5:00 p.m., excluding Saturdays, Sundays and legal holidays.
- **Rule 13 — the 120% rule.** A nonbinding written estimate must be provided on request; "the
  final billed charges shall not exceed 120% of the estimate amount, unless a Change
  Order/Addendum to Estimated Cost of Services form is completed and signed by shipper prior to
  the start of the move or upon additional service request by shipper."
  EXCEPTION: not required if requested fewer than **5 business days** before the move or if the
  shipment weighs under **1,000 pounds**.
- **Rule 14** — binding guaranteed estimates, same 5-day / 1,000-lb exceptions.
- **Rule 15** — binding not-to-exceed estimates: **no longer applicable effective 1-1-2026**
  (Docket No. T-100, Sub 49).
- **Rule 17** — hourly rates apply at a ratemaking distance of **50 miles or less**; Section III
  weight-and-distance rates may not be applied to hauls of 50 miles or less.
- **Rule 18** — quarter-hour rounding. Example in tariff: 6 hours 10 minutes bills as 6.25 hours;
  8 hours 50 minutes bills as 9 hours.
- **Rule 53 — maximum hourly rates.** Regular time: vehicle & 2 crew **$188.40**, & 3 **$243.30**,
  & 4 **$297.90**, & 5 **$352.95**; each additional crew **$54.80**, each additional vehicle
  **$52.50**. Overtime: **$235.15 / $310.25 / $385.15 / $460.10**; additional crew **$74.85**,
  additional vehicle **$65.75**. Time runs from arrival of vehicle and crew at origin to
  completion of all services at destination; a maximum of **1 hour travel time per 50 miles** may
  be added. An additional vehicle may be billed only after a standard 26-foot vehicle's capacity
  is exceeded, and extra men/vehicles must be agreed in writing before the move.
- **Rule 7 — valuation.** Basic Value Protection is the no-charge default at **$0.60 per pound**
  per article; the tariff's own example: a 65-inch, 50-pound LED television has a damaged value of
  **$30** regardless of cost. Full Value Protection requires a minimum valuation of **$5.00/lb**
  (single 26-ft vehicle) or **$6.00/lb** (more than one vehicle). **Rule 7(E)**: if the carrier
  neglects to obtain the shipper's signature in the valuation section, the shipment is "deemed
  released to coverage of up to **$15,000**, at no additional cost to the shipper."
- **Item 18** — FVP price **$1.00 per $100** of declared value (single vehicle) or **$1.25 per
  $100** (more than one). Storage-in-transit adds **15% of the FVP rate per 30-day period**.
  Articles or matched sets valued at **$5,000 or more** are excluded unless the shipper gives
  written notice.
- **Moving 101 (2026 PDF)**: articles worth more than **$100 per pound** are articles of
  extraordinary value and must be listed; unlisted, liability can be capped at $100/lb.
  Ready-to-assemble particle-board furniture: liability is **$0.60/lb or $50 per article,
  whichever is greater**, regardless of valuation chosen. NC is described as one of only **four
  states** that recognizes this furniture category.
- **Rule 28** — the Uniform Household Goods Bill of Lading must carry the carrier's **NCUC
  Certificate of Exemption number**, the carrier's own name (not an agent's), a preferred delivery
  date or window, a signed acknowledgment that the shipper received the rights brochure, and a
  **Commission telephone number** for questions. Electronic bills of lading must display on a
  screen no smaller than **7 inches** diagonally.
- **Rule 11** — payment due before relinquishing possession; **1% per month** finance charge after
  30 days; a card processing fee may be passed through but **only up to what the processor charges
  the carrier**, and it must be listed on the bill of lading.
- **Rule 52 — claims.** Written claim within **9 months** after a reasonable time for delivery has
  lapsed. Carrier must send an acknowledgment with claim procedures no later than **15 days** after
  receiving a written claim, with final resolution **within 60 days**. Moving 101 adds: suit must be
  filed within **two years and one day** of written denial.
- **Rule 51** — a carrier may not collect freight charges on a shipment totally lost or destroyed
  in transit.
- **Rule 29 / Moving 101** — constructive weight **7 pounds per cubic foot** where no adequate
  scale is available.
- **Rule 48 / Moving 101** — storage-in-transit up to **180 days**, billed in 30-day increments;
  beyond that it becomes permanent storage outside Commission jurisdiction.
- **Item 10** — piano/organ 38 inches or taller, or any grand: **$111.90** handling; under 38
  inches **$81.65**; laden home freezer **$93.30**; slate pool table **$128.25**. Flight carry
  inside a building: first flight **$33.55**, each additional **$16.85**; outside first flight
  **$32.15**, then **$0.60 per step**. Inside, a flight is at least **8 steps**; an elevator counts
  as one flight.
- **Item 20** (weight-and-distance moves) — elevator **$3.10 per 100 lb** at each end; stairs,
  inside or outside, **$2.20 per 100 lb per flight** at each end; excessive-distance extra carry
  **$2.20 per 100 lb**, an extra carry being each **50 feet or fraction after the first 75 feet**
  between the vehicle and the door or the apartment. Piano minimums: **$20.15** first flight,
  **$10.15** each additional, **$20.15** minimum elevator charge.
- **Item 1 — packing and unpacking (labor plus materials, max rates):** dish pack/drum **$74.05**;
  box not over 5 cu ft **$25.20**; 1.5 cu ft carton **$13.90**; 3 cu ft **$22.25**; 4.5 cu ft
  **$26.65**; 6 cu ft **$26.80**; 6.5 cu ft **$29.25**; wardrobe carton **$33.10**; twin mattress
  carton **$26.65**, double **$29.75**, king/queen **$47.95**; mattress cover **$13.20**;
  corrugated container for mirrors/glass/marble **$47.60**; crate **$26.95 per cu ft** with a
  **$107.85** minimum per crate; 2.5 cu ft office tote **$11.75**.
- **Item 3 — containers only, furnished to the shipper:** carton under 3 cu ft **$4.20**; 3 cu ft
  **$7.05**; 4.5 cu ft **$8.30**; 6 cu ft **$9.45**; dish pack **$17.35**; wardrobe **$22.50**;
  king/queen mattress carton **$26.40**; corrugated container **$18.50**; unprinted newsprint
  **$1.90 per pound**; 2-inch plastic tape **$7.20 per roll**; flat-screen TV box under 40 inches
  **$66.50**, 40 inches or more **$82.50**.
- **Item 4 — labor charges (weight-and-distance moves only):** **$41.85** per crew member per hour
  regular, **$51.40** outside 8-to-5 weekdays or any hour on weekends and holidays.
- **Rule 54** — incidental packing during an hourly move is billed at the Rule 53 hourly rate plus
  Item 3 container charges; packing done before move day is billed under Item 1 or Item 2.
- **Rule 23/24** — exclusive use of a vehicle carries minimum weights (7,000 lb for 1,000 cu ft or
  less; 7 lb per cu ft above that); reserving 300 cu ft or less sets a 2,100-pound minimum.

## 4. Federal layer (move crossing a state line)
- **49 CFR 375.213** — before the move the carrier must furnish DOT's "Ready to Move?" brochure
  (FMCSA-ESA-03-005) and the contents of appendix A, "Your Rights and Responsibilities When You
  Move" (FMCSA-ESA-03-006), by copy or website hyperlink. Printed cover must carry, in 12-point or
  larger bold type, "Your Rights and Responsibilities When You Move. Furnished by Your Mover, as
  Required by Federal Law." Source: https://www.ecfr.gov/api/versioner/v1/full/2026-08-01/title-49.xml?part=375
  (public page: https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-49/part-375 )
- **49 CFR 375.207** — every advertisement must carry the carrier's name as assigned and its
  U.S. DOT number, displayed as "U.S. DOT No. (assigned number)."
- **49 CFR 375.401(a)** — the carrier must conduct a physical survey, on-site or by live or
  recorded video, and give a written estimate based on it; the shipper may waive the survey in
  writing.
- **49 CFR 375.405(b)(5), (b)(8) and 375.407(a)** — a nonbinding estimate must state that the
  shipper will not be required to pay more than **110 percent** of it at delivery; the carrier must
  relinquish the shipment on payment of up to 110 percent, plus separately itemized additional
  services and impracticable-operations charges capped at **15 percent** of other charges due.
  Anything else must be billed after **30 days**.
- **49 CFR 375.103** — "reasonable dispatch"; withholding a shipment after the shipper offers the
  binding estimate or 110 percent of a nonbinding estimate is a violation.
- **49 CFR 375.303** — liability insurance may be sold only where the shipment is released at not
  more than **60 cents per pound** per article.
- **49 CFR 370.5(a)** — the carrier must acknowledge a written claim in writing within **30 days**
  of receipt unless it has paid or declined it in that time.
  https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-49/subtitle-B/chapter-III/subchapter-B/part-370/section-370.5
- **49 CFR 370.9(a)** — the carrier must pay, decline, or make a firm written compromise offer
  within **120 days**; if it cannot, it must report the status in writing at 120 days and every
  **60 days** thereafter.
  https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-49/subtitle-B/chapter-III/subchapter-B/part-370/section-370.9
- FMCSA booklet requirement, consumer facing: https://www.fmcsa.dot.gov/protect-your-move/consumer-rights
- Mover lookup: https://ai.fmcsa.dot.gov/hhg/search.asp (search by company or by state).
  Appendix A text: https://www.fmcsa.dot.gov/sites/fmcsa.dot.gov/files/RightsandResponsibilitiesBooklet_2022Update.pdf
  Released-value example in appendix A: a 10-pound stereo component worth $1,000 recovers no more
  than **$6.00**.

## 5. Raleigh curb, right-of-way and parking
Sources: https://raleighnc.gov/permits/services/right-way-occupancy ,
https://raleighnc.gov/transportation/services/right-way-services ,
https://raleighnc.gov/parking/services/parking-customer-service/parking-frequently-asked-questions ,
https://raleighnc.gov/parking/services/parking-customer-service/residential-parking-permit-program

- A permit is required to install an object in, or occupy, the right-of-way, and the right-of-way
  includes **the street, the sidewalk, and the sidewalk shoulder**. Occupancy types listed:
  sidewalk occupancy with pedestrian detour, occupying a lane, temporary full street closure, and
  use of parking spaces for dumpsters or storage. Applications go through the Permit and
  Development Portal.
- Right-of-Way staff works with Raleigh Parking on permits for legal parking spaces; **requests for
  parking passes for construction vehicles, moving vans and other vehicles are obtained directly
  from Raleigh Parking.** For timed or paid spaces the approved pass is picked up at
  **310 W. Martin Street** and must be displayed at all times while the space is occupied.
  No moving-van pass fee or lead time is published.
- Construction parking permit cost is set by **the number of days requested times the daily rate of
  each space**; issued at 310 W. Martin St., Suite 109, **919-996-3996**.
- Meters: payment required **8 a.m.–6 p.m. Monday through Friday** downtown including Glenwood
  South; **8 a.m.–8 p.m. Monday through Friday** in the Hillsborough District near NC State; free
  on weekends and after hours. **Enforcement runs 24 hours every day.** Meter or time-zone fine
  **$20**, plus a **$20 late fee** if unpaid within **21 days**.
- Residential Parking Permit: **$30 per permit cycle**, no proration; guest passes cover one
  **24-hour period**; restrictions typically 8 a.m.–5 p.m. and vary block by block; non-permit
  vehicles may park on a permit block **only once per day**.
- Temporary full street closure documentation includes a traffic control and pedestrian plan, a
  parking application when reserving metered spaces, a notification letter, a certificate of
  insurance, and a **$5,000 performance bond** for construction-type work.
- House relocation or oversized equipment transport in the right-of-way needs its own permit and
  coordination of police escort and overhead line work:
  https://raleighnc.gov/permits/services/house-move-and-oversized-equipment-transport

## 8. Could not verify / wrote around
- No published dollar fee or lead time for a Raleigh moving-van parking pass. The city publishes
  only the process and the display requirement, so the copy describes the process, not a price.
- No Wake-County-specific net domestic migration figure in the Census Vintage 2025 release; the
  OSBM 2023–24 figure is used instead and labeled by year.
- No NWS Raleigh page states an average annual count of 90-degree days; the daily-normals table is
  used instead.
- The brief's premise that Raleigh requires a permit "for parking a moving truck" is only partly
  right: a pass is needed for timed or paid spaces, and a right-of-way permit for occupying a lane
  or sidewalk. An ordinary truck in an unrestricted residential space needs neither.
