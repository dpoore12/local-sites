## Texas insurance (verified)
- TDI Commissioner's Bulletin B-0030-98: adopted Endorsement HO-145 "Exclusion of Cosmetic Damage to Roof Coverings Caused by Hail" (HO-A/B/C) and TDP-022 for dwelling forms, effective May 2, 1998. Text: "We do not cover cosmetic loss or damage to roof coverings caused by the peril of hail." Cosmetic means damage that "alters the physical appearance of the roof covering but does not result in damage that allows the penetration of water ... or does not result in the failure of the roof covering to perform its intended function." Endorsement may be attached only to a risk receiving a premium credit for an impact-resistant roof covering, must be signed by the insured, and is void unless signed.
  https://www.tdi.texas.gov/bulletins/1998/b-0030-8.html
  IMPORTANT: the TDI-prescribed cosmetic exclusion is limited to ROOF COVERINGS. TDI does not publish a garage-door cosmetic exclusion.
- TDI, "What to know about deductibles" (updated 10/14/2024): a percentage deductible must be translated into dollars; on a home insured for $150,000 a 5 percent deductible is $7,500, so a $6,500 roof repair pays nothing, while a $500-deductible policy pays $6,000. Moving from $500 to $1,000 can save as much as 20 percent of premium.
  https://www.tdi.texas.gov/tips/deductibles.html
- TDI home insurance guide: most policies do not cover wear and tear; photograph damage before repairs; make only temporary repairs; do not make permanent repairs before the company sees the damage; try to be present for the adjuster's inspection and consider having the contractor present; the company must tell you it received the claim within 15 days.
  https://www.tdi.texas.gov/pubs/consumer/cb025.html
- Insurance Code Chapter 542, Subchapter B (statute PDF): 542.055 acknowledge, begin investigating and request items not later than the 15th day after notice; 542.056 accept or reject in writing not later than the 15th business day after receiving all items, 45 more days if the insurer notifies that it needs more time; 542.057 pay not later than the fifth business day after notice of payment; 542.058 damages if payment is delayed more than 60 days; 542.059 deadlines extended 15 days for a weather-related catastrophe; 542.060 18 percent a year as damages plus attorney's fees, and for Chapter 542A actions simple interest at five percent above the Finance Code 304.003 rate.
  https://statutes.capitol.texas.gov/Docs/IN/pdf/IN.542.pdf
- Insurance Code Chapter 1813, Appraisal of Disputed Losses (added by SB 458, 2025, effective September 1, 2025): applies to personal residential property and personal auto policies; every such policy "must contain an appraisal provision that complies with this chapter"; appraisal is "a type of dispute resolution process solely to determine the amount of loss"; the amount determined is binding except for "fraud, accident, or material mistake"; commissioner adopts rules setting the period for completing an appraisal. Does not apply to TWIA or commercial policies.
  https://statutes.capitol.texas.gov/Docs/IN/htm/IN.1813.htm

## Trade standards
- ANSI/DASMA 102-2004 sec. 6.1: residential and commercial door systems "shall be designed to operate at a minimum of 10,000 cycles when they are properly selected, installed, operated, and maintained." Sec. 2.2 defines one cycle as closed to fully open and back to closed. Sec. 2.8: a residential sectional door is "normally expected to be operated less than 1,500 cycles per year."
  https://law.resource.org/pub/us/cfr/regulations.gov.docket.15/dasma.102.2004.html
- ANSI/DASMA 102 (2000 posting): torsion springs shall not be used to balance sectional doors with an index ratio under 6 to 1, index ratio being mean coil diameter divided by wire diameter.
  https://www.dasma.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/01/ANSIDASMA102.pdf
- DASMA TDS #190, Factors Affecting Spring Cycle Life: a nick or notch can be a weak point; dropped torsion springs can crack cast fittings; springs should be kept dry because rust reduces the effective area of the wire and corrosion pits let fatigue cracks accelerate; not knowing whether a spring meets the door's counterbalance requirement can reduce the cycle life of the door assembly and the motor and create a manual-operation safety issue; insufficient torque lets a door drift down from the open position; paint or added components can push door weight past the manufacturer's specification, and a technician can weigh it with the counterbalance disabled; doors in climates of extreme heat should be kept closed.
  https://www.dasma.com/wp-content/uploads/pubs/TechDataSheets/CommercialResidential/TDS190.pdf
- DASMA TDS #174, occupant checking a door for damage: occupants should not remove, adjust or repair doors, springs or components; stop the visual inspection if a problem is seen and do not operate the door; inspect jambs and header attachment, track fasteners and alignment, rollers in track without tension, brackets and hinges, loose cables, and sections from inside and outside for cracks, dents and buckling, paying attention around end-hardware fasteners because that can indicate structural damage; "any deformation of panels, tracks, or hardware can make a door questionable as to its operability."
  https://www.dasma.com/wp-content/uploads/pubs/TechDataSheets/CommercialResidential/TDS174.pdf
- DASMA TDS #175, post-event door operation by a trained technician: judge whether the door should be repaired or replaced; give particular attention to sections, stiles, struts, hinges, rollers and fasteners; "if stress or fatigue has compromised the door's performance, you should advise the property owner to forward your assessment and recommendations to the property owner's insurance company"; a registered design professional may need to be contacted; the assessment should state when damage requires replacement of the door system.
  https://www.dasma.com/wp-content/uploads/pubs/TechDataSheets/CommercialResidential/TDS175.pdf
- DASMA TDS #183, Garage Door Component Substitution: manufacturers "design and/or specify every component of a garage door assembly"; "replacing parts on tested, certified, or wind load rated products to standards such as ANSI/DASMA 102 and 108 with parts that are not identical may weaken the overall assembly and expose the party making an unauthorized substitution to liability"; a part that looks similar may not offer the same strength; differences in material thickness or composition may be invisible; extra fasteners, unique fastener locations, extended roller lengths, higher strength roller bearings and greater track thickness are examples of critical features; a certifying agency may invalidate certification if a component is substituted contrary to program guidelines.
  https://www.dasma.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/12/TDS183.pdf (also at /wp-content/uploads/pubs/TechDataSheets/CommercialResidential/TDS183.pdf)
- 16 CFR part 1211 (govinfo, CFR 2015 title 16 vol 2): applies to residential garage door operators manufactured on or after January 1, 1993 for sale in the United States; 1211.6(a) primary inherent entrapment protection; 1211.7(a)(1) except during the first 1 foot of travel from the full upmost position, reversal must begin within 2 seconds of contact and the door must return to and stop at the full upmost position; 1211.7(f)(1) an inherent system monitoring door position must do so at increments no greater than 1 inch; 1211.7(g)(1) if the lower limiting device is not actuated within 30 seconds of a close cycle the operator must reverse; 1211.6(b) secondary options include constant-pressure control, a connection for an external device, or an inherent secondary device; required instructions include the monthly test that the door must reverse on a 1½ inch object or a 2 by 4 laid flat.
  https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CFR-2015-title16-vol2/pdf/CFR-2015-title16-vol2-part1211.pdf

## Notes / discrepancies
- The task brief said Carrollton spans Dallas, Denton and Collin counties. site.json lists only Dallas and Denton. Per the brief's own rule, site.json wins; copy names Dallas and Denton only.
- The brief assumed TDI publishes a position on cosmetic damage exclusions generally. What TDI actually publishes (B-0030-98) is a prescribed cosmetic exclusion for ROOF COVERINGS only, tied to an impact-resistant roof premium credit and a signed endorsement. Copy states it that way.
- Texas appraisal: Chapter 1813 was added by SB 458 effective September 1, 2025 and requires an appraisal provision in residential property policies; it does not itself publish a day count for completing an appraisal, which the commissioner sets by rule. Copy says only what the statute says.
