## Differentiator: Florida's 2023 statutory overhaul

### Alimony — Fla. Stat. 61.08
Source: https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2025/61.08

- Statutory forms are temporary, bridge-the-gap, rehabilitative and durational. Permanent alimony is not among them.
- 61.08(11): the section applies to all initial petitions for dissolution pending or filed on or after July 1, 2023. History cite: s. 1, ch. 2023-315.
- 61.08(2)(a): the court must first make a specific factual determination of actual need and ability to pay; the burden is on the party seeking support.
- 61.08(5): short-term marriage is under 10 years; moderate-term is 10 to 20 years; long-term is 20 years or more. Length runs from the marriage date to the date the petition is filed.
- Bridge-the-gap: maximum 2 years, not modifiable.
- Rehabilitative: maximum 5 years, requires a specific written rehabilitative plan.
- Durational: unavailable for a marriage under 3 years. Caps at 50% (short-term), 60% (moderate-term), 75% (long-term) of the length of the marriage. Extension only on clear and convincing evidence of exceptional circumstances.
- 61.08(8)(c): amount is the obligee's reasonable need, or not more than 35 percent of the difference between the parties' net incomes, whichever is less.
- 61.08(9): an award may not leave the payor with significantly less net income than the recipient absent written findings.

### Retirement-based modification — Fla. Stat. 61.14(1)(c)
Source: https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2025/61.14
- The court may reduce or terminate support on written findings that the obligor has reached normal retirement age as defined by the Social Security Administration, or the customary retirement age for the profession, AND has taken demonstrative and measurable steps toward retirement or has retired.
- Obligor carries the burden by a preponderance; the burden then shifts to the obligee.
- 61.14(1)(c)3: a modification petition may be filed in reasonable anticipation of retirement, but no more than 6 months before.
- 61.14(1)(b): supportive-relationship reduction or termination, with a 365-day lookback.

### Time-sharing — Fla. Stat. 61.13
Source: https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2025/61.13
- 61.13(2)(c)1: "there is a rebuttable presumption that equal time-sharing of a minor child is in the best interests of the minor child." Rebuttal is by a preponderance of the evidence. Except where the parties agree and the court approves, the court must evaluate all of the subsection (3) factors and make specific written findings when creating or modifying a schedule.
- 61.13(2)(c): modification requires a substantial and material change of circumstances.
- 61.13(2)(b): the parenting plan must at minimum describe daily-task sharing, the time-sharing schedule, designate responsibility for health care and school matters (including the address used for school-boundary determination), describe methods and technologies for communicating with the child, and designate authorized exchange locations.
- Shared parental responsibility is ordered unless detrimental; rebuttable presumptions of detriment attach to a first-degree-misdemeanor-or-higher domestic violence conviction, the s. 39.806(1)(d) criteria, and enumerated s. 943.0435(1)(h)1.a. offenses.

### Child support — Fla. Stat. 61.30
Source: https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2025/61.30
- 61.30(11)(b)8: a "substantial amount of time" means a parent exercises time-sharing at least 20 percent of the overnights of the year.
- Gross-up: each parent's obligation (excluding day care and health insurance) is multiplied by 1.5, overnight percentages are cross-multiplied, and the difference is the transfer payment; day care and health insurance are then added back.
- A deviation of more than 5 percent from the guideline amount requires a written finding.
- Deviation factor (a)10 covers a child spending significant time, but less than 20 percent of overnights, with one parent. Factor (a)9 covers an obligation exceeding 55 percent of gross income.
- 61.30(1)(b): to modify, the guideline difference must be at least 15 percent or $50, whichever is greater.
- Voluntary unemployment or underemployment supports imputed income, with a rebuttable presumption of the median income of year-round full-time workers per Census reports.
- 61.30(17): the court may award retroactive support back to the date the parents stopped living together with the child, not to exceed 24 months before the petition was filed, and should consider an installment plan.

### Equitable distribution — Fla. Stat. 61.075 and 61.076
Sources: https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2025/61.075 and https://www.flhouse.gov/Statutes/2025/Chapter0061/All/
- The court sets apart nonmarital assets, then begins with the premise that distribution should be equal unless there is justification for an unequal split under the listed factors.
- Factors include contribution to the marriage including as homemaker, economic circumstances, duration, career or education interruption, desirability of keeping a business intact, desirability of retaining the marital home for a dependent child, and intentional dissipation or waste within 2 years before filing or after filing.
- 61.075(6)(a) marital: assets acquired during the marriage; enhancement or appreciation of a nonmarital asset from either party's efforts or from marital funds; paydown of principal on a note secured by nonmarital real property plus a coverture-fraction share of passive appreciation; interspousal gifts (real property requires a writing under s. 689.01); all vested and nonvested retirement, pension, profit-sharing and deferred-comp benefits accrued during the marriage; marital interests in a closely held business at fair market value, with enterprise goodwill a marital asset. Tenancy-by-the-entireties property is presumed marital.
- 61.075(6)(b) nonmarital: pre-marital assets, noninterspousal gift or bequest or devise or descent, income from nonmarital assets unless treated as marital, assets excluded by valid written agreement, forgery liabilities.
- 61.075(7): the cut-off date is the earliest of a valid separation agreement date, a date set in that agreement, or the date the petition is filed. The valuation date is what the judge finds just and equitable, and different assets may be valued as of different dates.
- 61.075(8): assets acquired after the marriage date are presumed marital.
- 61.075(3): in a contested case with no stipulation, the distribution requires specific written findings clearly identifying nonmarital assets and ownership interests, identifying marital assets with individual valuation of significant assets, identifying marital liabilities and who is responsible for each, and any other findings explaining the rationale.
- 61.075(5): on good cause the court may enter an interim partial distribution order during the case.
- 61.076(1): all vested and nonvested benefits accrued during the marriage in retirement, pension, profit-sharing, annuity, deferred compensation and insurance plans are marital assets.
- 61.076(2): if the parties were married at least 10 years during which a uniformed-services member performed at least 10 years of creditable service and the division includes uniformed services retired or retainer pay, the final judgment must identify the member, certify that the Servicemembers Civil Relief Act was observed if the decree issued while the member was on active duty and unrepresented, and state the amount in dollars or as a percentage of disposable retired pay.
- 61.076(3): such an order may not require payment more frequently than monthly.

### Residency and waiting period
- Fla. Stat. 61.021: one party must reside in the state 6 months before filing. https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2025/61.021
- Fla. Stat. 61.19: no final judgment until at least 20 days have elapsed from the filing of the original petition, unless the court finds that injustice would result from the delay. https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2025/61.19
- So Florida has no separation period, but there is a 20-day floor before a judgment.

### Relocation — Fla. Stat. 61.13001
Source: https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2025/61.13001
- "Relocation" means a change of principal residence at least 50 miles from the residence at the time of the last order or the pending action, for at least 60 consecutive days, not counting a temporary absence for vacation, education or the child's health care.
- Parents and everyone else entitled to time-sharing may consent in a signed written agreement that reflects consent, defines a time-sharing schedule for the nonrelocating parent, and describes transportation arrangements.

## Paternity

- Fla. Stat. 742.011 (amended by s. 1, ch. 2023-209): any pregnant woman, any man with reason to believe he is the father, or any child may bring a circuit-court proceeding. After birth a parent may request a determination of parental responsibility, child support, and creation of a parenting plan and time-sharing schedule under chapter 61. https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2025/742.011
- Fla. Stat. 742.10: a notarized voluntary acknowledgment creates a rebuttable presumption of paternity, rescindable within 60 days after signing or the date of a related administrative or judicial proceeding, whichever is earlier. After 60 days it establishes paternity and may be challenged only for fraud, duress or material mistake of fact, with the burden on the challenger and support not suspended during the challenge absent good cause. 742.10(5): however paternity was established, parental responsibility, child support, a parenting plan and time-sharing must still be established in an action under 742.011. https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2025/742.10
- Fla. Stat. 742.12: scientific testing. Written objections to results must be filed at least 10 days before the hearing. A statistical probability of 95 percent or more creates a rebuttable presumption of biological fatherhood and can support summary judgment. If the results exclude the alleged father the case is dismissed with prejudice. Costs are apportioned by the court. https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2025/742.12
- Ch. 2023-209 (effective July 1, 2023) amended Fla. Stat. 744.301(1): the mother of a child born out of wedlock and a father who has established paternity under 742.011 or 742.10 are both natural guardians. If the father has not established paternity, the mother is the natural guardian and is entitled to primary residential care and custody unless a court orders otherwise. https://laws.flrules.org/2023/209 — https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2025/744.301

## Mandatory financial disclosure — Fla. Fam. L. R. P. 12.285
Source: https://www.flcourts.gov/content/download/217912/file/Family-Law-Rules-of-Procedure.pdf

- 12.285(a)(1) excepts adoption, simplified dissolution, enforcement, contempt, protective injunctions, and uncontested dissolutions served by publication with no answer. The financial affidavit and child support guidelines worksheet provisions cannot be modified by agreement.
- 12.285(b)(2): documents are served within 45 days of service of the initial pleading on the respondent.
- 12.285(b)(1)(B): in a temporary financial relief proceeding, the responding party serves by 5:00 p.m. 2 business days before the hearing if by delivery, or 7 days before if by mail or e-mail, with no less than 12 days allowed.
- 12.285(e)(1): Form 12.902(b) if gross annual income is under $50,000; Form 12.902(c) if $50,000 or more. The affidavit requirement cannot be waived by the parties, and the affidavits must be filed with the court.
- Required documents include 3 years of tax returns; W-2, 1099 and K-1 forms for the past year if the return is unprepared; pay stubs for the 3 months before service; loan applications and financial statements from the prior 12 months; deeds from the last 3 years; promissory notes from the last 12 months; present leases; 3 months of checking statements and 12 months for other accounts; brokerage statements; the most recent retirement plan statement plus the summary plan description; life insurance declarations pages and health and dental cards; corporate, partnership and trust returns for 3 years where ownership is 30 percent or more; credit card and charge account statements; all premarital and marital agreements; and every document supporting a nonmarital, enhancement or unequal-distribution claim.

## Simplified dissolution — Form 12.901(a) instructions
Source: https://www.flcourts.gov/content/download/685807/file_pdf/901a.pdf
Requirements: both agree the marriage cannot be saved; no minor or dependent children in common; the wife has no minor or dependent children born during the marriage; the wife is not pregnant; assets and liabilities are already divided to both parties' satisfaction; neither seeks alimony; both give up the right to trial and appeal; both are willing to go into the clerk's office to sign the petition; both attend the final hearing at the same time; the 6-month residency is met.

## Military dimension — a genuinely Tampa fact pattern

- MacDill Air Force Base: host wing is the 6th Air Refueling Wing with 3,122 personnel, 33 mission partners, 5,695 acres, and two joint command headquarters — U.S. Central Command (established at MacDill January 1, 1983) and U.S. Special Operations Command (activated April 16, 1987).
  https://www.macdill.af.mil/About-Us/Fact-Sheets/Article/4160667/macdill-air-force-base/
  https://www.macdill.af.mil/About-Us/Fact-Sheets/Article/231802/6th-air-refueling-wing/
- Florida's Uniform Deployed Parents Custody and Visitation Act, Part IV of chapter 61, ss. 61.703 to 61.773, created by s. 1, ch. 2018-69. Source: https://www.flhouse.gov/Statutes/2025/Chapter0061/All/
  - 61.703(9): "deployment" means mobilization for less than 18 months under orders that are designated unaccompanied, do not authorize dependent travel, or otherwise do not permit family members to move to the location.
  - 61.709(1)(a): a deploying parent must notify the other parent of a pending deployment in a record not later than 7 days after receiving notice of deployment, unless reasonably prevented by the circumstances of service.
  - 61.709(1)(b): a proposed plan allocating each parent's share of custodial responsibility during deployment must follow as soon as reasonably possible.
  - 61.713: "a court may not consider a parent's past deployment or possible future deployment in determining the best interest of the child."
  - 61.761(3): absent an agreement to terminate, a temporary agreement granting custodial responsibility automatically terminates 30 days after the deploying parent gives notice of return.
  - 61.763: from return until termination, the court shall issue a temporary order granting the deploying parent reasonable contact, even if it exceeds the pre-deployment time.
- SCRA stay, 50 U.S.C. § 3932: applies to any civil action or proceeding, including any child custody proceeding, where the servicemember is in military service or within 90 days after release and has notice. On application the court shall stay the action for not less than 90 days. The application must include a communication stating how current duty materially affects the ability to appear and a date when the member will be available, plus a letter from the commanding officer stating that duty prevents appearance and that leave is not authorized. Filing the application is not an appearance and waives no defense. If an additional stay is refused, the court shall appoint counsel. https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/50/3932
- USFSPA, 10 U.S.C. § 1408: after effective service the Secretary concerned pays child support, alimony, or a stated property-division amount from disposable retired pay. § 1408(e)(1): the total under all court orders may not exceed 50 percent of disposable retired pay. § 1408(d)(2): direct property-division payments require 10 years of marriage overlapping 10 years of creditable service. Payments begin no later than 90 days after effective service and no more often than monthly. Percentage property awards rise with COLAs under § 1401a. Multiple orders are satisfied first-come, first-served. The combined cap with 42 U.S.C. § 659 process is 65 percent. https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/10/1408

## Fees

- R. Regulating Fla. Bar 4-1.5(f)(3)(A): no fee in a domestic relations matter may be contingent on securing a divorce or on the amount of alimony, support, or property settlement in lieu thereof. A carve-out permits a contingent fee for recovering post-judgment balances due under support, alimony or other financial orders. 4-1.5(f)(2) requires a signed written contract.
- Fla. Stat. 61.16(1): the court may order one party to pay a reasonable sum for the other's attorney fees, suit money and costs after considering the financial resources of both parties. https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2025/61.16
