## FEMA / ICC on garage doors and wind
- FEMA P-2181 Fact Sheet 3.2: "Garage doors can be relatively large, making them one of the largest openings in a building." "Because of their relative size, garage doors can be vulnerable to the wind blowing them in, pulling them out, or twisting them off their tracks." Recommends a **center post brace anchored into the garage slab and ceiling framing to reduce the unbraced length of the garage door**; replacement doors rated for the wind speed and pressure of the area; ASTM E1233 / DASMA TDS 115 cyclic and missile testing. https://www.fema.gov/sites/default/files/documents/fema_p-2181-fact-sheet-3-2-wall-openings.pdf
- ICC Building Safety Journal: garage doors are "generally the largest product in a single-family home"; FEMA Mitigation Assessment Team report "cited garage door damage as a key contributor to cascading pressurization of the building that led to a blow-off of the roof assembly"; IRC R309.4 requires openers listed and labeled to UL 325. https://www.iccsafe.org/building-safety-journal/bsj-technical/garage-door-provisions-in-the-international-residential-code/

## DASMA
- **TDS 152** Garage Doors and High Wind Events: "Garage doors are typically in the largest openings associated with building structures." A door is subject to being blown into the garage or pulled out of the opening, so **both positive and negative pressure ratings matter**. Positioning a vehicle against the door is not recommended. Keeping a door open during a high wind event leaves interior walls, ceilings and roof structure vulnerable to structural damage and possible collapse. DASMA does not recommend operating doors during high wind events. Adding non-manufacturer-specified reinforcement adds weight and may cause failure of tracks or other components. https://www.dasma.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/01/TDS152.pdf
- **TDS 153** Vertically Reinforcing Sectional Garage Doors: manufacturers commonly specify horizontal struts; where those are insufficient a vertical post system is required; posts transmit wind load to the opening header and the garage floor; a qualified design professional should assess the structure before installing vertical reinforcement; aftermarket posts may be a "component substitution." https://www.dasma.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/05/TDS153.pdf
- **TDS 192** Securing Garage Doors During High Wind Events: door sections catch the wind and push the door against the vertical track; **the vertical track is tilted back** so the door does not rub in normal operation, and **the top rollers typically rest in the curved portion of the track when the door is closed**; that combination converts part of the wind force into an **upward force that drives the door open**; the greater the wind force, the greater the lift. Post-Andrew studies found garage doors in the storm path had been opened — by the wind, not by homeowners — and once the opening is breached, pressure builds in the garage, "possibly blowing off the roof." Resistance to the door's tendency to rise depends partly on the mechanical connection of door to operator and on the status of the counterbalance system. https://www.dasma.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/12/TDS192.pdf
- **TDS 193** Wind Exposure Categories: exposure category can change design wind pressure for vehicular access doors by **30% or more**; Exposure B = urban/suburban or wooded terrain with numerous closely spaced obstructions the size of single-family dwellings or larger prevailing more than 1,500 ft in any direction; Exposure C = open terrain with scattered obstructions generally under 30 ft; ASCE 7 commentary indicates **60% to 80% of buildings correspond to Exposure B**; where IRC R301.2.1.4 is unchanged, exposure for multiple detached one- and two-family structures is based on site conditions **when all adjacent structures on the site have been constructed**, provided construction starts within one year. https://www.dasma.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/03/TDS193.pdf
- **TDS 161** Connecting Garage Door Jambs to Building Framing: schedules cover 10–60 psf; required design load is the positive wind load; may come from the DASMA wind load guide, a job-specific calculation, or a conservative design pressure from the local building department; supporting structural elements to be designed by a registered professional engineer; spacing under 6 in not recommended in concrete, under 8 in not allowed in CMU; allowable loads e.g. 556 lb/anchor at 2,500 psi concrete, 609 at 3,000 psi. https://www.dasma.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/05/TDS-161.pdf
- **TDS 1502** IRC and Wind Load Labels: quotes **R609.4** (garage doors tested to ASTM E330 or ANSI/DASMA 108 and meeting DASMA 108 pass/fail) and **R609.4.1** (permanent manufacturer label identifying manufacturer, model/series number, positive and negative design wind pressure rating, installation instruction drawing reference number, applicable test standard); labeling is new with the 2021 I-codes; not all doors are wind-load rated and a non-rated door gets no label; the label must state pressure in psf, not mph; the AHJ is the final authority; design pressure depends on exposure category, mean roof height, effective wind area, wind zone and topography. https://www.dasma.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/01/TDS-1502.pdf
- **TDS 190** Factors Affecting Spring Cycle Life: spring cycle life may not coincide with door cycle life (ANSI/DASMA 109 determines door cycle life, failure = any component causing the door to cease operation); torch-cutting a spring to length can leave brittle spots from overheating or molten splatter that cause premature failure; a nick or notch from dropping or throwing a spring creates a weak point; rust reduces effective wire area, corrosion pits accelerate fatigue cracks; applying a spring without confirming it meets the door's counterbalance requirement reduces cycle life of the door assembly **and of the motor**; excessive turns overload other counterbalance components, insufficient turns let the door drift down from the open position; paint or added components can push door weight past the manufacturer's specification and can be measured with a scale after the counterbalance is disabled (see TDS-176). https://www.dasma.com/wp-content/uploads/pubs/TechDataSheets/CommercialResidential/TDS190.pdf
- **TDS 174 / 175** (occupant and technician damage checks after a high wind event): occupants must not adjust doors or springs; inspection sequence is door alignment, opening frame (jambs and header attachment to the structure), track system for loose fasteners, twisting, the track "opening up" or bending, rollers, roller brackets and hinges; a technician should judge whether the system would withstand another event and, if stress or fatigue has compromised performance, advise the owner to forward that assessment to the insurer; a registered design professional may be needed. https://www.dasma.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/09/TDS174.pdf , https://www.dasma.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/12/TDS175.pdf
- **ANSI/DASMA 102**: "One cycle of a door is complete when it is moved from the closed position to the fully open position and returned to the closed position." Cycle-life testing per ANSI/DASMA 109. Torsion springs shall not balance sectional doors with an index ratio (mean coil diameter / wire diameter) of less than 6 to 1. Steel gauge per TDS 154. https://www.dasma.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/01/ANSIDASMA102.pdf

## 16 CFR Part 1211 (govinfo, 2025 CFR)
https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CFR-2025-title16-vol2/pdf/CFR-2025-title16-vol2-part1211.pdf
- 1211.1: applies to **all residential garage door operators manufactured on or after January 1, 1993** for sale in the US. 1211.2: a residential garage door operator serves a building of **one to four single-family units**, rated 600 volts or less.
- 1211.6: inherent primary entrapment protection required, **plus** one of: constant-pressure line-of-sight control that reverses to the upmost position when pressure is released and a supplied transmitter that can only open; a means to connect an **external** secondary device; or an **inherent** secondary device. A switch or relay in an entrapment circuit must survive 100,000 cycles; a malfunction must leave the door within 1 foot of the uppermost position.
- 1211.7: except for the first **1 foot** of travel from full up, the operator must begin reversal **within 2 seconds** of contact and return the door to and stop it at the full upmost position; test uses a **1-inch-high** obstruction on the floor and a 25 lbf pull or rated pull, whichever is greater; a system that monitors actual door position must do so **in increments no greater than 1 inch**; a system that does **not** monitor position must reverse and return to full up if the lower limit device is not actuated **within 30 seconds or less** after the close cycle starts.
- 1211.8: secondary devices are an **external photoelectric sensor**, an **external edge sensor**, an **inherent door sensor** independent of the 1211.7 system, or an equivalent device. Under 1211.8(c) the operator must **monitor for the presence and correct operation of the secondary device at least once during each close cycle**; if the device is absent or faulted (including an interrupted wireless signal or an open or short in the wiring), a closing door must open and **an open door must not close more than 1 foot below the upmost position**.
- 1211.11(d)(2): photoelectric test rod axis fixed **36 inches above the level surface below the door**.
- Endurance conditioning is run at **60°C (140°F)** ambient, or 18°F above the control's operating temperature; cold impact conditioning for outdoor devices is 3 hours at **-31.0 ±3.6°F**.
- Combination sectional overhead operator systems must comply with ANSI/DASMA 102 (incorporated by reference at 1211.6(c)).

## Colorado HOA law
- C.R.S. **38-33.3-302(3)(b)**: decisions approving or denying an owner's application for architectural or landscaping changes must be made in accordance with standards and procedures in the declaration, or duly adopted rules, regulations or bylaws, and **may not be made arbitrarily or capriciously**. https://dre.colorado.gov/hoa-frequently-asked-questions
- Same source: governing-document hierarchy is declaration (CCRs) > articles > bylaws > governance policies > rules and regulations > design guidelines; **the declaration is the highest authority**.
- Colorado legislative staff summary of CCIOA homeowner rights: the statutory list of things an association may not prohibit covers flags and political signs (38-33.3-106.5(1)(a)-(d)), replacing cedar shakes with less flammable roofing even where architectural standards require shakes (106.5(2)), defensible-space vegetation removal (106.5(1)(e)), disability modifications (106.5(1)(g)), xeriscape (106.5(1)(i)), rain barrels (106.5(1)(j)), renewable energy devices (106.5(1.5)), energy-efficiency measures including a garage or attic fan and its vents (106.7), and EV charging (106.8). **Garage door appearance is not on that list.** https://content.leg.colorado.gov/sites/default/files/homeowners-rights-in-ccioa.pdf
